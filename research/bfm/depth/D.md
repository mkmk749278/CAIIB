# Module D (Balance Sheet Management): depth checklist

Draft 1, 2026-11-20. Purpose: make sure our Module D lessons (D01–D07, plus B04–B08 for capital and B13/B13b/B14 for
liquidity) miss no sub-point the exam needs, by comparing our plans with what two public coaching classes teach under
each official syllabus sub-topic.

**Inputs used**
- Scope: `research/bfm/syllabus_official.md` (Module D, D1.1–D6.3) and `research/bfm/coverage.csv` (D rows; D5.3 and
  D5.4 are marked "partial"; C7.2/C7.5 partial rows point at D01 too).
- Our plans: `course/bfm/D/plans_D.md` (draft 1), `course/bfm/B/plans_B.md` (B01, B04–B08, B13, B14),
  `research/bfm/course_plan.md` §3 (incl. the B13b row, which has no plan text yet).
- Rule version: `research/bfm/regulatory_updates.md` (31 Dec 2026 cut-off).
- Two public YouTube class transcripts (auto-captions): "OB" = a third-party MCQ-style module D class (~3 h 38 min);
  "PR" = a third-party module D revision class (~3 h 31 min).

**Caveats**
- The transcripts are auto-captions with many recognition errors; some questions in them are incomplete or have
  disputed answers (PR says so itself more than once).
- They are used **only** to see which sub-points teachers cover and how deep. Nothing here is copied: no sentences,
  examples, numbers, mnemonics or questions. Where a class states a rule, %, limit or date, this file only records that
  the point is covered and names the primary source to check.
- [both] = both classes cover it; [one: OB] / [one: PR] = only that class.
- "Plan:" = whether our current plan has it: **yes / partly / no**.
- Facts come only from primary sources: RBI *Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and
  Provisioning Directions, 2025* ("IRACP MD"), *– Asset Liability Management Directions, 2025* ("ALM MD"), *– Prudential
  Norms on Capital Adequacy Directions, 2025* ("Capital MD"), the stressed-assets / resolution directions, the
  interest-rate-on-advances directions, BR Act 1949 (s. 29, Third Schedule), BCBS texts (Basel II/III, IRRBB 2016),
  IBC 2016. Versions as in force on **31 Dec 2026**.

## D1 Components of assets and liabilities; ALM

### D1.1 Components of a bank's balance sheet · our video: D01
Teachers cover:
- [both] Sources of funds (capital, reserves, deposits, borrowings, other liabilities) vs uses/application of funds
  (cash and RBI balances, bank balances and call/notice money, investments, advances, fixed assets, other assets);
  the two sides always equal. Plan: **yes**.
- [one: PR] Legal basis: BR Act s. 29; balance sheet and P&L in the forms of the Third Schedule (Form A, Form B);
  numbered schedules (balance sheet schedules incl. contingent liabilities; P&L schedules for interest earned, other
  income, interest expended, operating expenses). Plan: **partly** (Third Schedule named; no schedule map, no P&L side).
- [one: PR] Where items go in the P&L: interest earned vs other income (commission, brokerage, profit on sale /
  revaluation of investments, miscellaneous recoveries); interest expended vs operating expenses (e.g. deposit
  insurance premium, depreciation on bank property incl. leasehold, advertisement and printing); provisions and
  contingencies kept outside operating expenses. Plan: **no**.
- [both] Line-by-line placement traps: overdue term deposits stay in deposits; refinance from NABARD/EXIM and RBI
  borrowing are borrowings, not deposits; bills payable, interest accrued, inter-office adjustment, tax provisions are
  other liabilities; debentures are investments, not advances; advances shown by security (tangible security / bank or
  government guarantee / unsecured). Plan: **partly**.
- [both] Why each item behaves as it does: capital as permanent, loss-absorbing, last to be repaid; reserves as
  retained profit (statutory, capital, revenue, share premium, P&L balance); deposits as the main and cheapest source,
  with demand deposits carrying most liquidity risk; borrowings costlier and more volatile; balances with RBI above
  CRR kept for liquidity; G-secs dominate investments because of SLR and liquidity; advances carry the highest
  risk-return; fixed assets are non-earning. Plan: **partly** (shelves picture; reasons not listed).
- [one: OB] Call money (overnight) vs notice money vs term money: the difference is tenor. Plan: **no**.
- [one: OB] Contingent (off-balance sheet) items as part of the picture. Plan: **no** (D01); covered for capital in B06.

Add to our plan (D01):
1. A one-screen map of the Third Schedule forms and schedule numbers, both balance sheet and P&L (Form A/B, s. 29).
2. P&L classification drill: which income/expense goes under which head (statement-type questions).
3. Placement traps card: overdue deposits, refinance, bills payable, debentures, advances by security.
4. Call/notice/term money tenors (one line, linked to C01/C02).
5. One line per balance-sheet item on *why* it behaves as it does (cost, stability, risk, earning or non-earning).
Verify: BR Act 1949 s. 29 and Third Schedule (current forms and notes); RBI master direction on financial statements
(presentation and disclosure) for P&L heads; call/notice/term money tenors in RBI money-market directions.

### D1.2 What is ALM; significance; purpose and objectives · our video: D01
Teachers cover:
- [both] ALM defined as planning, acquiring and directing the flow of funds; ultimate aim: stable earnings (NII/NIM)
  and growth in net worth with risk kept within limits; covers both sides, not only liabilities and not only
  short-term liquidity. Plan: **partly** (misconception "ALM is only liquidity" listed).
- [both] Why ALM matters now: rate volatility after deregulation, product innovation, embedded options (prepayment,
  premature withdrawal), regulatory environment, management's view of risk and return. Plan: **no**.
- [one: OB] Two strands of ALM: price matching (lending yields above cost of funds; protect the spread) and
  liquidity matching (group assets and liabilities by maturity and study gaps). Plan: **no**.
- [both] NII and NIM defined (NII = interest earned − interest expended; NIM = NII ÷ average assets). Plan: **yes**
  (in D05), but see the denominator conflict below.
- [one: PR] A full NIM sum from portfolio yields and costs, with an adjustment for interest booked but not realised.
  Plan: **partly** (D05 has a NIM numerical; no unrealised-interest twist).
- [both] ALCO: who sits, its authority over the balance-sheet mix and pricing. Plan: **yes**.

Add to our plan (D01): the "why ALM matters now" list; price matching vs liquidity matching; ALM objectives in one
picture (stable NII/NIM, equity value, risk limits). (D05): a NIM case built from yields and costs, including the
reversal of unrealised interest on NPAs.
Verify: ALM MD 2025 (ALM purpose, ALCO composition and role); NIM denominator: our D05 plan says "average earning
assets", both classes use "average total assets". Check IIBF's own text and RBI disclosure definitions, teach the one
IIBF uses, and make it a trap card.

### D1.3 ALM as co-ordinated balance sheet management · our video: D01
Teachers cover:
- [one: OB] Balance sheet management coordinates asset side (liquidity, investments, loans, fixed assets), liability
  side (deposits, borrowings, capital) and income/expense management; profit depends on all of them.
  Plan: **no** (D01 chapters do not show the "coordination" view).
- [one: OB] Economic equity ratio (owners' funds ÷ total assets) as a shock-absorber measure; a fall means more
  leverage. Plan: **no**.
- [one: PR] Case: maturity mismatch, embedded options exercised by borrowers and depositors, rate cut squeezing NIM;
  identify liquidity, reinvestment/refinancing and interest-rate risks together. Plan: **no**.

Add to our plan (D01): a short chapter or picture showing ALM as the hub that ties asset, liability, income and
expense decisions (the syllabus wording itself asks for it); economic equity ratio (definition only); one
mixed-risk mini case.
Verify: standard definitions (no RBI number); keep our own wording.

## D5 Interest rate risk management

### D5.2 Measurement and measurement techniques · our videos: D05, D06
Teachers cover:

  sums. Plan: **yes**.

  ignores embedded options and changes in timing, ignores time value, assumes a parallel shift. Plan: **no**.
- [one: OB] Duration-based change in economic value (ΔEVE ≈ −duration gap × Δy) and its use for capital. Plan: **yes**
  (D06).
- [one: PR] Range of techniques: gap → duration → simulation (static/dynamic). Plan: **no**.
- [both] Earnings at Risk: likely fall in income from adverse rate moves at a confidence level; short-term focus.
  Plan: **no**.
Add to our plan: (D05) gap-limitations trap card; EaR definition; techniques ladder (gap, duration, simulation).
Verify: ALM MD 2025 (TGA/DGA statements, rate-shock size, EaR limits if any).

### D5.3 Strategies for controlling interest rate risk · our video: D06 (ledger: partial)
Teachers cover:

- [one: PR] Off-balance-sheet hedges: swaps, futures (FRAs). Plan: **no** (C08/C09 teach the products).
Add to our plan (D06): a short "what the bank can do" chapter: on-balance-sheet moves vs derivative hedges; aggressive
vs defensive gap management, with a matrix (expected rate move × gap sign → action). This closes the "partial" row.
Verify: ALM MD 2025 / Miscellaneous Directions on hedging the banking book with derivatives (note the PV01 cap was
