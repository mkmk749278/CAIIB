# Book check: units A9 (IFSC, GIFT City) and A10 (Technology in international banking)

Done 2026-11-26. We compared the book's Unit 9 (sections 9.1–9.8, about 8 pages) with A20 (draft), and its Unit 10
(sections 10.1–10.8, about 20 pages) with A22 (draft). Both lessons are unlocked Gemini drafts, so these findings go
into their **full independent reviews**. Written in our own words.

## A9 → A20: verdict
- **Scope:** covered. A20 has the following:
  - USD 20 m capital; CRR/SLR exemption; the 3 % retail deposit reserve ratio;
  - exposure limits; no savings accounts or cheques; foreign currency only (except a rupee account for expenses);
  - SNRR; outside PSL; no RBI liquidity support;
  - IFSCA (27 Apr 2020, IFSCA Act 2019); SEZ Act 2005; STT exemption; FPI relaxations; LCR/NSFR; one IBU per IFSC;
  - deposits not insured; escrow accounts.
- **Gaps:** none.
- **Stale points to check in the review:**

| # | Book says (our words) | Check | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | IBUs need RBI's prior permission and are "regulated and supervised by RBI"; derivatives need RBI approval | Since 2020 the licence and regulation are IFSCA's (IFSCA Banking Regulations 2020). The parent's home regulator still applies prudential rules | A20 review: confirm that A20 says IFSCA licenses and regulates, and add "RBI licenses IBUs ✗ (old)" as a trap |
| S2 | IBU exposure 5 % / 10 % of the parent's Tier I (single / group) | The book takes this from RBI's 2015 IBU circular. Check the current IFSCA rule or the parent's large-exposure framework | A20 review: verify A20's "5 % / 10 %" against the current text |
| S3 | IBUs cannot take deposits from individuals | IFSCA now allows some retail and individual deposits (hence the 3 % retail deposit reserve ratio, in the same unit). The book contradicts itself | A20 review: confirm A20 teaches the current IFSCA position |
