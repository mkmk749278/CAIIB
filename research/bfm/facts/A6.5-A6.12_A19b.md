What this lesson must carry. Foreign investment in India sits under FEMA section 6(2A): the Central Government makes rules for non-debt instruments through the FEM (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 (S.O. 3732(E), updated up to 2 September 2026), while RBI administers them (Rule 2(A)) via the Master Direction – Foreign Investment in India (updated 15 June 2026) and regulates debt instruments under FEMA 396/2019-RB. Non-debt instruments span nine classes—including equity, LLP capital, REITs/InvITs/AIFs, mutual funds/ETFs with more than 50% in equity, immovable property, and equity-backed DRs—whereas debt instruments cover G-Secs, corporate bonds, CPs, and funds with 50% or less in equity. Foreign investment means repatriable investment by a person resident outside India (PROI) in equity instruments of an Indian company or capital of an LLP. In an unlisted Indian company, any equity investment is FDI; in a listed Indian company, 10% or more on a fully diluted basis (counting all convertible instruments as converted) is FDI, while less than 10% is FPI. Once FDI, it stays FDI even if it drops below 10% ("once FDI, always FDI"). Under RBI's 11 November 2024 circular, an FPI breaching 10% gets 5 trading days from settlement to divest or reclassify its entire stake as FDI (requiring prior Government approval if applicable and investee company concurrence; forbidden in FDI-prohibited sectors). Entry is through the automatic route (default 100% for unlisted non-prohibited sectors, except unregulated/unlisted financial services which need Government approval) or Government route (via FIFP). Pricing follows arm's length fair value (floor on entry, ceiling on exit; no assured exit price; valuation certificate max 90 days old). Land-border country investors (and entities with land-border PMLA beneficial owners) need Government approval (from 1 May 2026, multilateral banks where India is a member are carved out); Pakistan investors are also barred from defence, space, and atomic energy; Pakistan and Bangladesh are barred from LLPs, investment vehicles, and convertible notes; Nepal/Bhutan citizens and NRIs there must pay in free foreign exchange for repatriable FDI; OCBs were derecognised on 16 September 2003. From 12 June 2026 (Third Amendment Rules 2026), all individual PROIs (not just NRIs/OCIs) may invest in listed shares under Schedule III (<10% individual, 24% aggregate). NRIs/OCIs investing on a non-repatriation basis (Schedule IV) are treated as deemed domestic (excluded from indirect foreign investment). Eligible investees are Indian companies, LLPs (only in 100% automatic sectors with no FDI-linked performance conditions; FPIs/FVCIs barred), DPIIT-recognised startups (from 4 February 2026 under G.S.R. 108(E), turnover cap doubled to ₹200 crore / 10 years; Deep Tech Startups: ₹300 crore / 20 years), and investment vehicles (REITs, InvITs, AIFs). Downstream investment by a foreign-owned or foreign-controlled Indian entity (FOCC) counts as indirect foreign investment and cannot use domestic borrowings. Core instruments are equity shares (partly paid: 25% upfront, balance in 12 months), fully paid mandatorily convertible CCDs and CCPS, share warrants (25% upfront, balance in 18 months), and startup convertible notes (₹25 lakh minimum single tranche, 10-year tenor), plus bonus (from 11 June 2025, even in prohibited sectors without changing shareholding pattern), rights, ESOPs/sweat equity/SBEB, and cross-border equity swaps (Rule 9A, Aug 2024). Eight sectors are prohibited for FDI (lottery, gambling/betting, chit funds, Nidhi, TDR trading, real estate business/farmhouses, tobacco manufacture, atomic energy/railway operations), plus foreign technology collaboration is barred in lottery and gambling/betting—with the key trap that NRIs/OCIs may subscribe to authorised chit funds without limit on a non-repatriation basis.
