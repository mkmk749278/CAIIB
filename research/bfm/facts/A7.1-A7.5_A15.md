**What this lesson must carry.** RBI's country-risk rules now sit in two of the November 2025 consolidated
directions: the **policy, rating and monitoring** rules in the Credit Risk Management Directions 2025, ch. VI
(paras 44–55), and the **provisioning table** in the Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning
Directions 2025, para 113. ECGC's grades are the **seven categories A1–D**, published as separate short-term (ST) and
medium/long-term (MLT) lists, issued by quarter, with single-country revisions in between. ECGC's own product sheets
(2025–26 sales literature) give the policy list and the percentage of cover. **The policy pays a share of the loss,
never all of it** (SCR 90 %, SSP / BWP / MBEP 80 %). Labels changed: RBI's para 113 and ECGC's legend now call D
"Very High Risk" and C2 "High Risk"; older books say "restricted" and "off-credit" (trap card).

## Points: risk and the risks in trade (A7.1)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Risk** = the chance that the result differs from what was expected, so that a loss happens; in trade, the chance that the money or the goods do not arrive as agreed. (Our own definition; no regulator defines it.) | own words |
| 2 | **Who suffers what** (the classic list, our own words): **buyer risk / credit risk** (the buyer does not pay: suffered by the exporter); **seller risk** (goods not shipped, short or poor: suffered by the importer, who may have paid in advance); **country risk** (events in the buyer's country stop payment: exporter); **exchange risk** (the rate moves before the money is converted: whoever invoices or pays in a foreign currency); **transport risk** (goods lost or damaged in transit: whoever bears it under the Incoterm, covered by **marine/cargo insurance**, not ECGC); **legal risk** (contract unenforceable abroad); **documentary / operational risk** (discrepancies, errors). A long dealing history lowers buyer and seller risk. | own words; Incoterms (A07); FAQ (marine risk excluded) |
| 3 | **Who carries the risk on each payment method** (link to A08): advance payment → importer; LC → issuing bank's undertaking; collections (D/P, D/A) and open account → exporter. | A08 (UCP 600 / URC 522 frame) |

## Points: country risk (A7.2)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Country risk** (our own words; RBI's current directions give no definition): the risk of loss from events in a **country**, not from the buyer's own fault: government action, war, unrest, payment moratorium, currency controls. **Transfer risk** is one part of it: the buyer **can and wants to pay in local currency**, but the government does not let it be **converted or sent out**. **Sovereign risk**: the borrower is the government itself and it does not pay. So every transfer-risk loss is a country-risk loss, but not the other way round. | own words; FAQ (political risks list) |
| 5 | **What rates a country** (general practice; ECGC does not publish its method): political stability, economic strength (growth, inflation), **external debt** and its servicing, **balance of payments** and foreign-exchange reserves, exchange-rate stability, policy consistency, the legal system. | Leads (OB class); general practice |
| 6 | **ECGC's grades: seven**, legend on its list: **A1 Insignificant · A2 Low Risk · B1 Moderately Low Risk · B2 Moderate Risk · C1 Moderately High Risk · C2 High Risk · D Very High Risk**. Separate **short-term (ST)** and **medium- and long-term (MLT)** lists; a country can sit in different grades on the two. | CCL p.10; circulars page |
| 7 | **How often**: lists are issued **quarter by quarter** (FY 2026–27: w.e.f. 20 May, 1 Jul, 1 Oct, 14 Oct 2026), with **single-country revisions in between** (e.g. Bangladesh 8 Aug 2024). RBI: ECGC gives banks **quarterly updates on request** and alerts them to sudden major changes in between. | circulars page; CRM 51 |
| 8 | **The 1 Jul 2026 MLT list** (illustrative of the shape only; re-read the latest list at the freeze): 237 countries/territories: A1 49 · A2 63 · B1 32 · B2 22 · C1 26 · C2 23 · D 22. Examples: USA, UK, Germany, Japan, Singapore A1; UAE, Saudi Arabia, China A2; Nepal B1; Bangladesh B2; Kenya, Russia C1; Sri Lanka, Ukraine, Iran, Venezuela C2; Afghanistan, Pakistan, Yemen, Sudan D. | CCL |
| 9 | **Open cover vs restricted cover** (ECGC): for the large majority of countries ECGC places **no limit on political-risk cover** (**open cover**); where political risk is very high, cover is **restricted**: revolving limits (normally a year) or **case-by-case specific approval** (normally valid six months). MBEP covers buyers in open-cover countries only; SCR can cover buyers in a **Restricted Cover Category (RCC)** country only with specific approval. (FAQ wording partly from a summary: say "for most countries… for a few high-risk ones…", no group numbers.) | FAQ (summary); SCR; MBE |
| 10 | **RBI rule 1: policy.** Every bank has a **Board-approved country risk management policy** (part of its credit risk policy): identify, measure, monitor, control country exposure; contingency plans and **exit strategies**; reviewed periodically by the Board or its Risk Management Committee. **"For the present"** the full requirement applies to a country where the bank's **net funded exposure is 1 % or more of its total assets**. | CRM 44, 45 |
| 11 | **RBI rule 2: scope.** Country exposure counts **funded** (loans, deposits, investments, trade receivables, nostro/vostro balances…) **and non-funded** (LCs, guarantees, LC confirmations, committed lines…) exposure, from **domestic and foreign branches**. Exposure to an Indian borrower heavily dependent on one country is **indirect** country risk. | CRM 47, 48 |
| 12 | **RBI rule 3: rating and monitoring.** Banks should move to their **own internal rating** of countries (needed where net funded exposure ≥ 1 % of total assets); it may **not be better** than an international rating agency's rating of that country. Ratings reviewed **at least once a year** (sooner after major events). Exposures monitored **on a real-time basis**; stress tests; Board/RMC reviews exits from "high risk & above" countries. **Until** a bank has its own rating it **may use ECGC's seven-category classification** for classification and provisioning. | CRM 49–54, 51 |
| 13 | **RBI rule 4: provisions** on **net funded** country exposure, graded **0.25 % to 100 %**: A1 Insignificant **0.25** · A2 Low **0.25** · B1 Moderately low **5** · B2 Moderate **20** · C1 Moderately high **25** · C2 High **100** · D Very high **100**. Only where net funded exposure to the country is **≥ 1 % of total assets**; **in addition** to asset-classification provisions (but total on a doubtful/loss asset not above 100 % of the outstanding); **no provision on India (home country)**; foreign branches' exposure to their host country **is included**; **short-term** exposures (contractual maturity **under 180 days**) may carry a lower provision, "say **25 %** of the requirement". | IRP 113(1)–(8) |
| 14 | **Trap: old labels.** Older books list "insignificant, low, moderate, high, very high, **restricted, off-credit**" with 0.25/0.25/5/20/25/100/100; today's RBI para and ECGC legend use **moderately low · moderate · moderately high · high · very high** for B1–D. The **percentages are unchanged**. Older books also give a **2 %** threshold; today's text says **1 %**. | IRP 113; CCL legend |

## Points: export credit insurance (A7.3)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | **Export credit insurance** (our own words): insurance that pays the **exporter** (or, under bank covers, the **bank**) a share of the loss when an export sold on credit is **not paid** because of **commercial** or **political** risk. It insures the **payment**, **not the goods**: loss or damage to goods is for **marine / general insurers**. | FAQ (risks not covered: "loss or damage to goods which can be covered by general insurers") |
| 16 | **Why**: lets the exporter offer credit terms (D/A, open account) and enter new markets; makes **bank finance easier** (the policy can be assigned to the bank; A15b); gives information on buyers and countries; helps recover bad debts. | ECO ("How does ECGC help exporters") |
| 17 | **Commercial risks** covered (SCR): buyer's **insolvency**; buyer's **failure to pay** within a set period, **normally four months from the due date** (protracted default); buyer's **failure to accept the goods** (subject to conditions); and, where there is an LC, the **LC opening bank's** insolvency or failure to pay (four months). | SCR product page; FAQ |
| 18 | **Political risks** covered: government action in the buyer's country that **blocks or delays the transfer** of payment; **war, civil war, revolution, civil disturbance**; **new import restrictions or cancellation of a valid import licence**; **interruption or diversion of the voyage** outside India causing extra freight/insurance not recoverable from the buyer; any other cause of loss **outside India**, not normally insured by general insurers, **beyond the control of both** exporter and buyer. | SCR product page; FAQ |
| 19 | **Not covered** (standard policy): **commercial disputes**, including quality disputes (unless the exporter gets a decree from a competent court in the buyer's country); causes **inherent in the goods**; the buyer's failure to get an **import or exchange authorisation** in his country; insolvency or default of the exporter's **agent** or the **collecting bank**; **loss or damage to goods** insurable with general insurers; **exchange-rate fluctuation**; the exporter's **own failure or negligence** under the contract. | FAQ |
| 20 | **Never 100 %**: the policy pays a **percentage** of the loss: **SCR 90 %** ("whether commercial or political" per FAQ); **SSP 80 %**; **BWP 80 %**; **MBEP 80 %** (90 % with extra premium); **MEP 90 %**; BEP 90 % for standard-policy holders, 80 % others. The exporter keeps the rest (**risk-sharing**, so the exporter still chooses buyers carefully). | SCR, SSP, MBE, PRD, FAQ |
| 21 | **Shipment timing**: SCR cover runs **from the date of shipment** (post-shipment risk). **Pre-shipment risks** (contract cancelled, import ban, buyer insolvent before shipment) are **not** under the standard policy; ECGC sells a **pre-shipment risk cover as an add-on** (under MBEP; **manufacturer exporters**; buyer's insolvency, contract frustration, import restriction; **80 %**). | FAQ; MBE |
| 22 | **Credit limit on each buyer**: commercial risk on a buyer is covered only up to the **credit limit ECGC approves** on that buyer (or a discretionary limit within the policy's rules); the exporter applies for it. (A15b detail.) | SCR ("obtaining valid credit limit on buyers and LC opening banks"); FAQ |

## Points: ECGC (A7.4)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 23 | **History**: registered **30 July 1957** in Mumbai as the **Export Risks Insurance Corporation (ERIC)**, a state-owned company; first policy 14 Oct 1957. After it began **covers to banks (1962–64)**, renamed **Export Credit & Guarantee Corporation Ltd (1964)**; **Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India Ltd (1983)**; **ECGC Ltd (August 2014)**. | ECH; ECO |
| 24 | **What it is**: **wholly owned by the Government of India**, under the **Ministry of Commerce & Industry**; Board with Government, RBI, banking, insurance and exporters' representatives; an export-promotion body; registered with **IRDAI** as an insurer (IRDA registration no. 124). | ECO; site footer |
| 25 | **What it does**: (1) **credit risk insurance to exporters** (policies); (2) **export credit insurance to banks** so exporters get better finance (covers; A15b); (3) **overseas investment insurance** for Indian companies' equity or loans in joint ventures abroad. Also: country ratings, buyer credit-worthiness information, help in recovering bad debts. | ECO |
| 26 | **NEIA** (National Export Insurance Account): set up by the **Government of India**, operated by ECGC, managed by the **NEIA Trust**, to cover **medium- and long-term (project) exports** and banks' **buyer's credit** where ECGC alone cannot carry the risk. (A15b: one line.) | NEI |
| 27 | **Policy vs cover (words)**: what ECGC sells **to an exporter** is a **policy**; what it gives **to a bank** is a **cover** or **guarantee** (ECIB, A15b). | ECO; PRD |

## Points: ECGC policies for exporters (A7.5)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | **SCR, Shipments Comprehensive Risks Policy, the "Standard Policy"**: for exporters with anticipated annual export turnover **above ₹5 crore** (and projected premium under ₹20 lakh); **whole-turnover**: **all shipments** must be covered (shipments under LC may be excluded); 12 months; commercial, political and LC-opening-bank risks; **90 %**; premium in advance on projected turnover; **monthly declaration of shipments by the 15th** of the next month; report bills **unpaid 30 days after due date** by the 15th; claim within **360 days from the due date or 540 days from the policy's expiry, earlier**; **no-claim bonus 5 % a year, up to 50 %**. | SCR |
| 29 | **SEP, Small Exporters Policy**: anticipated one-year export turnover **up to ₹5 crore**; maximum liability up to ₹2 crore; essentially the standard policy with improvements: overdues declared after **60 days** (not 30), **waiting period halved to 2 months**; minimum premium ₹5,000. (Percentage of cover not on ECGC's page: do not teach a figure.) | PRD (SEP) |
| 30 | **MEP, Micro Exporters Policy** (erstwhile SME policy, renamed 2014): export turnover **up to ₹100 lakh (₹1 crore)**, any exporter, MSME certificate not needed; **90 %**. | PRD (MEP) |
| 31 | **SSP, Specific Shipment Policy**: **pick and choose**: one contract or shipment, for exporters **without** a whole-turnover policy (or with exclusions); all exporters; **80 %**; non-renewable, no NCB. (A short-term SSP and a medium/long-term SSP both exist.) | SSP; PRD |
| 32 | **BWP, Export (Specific Buyers) Policy**: shipments to **one or more chosen buyers**; for exporters without a standard policy; 12 months; **80 %**. | PRD (BWP) |
| 33 | **ETP, Export Turnover Policy**: for **large exporters** paying **at least ₹20 lakh** premium a year; whole-turnover; **90 %**. | PRD (ETP) |
| 34 | **Exposure-based**: **BEP, Buyer Exposure Policy** (many shipments to **one particular buyer**; premium on the approved loss limit; 90 % for standard-policy holders, 80 % others); **MBEP, Multi-Buyer Exposure Policy** (medium and large exporters with **many buyers**: mid-size turnover ₹20–100 crore, large ₹100 crore+; premium on the **aggregate loss limit**, not on each shipment; **80 %**, 90 % with extra premium). | PRD (BEP); MBE |
| 35 | **Others on ECGC's list** (names only, one line each): **Consignment Exports Policy (Stockholding Agent)**: goods sent abroad to an agent, sold later; **Services Policy** and **Specific Services Policy**; **IT-Enabled Services Policy (single customer)**; **Software Project Policy**; **Construction Works Policy** and **Specific Policy for Supply Contract** (medium/long-term project exports); **Exporter factoring**; **Overseas Investment Insurance** (ECO). | PRD (menu) |
| 36 | **Turnover vs specific** (the exam idea): **whole-turnover** policies (SCR, SEP, ETP) cover **every shipment**, so the bad risks cannot be picked out and premium is lower; **specific** policies (SSP, BWP) cover only what the exporter chooses, at **80 %**. | SCR; SSP; PRD |
