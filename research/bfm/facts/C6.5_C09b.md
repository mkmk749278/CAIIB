# Fact sheet · C6.5 Developments in Indian markets and RBI guidelines on risk exposure (video C09b)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1]–[F36]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text and paragraph number.


What this lesson must carry. Derivatives in India are governed by RBI under Sections 45U and 45W of the RBI Act, 1934 and Sections 10(4) and 11 of FEMA, 1999. For legal validity under Section 45V, at least one party to an OTC derivative must be a market-maker or RBI-regulated entity / CCP. Market-makers quote prices without needing an underlying exposure, while users are classified as non-retail (AIFIs, NBFCs/SPDs/HFCs, IRDAI insurers, PFRDA pension funds, SEBI MFs/AIFs, resident entities with ₹500 crore net worth or ₹1,000 crore turnover, and non-resident non-individuals) or retail (all others, with opt-down and conditional opt-up). Insurers, mutual funds, pension funds, and AIFs deal only with approval of their respective sectoral regulators. Under the Market-makers in OTC Derivatives Directions, 2021, banks must have a Board-approved policy, keep front and back offices physically and functionally separate, and perform suitability and appropriateness checks (not mandatory for plain-vanilla FX forwards and European FX options up to 13 months), verifying that the user has a risk management framework commensurate with the product and giving Product and Risk Disclosure Statements. Single-name CDS and TRS on eligible corporate bonds are permitted (no CDS/TRS to individuals; no related-party reference entity). Standard OTC documentation follows the ISDA 2002 Master Agreement chain: Master Agreement, Schedule, and Confirmation — for a specific trade, the Confirmation prevails over the Schedule, and the Schedule prevails over the printed Master Agreement (s. 1(b)). Three market bodies anchor the market: FEDAI (set up 1958, forex SRO), FIMMDA (incorporated 4 May 1998, fixed income/money/derivatives SRO), and FBIL (incorporated 9 December 2014, RBI-regulated benchmark administrator under s. 45W). Milestones: NSE currency futures started 29 August 2008; IRFs started June 2003, relaunched 31 August 2009, and cash-settled 10-year G-Sec IRFs launched 5 December 2013. Major 2025–2026 RBI changes current on 31 December 2026: no rebooking of cancelled INR FX derivatives (10 Oct 2026; rollover on maturity still allowed); hedging without proving underlying cut from USD 100 million to USD 5 million across all ADs and exchanges with a user undertaking and 2-year document retention; FERR of 20% of INR notional in zero-interest cash with RBI (not CRR, not HQLA, daily CIMS by 5 PM T+1) on user current-account hedges above USD 2 million buying FC against INR (10 Oct 2026) and ADs' own balance-sheet hedges buying FC (12 Oct 2026); NDDCs involving INR to non-residents restricted to hedging only (12 Oct 2026); related-party bar on INR FX derivatives with two exceptions (20 Apr 2026); LEI (20-character) mandatory for non-individual OTC trades and UTI (max 52-character) issued, coming 1 January 2027; 0.25% net worth gross PV01 cap deleted (19 Oct 2026); and OTC novation restricted to market-maker-to-market-maker transfers with upfront MTM exchange and remaining-party consent (22 Sep 2026).
