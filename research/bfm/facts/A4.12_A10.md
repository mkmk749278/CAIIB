## Points
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Complying presentation → must honour**: when the issuing bank finds a presentation complying, it must honour; a confirming bank must honour or negotiate and forward the documents; a nominated bank that honours or negotiates forwards them to the confirming or issuing bank. | UCP 15 |
| 2 | **Issuing bank reimburses the nominated bank** that has honoured or negotiated a complying presentation and forwarded the documents, whether or not the nominated bank prepaid or bought before maturity; this undertaking is independent of the issuing bank's undertaking to the beneficiary. Same for the confirming bank to another nominated bank. | UCP 7(c), 8(c) |
| 3 | **Applicant cannot stop payment** because of a quality or contract dispute: the credit is separate from the sale (Art. 4); banks deal in documents (Art. 5). Only the **fraud exception** (court order; established fraud the bank knows of, or irretrievable injustice) can restrain payment. The bank, not the applicant, decides compliance. | UCP 4, 5; A08 F22 |
| 4 | **Discrepancy → waiver**: the issuing bank may, **in its sole judgement**, approach the applicant for a waiver; this does not extend the five banking days (A09 F11). If the applicant waives and the issuing bank accepts, it honours; the beneficiary's alternative is to correct and re-present within expiry and the 21-day limit. | UCP 16(b); A09 F4, F11 |
| 5 | **Who bears a discrepancy**: the issuing bank reimburses a nominated (or confirming) bank only for a **complying** presentation (Art. 7(c), 8(c)). If a nominated or confirming bank paid against discrepant documents and the issuing bank refuses in time, **the paying bank bears it**: its confirmation covers complying presentations only (Art. 8(a)). It can recover from the beneficiary only if it paid **with recourse / under reserve / against an indemnity** (practice; a non-confirming nominated bank normally negotiates with recourse). A confirming bank that negotiated a **complying** presentation is **without recourse** (Art. 8(a)(ii)). An issuing bank that misses the Art. 16 notice or five days is **precluded** and must pay. | UCP 7(c), 8(a), 8(c), 16(f); A08 F9; recourse: practice |
| 6 | **Refusal notice content** (recap): one notice; each discrepancy; the documents held / held pending waiver / returned / per instructions; by close of the fifth banking day following presentation. | UCP 16(c), (d); A09 F11 |
| 7 | **Transfer to several second beneficiaries** (variant 1): only if the credit is **transferable** and **allows partial drawings or shipments**; transferred **once** (no onward transfer to a third beneficiary, but transfer back to the first beneficiary is not a further transfer); the transferring bank need not transfer except to the extent and manner it **expressly consents**; amount, unit price, expiry, presentation period and latest shipment may be reduced, insurance % increased; the first beneficiary may substitute its own invoice (and draft). Each second beneficiary may accept or reject an amendment for itself. | UCP 38(a), (b), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h); A08 F14 |
| 8 | **Standby nature (ISP98)**: irrevocable, independent, documentary and binding **without saying so**; the issuer's duty to the beneficiary is not affected by its rights against the applicant. | ISP 1.06, 1.07 |
| 9 | **Standby claim (variant 2)**: a demand need not be a separate document unless required; where a separate demand is required it carries a demand for payment, its date, the amount and the beneficiary's signature. Where a statement of default is required but its content is not specified, it complies if it represents that payment is due because the drawing event in the standby has occurred, is dated, and is signed by the beneficiary. The issuer does not investigate whether the default actually happened. | ISP 4.16, 4.17, 1.06(d), 1.08 |
| 10 | **Standby dishonour timing**: notice within **3 business days** after presentation is deemed not unreasonable; beyond **7 business days** is deemed unreasonable; counted from the business day after presentation; notice must state all discrepancies; failure to give timely notice **precludes** the issuer from asserting that discrepancy. Contrast UCP 600: a hard maximum of 5 banking days. | ISP 5.01, 5.02, 5.03 |
| 11 | **Standby: place of presentation closed on the last day** → the last day extends automatically to **30 calendar days after the place re-opens**, unless the standby says otherwise. Contrast UCP 600: Art. 29 (next banking day) for ordinary closures, and **no** extension for force majeure (Art. 36). | ISP 3.14(a); A09 F16, F17 |
| 12 | **Indian rules on an SBLC for an import** (variant 2 background): FEM (Guarantees) Regulations 2026: underlying not prohibited under FEMA; quarterly Form GRN by the resident surety; the old Master Direction condition list is not current. | A08 F21 |
| 13 | **Case checklist (30 seconds)**: (1) subject to UCP 600? (2) who are applicant, issuing, advising / confirming, nominated, beneficiary? (3) how available (sight / deferred / acceptance / negotiation)? (4) dates: latest shipment, expiry, shipment + 21; (5) the question asked: honour, refuse, waive, transfer, or claim? | A08, A09 (method) |
| 14 | **Safeguards for the issuing bank** (syllabus A4.6): it treats the LC as a non-fund limit on the applicant: a sanctioned limit, a **cash margin**, the transport document made out **to the order of the issuing bank** (so it controls the goods until paid), and insurance covering the goods. | Bank credit practice (no UCP article; label "practice") |
| 15 | **Legal risk**: local law and courts can override the UCP's contractual rules (UCP binds only as terms of the credit, Art. 1), e.g. a court injunction, exchange controls or sanctions in the issuing country. | UCP 1 (incorporation); practice |
| 16 | **Re-presentation**: after a refusal, the beneficiary may present corrected documents only while the credit is alive: before expiry **and** within the presentation period (21 days after shipment if none is stated). | UCP 14(c), 6(d)–(e); A09 F4 |
| 17 | **ISP98 business day**: a day on which the place of business at which the relevant act is to be performed is regularly open. | ISP 1.09(a) |

| 18 | **Presentation to a nominated bank counts**: the deadlines (expiry, shipment + 21 days) are met when documents are presented to the nominated bank in time; their later arrival at the issuing bank does not make them late, and the issuing bank's five banking days run from when they reach it. | UCP 2 (presentation), 6(d)–(e), 14(b)–(c), 35 |

## Traps
- The applicant asks the bank not to pay because the goods are poor (no: Arts. 4–5; only a court, on fraud).
- The applicant's waiver binds the issuing bank (no: the issuing bank decides; it may approach the applicant).
- An advising bank is liable to pay (no: it checks apparent authenticity only, A08).
- A confirming bank that negotiated **complying** documents can recover from the beneficiary (no: without recourse). Against discrepant documents its confirmation does not apply.
- Second beneficiary transfers on to its own supplier (no: once only).
- ISP98's 3/7 business days and UCP 600's 5 banking days are the same rule (no). And ISP98 does not "allow 7": within 3 is always timely, beyond 7 always late, between depends on the facts.
- A standby closure gets the next banking day (no: 30 calendar days after re-opening, ISP 3.14, unless the standby says otherwise).
