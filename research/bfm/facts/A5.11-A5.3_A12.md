## Points
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Words**: a **sight / demand** bill is **purchased**; a **usance** bill is **discounted**; a bill under an **LC** is **negotiated**. All three are post-shipment credit in rupees, the first post-shipment form. | CF 266; FEDAI 2.1(a) (the three verbs); our teaching words for which verb fits which bill |
| 2 | **Rate on purchase / discount / negotiation**: the AD's **current bill buying rate**, or the **contracted (forward) rate** if the exporter has booked one. | FEDAI 2.1(a) |
| 3 | **Interest up front**: interest for the **normal transit period and / or the usance period is recovered upfront**, at the same time. | FEDAI 2.1(a) |
| 4 | **NTP** = average time from purchase / discount till the proceeds arrive (not the time the goods take). Foreign-currency DP / sight bills not under LC: **25 days**. Usance bills with a **fixed due date** (from shipment, from bill date): due date known, **NTP not applicable**. Usance bills **after sight**: notional due date = NTP + usance period (+ grace, if any) from the date of discount (A05). **No change in due date after purchase / discount / negotiation.** | FEDAI 2.3(a), (b); A05 fact sheet |
| 5 | **Interest rate**: rupee export credit (pre- and post-shipment) at the bank's **Board-approved** policy, subject to the Interest Rates on Advances Directions 2025. Rates in exam sums are given. | CF 291 (Board policy); FEDAI 2.2(a) (rates per RBI guidelines) |
| 6 | **Overdue bill** (RBI definition): **demand** bill not paid before the expiry of the **NTP plus grace period**; **usance** bill not paid **on the due date**. | CF 2(1)(xxvii) |
| 7 | **Overdue interest**: recovered if payment is not received within the NTP (demand) or by the notional / actual due date (usance). | FEDAI 2.2(b) |
| 8 | **Early realisation**: interest for the **unexpired period is refunded**; the bank also pays or recovers the **notional swap cost** (as for early delivery of a forward). | FEDAI 2.2(c) |
| 9 | **Crystallisation**: converting the exporter's **foreign-currency liability into a rupee liability** when the bill is not paid on the due date. Each AD **formulates its own policy** for it, and the policy must be **transparently available to customers**. The current rules set **no fixed day**. | FEDAI 2.1(b)(i)–(ii) |
| 10 | **Crystallisation rate**: the AD's **TT selling rate**. The amount recoverable thereafter = the **crystallised rupee amount plus interest and charges**. | FEDAI 2.1(b)(iii) |
| 11 | **Interest at crystallisation**: recovered **on the date of crystallisation for the overdue period** at the appropriate rate, and **thereafter till the date of recovery** of the crystallised amount. | FEDAI 2.1(b)(iv) |
| 12 | **Externalisation problems**: bills payable in countries with externalisation issues are crystallised per the AD's policy, even if a local-currency payment advice has come. | FEDAI 2.1(b)(v) |
| 13 | **Proceeds after crystallisation**: converted at the **TT buying rate** (or the contracted rate, if any). | FEDAI 2.1(c) |
| 14 | **Dishonour before crystallisation**: the bank recovers the **rupee equivalent of the bill and foreign-currency charges at the TT selling rate**, plus appropriate interest and rupee charges. | FEDAI 2.1(d) |
| 15 | **Why TT selling** (teaching logic, from the counter of A01): the bank paid her rupees for dollars it never received; to square its position it must buy dollars, i.e. it **sells** dollars to her account. Reversal = the other side of the counter (same as cancelling a forward purchase contract, FEDAI 6.4(ii)(a)). | FEDAI 2.1(b)(iii), 6.4(ii)(a); `sources/fedai_rules_cutoff.md` |
| 16 | **Post-shipment period**: demand bills: NTP set by FEDAI; usance: max **365 days from shipment** incl. NTP and grace. | CF 269, 270 |
| 17 | **Overdue rupee post-shipment credit** may be extinguished from the exporter's **rupee resources** to save interest; the **EDF / EDPMS entry stays outstanding** and the duty to realise continues. Adjusted bills stay in EDPMS till realised. | CF 267, 268 |
| 18 | **Foreign-currency route (EBR / PSCFC)**: discounting in actual foreign exchange at the **appropriate spot rate**; rupee equivalent paid to the exporter and used to liquidate packing credit; **overdue bills: interest from the due date to the date of crystallisation per the bank's interest policy**; if the bill is not paid, the bank remits the bill value to the overseas rediscounting bank **without RBI's prior approval**. | CF 356–360 |
| 19 | **Packing-credit statement case** (from A11b, for chapter 4): 360 days or not export credit ab initio [A11b F3]; no export → domestic rate + penal [F4]; liquidated by export-bill proceeds [F6]; running account only for good track record / EOU, SEZ, FIFO [F8]; limits need-based, "lower of FOB and cost less margin" is bank practice [F24, Leads]; PCFC no export → TT selling [F22]. | `A5.3_A11b.md` |
| 20 | **Bills sent for collection** (not purchased / discounted): proceeds converted at **TT buying** (or the contract rate), only after the money is credited to the bank's Nostro; nothing was advanced, so nothing to crystallise. | FEDAI 2.5(a) |

## Leads, not current rules (teach as history, never as today's rule)
- **"Crystallise on the 30th day"**: older FEDAI Rule 2 (transcription OLD) said ADs crystallise unpaid **demand**
  bills on the **30th day after the transit period**, and unpaid **usance** bills on the **30th day from the notional
  or actual due date**; a 30th day falling on a holiday or Saturday → the next working day; at the **spot TT selling
  rate** ruling that day. The **10th edition removed the fixed day**: today it is the bank's own, published policy
  [F9]. Exam cases state the day. Teach: "the bank's policy decides; the case tells you the day; old books say
  thirty days." Not verified in an official FEDAI copy of the old edition; no source found on what banks use today,
  so the lesson makes no claim about it.
- **Margin on export bills not under an LC**: not in FEDAI Rule 2 or CF Chapter XV; bank practice, set in the
  sanction. Taught as "many banks hold back a margin; the case will say" (N017a), never as a rule.
- Day count: Indian banks compute rupee interest on a **365-day year**; exam sums state the rate and the days. Our
  sums use 365 and say so.
