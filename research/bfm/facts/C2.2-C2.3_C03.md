What this lesson must carry. Short-term discount instruments in India — Treasury Bills (T-Bills), Cash Management Bills (CMBs), Commercial Paper (CP), and Certificates of Deposit (CD) — are issued at a discount to face value and redeemed at par, and all four follow the Actual/365 fixed day-count convention and rear-ended discount pricing formula (Yield = [(100 − P) / P] x (365 / D) x 100 and Price = 100 / [1 + (Y x D) / (365 x 100)], with price per ₹100 rounded to 4 decimal places). T-Bills are issued only by the Government of India (managed by RBI's Internal Debt Management Department under Sections 20 and 21 of the RBI Act, 1934, the Government Securities Act, 2006, and the consolidated GoI General Notification dated 26 March 2025) in three auctioned tenors — 91-day, 182-day, and 364-day — with a minimum bid of ₹10,000 (not the pre-2016 ₹25,000), auctioned weekly on Wednesdays via RBI's E-Kuber platform with T+1 settlement in RBI books; 14-day Intermediate T-Bills (ITBs) are non-marketable and exclusively for State Governments and UTs. Cash Management Bills (CMBs), announced on 10 August 2009 and first issued on 12 May 2010, are non-standard discounted instruments of less than 91 days to meet temporary GoI cash flow mismatches; they share the generic character of T-Bills, qualify as SLR securities under Section 24 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (retained in RBI's CRR and SLR Directions, 2025), qualify for repo, settle on T+1, and have no non-competitive bidding window. Across both T-Bills and dated G-Secs, auctions divide bids into competitive (institutional players quoting exact price/yield) and non-competitive (retail investors placing one bid of at least ₹10,000, capped at 5% within the notified amount, max ₹2 crore per security for dated G-Secs, allotted at the weighted average price/yield of successful competitive bids; for T-Bills, State Governments, UTs with legislature, eligible Provident Funds, Nepal Rashtra Bank, and Royal Monetary Authority of Bhutan also place non-competitive bids outside the notified amount with no cap; RRBs and Co-operative Banks get non-competitive bidding in dated G-Secs only). Auctions are yield-based for new dated G-Secs (cut-off yield sets the coupon), spread-based for new FRBs, and price-based for re-issues of dated G-Secs and all T-Bills. In a Uniform Price auction every winner pays the cut-off rate, whereas in a Multiple Price auction each winner pays their own bid; crucially, from 1 April 2024 RBI reversed its July 2021 Uniform Price split and mandated Multiple Price for all GoI market borrowing securities (verified live across 3-yr to 50-yr G-Secs, SGrBs, and T-Bills in Oct–Nov 2026). For CP (RBI Master Direction 2024, effective 1 April 2024: ₹5 lakh & multiples, 7 days to 1 year, discount to face value, SCB as IPA, min A3 rating from one CRA — old ₹1,000 crore mandatory dual-rating repealed — buyback allowed after 7 days, cut from old 30 days) and CD (RBI CD Master Direction 2021 for SCBs/RRBs/SFBs, 7 days to 1 year, ₹5 lakh & multiples replacing old ₹1 lakh, bank buyback allowed after 7 days, no loans against CDs; AIFI CDs under Nov 2025 Directions: 1–3 years, within 100% NOF Umbrella Limit, AIFI buyback prohibited), the exact same Actual/365 rear-ended discount formulas compute price and yield.
