## A. Whose rate is it? (concept, the base of every sum)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A merchant rate is the **bank's** price: "buying" and "selling" are always from the **bank's** side, never the customer's. | Concept; FEDAI 2.1(a), 4.1, 4.4 name every rate as the AD's buying or selling rate |
| 2 | Foreign currency coming **in** to the bank (export proceeds, inward remittance, a bill the bank buys) → the bank **buys** it → a **buying** rate. Going **out** (outward remittance, import bill) → the bank **sells** → a **selling** rate. | FEDAI 2.1(a), 2.5(a), 3.1(a), 4.1, 4.4 |
| 3 | In a direct quote the bank buys low and sells high: its buying rate is the **lower** rupee figure, its selling rate the **higher**. | Concept (FEDAI 5.7 for the direct quote) |
| 4 | Merchant quotes in India are **direct**: "so many Rupees and Paise for 1 unit or 100 units of foreign currency" (100 units for currencies such as the yen). | FEDAI 5.7 |
| 5 | Settlement may be in rounded rupees or in actual paise, as per the bank's own policy. | FEDAI 5.8 |

## B. Where the merchant rates come from (concept and exam convention)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Banks deal with each other in the **interbank** market; merchant (customer) rates are built on the interbank rate plus the bank's margin. | A00 sheet (A1.1 market rows); concept |
| 7 | A two-way quote has a **bid** (the price at which the quoting bank buys; the lower figure) and an **ask / offer** (the price at which it sells; the higher figure). The gap is the **spread**. | Concept |
| 8 | **Margins are not fixed by FEDAI or RBI.** Banks are free to set their own charges, which should not be out of line with the average cost of the service; customers with low volumes should not be penalised. In exam sums the margin is given. | FEDAI General Guideline 1 |
| 9 | Banks must display their card rates prominently (website and/or B-category branches) and declare the threshold up to which card rates apply and how often and when the card rate is published. | FEDAI General Guideline 2 |
| 10 | **Exam convention (Macmillan-style sums; not a FEDAI rule):** TT selling = interbank ask + margin; bill selling = **TT selling** + further margin; TT buying = interbank bid − margin; bill buying = interbank bid − (larger) margin. So the ladder from the top: bill selling > TT selling > ask > bid > TT buying > bill buying. Rates worked to 4 decimal places. Where a question states its own base, follow the question. | Convention; checked against our numericals (`A/A1/numericals.md`, `tools/check/fx_merchant_rates.py`) |
| 11 | Why TT rates are closest to the market: rupees and foreign currency move together (the money is already in, or goes out at once from, the Nostro). Why bill rates are wider: the bank pays first and waits (bill buying) or handles import documents (bill selling), so it adds a margin. Interest for the waiting period is charged **separately** (normal transit / usance interest, FEDAI 2.1(a): A12). | Concept; FEDAI 2.1(a) |
| 12 | Currency notes have the widest spread (handling, storage, insurance, shipping of notes); in exam sums the note rate is given, never derived. | Concept |

## C. Which rate for which deal (FEDAI rules; the core table)
| # | Deal | Rate | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | Inward remittance converted to rupees | **TT buying** (or contracted rate) | 4.4 |
| 14 | Outward remittance (fees, advance payment for imports, gifts) | **TT selling** (or contracted rate) | 4.1 |
| 15 | Export bill purchased / discounted / negotiated | **bill buying** (or contracted rate); interest for normal transit / usance recovered upfront | 2.1(a) |
| 16 | Export bill sent **on collection** (or goods on consignment) | **TT buying** (or contract rate), converted only **after** the foreign currency is credited to the Nostro | 2.5(a) |
| 17 | Import bill retired | hedge-contract rate if any; otherwise **bill selling** | 3.1(a) |
| 18 | Export bill crystallised into a rupee liability (unpaid on due date) | **TT selling**; plus interest for the overdue period. **When** to crystallise is the bank's own policy, made transparently available to customers (no fixed day in the rules) | 2.1(b)(i)–(iv) |
| 19 | Proceeds of a crystallised export bill received later | **TT buying** (or contract rate) | 2.1(c) |
| 20 | Export bill dishonoured before crystallisation | recover the rupee equivalent of the bill and foreign charges at **TT selling**, plus interest and rupee charges | 2.1(d) |
| 21 | Import bill under an LC crystallised | **same as retirement** (hedge rate or bill selling), as per the bank's stated policy: **no flip** | 3.1(b), 3.3 |
| 22 | Forward **purchase** contract cancelled | **TT selling** (of the contracting AD) | 6.4(ii)(a) |
| 23 | Forward **sale** contract cancelled | **TT buying** | 6.4(ii)(b) |
| 24 | Cancelled **before** maturity | the appropriate **forward** TT rate (A06) | 6.4(ii)(c) |
| 25 | Foreign currency notes, TCs, prepaid cards, debit/credit cards encashed or sold | the AD's buying or selling rate for that instrument at the time | 4.2 |
| 26 | A booked contract (FEDAI: "contracted rate" / "Fx contract rate"; usually a forward contract) **overrides** the card rate. | 2.1(a), 2.5(a), 3.1(a), 4.1, 4.4 ("or contracted rate") |

## D. The pattern to remember (our wording of rows 18–23)
- **Undoing a deal the bank already did → the other side of the counter**, at the **TT** rung (no documents now):
  bought export bill unpaid → TT selling; purchase contract cancelled → TT selling; sale contract cancelled → TT buying.
- **Nothing undone → no flip**: an unpaid import bill is crystallised at the same rate as retirement (3.1(b)).

## Traps (each becomes a trap card or a check question)
- "The customer is buying, so use the buying rate." Wrong: always the bank's side (row 1).
- "Export bill on collection → bill buying." Wrong: TT buying, after the Nostro credit (row 16).
- "Crystallisation of an export bill → bill selling." Wrong: TT selling (row 18).
- "Every crystallisation flips." Wrong: import bill crystallisation = same as retirement (row 21).
- "An importer's advance payment by wire → bill selling." Wrong: it is a remittance → TT selling (row 14).
- "Bill selling margin goes on the market ask." In the exam convention it goes on **TT selling** (row 10).
- "FEDAI fixes the margins." Wrong: banks set their own (row 8).

## Question blueprint (layer 3)
define (direct quote; bid, ask, spread; TT vs bill rate) · who (whose side decides the rate) · compare (TT vs bill;
buying vs selling; collection vs purchase) · exception (forward contract overrides; import crystallisation does not
flip) · calculate (the four merchant rates from an interbank quote and margins; rupee amount of a deal) · apply to a
case (which rate for this deal; which rate when it goes wrong).
