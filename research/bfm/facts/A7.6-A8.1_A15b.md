# Fact sheet · A7.6 ECGC's products for banks · A7.7 Other aspects of ECGC policies and guarantees · A7.8 Common "to do" points · A7.9 Claims · A8.1 EXIM Bank (video A15b)

Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-22. Rules as at the **31 Dec 2026** cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited
as [F3]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text. A7.1–A7.5 (risks, country risk, ECGC and its policies for
exporters) are A15's sheet (`A7.1-A7.5_A15.md`); rows reused from it are marked "(A15 row n)".

**What this lesson must carry.** What ECGC gives **to a bank** is an **ECIB cover** ("Export Credit Insurance for
Banks"), not a policy: it protects the **bank's export advance** against the **exporter-borrower's** **protracted
default or insolvency** (not the overseas buyer's). ECGC's product page (2026) lists the short-term ECIB family:
**WT-PC, WT-PS** (whole turnover), **IN-PC, IN-PS** (individual), **BI-PC** (branch-wise), **SI-PC**, **Surety Cover**
(formerly Export Performance Cover), **EF** (Export Finance), **EPF** (Export Production Finance); and medium/long-term:
IN-PC, IN-PS, Surety Cover and **Buyer's Credit Cover**. **Cover is a share, never 100 %**: individual covers
66⅔ % (PC) and 60–75 % (PS); whole-turnover covers 50–75 % (PC) or 50–95 % (PS) set by the bank's claim-premium
ratio, with an **enhanced 90 %** for small exporters' accounts. The bank's three clocks: **declare and pay premium
monthly**, **report default within 4 months of the due date** (or within a month / 30 days of recalling the
advance, earlier), **claim within 6 months** of the date the default report is due; **recall the advance before
claiming**; **recoveries after a claim are shared in the same ratio as the loss**. ECGC products are **ECGC's own
contract terms**, not RBI rules. **EXIM Bank** is a statutory corporation under the **Export-Import Bank of India Act,
1981** (Act 28 of 1981, assented 11 Sep 1981; in force **1 Jan 1982**), capital wholly subscribed by the Central
Government, its business in **s. 10** (not s. 9), regulated by RBI as an **All India Financial Institution (AIFI)**.

## Points: ECGC's products for banks (A7.6)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What an ECIB cover is**: insurance **for the bank** that lends to exporters. It pays the bank **a percentage of its loss** when the **exporter-borrower** does not repay the export advance because of **protracted default** or **insolvency**. The overseas buyer's default is the **exporter's** risk (his policy, A15); the exporter's default is the **bank's** risk (ECIB). | ECB (each product: "Protection offered"); WPB preamble; DAR ("banks obtain… covers to protect against losses… due to default or insolvency of the exporter borrower") |
| 2 | **Two meanings to keep apart** (ECGC's own definitions): **protracted default** = the exporter's failure to repay the insured debt by its due date (a **genuine dispute** with the bank over the debt may not count); **insolvency** = bankruptcy / winding-up events of the exporter. | WPB Part III defs (8), (12) |
| 3 | **The current list** (ECGC product page, 2026). **Short term**: ECIB (WT-PC) Whole-turnover Packing Credit · ECIB (WT-PS) Whole-turnover Post-shipment · ECIB (IN-PC) Individual Packing Credit · ECIB (IN-PS) Individual Post-shipment · ECIB (BI-PC) Branch-wise Packing Credit · ECIB (SI-PC) · ECIB (Surety Cover), **"formerly Export Performance Cover"** · ECIB (EF) Export Finance · ECIB (EPF) Export Production Finance · DCIB (domestic credit, not export). **Medium/long term**: IN-PC (MLT), IN-PS (MLT), Surety Cover (MLT), **Buyer's Credit Cover**. All are ECGC product names (not RBI terms). | PRS |
| 4 | **Old names in books (trap)**: "Export Performance **Guarantee**", "Export Finance **Guarantee**", "Export Production Finance **Guarantee**", "Packing Credit **Guarantee**", "Overseas Lending Guarantee", "Transfer Guarantee" (DEP lead). ECGC now sells them as **ECIB covers** (Surety Cover = old export performance cover; EF; EPF). **Overseas Lending Guarantee and Transfer Guarantee are not on ECGC's 2026 list**: teach as history only. | PRS; DEP (lead) |
| 5 | **Packing credit (pre-shipment) side**: WT-PC, IN-PC, BI-PC (and SI-PC) cover **all packing credit / pre-shipment advances** made for exports as per RBI guidelines. **Post-shipment side**: WT-PS and IN-PS cover advances by **purchase, negotiation or discount of export bills** and advances against **bills sent on collection**. **EF** covers post-shipment advances **against export incentives** (cash assistance, duty drawback). **Surety Cover** covers **non-fund** facilities: bank guarantees and LCs for exports. | ECB; EFP |
| 6 | **Whole turnover vs individual (the exam idea)**: under **WT** covers the bank insures **all** its export-credit accounts in one proposal (it may exclude only set categories: **MSME/SSI, PSUs, Off-shore Banking Units**, and for WT-PS also **exports to associates and advances against LCs**); ECGC says WT covers give a **higher percentage of cover and a lower premium** than individual covers. Eligibility: **generally at least 50 accounts and an annual assured premium of ₹50 lakh**. **Individual** (IN-PC, IN-PS) covers are taken **account by account, selectively**, by banks **without** a WT cover or for accounts **excluded** from it; each account must be **Standard** and its credit rating acceptable to ECGC. | ECB (WTPC, WTPS eligibility and highlights; INPC, INPS eligibility) |
| 7 | **IN-PC (Individual Packing Credit)**: 12 months; cover **66⅔ %**; premium **12 paise per ₹100 per month** on the **highest amount outstanding** on any day of the month; maximum liability 66⅔ % of the PC limit; processing fee ₹1,000. (BI-PC, branch-wise: same **66⅔ %** and **12 paise**, falling to 10 paise if the branch remits at least ₹50,000 premium a month; one proposal for several accounts of one branch.) | ECB (INPC); BIP |
| 8 | **IN-PS (Individual Post-shipment)**: cover depends on the **exporter's own ECGC policy**: exporter holding a whole-turnover SCR policy: **75 %** on non-associate buyers, **60 %** on associate buyers (shipment covered for comprehensive risk); premium **6 paise** (no exclusions) or **9 paise** (exclusions); maximum liability **75 %** of the post-shipment limit. **Exporter without** a whole-turnover policy: **60 %**, **no cover on associate buyers**, premium 9 paise (all LC bills) or 13 paise; maximum liability 60 %. So the bank gets more cover when its exporter is an ECGC policyholder. | ECB (INPS table) |
| 9 | **WT-PC (Whole-turnover Packing Credit)**: 12 months; **cover 50–75 %**, fixed by the bank's **claim-premium ratio** and its **"Set Limit"** (the premium paid in the previous year); **90 % for MSME/SSI accounts** (sales literature wording); premium **9 paise per ₹100 per month** for a fresh cover, **6 to 13.5 paise** on renewal by claim-premium ratio, charged on the **average daily product** for the month; maximum liability per the Schedule; processing fee ₹10,000; a **Discretionary Limit** up to which new / taken-over accounts are covered without ECGC's prior approval. | ECB (WTPC) |
| 10 | **WT-PS (Whole-turnover Post-shipment)**: 12 months; **cover 50–95 %**, by claim-premium ratio, Set Limit and **whether the exporter holds an ECGC policy**; bills on **associates of policyholders 60 %**, **non-policyholders 50 %**; premium 9 paise fresh, 5 to 13.5 paise on renewal (also by whether LC bills are excluded); average daily product; fee ₹10,000; discretionary limit as WT-PC. | ECB (WTPS) |
| 11 | **Enhanced 90 % for small exporters (WT-ECIB)**: introduced **July 2022** for **manufacturer-exporter** accounts with aggregate limits **up to ₹20 crore**; extended to **₹50 crore** (July 2023, ECGC AR; DAR says up to ₹50 crore **"without any incremental cost" w.e.f. 1 Oct 2025**); a further tier for fund-based export working capital limits **up to ₹80 crore per exporter/group** (excluding **gems, jewellery & diamond** and **merchant exporters/traders**) w.e.f. **1 Jul 2024**. Purpose: let the bank pass on a **lower interest rate**. **Collateral-free cover** under WT-ECIB for MSE export working capital limits **up to ₹10 crore**, no extra premium, w.e.f. **1 Jul 2025**. These are ECGC scheme terms for banks that opt in; normal WT cover stays 50–75/95 %. | EAR p.109–110; DAR pp. 204–205 and highlights; PIB (leads) |
| 12 | **Trap: "ECIB cover is 90 %" / "66⅔ %" / "75 %".** Each is true of one product only: 66⅔ % = IN-PC/BI-PC (and MLT PC "66.66 %"); 75 % = IN-PS max, EF, Surety; 90 % = WT for eligible small-exporter accounts (2022 on). Older books' "ECGC pays the bank 75 % (90 % for small exporters)" mixes two products. Ask "which cover, which account". | ECB; EFP; MLT; EAR |
| 13 | **Surety Cover (ECIB-SC), formerly Export Performance Cover**: protects the bank's **non-fund** exposure for export: **bid bond**, **due performance** guarantee, **advance payment** guarantee, and **LCs for importing raw materials** for export. Eligible exporter: Standard, ECGC credit score **50 % and above**, with a working-capital limit from the bank. Cover **max 75 %**; premium **10–12 paise per ₹100 per month** on the insured debt by credit score; maximum liability 75 % of the guarantee value or of cover sought, lower; period = the guarantee's, **not over 12 months** (extendable). Loss arises when the guarantee is invoked and the exporter does not repay the bank. | ECB (Surety) |
| 14 | **EF (Export Finance)**: post-shipment advances **against export incentives** (cash assistance, duty drawback); any AD bank; **75 %**; **6 paise** per ₹100 per month on highest outstanding; maximum liability 75 % of the post-shipment limit; ECGC approval to extend a due date beyond **360 days**. Banks holding WT-PS get a concessional rate and higher cover. **EPF (Export Production Finance)** is on the list but ECGC publishes no product page (2026-11-22): name only. | EFP; PRS |
| 15 | **Medium/long-term (project) covers to banks**: **PC cover 66.66 %** (premium 0.12 % per month on highest outstanding); **PS cover 60–75 %** (0.06–0.13 % a month); **Surety cover 75 %** (premium **0.70 % a year** on the guarantee value; for project bonds: advance payment, due performance, retention money, guarantees for the exporter's overseas borrowings); a **separate cover for each project**, before disbursement / issue; advance deposit of one month's premium. | MLT |
| 16 | **Buyer's Credit Cover (to banks)**: a bank in India lends **to an overseas buyer** to pay for Indian machinery or a project by an Indian project exporter; ECGC covers the **bank** against the **overseas borrower's** insolvency or default (**commercial**) and **moratorium, transfer embargo, war/civil unrest** in the borrower's country (**political**). Cover **75 %** (interest portion also 75 % on request); normally **up to 10 years**, extendable to **15**; processing fee ₹25,000 + **commitment fee 0.5 % a year** on the undrawn credit; **25 % of premium upfront**, rest at each disbursal; a standby LC / BG where the buyer's report is weak and credit exceeds 3 years. Here the risk is the **foreign borrower's**, unlike every other ECIB cover. | MLT |
| 17 | **NIRVIK** ("Niryat Rin Vikas Yojana", announced 2019–20 as an enhanced ECIB scheme): **no ECGC primary text found**; it is **not** on ECGC's 2026 product list or in ECGC's Annual Report 2024-25. Do not teach it as a current product; the enhanced-cover idea reached banks as the WT-ECIB 90 % (row 11). | PRS; EAR (searched: no "NIRVIK"); leads: coaching and news pages |

## Points: other aspects of ECGC policies and guarantees (A7.7)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | **Premium basis, exporter's SCR**: on **projected annual exports**, **minimum ₹10,000**; paid **in advance** into a **Deposit Premium Account** and adjusted against each month's declared shipments; rates vary by **payment terms** (DP/DA/OD), the **buyer's country grade** and **comprehensive vs political-only** cover. A shipment whose premium was not covered by the deposit balance **is not insured**. | FAQ (SCR); SCB cl. 9–10 |
| 19 | **Premium basis, bank's ECIB**: **paise per ₹100 per month**: on the **highest amount outstanding** on any day (individual covers, EF, MLT) or on the **average daily product** for the month (WT covers); **in advance** as a **deposit** "to comply with IRDA regulations / **Section 64VB of the Insurance Act**" (no cover without premium received). Premium **stops** after the month in which the **Report of Default** is filed (or insolvency); payable again if the bank asks for more time to claim. | ECB (obligations i); WPB cl. 9 |
| 20 | **No-claim bonus**: SCR **5 % a year, up to 50 %** (A15 row 28). (FAQ also records an older "10 % once in two years" for the standard policy, compared with SEP's yearly 5 %: old wording.) ETP holders who also held SCR get an extra 10 % discount. | FAQ (SCR, SEP, ETP) |
| 21 | **Credit limit on buyers** (exporter's policy): commercial risk on a buyer is covered **only up to the credit limit ECGC approves** on that buyer (DP/DA/OD terms); ECGC sets it from credit reports; **status-enquiry fee ₹500** per application; it is a **revolving** limit, valid while there is **no gap over 12 months** between shipments; it limits **ECGC's exposure**, **not the value of shipments**; political risk is not limited by it. If ECGC refuses or cuts a limit, the exporter may pay political-only premium or none. | FAQ (SCR); A15 row 22 |
| 22 | **Discretionary credit limit** (no application needed), SCR wording: **₹40 lakh** per buyer on **DP/CAD** (claims limited to two buyers a year); **₹20 lakh** on **DA/OD** for an exporter who paid at least **₹5 lakh** premium in the previous policy year; or, with a clean two-year payment record with that buyer on the same terms, up to the highest earlier outstanding, max **₹80 lakh** per buyer (sub-limit **₹30 lakh** for DA/OD). Not available on buyers on ECGC's adverse list. | SCB cl. 21; FAQ (SCR) |
| 23 | **Maximum liability**: ECGC's ceiling for the whole policy (SCR: fixed on the exporter's estimate of the maximum outstanding at any time; can be enhanced). Bank covers: per Schedule (WT) or a set % of the sanctioned limit (individual). | FAQ (SCR); ECB |
| 24 | **Extensions of the due date need ECGC's prior approval**: exporter (SCR): extending a DA bill, **converting DP to DA**, or reselling unaccepted goods at a loss above **25 %** of invoice value, all with **prior approval** (and extra premium); **SEP** holders may extend a DA bill **without** prior approval if a DA credit limit is in force. Bank: **PC due-date extension beyond 360 days** and **PS beyond 180 days (360 for status holders)** need ECGC's prior approval; WT-PS proviso: **no liability** for advances outstanding over **180 days** unless agreed. | FAQ (SCR, SEP); ECB; WPB proviso (a) |
| 25 | **Assignment of the exporter's policy to the bank**: the SCR policy **or any amount payable under it** may be assigned **only with ECGC's prior written approval**. ECGC is **not liable** for a shipment whose receivable the exporter assigned **without full recourse** unless ECGC approved. As a condition of paying a claim, the exporter must give ECGC a letter from the **bank holding the documents** that it holds them **in trust for ECGC**. So assigning a **policy** gives the bank the **exporter's** claim money if the **buyer** fails; an **ECIB cover** is the bank's **own** insurance if the **exporter** fails. (The form of the bank's assignment letter is not on ECGC's site; not taught.) | SCB cl. 16, 18(a), 26 |
| 26 | **Policyholder helps the bank**: under IN-PS / WT-PS the bank must check that a policyholder exporter **complied with his policy** (credit limits on buyers, premium, declarations) so that the **higher cover and lower premium** for policyholders' accounts apply. | ECB (INPS xii; WTPS xv) |
| 27 | **Lists the bank must check**: **SAL, Specific Approval List** (exporters and connected persons on whom ECGC's **prior written approval** is needed before the bank lends under WT cover); **BSAL, Buyer Specific Approval List** (buyers on whom bills must **not** be financed post-shipment without approval). Plus **credit reports on overseas buyers not over one year old** before financing bills. | ECB (WTPC iv; WTPS iv–vi; INPS iv–v) |
| 28 | **Whole-turnover principle** (exporter side, A15 row 36): **all** shipments on credit must be covered so the insurer is not left only the bad risks; shipments under LCs **confirmed by banks in India** may be excluded; **associates** normally excluded (political cover possible). Bank side: WT cover means every eligible account is declared (row 6). | FAQ (SCR); ECB |
| 29 | **Renewal and reduction**: covers run **12 months** and are renewed; the **renewal premium and cover %** move with the bank's **claim-premium ratio**. ECGC may at any time **reduce the percentage of cover** or exclude an exporter **prospectively** by written notice. | ECB (WTPC/WTPS premium); WPB "Cancellation and Reduction" |

## Points: common "to do" points (A7.8)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | **Exporter (SCR)**: get **credit limits** on buyers (and on LC-opening banks); **specific approval** for restricted-cover countries; **declare all shipments by the 15th of the next month** (a **NIL** declaration if none); declare bills **overdue 30 days or more** by the 15th; pay premium in advance; take **prompt steps to prevent or minimise loss**; prior approval for extensions / DP→DA; **inform ECGC within 30 days** of any event likely to cause loss; **claim within 360 days of the due date or 540 days from policy expiry, earlier**. (BWP: shipments statement within **15 days after each quarter**.) | SCB cl. 8, 15–18; SCR; FAQ (SCR, BWP) |
| 31 | **Bank (ECIB), monthly**: keep a **premium deposit** with ECGC; submit the **monthly declaration** of advances with premium: **by the 10th** of the next month for **individual** covers (IN-PC, IN-PS, EF) and **by the end** of the next month for **WT** covers (and BI-PC); under WT, **omit no account** (except excluded categories). | ECB; EFP; BIP |
| 32 | **Bank, notifications and limits**: seek higher maximum liability if the limit is raised (individual covers); under WT, notify ECGC **within 30 days** of a limit sanctioned to a **new** exporter (within the discretionary limit) and of any fresh sanction, enhancement, reduction, cancellation or change in management/status. | ECB (INPC iii; WTPC v–vi) |
| 33 | **Bank, prior approval of ECGC before**: extending PC beyond **360 days** / PS beyond **180 days (status holders 360)**; allowing further operations in an account that has slipped to **Sub-standard or NPA**; any **restructuring / nursing / rehabilitation**; any **OTS / compromise**; **sale of the financial asset to an ARC**; financing bills on **restricted-cover-country** buyers (if the exporter's policy has no approval). Under WT also: limits beyond the **discretionary limit** for new/taken-over accounts; limits where the account is **not Standard**; PC component of a combined PC+CC limit above **₹25 crore or 25 %**; exporter/group limits of **₹200 crore or more**; per-buyer exposure above **₹100 crore**; diversification into unrelated business (≥ **10 %** of export turnover, exporters ≥ ₹500 crore). | ECB (INPC iv; INPS vi; WTPC vii; WTPS ix) |
| 34 | **Bank, on default**: file the **Report of Default within 4 months of the due date (or extended due date)** or **within one month of calling up the advance**, **whichever is earlier** (WT-PS wording: **30 days** from recall or 4 months from the due date of the earliest outstanding credit); Surety Cover: within **4 months of cover expiry or 30 days** after calling on the exporter to repay, earlier; **recall** the whole debt (recall notice / legal notice) **before lodging the claim**; take **recovery action** including legal proceedings. | ECB (INPC v, vii–viii; Surety iii; WTPC viii, x–xi); WPB cl. 10(d), proviso (k) |
| 35 | **What loses the bank its claim** (WT-PS wording; each "no liability" unless ECGC approved): advances beyond **180 days**; OTS/compromise **without approval**; assigning cover rights or **selling the account to an ARC/bank without approval**; claim **not filed within 6 months** of the default report (unless extended); restructuring **without approval**; accounts on **SAL** or bills on **restricted-cover countries** without approval; **no recall** before claim; untrue statements in the proposal (cover **void**, premium forfeited); **false or fraudulent claim** (cover void, earlier claims refundable). **Never covered**: unpaid **interest** and **exchange loss** on crystallised bills; the uninsured share. | WPB provisos (a)–(m), cl. 4–5 |
| 36 | **What loses the exporter his claim** (SCR wording): premium not paid / shipment not declared (no premium = no cover); credit period extended or composition accepted **without approval**; receivable assigned without recourse **without approval**; documents on DP/CAD terms released to the buyer **without payment**; claim filed **after the 360/540-day limit** (late filing considered on merits only if beyond the exporter's control). | SCB cl. 10, 18 |

## Points: claims (A7.9)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 37 | **Exporter's claim: when payable ("time for ascertainment of loss")**: **protracted default** → **4 months after the due date**; **buyer's insolvency** → **4 months after the due date or one month after the debt is admitted** to rank against the insolvent's estate, **earlier**; **non-acceptance of goods** → one month after resale/disposal with ECGC's approval; **transfer delay** → 4 months after the due date or after transfer formalities for the buyer's local deposit, **later**; **diversion of voyage** → on proof of loss; **other political events** → **4 months after the event**. ECGC may extend the waiting period for countries with long transfer delays. **SEP**: waiting period **2 months** (halved). | SCB cl. 15; FAQ (SCR, SEP) |
| 38 | **Disputes**: where the buyer refuses because of a dispute on contract performance, ECGC considers the claim only **after the dispute is settled**, normally by a **decree from a court in the buyer's country** (A15 row 19). | FAQ (SCR) |
| 39 | **Exporter's claim: how much**: **90 %** of the loss under SCR (commercial or political); exporter bears 10 %; other policies' shares in A15 row 20. Paid **in rupees**; on devaluation before payment, the claim uses the devalued rate. | FAQ (SCR); SCB cl. 17 |
| 40 | **Bank's claim (ECIB): when and how much**: ECGC ascertains the loss after **4 months from the due date** (protracted default, with the Report of Default filed) or, for **insolvency**, 4 months after the due date or **one month after insolvency**; the bank must **claim within 6 months** of the date the default report is due (or filed) unless ECGC extends in writing; ECGC pays the **insured percentage** of the loss (rows 7–16). ECGC's **simplified claim procedure** limit was raised to **₹10 crore** (from ₹5 crore) **w.e.f. 1 Feb 2025**. | WPB cl. 12; ECB; MLT; EAR p.110; DAR |
| 41 | **Recoveries after a claim** (exporter and bank alike): **recovery expenses** are a **first charge** on what is recovered; the rest is **shared with ECGC in the same proportion in which the loss was borne** (e.g. 90:10 under SCR), whether that gives ECGC more or less than it paid; sums are held **in trust for ECGC** and paid forthwith (late remittance by a bank attracts interest). A claim **does not end the duty to recover**, nor the exporter's FEMA duty to realise export proceeds. | SCB cl. 23–25; WPB cl. 13; FAQ (SCR) |
| 42 | **Subrogation** (insurance word, JAIIB prerequisite): after paying, ECGC steps into the insured's rights: on its advice the exporter must **assign to ECGC his rights under the contract**, deliver the goods and documents, and assign any bills, guarantees or securities. | SCB cl. 23 |

| 45 | **Law and dates**: **Export-Import Bank of India Act, 1981** (**Act No. 28 of 1981**, dated **11 Sep 1981**); Act in force **1 Jan 1982** (S.O. 919(E), 29 Dec 1981) except **s. 26** (transfer of IDBI's export-finance business) from **1 Mar 1982**; EXIM Bank's site: "finally set up in 1982", operations from March 1982. | 46 | **Purpose (long title, short quote)**: "for providing financial assistance to exporters and importers, and for functioning as the **principal financial institution for co-ordinating the working of institutions engaged in financing export and import** of goods and services with a view to promoting the country's international trade". It finances **imports** too, not only exports. | ACT long title; s. 10(1) |
| 47 | **Ownership and control**: authorised capital **₹10,000 crore** (s. 4(1), as substituted by Act 11 of 2012; the Government may raise it by notification); issued capital **wholly subscribed by the Central Government** (s. 4(2)), so **wholly owned by GoI**; Board: chairman / MD and two whole-time directors (Government), one director each from **RBI**, **IDBI** ("Development Bank") and **ECGC**, up to 12 Government nominees (s. 6); acts on **business principles with due regard to public interest**, bound by Government policy directions (s. 5(3)–(4)); net profits after reserves go to the Central Government (s. 23); wound up only by Government order (s. 38). | ACT ss. 4–6, 23, 38 |
| 48 | **Business, s. 10 (not s. 9: s. 9 is directors' disqualifications)**. **s. 10(1)**: loans and advances **in or outside India**, alone or **with any bank or FI in or outside India**, for **export or import**; and the **principal co-ordinating institution**. **s. 10(2)** main heads, in our words: (a) **refinance** to scheduled banks / notified FIs for their export-import loans; (b) **underwrite** shares, bonds of export-import companies; (c) **bid bonds and guarantees** in or outside India; (d) **discount / rediscount / negotiate export-import bills** and lend against them; (e) **issue, confirm, endorse LCs**; (f) **government-to-government plus commercial** credit; (g) **lines of credit to foreign governments, FIs or persons abroad**; (h) loans abroad **for Indian joint ventures**; (i) loans in India for **equity in overseas JVs**; (j) **leasing** of machinery for export/import; (k) invest in foreign development banks / EXIM banks; (l) **foreign-exchange dealings** needed for its work; (q) **research, surveys, techno-economic studies**; (r) technical, administrative, financial **assistance**; (s) plan, promote, finance **export-oriented concerns**; (t) **subsidiaries**; (u) **agent of** Central/State Government, RBI, IDBI; (v) **market and credit information** on international trade; (w) other business the Government authorises. | ACT ss. 9, 10 |
| 49 | **Resources**: may **issue bonds and debentures** (with or without Government guarantee); **borrow from RBI** (short-term against trustee securities; against trade bills maturing within 5 years; from RBI's NIC (LTO) Fund) and from bodies the Government approves; borrow **foreign currency** abroad with Government consent (s. 13); receive grants (s. 14); and **accept deposits only for 12 months or more**, on terms **approved by RBI** (s. 12(1)(d)). So "EXIM Bank does not take deposits" is **false as law**; it takes **no short-term (under 12 months) deposits**, and **no current or savings accounts** appear in the Act. It may **not lend against its own bonds** (s. 10(4)). | ACT ss. 10(4), 12–14 |
| 50 | **Export Development Fund** (ss. 15–20): a special fund for loans or arrangements that **banks and FIs would not ordinarily give**, each with **prior Central Government approval** and only where needed "as a matter of priority" for the country's international trade. | 51 | **Banking law that applies**: the **Banking Regulation Act 1949 does not apply except ss. 34A and 36AD** (s. 36); the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act 1891 applies** (s. 35). **RBI regulates it as an AIFI**: the RBI (All India Financial Institutions – Miscellaneous) Directions 2025 (28 Nov 2025, under **s. 45L of the RBI Act**) apply to the five AIFIs: **EXIM Bank, NABARD, SIDBI, NHB, NaBFID**. EXIM Bank is **not a scheduled commercial bank**. | ACT ss. 35–36; AIF para 3 |
| 52 | **Current products (EXIM site, 2026)**, grouped by the site: **Financing India's exports**: **Lines of Credit**, **Buyer's Credit**, pre-shipment and post-shipment credit, guarantees and LCs. **Export competitiveness**: **term loans** (Lending Programme for **Export Oriented Units**; **Production Equipment Finance**; **R&D finance**), export product development, **Overseas Investment Finance**, **import finance**, Sustainable Finance Programme. **MSME exports**: **Ubharte Sitaare Programme**, **Trade Assistance Programme**, **export factoring** (through **India Exim Finserve IFSC Pvt Ltd**, a wholly owned subsidiary in GIFT City, regulated by IFSCA). **Promotional**: Grassroots Initiatives for Development (GRID), Marketing Advisory Services, **Research & Analysis**. Also **Project Exports** support. | EXA |
| 53 | **Lines of Credit (LOC)**: EXIM Bank lends to **overseas governments, financial institutions, regional development banks and other entities abroad**, so that buyers there import **Indian** goods, services and projects on **deferred credit**; the Indian exporter is paid by EXIM Bank, **without payment risk** on the importer. Two kinds: EXIM's **own** LOCs and **Government of India-supported** concessional LOCs under **IDEAS** (Indian Development and Economic Assistance Scheme, from **2003-04**), handled by the **MEA's Development Partnership Administration**. Under GoI LOCs at least **75 %** of contract value must be **sourced from India** (10 % relaxation case by case); LOC may finance **up to 100 %** of contract value. | EXA (Lines of Credit) |
| 54 | **Buyer's Credit**: credit to an **overseas buyer** (or a bank/institution abroad) to import **Indian** goods or services on **deferred payment**; the Indian exporter gets **non-recourse** payment, converting a deferred contract into a **cash** contract; usable **only for Indian exports**. Trap: this is **not** the "import buyer's credit" Indian importers take from banks abroad (A13). | EXA (Buyer's Credit); DEP (lead, A13) |
| 55 | **Overseas Investment Finance**: term loans to Indian companies for **equity in, or onward lending to, overseas JVs / WOS**; term loans and guarantees **to the overseas JV/WOS** itself; INR or foreign currency per RBI rules; usually **5–7 years**. | EXA (OIF); ACT s. 10(2)(h)–(i) |
| 56 | **Exporter finance and term loans** (Corporate Banking page): **pre-shipment and post-shipment credit** to exporters with a **track record** (margin about 15–20 % pre-shipment, 10 % post-shipment; ECGC cover as security), non-fund LCs and guarantees; term loans to EOUs for expansion, modernisation, equipment (minimum export orientation **10 % of turnover or ₹5 crore**, lower; usually 7–10 years); **R&D finance** for firms with exports ≥ **20 %** of turnover (up to 80 % of project cost, up to 7 years). | EXA (Corporate Banking) |
| 57 | **Ubharte Sitaare Programme**: finance (equity / equity-like, term loans) **and handholding** for small and mid-sized Indian companies with export potential and distinctive technology, products or processes (turnover up to about **₹500 crore**), even if now underperforming. **Trade Assistance Programme (TAP)**: **credit enhancement** (e.g. **irrevocable reimbursement undertakings**) so commercial banks can support exports to **new, high-risk or untapped** markets; tenor up to **365 days**. | EXA (USP, TAP) |
| 58 | **"Which facility can't EXIM Bank give?"** (recalled Jun 2026; the options are not recorded). Safe statements from the Act: it does **not** take **short-term (under 12-month) deposits** or offer savings/current accounts (s. 12(1)(d) allows only 12-month-plus deposits); it does **not** lend **against its own bonds** (s. 10(4)); it is not an **insurer** (export credit insurance is **ECGC's**, row 1; EXIM Bank **gives guarantees** under s. 10(2)(c), so "guarantees" is **not** the answer). Everything else in the usual option list (LOC, buyer's credit, refinance, pre/post-shipment credit, LCs, OIF, leasing, research) **is** in s. 10. | ACT ss. 10, 12; REC |
| 59 | **ECGC vs EXIM Bank (compare)**: ECGC = **company** under the Companies Act, **insurer** (IRDAI-registered), Ministry of **Commerce**, sells **policies** (exporters) and **covers** (banks) (A15 rows 23–25). EXIM Bank = **statutory corporation** under its own **1981 Act**, a **lender / financier** regulated by **RBI as an AIFI**, under the **Ministry of Finance** (Department of Financial Services logo on its site). They meet in NEIA (row 43–44). | A15 rows 23–25; ACT; AIF; EXA (footer) |
