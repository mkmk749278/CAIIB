What this lesson must carry. Once foreign investment passes the eligibility and instrument checks from the previous class, the Authorised Dealer Category-I (AD) bank executes four compliance workflows under the FEM (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 (updated up to 2 September 2026), FEM (Mode of Payment and Reporting of Non-Debt Instruments) Regulations, 2019 (FEMA 395/2019-RB, amended up to 13 June 2026), and RBI Master Direction – Foreign Investment in India (updated up to 15 June 2026). First, pledge of shares (Rule 9(8); MD Para 7.11) is allowed without prior RBI approval in four pathways: a promoter securing an ECB (co-terminus with ECB maturity, Statutory Auditor end-use certificate, prior AD bank NOC), or a non-resident pledging unencumbered shares/units in favour of an Indian bank (for the investee company's bona-fide credit; Statutory Auditor certificate; Section 19(2) Banking Regulation Act 30% cap), an overseas bank (for genuine overseas business of the non-resident/promoter/group only—no Indian investment or capital inflow; CA/CPA certificate), or an RBI-registered NBFC (ex-ante Board resolution, ex-post Statutory Auditor certificate, 30-day sell-down if invocation breaches credit concentration norms). On invocation, entry routes, sectoral caps, and pricing rules at the time of creation of pledge apply; any other pledge requires prior RBI approval with five listed documents. Second, reporting runs on FIRMS via the Single Master Form (SMF) (9 forms; Entity Master first; Business User eKYC by AD in 3 working days; auto-acknowledged since 4 January 2023; AD verification in 5 working days): 30-day forms are FC-GPR, ESOP, DRR (domestic custodian), LLP(I), InVI, DI (plus 30-day DPIIT notice), and CN; 60-day forms are FC-TRS (from transfer or payment, whichever is earlier; non-repatriable PROI ↔ resident sale exempt) and LLP(II); stock-exchange trades use LEC(FII) and the new LEC(IFI) (13 June 2026, replacing LEC(NRI)); and the annual FLA Return is due by 15 July on FLAIR (unaudited figures allowed, then revised with RBI approval; share-application-money-only and non-repatriation-only entities exempt). Third, delays up to 3 years attract the uniform Late Submission Fee (LSF) (30 September 2022): ₹7,500 flat for FLA/non-flow returns, and ₹7,500 + (0.025% × A × n) (capped at 100% of A, rounded up to nearest ₹100, payable in 30 days) for flow returns; delays over 3 years require compounding. Fourth, inward funds (including via SRVAs and any repatriable FX/Rupee account since 15 January 2025) require FIRC/debit statement, KYC, 90-day Valuation Certificate, Declaration & CS Certificate, and equity must be allotted within 60 days or refunded within 15 days thereafter; AD verifies applicant bonafides, prescribed payment mode, and Companies Act interest, and late refund remains a FEMA contravention even if Companies Act interest is paid.
