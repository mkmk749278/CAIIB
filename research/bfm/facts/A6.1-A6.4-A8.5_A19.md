# Fact sheet · A6.1–A6.4 External Commercial Borrowings (2026 Overhaul) · A8.5 ADR, GDR, IDR, FCCB, Rupee-Denominated Bonds (video A19)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 December 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1]–[F28]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text and paragraph number. Sister sheets: A19b (FDI/FPI under NDI Rules), A13 (trade credit).
What this lesson must carry
On 16 February 2026, RBI overhauled External Commercial Borrowings through the FEM (Borrowing and Lending) (First Amendment) Regulations, 2026 (FEMA 3(R)(5)/2026-RB, dated 9 Feb 2026) and deleted Paras 1–12 of the 2019 ECB Master Direction (A.P. DIR 22). Under the 2026 framework, any non-individual Indian entity incorporated, established, or registered under a Central or State Act is an eligible borrower (entities under CIRP/restructuring if permitted in the plan). Recognised lenders widened to any person resident outside India (abolishing the FATF/IOSCO country test), overseas branches of RBI-regulated lenders, and IFSC financial institutions/branches. The borrowing limit is the higher of USD 1 billion outstanding ECB or 300% of net worth (external plus domestic outstanding borrowing per last audited standalone balance sheet; financial-sector regulated borrowers are exempt; old USD 750M/FY cap and 7:1 ratio are abolished). Standard MAMP is 3 years; manufacturing entities may borrow for 1 to 3 years up to USD 150 million outstanding (abolishing old 5/7/10-year tiers). The fixed all-in-cost ceiling is removed for MAMP ≥ 3 years ("prevailing market conditions"; related-party loans at arm's length; <3-year manufacturing ECBs follow the Trade Credit ceiling: Benchmark + 300 bps FCY / + 250 bps INR). Currency conversion is now two-way (including INR to FCY). Regulation 3A sets the barred end-use list (chit funds, Nidhi, real estate business/farmhouses, agriculture/plantation with exceptions, TDR trading, securities except strategic corporate actions, restricted/NPA domestic rupee loan repayment, restricted on-lending; general working capital and GCP are now permitted). RBI-regulated entities (banks, AIFIs, NBFCs) are strictly barred from issuing any type of guarantee for ECBs. Reporting runs Form ECB 1 → LRN before drawdown → Revised Form ECB 1 & event-based Form ECB 2 within 7 calendar days from month-end (AD to RBI in 7 calendar days); LSF from 1 April 2026 (A.P. DIR 25) is flat ₹7,500 for Form ECB 1 / Revised ECB 1, and ₹7,500 + (0.025% × A × n) per delayed Form ECB 2. The 70% mandatory hedging rule is removed; unused proceeds park in unencumbered FDs up to 1 year; ECB-to-equity conversion (MAMP-exempt) uses the agreement-date exchange rate (or lower liability rate). ADRs (US) and GDRs (global) follow Issuer → Domestic Custodian → Foreign Depository → holder, with two-way fungibility within headroom and Form DRR in 30 days. FCCBs are ECBs until conversion and outside the FDI cap till converted. IDRs are issued by foreign companies in Indian Rupees only, with immediate repatriation, USD 5 billion overall cap, 1-year lock-in, 25%/year partial two-way fungibility, 30-day resident sale rule, and prior sectoral regulator approval for foreign banks with Indian presence. Overseas Rupee-Denominated Bonds (Masala Bonds) shift FX risk to the foreign investor; Indian banks may issue them for AT1 PDIs, Tier 2 capital, and long-term infrastructure/affordable housing, with overseas FCY/RDB AT1 PDIs capped from 1 October 2025 at 1.5% of RWAs (repealing the 2021 49% cap).
