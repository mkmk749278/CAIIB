# Fact sheet · D6.3 Economic capital and RAROC (video D07b)

Sister sheets: D07 (profit planning, six variables, EL vs UL concept, risk aggregation and capital allocation), D05/D06/D06b (interest rate risk, TGA/DGA, EaR, MCLR/EBLR), B03 (standard deviation), B04 (ICAAP), B09/B09b (VaR multiples).


What this lesson must carry. Traditional bank profitability ratios—Return on Assets (ROA = Net Income ÷ Average Total Assets) and Return on Equity (ROE = Net Income ÷ Average Total Equity)—do not adjust for risk; ROE in particular can be artificially boosted by higher financial leverage while hiding asset quality and solvency risks. Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital (RAROC) solves this by placing risk-adjusted return and loss-absorbing capital on the same scale. Under the Basel and RBI framework, Expected Loss (EL = PD × EAD × LGD) is a recurring cost of lending covered by loan pricing and provisions, whereas Unexpected Loss (UL) represents rare peak losses above EL absorbed by economic capital (the distance between Value-at-Risk at a chosen confidence level over a 1-year horizon and EL). The three-step RAROC method first measures risk, next allocates economic capital at a chosen confidence level and time horizon, and finally measures risk-adjusted performance using the formula RAROC = (Revenue − Funding and Operating Costs − Expected Loss) ÷ Economic Capital. A loan, project, or business line creates shareholder value only when its RAROC exceeds the bank's hurdle rate (the minimum acceptable return on capital, linked to the cost of equity); if RAROC falls below the hurdle rate, it destroys value and must be rejected or repriced. In risk-based loan pricing, the break-even lending rate must cover funding costs, operating costs, expected loss, and the capital charge (Economic Capital % × Hurdle Rate); pricing that ignores EL and the capital charge underprices risk and erodes capital. RBI's Interest Rate on Advances Directions require credit risk premiums to reflect credit ratings, expected losses, and collateral, and explicitly allow CAPM to estimate cost of equity. When comparing two trading desks or business units that earn identical accounting profit with different volatility, Risk-Adjusted Performance Measurement (RAPM) computes Risk Capital = z × Notional × Volatility (with the standard normal confidence multiplier z given in exam questions: 1.645 for 95%, 2.326 or 2.33 for 99%, 3.090 for 99.9% one-tailed) and divides net profit by risk capital; equal profit does not mean equal performance because higher volatility absorbs more capital. Economic Value Added (EVA), developed by Stern and Stewart (1991), measures absolute rupee value creation as EVA = Profit After Tax (or NOPAT) − (Invested Capital × Cost of Capital), where Cost of Capital is typically WACC = We × Ke + Wd × Kd × (1 − t), with Cost of Equity computed via CAPM: Ke = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf). Positive accounting profit can still produce negative EVA because accounting profit ignores the opportunity cost of equity capital. Finally, Earnings at Risk (EaR) under RBI's ALM Directions, 2025 measures potential 1-year Net Interest Income loss under interest-rate scenarios, whereas RAROC measures percentage risk-adjusted return on allocated economic capital across risks against a hurdle rate.
