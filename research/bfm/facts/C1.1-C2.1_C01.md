What this lesson must carry. An integrated treasury merges a bank's domestic money-market, foreign-exchange, and securities (debt and equity) desks into one coordinated unit that bridges the core banking book (deposits and loans) with financial markets. Under RBI's consolidated 2025–2026 directions as at 31 December 2026, its scope spans statutory reserve maintenance (CRR 3.0% of NDTL with 99% daily minimum since 16 October 2026 and zero RBI interest; SLR 18% of NDTL), multi-currency liquidity and ALM support (including internal transfer pricing and HQLA management under the Treasurer), merchant hedging and foreign-currency loan clearance, proprietary trading and investment management (HTM, AFS, and FVTPL including HFT), and derivative risk management. Following India's 1990s market liberalisation, treasury shifted from a reserve-compliance cost centre to a major profit centre due to low credit risk, high leverage, low operating cost, and high turnover over thin margins. Treasury earns from merchant spreads, money-market deployment, investment income, and trading/arbitrage gains, whereas strategic stakes in subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures are held sui generis at cost and barred from HFT. Crucially, banks cannot short-sell equities: RBI allows short positions only in permitted derivatives and Central Government dated securities. Covered interest arbitrage earns near-risk-free returns when the forward rate departs from interest-rate parity, but vanishes between two freely convertible currencies when swap points match the interest differential. Globalisation links Indian treasuries to global rates and cross-border flows, but globalisation is not deregulation: India maintains partial capital-account convertibility under FEMA 1999 (current-account items including royalties and technical fees are generally free; capital-account items such as FDI, FPI, ECB, ADR/GDR/FCCB, and cross-border M&A are restricted to permitted schedules). Globalisation brought three impacts: domestic rates and USD/INR track global rates and volatile portfolio flows; new infrastructure and regulators emerged (CCIL guaranteed settlement, depositories, SEBI, IRDAI, and RBI's Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets constituted 4 November 2026); and products widened to rupee IRDs, IRFs, CDS, and TRS. AD Category-I banks' overseas foreign-currency borrowing is capped at 100% of unimpaired Tier I capital or USD 10 million, whichever is higher (borrowings above 50% of Tier I need a Board policy, 12.0% CRAR, and 3-year minimum maturity; export credit, Tier II subordinated debt, FC capital instruments, and RBI-approved loans are excluded). Finally, the global forex market — an OTC, virtual, screen-based, 24-hour market with two-way quotes and narrow spreads — traded $9.6 trillion per day in April 2025 (BIS Triennial Survey), with the US dollar on 89.2% of trades; onshore deliverable FCY/INR hours run 9:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. IST with beyond-hours dealing permitted, Nostro balances earn no interest unless swept, and FC surpluses may be placed overnight or in overseas money-market/sovereign debt under 1 year rated at least AA(-) / Aa3.
