# Fact sheet · D1.1 Components of a bank's balance sheet (video D01)

Sister sheets: B06 (off-balance-sheet CCF & credit risk capital), B13 (liquidity risk), C02 (money-market instruments & call/notice/term money limits), D01b (ALM, ALCO, treasury & FTP — next class), D02–D04 (IRACP & provisioning).


What this lesson must carry. A commercial bank's annual accounts are governed by Section 29 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, which mandates a Balance Sheet in Form A and a Profit and Loss Account in Form B of the Third Schedule as on 31 March (notified via S.O. 86(E) dated 29 Jan 1992; forms notified via S.O. 240(E) dated 26 Mar 1992), compiled under the RBI (Commercial Banks – Financial Statements: Presentation and Disclosures) Directions, 2025 (updated as on 1 July 2026). Unlike corporate balance sheets under the Companies Act, a bank's Third Schedule balance sheet has no current versus non-current split. Form A is organised as two equal shelves plus a dotted off-balance-sheet shelf: Sources of Funds (Capital & Liabilities, Schedules 1–5) — Capital (1), Reserves & Surplus (2), Deposits (3), Borrowings (4), and Other Liabilities & Provisions (5); Uses of Funds (Assets, Schedules 6–11) — Cash & Balances with RBI (6), Balances with Banks & Money at Call and Short Notice (7), Investments (8), Advances (9), Fixed Assets (10), and Other Assets (11); and below the total, Schedule 12 Contingent Liabilities plus Bills for Collection (no schedule number). Each balance-sheet item behaves differently in cost, stability, risk, and earning power: Capital and Reserves are permanent loss-absorbing buffers (commercial banks must transfer at least 25% of net profit to Statutory Reserve, above the 20% statutory floor in s. 17(1); Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR) was discontinued w.e.f. 18 May 2026); Deposits are the primary, cheapest funding source (with demand deposits carrying highest liquidity risk); Borrowings are costlier and more volatile; Cash in hand and CRR balances with RBI (3.0% of NDTL, zero interest) and Fixed/Other Assets are non-earning; Government Securities dominate Schedule 8 Investments because of the 18% SLR requirement and liquidity; Advances carry the highest yield and highest credit risk; and Call/Notice/Term Money differ purely by tenor (overnight, 2–14 days, 15 days–1 year). Key placement traps: matured/overdue term deposits stay in Schedule 3 (under Demand Deposits); RBI/NABARD/EXIM refinance sits in Schedule 4 Borrowings (with advances kept gross); bills payable, interest accrued but not due, net credit inter-office adjustments, and Standard Asset provisions sit in Schedule 5; corporate debentures/bonds sit in Schedule 8 Investments (not Advances); Advances (Schedule 9) are reported net of NPA provisions and classified by security (tangible assets, bank/govt guarantee, or unsecured — rights and licenses are unsecured); and non-interest-bearing staff loans sit in Schedule 11. Form B (Profit & Loss Account) has four numbered schedules: Schedule 13 Interest Earned, Schedule 14 Other Income (commission/brokerage, net profit/loss on sale or revaluation of investments, forex profit, miscellaneous), Schedule 15 Interest Expended (including broken period interest expensed, not capitalised), and Schedule 16 Operating Expenses (12 items including DICGC deposit insurance premium under Insurance and depreciation on bank property including leasehold and non-banking assets). Provisions and Contingencies (NPA, NPI, and income-tax provisions) sits outside Schedule 16 as a separate line under Expenditure, recognised above the line before Net Profit, while reserve transfers and dividends occur below the line. Amendments issued in April and July 2026 for ECL Stage 1/2/3 presentation and Pillar 3 disclosure migration take effect only on 1 April 2027 ("issued, coming").
