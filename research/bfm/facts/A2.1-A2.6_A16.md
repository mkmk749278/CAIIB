# Fact sheet · A2.1 Capital and current account transactions · A2.3 Permissible and non-permissible remittances · A2.4 Operational guidelines · A2.5 LRS remittances for current account purposes · A2.6 TCS (video A16)

Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-22. Rules as at the **31 Dec 2026** cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited
as [F3]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text. A2.2 (FEMA sections), A2.7 (LRS and capital account: ODI,
IFSC, loans to NRI relatives), A2.8 (reporting) and A2.9 (other remittance facilities, travel cash and retention) are
A16b's sheet; rows here that A16b will reuse are marked "(A16b)".

**What this lesson must carry.** FEMA splits every foreign exchange deal into **capital account** (it changes a
resident's assets or liabilities **outside India**, or a non-resident's **in India**: s. 2(e)) and **current
account** (everything else: s. 2(j)). Current account drawal is **free** (s. 5) except where the **Central
Government's** FEM (Current Account Transactions) Rules 2000 say otherwise: **Schedule I = prohibited**, **Schedule II
= needs the named Ministry's approval**, **Schedule III = individuals within the LRS limit** (RBI approval above it).
The **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)** lets **resident individuals, minors included**, send up to **USD 250,000
per financial year (April–March)** for permitted current **or** capital account purposes **or both, in one combined
limit**; it is **not** for companies, firms, HUFs or trusts. RBI's LRS Master Direction (MD No. 7/2015-16, last
updated **6 Sep 2024**, unchanged on RBI's site on 22 Nov 2026) is the operating text: **PAN for every LRS
remittance**, **Form A2**, a **designated branch**, **no bank credit for capital account remittances**, clubbing for
family members (co-owners only for capital uses), no frequency cap, **money brought back does not restore the
limit**. **Above USD 250,000** the AD can still release, **without RBI**, for **emigration, medical treatment and
studies** on the country's / hospital's / university's estimate. **TCS** sits in the **Income-tax Act, 2025, s.
394(1), Table Sl. No. 7 (LRS) and Sl. No. 8 (overseas tour package)**; from **1 Apr 2026** (Finance Act 2026) it is
**2 % above ₹10 lakh for education or medical**, **20 % above ₹10 lakh for other purposes**, **nil on education
funded by a loan from a financial institution**, and **2 % from the first rupee on overseas tour packages**; it is
**credit for tax**, not a lost cost (s. 390(5)). FY 2025-26 rates (5 % / 20 % / tour 5 %-20 %) and the ₹7 lakh era
are dated-question traps.

## Points: capital and current account transactions (A2.1)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Capital account transaction** (definition): one that **alters the assets or liabilities, including contingent liabilities, outside India of persons resident in India**, or **in India of persons resident outside India**, and includes the s. 6(3) list. | FEMA s. 2(e) |
| 2 | **Current account transaction**: **any transaction other than a capital account transaction**; it includes (i) payments for **foreign trade**, other current business, services, and short-term banking and credit facilities in the ordinary course; (ii) **interest on loans and net income from investments**; (iii) **living expenses of parents, spouse and children abroad**; (iv) **foreign travel, education and medical care** of parents, spouse and children. Quick test: **does it create or change an asset or liability abroad?** Yes = capital; no = current. | FEMA s. 2(j); FAQ preamble ("do not alter his/her assets or liabilities … outside India") |
| 3 | **Who controls which**: **current account** drawal is **free** through an authorised person; the **Central Government** (in consultation with RBI) may impose reasonable restrictions **by rules** (hence the CAT Rules are a **Government** notification). **Capital account**: since **s. 6(2A)** (inserted by the Finance Act 2015, in force **15 Oct 2019**) the **Central Government** prescribes non-debt capital account transactions (e.g. the Overseas Investment Rules 2022) and **RBI** the debt instruments (s. 6(2)); read by the c1 expert review as a lead: re-read s. 6 in India Code at the freeze. | FEMA ss. 5, 6(1)–(2); CAT preamble (G.S.R. 381(E), made under s. 5 and s. 46) |
| 4 | **One limit for both**: LRS lets a resident individual remit up to **USD 250,000 per FY** for **any permitted current or capital account transaction or a combination of both**; the Schedule III current-account purposes are **subsumed** in the same USD 250,000. | LRS A.1, A.7; ORF 3 |
| 5 | **Sorting drill (our examples, each tied to the text)**: son's **tuition fees** = current (s. 2(j)(iv)); **interest** received/paid on a loan = current (s. 2(j)(ii)); **buying shares of a foreign company** = capital (asset abroad; LRS A.6 "OPI"); **opening a bank account abroad** = capital (LRS A.6); **interest-free rupee loan to an NRI brother** = capital (LRS A.6 lists it among LRS capital uses); **gift to a friend abroad** = current (Schedule III "gift"). | FEMA s. 2(e), 2(j); LRS A.6; CAT Sch. III |
| 6 | **"Drawal"** includes opening an LC and **using an international credit card, debit card, ATM card** or anything that creates a foreign exchange liability; "currency" includes cards. So the CAT Rules reach card use too. | CAT r. 2(b); FEMA s. 2(h); ORF 1.2 |

| 27 | **Source and mode of funds**: payment must come from the **remitter's own funds**, by **cheque on his account, debit to his account, DD/pay order**, or his **credit/debit/prepaid card**. The AD **certifies** the remittance is not by or to ineligible entities. | LRS B.8, B.9 |
| 28 | **No bank credit**: banks shall **not extend any credit facility** to resident individuals **to facilitate capital account remittances** under LRS. **Trap**: RBI's FAQ says ADs **may** give fund and non-fund facilities **for current account remittances** under LRS (so an education loan remitted abroad is fine; a loan to buy US shares is not). | LRS A.12, B.10; FAQ Q16 |
| 29 | **Clubbing**: remittances **can be consolidated for family members**, each member complying with the scheme. | 30 | **AD's duties**: no RBI document list for current account; the AD takes the **s. 10(5) declaration** and information that reasonably satisfies it; keeps records; on refusal or unsatisfactory compliance it **refuses in writing** and **reports to RBI** if it suspects a contravention. **KYC and AML** rules apply. Tax deduction on remittances to non-residents follows the **tax laws**, not RBI. | LRS B.2–B.6; ORF 6.1–6.3, 7; FAQ Q20 |
| 31 | **Foreign currency accounts**: individuals may **open, hold and maintain a foreign currency account with a bank abroad** for LRS, **without RBI approval**. | 32 | **Income and surrender**: income on LRS investments may be **retained or reinvested**; received / unspent foreign exchange not reinvested is **repatriated and surrendered within 180 days** (FEMA 9(R)/2015-RB reg. 7). (Detail and the travel retention limit: A16b.) | LRS A.17; FAQ Q4 (A16b) |

## Points: TCS on LRS remittances and overseas tour packages (A2.6)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 44 | **Where the rule sits now**: **Income-tax Act, 2025, s. 394(1), Table Sl. No. 7** (remittance under RBI's LRS; collector = **authorised dealer**) and **Sl. No. 8** (sale of an **overseas tour programme package**; collector = **seller**). The 1961 Act's **s. 206C(1G)** is history (the 1961 Act is repealed by Act 30 of 2025). The new Act applies from **tax year 2026-27** (1 Apr 2026). | ITA s. 394(1) Table; FB26 cl. 73 ("section 394 of the Income-tax Act, 2025"); MEM26 p. 48 |
| 45 | **Rates from 1 Apr 2026 (FY 2026-27, the exam year's rule)**: **LRS above ₹10 lakh: (a) 2 % for education or medical treatment; (b) 20 % for other purposes.** **Overseas tour package: 2 % on any amount (no threshold).** | ITA s. 394(1) Sl. 7–8 as amended by FB26 cl. 73(e), (f); MEM26 p. 48 table; FA26 (lead: enacted unchanged) |
| 46 | **Education on a loan: nil.** No TCS by the AD if the amount remitted **is a loan from a financial institution** (s. 129(3)(b): a **banking company**, incl. s. 51 BR Act banks, or a notified institution) **for pursuing any education**. | ITA s. 394(4)(b), s. 129(3)(b) |
| 47 | **Threshold**: Sl. 7 applies to **"an amount or aggregate of the amounts exceeding ten lakh rupees"** in the year; tax is computed on the amount **above ₹10 lakh** (our reading; the 1961 Act's proviso said "in excess of" in words, see row 52). The ₹10 lakh is **one threshold for all LRS purposes together**, tracked per remitter by the AD (lead: per-AD tracking is market practice). **Not** the same as the USD 250,000 FEMA limit (two different laws). | ITA s. 394(1) Sl. 7 col. B; MEM23 table ("in excess of Rs. 7 lakh"); DEP |
| 48 | **No double collection**: the AD does not collect on an amount on which a **tour operator already collected under Sl. 8**; neither the AD nor the seller collects if the buyer is **liable to deduct TDS** on it and **has deducted** it. | ITA s. 394(4)(a), (5) |
| 49 | **When collected**: at the **earlier of debiting the buyer's account or receiving the money** (cash, cheque, draft or any other mode). | ITA s. 394(1)(c) |
| 50 | **No PAN**: the payer must give a **valid PAN**; if not, TCS is the **higher of twice the specified rate or 5 %, capped at 20 %**. So tour package 2 % → **5 %**; LRS education/medical 2 % → **5 %**; other LRS 20 % → **20 %**.
