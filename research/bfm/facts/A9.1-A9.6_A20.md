What this lesson must carry. An International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) is a special financial zone carved inside a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) under Section 18 of the SEZ Act, 2005 (only one IFSC per SEZ) to bring offshore international financial business—ECBs, trade finance, derivatives, fund management, insurance, and aircraft/ship leasing—onto Indian soil while serving as a testing ground for financial reforms. India's first operational IFSC is at GIFT City, Gandhinagar, Gujarat (886 acres: 261 acres SEZ, 625 acres DTA; notified and operational in 2015, not 2019). Under Regulation 3 of FEMA 339/2015-RB, every financial institution or branch in an IFSC is treated as a person resident outside India for FEMA, though domestic Indian laws still apply unless specifically exempted. Since 2020, the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA)—established under the IFSCA Act, 2019 at Gandhinagar (taking over the powers of RBI, SEBI, IRDAI, and PFRDA inside IFSCs from 1 October 2020)—is the unified regulator. Under the IFSCA (Banking) Regulations, 2020 (amended 2023 and 2024) and the IFSCA Banking Handbook, Indian banks and foreign banks licensed by IFSCA (not RBI) may set up an IFSC Banking Unit (IBU) (branch; USD 20 million minimum parent-level capital) or an IFSC Banking Company (IBC) (subsidiary; USD 50 million minimum capital), backed by a home-regulator No Objection Letter and a parent-bank liquidity undertaking (Letter of Comfort). IBUs follow both IFSCA and home-regulator prudential norms, are exempt from CRR and SLR on non-individual liabilities, maintain a daily 3% Retail Deposit Reserve Ratio (RDRR) on individual deposits, maintain LCR/NSFR (at parent level with IFSCA prior approval), face 5% single-borrower and 10% group-borrower exposure ceilings of parent Tier 1 capital, receive no RBI Lender of Last Resort (LOLR) support, have no separate deposit insurance scheme (DICGC trap), and comply with IFSCA's 2022 AML/CFT/KYC Guidelines. IBUs transact in 15 specified foreign currencies (expanded July 2024), maintain separate Nostro accounts and an INR expense account (SNRRA), cannot handle cash or join domestic money/forex/payment systems, and may offer individual current/savings/term accounts versus corporate current/term accounts only. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026) and Finance Acts, IFSC units get a 100% tax holiday for 10 out of 15 years (s. 147, formerly s. 80LA), non-resident IFSC-exchange transfer exemptions (s. 70(1)(r), formerly s. 47(viiab)), specified fund/derivative/leasing exemptions (Schedule VI, formerly s. 10(4D)–(4F), extended to 31 March 2030), and 0% STT/CTT/GST/stamp duty. FPIs at GIFT City enjoy up to 23.5-hour trading, non-agri commodity derivatives, Segregated Nominee Account Structure, and SEBI's 2024 up-to-100% NRI/OCI/RI corpus relaxation. Recent RBI updates include July 2024 expanded LRS remittances to IFSCs, October 2025 3-month exporter FCA repatriation window in IFSCs, and October 2026 hedging-only restriction on INR NDDCs offered by AD Cat-I banks with IBUs.
