## A3.1 Correspondent banking
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Correspondent banking = one bank (the **correspondent**) providing banking services to another bank (the **respondent**). Services include cash management (e.g. interest-bearing accounts in several currencies), international wire transfers, cheque clearing, payable-through accounts and foreign exchange services. | KYC, definitions |
| 2 | Why banks use it: to pay and collect abroad without opening a branch there. | Concept |
| 3 | Other services a correspondent commonly gives: collection of bills, LC advising and confirmation, reimbursement under LCs, guarantees, lines of credit, investment of surplus balances, credit reports. Some need an account (payments and receipts in the currency, reimbursements, surplus investment); others need only a SWIFT relationship (exchanging authenticated messages, e.g. LC advising). | Concept (the Swift RMA: an exchange of messaging keys between two banks; **(freeze)** confirm wording on swift.com) |
| 4 | Every bank must have a **Board-approved policy** (or a committee headed by the Chairman/CEO/MD) on cross-border correspondent relationships. | KYC para 73 |
| 5 | Before a relationship: understand the respondent's business, reputation and quality of supervision (incl. any ML/TF action); **assess its AML/CFT controls**; gather management, business, purpose of the account, third parties using it, home-country supervision. | KYC para 73(1)–(2) |
| 6 | **Prior approval of senior management** for a new correspondent relationship, with **post facto approval of the Board** (or empowered committee). | KYC para 73(3) |
| 7 | Payable-through accounts: the correspondent must be satisfied that the respondent has done CDD on the customers with direct access, and does ongoing due diligence. The respondent must give CDD data immediately on request. | KYC para 73(5)–(6) |
| 8 | **No correspondent relationship with a shell bank**, and the respondent must not let shell banks use its accounts. Be cautious with banks in jurisdictions with strategic AML deficiencies (FATF). | KYC para 73(7)–(9) |
| 9 | Shell bank = a bank with no physical presence ("meaningful mind and management") in its country of incorporation and licence, and not part of a regulated group under effective consolidated supervision. A local agent or low-level staff is not physical presence. | KYC, definitions |

## A3.2 Nostro, Vostro, Loro
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Nostro** ("ours"): our bank's account with a bank abroad, in that country's currency (e.g. an Indian bank's USD account in New York). | Concept |
| 11 | **Vostro** ("yours"): a foreign bank's account with us, usually in rupees (e.g. a London bank's rupee account in Mumbai). The same account is Nostro to the holder and Vostro to the bank that keeps it. | Concept |
| 12 | **Loro** ("theirs"): a third bank's account, spoken of by two other banks (e.g. bank A tells bank B about bank C's account with A). | Concept |
| 13 | **Mirror account**: the bank's own copy of its Nostro, kept in its books, to reconcile against the correspondent's statement. | Concept |
| 14 | Rupee Vostro accounts of non-resident banks are opened by AD banks under FEM (Deposit) Regulations, 2016, Reg. 7(1). | SRVA para 3 (cites the regulation) |
| 15 | Since **2 Sep 2026**, AD banks **no longer** send RBI (i) the yearly list of branches keeping rupee accounts of non-resident banks, or (ii) reports of temporary overdrawals by overseas branches or correspondents not adjusted within five days. | NRB |
| 16 | **Inward payments (from 9 Oct 2026)**: inform the customer **immediately** on receipt of the inward message (after hours: at the start of the next business day); reconcile Nostro credits near real time or at intervals **normally not over one hour**; endeavour to credit payments received during forex market hours **the same business day**, and those after hours the next business day; straight-through processing allowed for individual residents on risk assessment. | XB para 3–4; FEDAI 4.5 (as amended 7 Sep 2026) |
| 17 | Export proceeds on collection: converted at TT buying only **after** the money is credited to the Nostro (2.5(a)). As amended **7 Sep 2026**: the bank **shall endeavour** to credit proceeds received in forex market hours the **same business day**, and after hours the next business day; on receipt of the Nostro credit advice and compliance checks, transfer to the exporter's account **within two working business days** (outer limit) (2.5(b)). If late: compensation at the **minimum interest rate charged on export credit**, plus exchange-rate compensation per the bank's policy (2.5(c)). | FEDAI 2.5(a)–(c) |
| 18 | Why TT rates are better than bill rates: with a TT the Nostro is credited (or debited) at once; with a bill bought, the bank pays first and its Nostro is credited weeks later. | Concept (links A01, F11) |

## SRVA (Special Rupee Vostro Account): trade settled in rupees
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | AD banks may open SRVAs of **their own branch outside India** or of **a bank resident outside India** (FEM Deposit Regs, Reg. 7(1)). **No prior RBI approval** (since Aug 2025, now in para 3). | SRVA para 3; Annex row 8 |
| 20 | SRVA is an **additional** arrangement for invoicing, payment and settlement of exports and imports in **INR**. **All permissible current and capital account transactions** under FEMA may also be settled through it (incl. merchanting trade). | SRVA para 4; SRVA-FAQ Q10, Q19 |
| 21 | AD banks with an SRVA may open an **additional current account** for an exporter or importer, **exclusively** for these export/import settlements. | SRVA para 4 |
| 22 | Funded by inward remittances or transfers from other repatriable INR accounts (RVA, SNRR); proceeds of permissible transactions may be held in it. Balances are **freely repatriable**. | SRVA para 5; SRVA-FAQ Q8, Q18 |
| 23 | Surplus balances may be invested in debt instruments under the RBI (Non-resident Investment in Debt Instruments) Directions, 2025 (G-secs, T-bills; since Oct 2025 also NCDs, bonds and CP). No FPI licence needed for T-bills and G-secs. | SRVA para 6, Annex row 9; SRVA-FAQ Q13 |
| 24 | An existing ordinary Rupee Vostro account **cannot** be used as an SRVA. One foreign bank may hold SRVAs with several AD banks; an AD bank may open several SRVAs from one country. The exchange rate is market determined (cross rates where no direct quote). | SRVA-FAQ Q3–Q7 |
| 25 | The AD bank in India reports SRVA transactions; SRVA details are listed in FEDAI's **SRVA directory**. Benefit: less exchange-rate risk for Indian traders. | SRVA para 7; SRVA-FAQ Q14–Q16 |

## A3.3 Electronic transmission and payment systems

|---|---|---|---|
| 26 | **SWIFT** | A cooperative (S.W.I.F.T. SC) owned by its member financial institutions; incorporated 1973, head office La Hulpe, Belgium. **Carries messages, not money**: the money moves in the banks' accounts (Nostro/Vostro) and in payment systems. | SWIFT (freeze) |

| 28 | **Fedwire** Funds Service | Federal Reserve Banks; USA; USD; **real-time gross settlement**; transfers immediate, final and irrevocable. | FED |
| 29 | **CHIPS** | The Clearing House (private sector); USA (New York); USD high-value; continuous intraday settlement with a **liquidity-saving mechanism** that matches and offsets payments (net-type, needs far less funding than gross). | TCH |
| 30 | **CHAPS** | Bank of England (operator since Nov 2017); UK; sterling high-value; each payment settled **individually in real time** in the BoE's RTGS (gross). | BOE (freeze) |
| 31 | **T2** (formerly TARGET2) | Eurosystem / ECB; euro RTGS; **T2 replaced TARGET2 in March 2023**. | ECB |
| 32 | **RTGS** (India) | Owned and operated by RBI; real-time gross; **24x7x365 since 14 Dec 2020**; minimum **Rs 2 lakh**, no maximum; RBI waived its processing charge from 1 Jul 2019. Beneficiary bank credits within **30 minutes** of the message; if it cannot, returns within **one hour** (or before the end of the RTGS day); delay → compensation at **repo rate + 2%**, credited automatically. No future-dated transactions. Membership needs INFINET/SFMS/domestic SWIFT. | RTGS-FAQ |
| 33 | **NEFT** (India) | Owned and operated by RBI; **half-hourly batches, 24x7x365**; no minimum; credit expected within **two hours** of batch settlement; outward cross-border only to Nepal (Indo-Nepal Remittance Facility). | NEFT-FAQ |
| 34 | **SFMS**, **INFINET**, **IDRBT** | **IDRBT** (Institute for Development and Research in Banking Technology, Hyderabad, set up by RBI) built **INFINET** (the banks' closed private network, June 1999) and **SFMS** (messaging, launched 14 Dec 2001); **RTGS and NEFT were developed at IDRBT** on SFMS and INFINET. RBI **owns and operates** RTGS and NEFT. IDRBT moved INFINET, SFMS, RTGS and NEFT infrastructure to its subsidiary **IFTAS** (Feb 2015); RBI took over IFTAS (2019). RTGS **went live on 26 Mar 2004** with four banks; customer transactions from 29 Apr 2004. Trap: "IDRBT runs RTGS" (it developed it; RBI runs it). | IFTAS; RBI Annual Policy Statement 2004-05 (https://rbi.org.in/Upload/Notification/Pdfs/53294.pdf); RBI press release 16 Aug 2004 (https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/PressRelease/PDFs/55844.pdf); IDRBT history (https://www.idrbt.ac.in/the-journey-of-idrbt/) (search excerpts; freeze: open once) |

## Traps
- Nostro and Vostro reversed (the same account has two names, by whose side you stand on).
- "SWIFT moves the money" (it carries messages).
- "CHIPS is run by the Federal Reserve" (The Clearing House runs CHIPS; the Fed runs Fedwire).
- "CHAPS is a net system" (it settles each payment gross, in real time).
- "RTGS has a maximum" / "NEFT has a minimum" (RTGS: min Rs 2 lakh, no max; NEFT: no min).
- "SRVA needs RBI approval" (not since Aug 2025).
- "An old Rupee Vostro account can become an SRVA" (no).
