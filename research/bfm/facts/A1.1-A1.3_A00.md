## A1.1 What foreign exchange is, and the market
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Foreign currency" means any currency other than Indian currency. And "currency" (s. 2(h)) is wide: notes, postal notes and orders, money orders, cheques, drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit, bills of exchange, promissory notes, credit cards and instruments RBI notifies (debit and ATM cards: Notification FEMA 15/2000-RB, 3 May 2000, https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_FemaNotifications.aspx?Id=169). Only "currency notes" (s. 2(i)) means notes and coins. So foreign currency is not just the note. | FEMA s. 2(h), 2(i), 2(m) |
| 2 | "Foreign exchange" means foreign currency **and includes** (i) deposits, credits and balances payable in any foreign currency; (ii) drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit or bills of exchange, expressed or drawn in Indian currency but payable in any foreign currency; (iii) drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit or bills of exchange drawn by banks, institutions or persons outside India, but payable in Indian currency. | FEMA s. 2(n) |
| 3 | So foreign exchange is wider than foreign currency: a claim, deposit or instrument can be foreign exchange. Clause (iii) is the surprise: an instrument payable **in rupees** is foreign exchange if drawn by someone outside India. | FEMA s. 2(n)(iii); our reading |
| 4 | "Indian currency" means currency expressed or drawn in Indian rupees (excluding special bank notes and special one-rupee notes under RBI Act s. 28A). | FEMA s. 2(q) |
| 5 | FEMA came into force on 1 June 2000, replacing FERA 1973. | RBI-FEMA, RBI-CHR (row 1999) |
| 6 | Exchange rate = the price of one currency in terms of another. India quotes **direct**: rupees for 1 (or 100) units of foreign currency. | Concept; FEDAI 5.7 |
| 7 | Why it matters: a weaker rupee raises the rupee cost of an import and the rupee value of an export receipt. | Concept |
| 8 | Market features: over the counter (no single exchange building), mostly electronic, deals across centres round the clock on weekdays as the trading day moves round the world. | Concept |
| 9 | Onshore FCY/INR market hours in India: 9.00 a.m. to 5.00 p.m. IST; Saturday not a working day for forex (except individual transactions under 1.2). | FEDAI 1.1, 1.3 |
| 10 | Two tiers: **interbank** (banks dealing with each other, the wholesale market) and **merchant** (bank with customer); merchant rates are built off interbank rates. | Concept; FEDAI (merchant rates) |
| 11 | Participants and motives: central bank (reserves, steadying the market), banks (customer cover, trading), corporates (trade, hedging), investors and funds, brokers, individuals. Motives: trade, investment, hedging, speculation, arbitrage. | Concept |
| 12 | Value dates: **cash/ready** = same day; **tom** (value next day) = next working day; **spot** = second working day after the deal; **forward** = beyond spot, period counted from the spot date. | FEDAI 5.2 |
| 13 | Spot is the second working day, so holidays are skipped (a holiday on day 2 pushes spot later). The preceding / next rule is only for the **fixed or last delivery date of a merchant forward contract**: a holiday known at least 3 working days before (1.4) → the **preceding** working day; a suddenly declared one → the **next** working day (5.2). Interbank contracts (8.09): a maturity later declared a holiday is preponed only if it is a month-end known holiday; otherwise postponed to the next working day. | FEDAI 1.4, 5.2, 8.09 (expert review v1 read the 10th ed. text) |
| 13a | Spot is the base rate of the interbank market; forward periods are counted from the spot value date. | FEDAI 5.2; recalled Dec 2025 Q71 |
| 14 | Arbitrage: buying in one centre and selling in another to profit from a price difference. **Simple** = two centres/currencies; **compound** (triangular) = three or more. It pulls prices together. | Concept |

## A1.2 Why rates move
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | At bottom, the rate is price set by demand for and supply of the currency (the see-saw). | Concept |
| 15a | More rupees per dollar = a weaker rupee (direct quote). | Concept; FEDAI 5.7 |
| 15b | Balance of payments (JAIIB refresher): current account = trade, services, remittances (transfers); capital account = investment and loans. Not the bank's current deposit account. | Concept |
| 16 | **Fundamental** factors: balance of payments (current account deficit weakens; surplus strengthens), inflation (home inflation **higher than abroad** weakens the currency), interest rates (higher rates attract capital, strengthen in the short run), growth, fiscal position, foreign investment flows (FDI/FPI inflows strengthen), political stability. | Concept |
| 17 | **Technical** factors: government controls, capital-flow restrictions, the market's demand and supply position at the time. | Concept |
| 18 | **Speculative** factors: expectations; traders buying a currency they expect to rise make it rise; can cause sharp swings. | Concept |
| 19 | PPP (idea): the same basket of goods should cost the same in two countries; higher inflation → currency weakens over time. | Concept |
| 20 | Interest rate parity (idea): the higher-interest currency trades at a **forward discount**; the lower-interest one at a forward premium. Numbers taught in A05. | Concept |
| 21 | Trap: "higher inflation makes a currency stronger" is wrong; "higher-interest currency is at a forward premium" is wrong. | from 16, 20 |

## A1.3 Exchange rate mechanism: regimes and India's path
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 21a | **Devaluation** = an official cut in a fixed/pegged rate; **depreciation** = a fall under a float by market forces; **appreciation** = a rise under a float. | Concept |
| 22 | Regimes: **fixed** (pegged to a currency or basket; central bank holds it), **floating** (market sets it), **managed float** (market sets it; central bank intervenes to smooth it, no announced target). | Concept; RBI-DRG |
| 23 | India moved "from a par value system to a basket-peg and further to a managed float". | RBI-DRG |
| 24 | After Bretton Woods broke down in 1971, the rupee was linked with the pound sterling. | RBI-DRG |
| 25 | From September 1975 the rupee was pegged to a basket of currencies (to avoid the weakness of a single-currency peg), till the early 1990s. | RBI-DRG |
| 26 | July 1991: devalued in two steps (1 and 3 July 1991), cumulatively about 18% in USD terms (RBI-CHR; RBI-DRG gives 9% and 11%). | RBI-CHR, RBI-DRG |
| 27 | **LERMS, March 1992**: a dual exchange rate system, the first step to a market-determined rate. "40 per cent of exchange earnings had to be surrendered at an official rate determined by the Reserve Bank"; "the balance 60 per cent … converted at rates determined by the market". RBI sold FX at the official rate only for essential imports (oil, fertiliser, life-saving drugs) and Government debt service. Official rate cut in early December 1992; "ultimate convergence of the dual rates … effective from March 1, 1993". | RBI-CHR; RBI page https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=2253 (read 2026-11-20) |
| 28 | **March 1993**: the dual rates unified; a unified, market-determined exchange rate (convergence from 1 March 1993). | RBI-DRG ("replaced by a unified exchange rate system in March 1993"); RBI-CHR ("1993 Unified Exchange rate") |
| 29 | **August 1994**: rupee convertible on the current account; India accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. | RBI-CHR |
| 29a | Current account convertibility does not mean no rules: the Central Government may impose reasonable restrictions on current account transactions (FEMA s. 5); the FEM (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000 prohibit a few items, need approval for some and cap others. Most current transactions need no special permission. | FEMA s. 5; CAT Rules 2000 (expert review v2) **(freeze: read the Rules' schedules once)** |
| 30 | RBI's stance today: foreign exchange operations "to maintain orderly market conditions and contain excessive volatility in the exchange rate"; agenda 2026-27 "to curb excessive volatility in the USD/INR exchange rate"; operations "principle-based, guided by the objective of ensuring orderly movements in the exchange rate". No fixed or pre-announced target or band. | RBI-AR V.20, V.22, V.49; RBI-DRG |
| 31 | RBI acts through the onshore/offshore OTC and exchange-traded currency derivative segments (buying and selling dollars from its reserves). | RBI-AR V.20 |
| 32 | **FBIL** (Financial Benchmarks India Pvt Ltd) computes and publishes the reference rate since **10 July 2018** (took over from RBI). | RBI-PR |
| 33 | USD/INR reference rate: from actual spot USD/INR trades on electronic platforms (Refinitiv, CCIL), in a randomly chosen 15-minute slot between 11.30 and 12.30; outliers removed; volume-weighted average; published to 4 decimals at around 1.30 p.m. on Mumbai business days. EUR, GBP, JPY via cross rates (JPY per 100). | FBIL §4, 7–11 **(freeze: check for a later version)** |
| 34 | FBIL publishes rates for more of India's trading partners from 5 Jan 2026. | RBI-AR (Ch. V) |
| 35 | The reference rate is a benchmark (used e.g. for converting foreign currency assets and liabilities in regulatory returns), not a rate RBI imposes on deals. | RBI-PR; RBI MD 11355 title (search) |

## What the exam asks here (question blueprint)
- **Define**: foreign exchange under FEMA (what is included, clause (iii)); spot; arbitrage.
- **Who / when**: who publishes the reference rate (FBIL, since 2018); LERMS 1992; unified rate 1993; current
  account convertibility 1994; spot = second working day.
- **Purpose**: why RBI intervenes (volatility, orderly conditions, not a level).
- **Compare**: fixed vs floating vs managed float; interbank vs merchant; simple vs compound arbitrage; cash, tom,
  spot, forward.
- **Exception**: a holiday moves the value date (known: earlier; sudden: later).
- **Apply to a case**: which factors strengthen or weaken the rupee; spot the irrelevant one.
- Recalled: "Factors affecting foreign exchange rates" (Jun 2023); settlement-date questions (Jun 2023, Jul 2024).
