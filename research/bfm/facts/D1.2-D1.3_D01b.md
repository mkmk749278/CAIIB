# Fact sheet · D1.2 What is ALM; significance; purpose and objectives · D1.3 ALM as co-ordinated balance sheet management · C7.1–C7.2, C7.4–C7.5 Treasury and ALM (video D01b)
Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1] … [F39]. Our own words; every regulatory row cites its primary text with full URL and paragraph number.


What this lesson must carry. Asset-Liability Management (ALM) is the coordinated planning, measurement, and management of a bank's assets, liabilities, off-balance sheet items, income, and expenses so that Net Interest Income (NII), Net Interest Margin (NIM), and Market Value of Equity (MVE) stay stable within Board-approved risk limits. Deregulated interest rates, market-linked investments, competition, rate/FX volatility, and embedded customer options (loan prepayments and premature deposit withdrawals) make ALM essential. ALM combines price matching (keeping yields above funding costs) and liquidity matching (managing cash-flow gaps across maturity buckets), recognising that liquidity risk turns into interest-rate risk when mismatches must be refinanced at market rates. Under the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Asset Liability Management) Directions, 2025 (effective 28 Nov 2025), governance has four tiers: the Board of Directors approves ALM/liquidity, investment, and derivative policies and limits and reviews them at least annually; the Risk Management Committee (RMC) evaluates liquidity and its interaction with other risks; the Asset Liability Management Committee (ALCO), headed by the CEO/CMD or Executive Director, implements strategy, decides funding/maturity mix, product pricing, and transfer pricing policy; and the ALM Support Group runs analysis and simulations. From 1 October 2026, banks must publish deposit rate schedules in advance on their websites—bulk deposit rates (₹3 crore and above) by 10:00 am (grace till 10:10 am) each business day—offer uniform rates across branches without negotiation, and may differentiate bulk rates by LCR run-off rates. Treasury manages the bank's central pool of funds and HQLA, executes ALCO decisions, gives pricing inputs, and hedges residual mismatches using permitted derivatives (IRS for repricing gaps, FRA/IRF for future rate gaps, currency swaps for FX mismatches) without trying to eliminate every mismatch (since maturity transformation earns the term premium). Five core Board/ALCO policies govern treasury and ALM—ALM, Liquidity, Investment, Derivatives, and Transfer Pricing—alongside FIMMDA and FEDAI codes of conduct. Funds Transfer Pricing (FTP) uses market-based internal transfer rates (incorporating market rate, liquidity/hedging cost, and reserve cost) to split total bank spread three ways—deposit spread, lending spread, and treasury mismatch spread—centralising rate and liquidity risk in Treasury while leaving credit risk with lending units.

| Why ALM matters now: Five drivers make ALM critical today: (a) deregulated interest rates on deposits, loans, and market-linked investments; (b) intense competition squeezing spreads; (c) rate and FX volatility; (d) product innovation and embedded options (prepayments, premature withdrawals); and (e) stricter regulatory standards (LCR, NSFR, IRRBB). | ALM99 intro; ALM25 paras 33, 38 | 4 |
| ALM objectives (earnings & economic value): ALM aims for stable short-term earnings (protecting Net Interest Income / NII and Net Interest Margin / NIM under the earnings perspective) and growth/protection of Net Worth (Market Value of Equity / MVE under the economic value perspective), keeping all risks within Board-approved limits. | ALM25 paras 14, 251 | 5 |
| Liquidity defined: Liquidity is a bank's capacity to fund increases in assets and meet both expected and unexpected cash and collateral obligations at reasonable cost without unacceptable losses. Failure even at one bank can have systemic implications. | ALM25 para 19 | 6 |
| Funding vs Market Liquidity Risk: Liquidity risk manifests in two forms: Funding Liquidity Risk (inability to meet current and future cash/collateral needs efficiently) and Market Liquidity Risk (inability to offset or exit a position at market price due to inadequate depth or disruption). | ALM25 paras 4(8), 4(10), 19 | 7 |
| Price matching vs liquidity matching: ALM works on two linked fronts: price matching (keeping lending yields above cost of funds to protect NII/NIM) and liquidity matching (grouping cash inflows and outflows by maturity bucket to control funding gaps). | ALM25 paras 34, 255; ALM99 intro | 8 |
| ALM as coordinated balance-sheet management: ALM acts as the hub coordinating asset decisions (loans, investments, liquid assets), liability decisions (deposits, borrowings, capital), off-balance-sheet items, and income and expense management into one risk-return plan. | ALM99 para 5.2; ALM25 paras 26(1)–(2), 33 | 9 |
| Embedded options (behavioural option risk): Loan prepayments, premature deposit closures, and call/put features let customers alter cash-flow timing. Borrowers prepay fixed loans when rates fall (reinvestment risk) and hold them when rates rise, while depositors withdraw early when rates rise—squeezing NII and MVE. | ALM25 paras 33, 38, 268; SRP98.15, 98.35 | 10 |
| Liquidity risk turning into interest-rate risk: A maturity mismatch (borrowing short, lending long) means maturing deposits or borrowings must be refinanced at prevailing market rates; thus a liquidity gap immediately becomes an interest-rate risk to NII. | SRP98.15; ALM25 paras 34, 254 | 11 |
| Gap sign rule: In any time bucket, Gap = Rate Sensitive Assets (RSA) − Rate Sensitive Liabilities (RSL) (or Inflows − Outflows in liquidity). A positive gap (RSA > RSL) gains when rates rise and loses when rates fall; a negative gap (RSL > RSA) loses when rates rise and gains when rates fall. | ALM99 para 8.3 | 12 |
| Bucket placement of fixed-rate vs floating-rate items: Rate-sensitive assets, liabilities, and OBS items are slotted by residual maturity or next repricing date, whichever is earlier. A fixed-rate item sits in its residual maturity bucket; a floating-rate item sits in its next repricing bucket. | ALM25 paras 254, 267 | 13 |
| Time buckets in IRS and SLS: The Interest Rate Sensitivity (IRS) statement uses 11 buckets (1–28 days through Over 15 years, plus Non-sensitive). In the Structural Liquidity Statement (SLS), net cumulative negative mismatches cannot exceed 5% (next day), 10% (2–7 days), 15% (8–14 days), and 20% (15–30 days) of cumulative outflows. | ALM25 paras 43, 266 | 14 |
| Why a bank does not eliminate every mismatch: Perfect matching is neither possible nor desirable: maturity transformation (borrowing short, lending long) earns the term premium that drives bank income, and eliminating economic-value risk simultaneously raises earnings volatility—a deliberate trade-off is required. | SRP98.28 fn 4; ALM99 para 7.2 | 15 |
| Economic Equity Ratio: Defined as Owners' Funds (Net Worth) ÷ Total Assets (standard banking ratio; Net Worth is defined in ALM25 para 4(12) as paid-up capital + free reserves + share premium + P&L credit balance less accumulated losses/intangibles, excluding revaluation reserves and provisions).
