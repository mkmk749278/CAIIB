# Fact sheet · D5.2 Duration Gap Analysis · Loan Pricing Benchmarks: Base Rate, MCLR, EBLR (video D06)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1]–[F30]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text with exact paragraph/clause numbers and full URLs. Sister sheets: D05 (sources of IRR, TGA, repricing gap, ΔNII, NIM, EaR) and D06b (IRR strategies, controls, IRRBB Pillar 2/3).


What this lesson must carry. Interest rate risk affects both short-term earnings (TGA, covered in the previous class) and long-term net worth or Market Value of Equity (MVE), measured by Duration Gap Analysis (DGA) under the RBI (Commercial Banks – Asset Liability Management) Directions, 2025 (28 Nov 2025). Banks must run both TGA and DGA across their global balance sheet (banking and trading books) and separately for any currency accounting for 5% or more of global assets or liabilities. Modified Duration (MD) measures the approximate percentage change in an instrument's value for a 100-basis point (1%) interest rate move. Modified Duration Gap is MDG = MDA − (RSL / RSA) × MDL, where W = RSL / RSA is the leverage weight. The rupee change in equity is ΔE = −MDG × RSA × Δi (with Δi written as a decimal, e.g., 0.02 for +200 bps); equivalently, Modified Duration of Equity (MDOE) = MDG × (RSA / Equity), so a 200 bps shock changes MVE by MDOE × 2%. A decline in banking-book economic value exceeding 20% of MVE under a 200 bps standardised shock marks the bank as a supervisory outlier under Pillar II; this decline is an economic impact, not an accounting loss, since the banking book is not marked to market. On loan pricing, under the RBI (Commercial Banks – Interest Rates on Advances) Directions, 2025 (28 Nov 2025), floating rupee loans moved from BPLR (up to 30 June 2010) to Base Rate (1 July 2010–31 March 2016; one rate per bank, reviewed at least quarterly), to MCLR (from 1 April 2016; four components: Marginal Cost of Funds [92% borrowings + 8% return on net worth], Negative Carry on CRR [CRR × MCF / (1 − CRR)], Operating Costs, Tenor Premium; five mandatory maturities: overnight, 1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y; reviewed monthly; reset period 1 year or lower matching the linked MCLR tenor; anchored to the date of first disbursement), to EBLR (mandatory for retail/personal and MSE floating loans from 1 Oct 2019, and Medium Enterprises from 1 Apr 2020; benchmarked to RBI policy repo rate—5.50% from 7 Oct 2026—or FBIL 3M/6M T-bill or other FBIL rate; uniform within a loan category; reset at least once in three months). Under EBLR, credit risk premium changes only on substantial change in credit assessment; other spread components change once in three years, except that from 1 October 2025, banks may reduce other spread components earlier than three years for customer retention on justifiable, non-discriminatory grounds. For EMI-based floating personal loans, from 1 October 2025, offering a switchover to a fixed rate at reset became optional at the bank's discretion (changed from mandatory), while no negative amortisation and quarterly borrower statements remain mandatory. Fixed-rate loans above 3 years are exempt from benchmark floor rules; fixed-rate loans up to 3 years cannot be priced below the sum of MCLR components.

| Global scope & 5% currency rule: Both DGA and TGA apply to the bank's global position (domestic and overseas branches) across rate-sensitive assets, liabilities, and off-balance sheet items. | Modified Duration (MD) definition: Modified Duration of an asset or liability measures the approximate percentage change in its value for a 100-basis point (1%) change in the interest rate. (Macaulay Duration D converts to Modified Duration via MD = D / (1 + y).) | ALM25 para 4(11), para 259 | 4 |
| MDA and MDL: MDA is the weighted average Modified Duration of all Rate Sensitive Assets (RSA); MDL is the weighted average Modified Duration of all Rate Sensitive Liabilities (RSL). Positive rate-sensitive OBS notional amounts are added to RSA; negative OBS notional amounts are added to RSL. | ALM25 paras 259, 263(2) | 5 |
| Modified Duration Gap (MDG) formula: MDG = MDA − (RSL / RSA) × MDL, where W = RSL / RSA is the liability-to-asset weight. A higher absolute MDG means greater exposure of the bank's net worth to interest rate shocks. Omitting the W = RSL / RSA weight is a classic exam error. | ALM25 paras 260–261; DGA06 para 8 | 6 |
| Change in Market Value of Equity (ΔE): ΔE = −[MDG] × RSA × Δi, where Equity means Net Worth and Δi is the interest rate change written as a decimal (1% = 0.01; +200 bps = +0.02). With positive MDG (typical for banks that borrow short and lend long), a rate rise causes MVE to fall; a rate cut causes MVE to rise. | ALM25 para 261 | 7 |
| Modified Duration of Equity (MDOE): MDOE = MDG × Leverage, where Leverage = RSA / Equity. For a 200 bps (2%) parallel rate shock, the approximate percentage change in MVE is −MDOE × 2%. Both ΔE / Equity and MDOE × Δi produce the exact same percentage impact. | DGA06 para 9; ALM25 para 263(8) | 8 |
| Market value vs book value: Market values (via discounted cash flows) should ideally be used for RSA and RSL; for simplicity, book values (inclusive of notional value of rate-sensitive OBS items) may be used as an approximation. | ALM25 paras 257, 263 | 9 |
| Economic impact, not an accounting loss: An estimated drop in MVE under a DGA shock measures the economic impact on net worth if the shock occurs; it is not an accounting loss because the banking book is held at amortised cost and not marked to market. | ALM25 para 262; DGA10 para 3; BIS-SRP98 para 98.6 | 10 |
| Supervisory outlier threshold (200 bps / 20% MVE): Under Pillar II, a bank is treated as a supervisory outlier (excessive IRR) if a standardised 200 bps interest rate shock reduces the economic value of its banking book by more than 20% of MVE. Although banks compute DGA across the entire balance sheet (banking + trading books), the outlier test applies to the banking book, not the combined whole-bank figure. | ALM25 paras 264, 265, 277; DGA10 para 8 | 11 |
| 11 time buckets & mid-point proxy: The Interest Rate Sensitivity (IRS) statement under TGA and DGA uses 11 time buckets (1–28 days, 29 days–3m, 3m–6m, 6m–1y, 1–3y, 3–5y, 5–7y, 7–10y, 10–15y, over 15y, and Non-sensitive). Where individual MD is not computed, the mid-point of each time bucket proxies maturity. | ALM25 paras 263(3), 266 | 12 |
