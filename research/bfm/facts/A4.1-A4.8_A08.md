## Points
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | UCP 600 applies when the credit **expressly states** it is subject to UCP; binding on all parties unless modified or excluded by the credit; applies to standby LCs to the extent applicable. | UCP 1 |
| 2 | **Parties**: applicant (at whose request the credit is issued); beneficiary (in whose favour); issuing bank (issues at the applicant's request or on its own behalf); advising bank (advises at the issuing bank's request); confirming bank (adds confirmation on the issuing bank's authorisation or request); nominated bank (with which the credit is available, or any bank if available with any bank). | UCP 2 |
| 3 | A credit is **irrevocable**, even if it does not say so; there is no revocable credit under UCP 600. | UCP 2 ("credit"), 3 |
| 4 | **Honour** = pay at sight (sight payment) · incur a deferred payment undertaking and pay at maturity (deferred payment) · accept a draft and pay at maturity (acceptance). **Negotiation** = purchase by the nominated bank of drafts (drawn on another bank) and/or documents under a complying presentation, by advancing or agreeing to advance funds before reimbursement is due. | UCP 2 |
| 5 | **Independence**: a credit is separate from the sale contract; banks are not bound by it even if the credit refers to it; the issuing bank should discourage attaching the contract or proforma invoice. | UCP 4 |
| 6 | **Banks deal with documents, not goods**, services or performance. | UCP 5 |
| 7 | A credit must state **where** it is available (a bank, or any bank) and **how** (sight payment, deferred payment, acceptance or negotiation); must **not** be available by a draft drawn **on the applicant**; must state an **expiry date** for presentation. | UCP 6 |
| 8 | The **issuing bank** is irrevocably bound to honour from the time it issues the credit, and must reimburse a nominated bank that honoured or negotiated a complying presentation. | UCP 7 |
| 9 | The **confirming bank** gives a definite undertaking **in addition to** the issuing bank's; bound from the time it adds confirmation; **negotiates without recourse**. A bank asked to confirm but unwilling must tell the issuing bank without delay and may advise without confirming. | UCP 2, 8 |
| 10 | The **advising bank** (not confirming) advises **without any undertaking** to honour or negotiate; by advising it signifies it has satisfied itself of the **apparent authenticity** and that the advice reflects the terms. | UCP 9 |
| 11 | Amendment or cancellation needs the agreement of the **issuing bank, the confirming bank (if any) and the beneficiary**; partial acceptance of an amendment = rejection; "in force unless rejected within x days" is disregarded. | UCP 10 |
| 12 | A **nominated bank** that is not confirming has **no obligation** to honour or negotiate unless it expressly agrees and tells the beneficiary; merely receiving, examining and forwarding documents does not make it liable. | UCP 12 |
| 13 | **Disclaimers**: banks are not responsible for the form, accuracy or genuineness of documents or for the goods (34); for delays and losses in transmission (35; but a complying presentation lost between banks must still be honoured or reimbursed); for force majeure (36; a credit that expires during the interruption is not honoured after it); for acts of an instructed party, at the applicant's risk (37). | UCP 34–37 |
| 14 | **Transferable credit**: must specifically say "transferable"; the bank need not transfer except as it consents; transferable **once** (a second beneficiary cannot transfer onward; the first beneficiary is not a "subsequent" one); to **more than one** second beneficiary only if **partial drawings/shipments are allowed**; amount, unit price, expiry, presentation period and latest shipment date may be **reduced**, insurance % **increased**; the first beneficiary may **substitute its own invoice** and draw the difference; transfer charges paid by the first beneficiary unless agreed. | UCP 38 |
| 15 | A credit not stated to be transferable can still have its **proceeds assigned** (assignment of proceeds, not of the right to perform). | UCP 39 |
| 16 | **Back-to-back credit**: a second, separate credit issued on the strength of an export credit received (by the middleman's bank, in favour of the supplier); two independent credits, unlike a transfer of one credit. | Market practice (not a UCP article; label "practice") |
| 17 | **Red clause**: lets the nominated bank make an **advance to the beneficiary before shipment**, under the issuing bank's (and so the applicant's) undertaking to repay if the beneficiary does not ship; **green clause** adds that the goods are warehoused in the bank's name as security. **Revolving credit**: the amount reinstates automatically (by time or by value), cumulative or non-cumulative. | Market practice (label "practice"); UCP has no article |
| 18 | **Standby LC (SBLC)**: a credit used as a **guarantee**, paid on the beneficiary's statement of default; may be issued subject to UCP 600 (to the extent applicable, Art. 1) or **ISP98** (ICC Publication No. 590). | UCP 1; ISP98 (name only) |
| 19 | RBI (imports): ADs follow normal banking procedure and **UCPDC** when opening import LCs; except negative-list goods (licence under FTP), ADs may **freely open LCs** and allow import remittances. | MDIMP B.1(ii), B.2 |
| 20 | RBI (advance for imports): advance above **USD 200,000** needs an unconditional, irrevocable **SBLC** or guarantee from an international bank of repute abroad (or an AD guarantee backed by one); the AD may waive it up to **USD 5 million** for importers with a good track record (Board policy); PSUs / Government need a Ministry of Finance waiver above **USD 100,000**. | MDIMP C.1 (as updated 12 Jan 2026; **verify** against the FEM (EIGS) Regulations 2026 regime in force from 1 Oct 2026) |
| 21 | **Guarantees and SBLCs with a non-resident party (2026 regulations)**: principles-based, no list of import-SBLC conditions. A resident may act as surety or principal debtor if (a) the underlying transaction is not prohibited under FEMA and (b) surety and principal debtor may lend to / borrow from each other under the Borrowing and Lending Regulations, 2018. Clause (b) does **not** apply where both surety and principal debtor are resident (e.g. an Indian bank's SBLC for an Indian importer in favour of a foreign supplier), or to an AD guarantee backed by a non-resident counter-guarantee or 100% deposit collateral. Reported quarterly (Form GRN) to an AD within **15 days** of quarter-end; AD to RBI within **30 days**; late submission fee ₹7,500 + 0.025% × A × n. The old import-SBLC condition lists (Master Direction) are not the current law: do not teach them as current. | GUAR regs 3, 5, 7, 8 |
| 22 | **Fraud exception** (not a UCP article): courts enforce a bank's LC or guarantee undertaking as independent of the underlying contract, and restrain payment only for established **fraud** of which the bank has notice, or irretrievable injustice. Indian lead case on bank guarantees: U.P. Co-operative Federation v. Singh Consultants and Engineers (SC, 1988). | Case law (**verify** the citation before the script names it; the script states the principle only) |

## Traps
- "The bank checks the goods" (no: documents, Art. 5).
- "A credit is revocable unless stated" (no: irrevocable even if silent, Art. 3).
- Advising vs confirming bank (advising: no undertaking; confirming: definite undertaking, without recourse).
- Transferable vs back-to-back (one credit transferred once vs two separate credits).
- Red vs green clause (advance vs advance against warehoused goods).
- Acceptance vs deferred payment (deferred payment: no draft).
- A credit available by a draft drawn on the applicant (not allowed, Art. 6(c)).
