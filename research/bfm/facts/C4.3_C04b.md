# Fact sheet · C4.3 Payment and settlement systems (video C04b)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F3]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text opened on 2026-11-24 with full https:// URL. Sister sheet: C04 (CRR, SLR, NDTL and monetary policy, C4.1) and A02 (SWIFT, CHIPS, CHAPS, Nostro/Vostro, A3.3).


What this lesson must carry. Indian banks move money and securities across four linked layers. First, the Centralised Payment Systems (CPS) owned and operated by RBI: RTGS (launched 26 March 2004, 24x7x365 from 14 December 2020) settles large-value payments (minimum ₹2,00,000, no maximum) continuously, one by one without netting, final and irrevocable in RBI's books; NEFT (launched November 2005, 24x7x365 from 16 December 2019) settles in 48 half-hourly batches on a Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) basis with no RBI minimum or maximum (cash walk-in capped at ₹50,000). Both are credit-push, carry no RBI processing fee (since 1 July 2019), require 20-digit LEI for non-individual transfers of ₹50 crore and above (since 1 April 2021), and offer free beneficiary bank account name look-up. Second, the communication backbone: INFINET (a Closed User Group MPLS network built by IDRBT in 1999) and SFMS (domestic messaging standard for RTGS/NEFT/Government payments), both now operated 24x7 by IFTAS, a wholly owned RBI subsidiary — contrasted with SWIFT for cross-border messages. Third, the market plumbing: NDS (launched 15 February 2002) and NDS-OM (launched 1 August 2005, owned by RBI and managed by Clearcorp), an anonymous, screen-based, order-driven G-sec trading platform matching on price-time priority with Straight Through Processing (STP) straight to CCIL; access is governed by the Access Criteria for NDS-OM Directions, 2025 (updated 27 April 2026) across Direct Access, Indirect Access, and Stock Broker Connect. CCIL (commenced 15 February 2002) is an authorised Central Counterparty (CCP) — never a regulator — guaranteeing settlement via novation and a Settlement Guarantee Fund (SGF) across G-secs, TREPS and CROMS (money market), FX-CLEAR (USD-INR spot and swaps), and ASTROID (MIBOR OIS). G-secs settle on Delivery versus Payment (DvP) — specifically DvP III (net securities, net funds) since 2 April 2004 — eliminating principal risk, whereas cross-border forex trades face time-zone principal risk when settlement legs do not overlap. Corporate securities, equities, CPs and CDs are held in demat form with SEBI-regulated depositories NSDL (1996) and CDSL (1999) under the Depositories Act, 1996, via Depository Participants (DPs), while banks hold G-secs in RBI's SGL/CSGL accounts. Fourth, the law and overseer: the Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007 (assented 20 December 2007, effective 12 August 2008) makes RBI the regulator (s. 3), mandates RBI authorisation for payment systems (s. 4; excluding stock exchanges under s. 34), legally protects netting and settlement finality even on insolvency (s. 23), and criminalises electronic transfer dishonour for insufficient funds (s. 25). Critically, from 9 May 2025, the erstwhile BPSS was replaced by the 6-member Payments Regulatory Board (PRB) (three RBI members led by the Governor as Chairperson, three Central Government nominees, plus RBI's Principal Legal Adviser as permanent invitee) under the Payments Regulatory Board Regulations, 2025 (in force 20 May 2025).
