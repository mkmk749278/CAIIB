# Fact sheet · D3.1 Asset classification (case form) · D3.2 Provisioning norms (case form) and post-NPA resolution (video D04)

Our own words; every row cites its primary text with paragraph/section number and full URL. Sister lessons: D02 (asset classification rules: 90-day test, SMA, working capital, security erosion) and D03 (provisioning tables: standard by sector, sub-standard, doubtful, loss, PCR, UFCE, fraud, wilful defaulters).


What this lesson must carry. On 31 December 2026, commercial banks apply the Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025 (updated 1 October 2026) alongside the Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions, 2025 (updated 1 October 2026). The Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Directions, 2026 were issued on 27 April 2026 and take effect on 1 April 2027 ("issued, coming"): Loss Given Default (LGD) belongs to ECL, never to an IRACP provisioning sum. Under IRACP 2025, an NPA is sub-standard for up to 12 months (15% general provision without allowance for ECGC or security; 25% if unsecured ab-initio where realisable security is ≤ 10%; 20% for unsecured infrastructure loans with escrow safeguards), doubtful after 12 months in sub-standard (100% on the unsecured shortfall plus 25% for D1 up to 1 year, 40% for D2 1–3 years, and 100% for D3 beyond 3 years on the secured portion), and loss (write off, or 100% if kept on books). Realisable security below 50% of last assessed value moves an NPA straight to doubtful; realisable security below 10% of outstanding ignores security and moves it straight to loss. Interest Suspense is never a provision: deduct it from the advance balance first before computing provisions. For ECGC (doubtful) and CGTMSE / CRGFTLIH / NCGTC (NPA) covered accounts, deduct realisable tangible security (net of haircut and realisation costs, never book value) first, then deduct the eligible guarantee cover from the unsecured shortfall (no provision on the guaranteed portion), and provide 100% on the net uncovered unsecured shortfall plus the class rate on the secured portion. PCR is provisions divided by Gross NPA, with no RBI regulatory minimum. After NPA, restructuring downgrades a standard account to sub-standard immediately; upgrade requires satisfactory performance during the monitoring period (10% of principal plus capitalised interest repaid, with a 1-year floor from first payment on the longest-moratorium facility), plus investment-grade bank-loan rating (BBB- or better) from 1 CRA (exposure ≥ ₹100 crore) or 2 CRAs (≥ ₹500 crore). MSME accounts below ₹25 crore follow a 1-year specified period (for others, specified period = 20% repaid; default after upgrade before specified period ends triggers a fresh resolution plan and 15% additional provision). Under IBC 2016, CIRP before NCLT is initiated by a financial creditor (s. 7), operational creditor (s. 9), or corporate debtor (s. 10) (minimum default ₹1 crore); NCLT declares a moratorium (s. 14), management shifts to an IRP/RP and the Committee of Creditors (CoC), and CIRP faces a 180-day timeline and mandatory 330-day upper cap (s. 12(3)). NARCL (51% PSB-owned, paired with IDRCL where PSBs/Public FIs hold max 49%) acquires large stressed assets (>₹500 crore, target ~₹2 lakh crore) for 15% cash and 85% Security Receipts backed by a ₹30,600 crore Government of India guarantee valid for 5 years, whereas regular ARCs (RBI ARC Directions, 2025: min NOF ₹300 crore, min SR investment 15% of transferor SRs or 2.5% of total SRs, 5–8 year realisation) have no sovereign guarantee.
