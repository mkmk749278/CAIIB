# Fact sheet · A5.1, A5.5 (with A5.2 write-off and set-off) · Export rules: IEC to realisation (video A11)

Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-22. Rules as at the **31 Dec 2026** cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited
as [F3]. Our own words; RBI and DGFT texts cited by regulation / paragraph.

**The big change this lesson must carry:** from **1 October 2026** exports are governed by the new FEM (Export and
Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 and RBI's short Directions circular of 16 Jan 2026. The old Master
Direction – Export of Goods and Services (updated to 17 July 2026), the Import MD and 96 + 71 old circulars **stand
superseded** from that date [F1]. Many fixed numbers that coaching books still teach (21 days to submit documents, the
5 % / 10 % write-off limits, the exempt list, EDF waivers, the RBI caution list) are **not in the 2026 rules**: the AD
bank now decides under its own Board-approved policy and SOP. At the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off the 2026 rules are current;
the old figures appear only as trap cards ("older books say…").

## Points
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Which rule book**: from 1 Oct 2026, the FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 replace the 2015 export regulations (one set for exports and imports); RBI's directions of 16 Jan 2026 (A.P. DIR 20) start the same day and supersede the Export MD, the Import MD and the listed circulars. ADs must follow FEMA, rules, regulations, directions **and the Foreign Trade Policy**; references to RBI go through the **PRAVAAH** portal; doubtful transactions are reported to the **Directorate of Enforcement**. Purpose stated: ease of doing business, especially small exporters and importers; empower ADs. | R26 reg 1; DIR paras 1–4 |
| 2 | **Two regulators**: DGFT (Ministry of Commerce) runs the Foreign Trade Policy under the FT (D&R) Act: what may be exported, IEC, documents. RBI runs the foreign-exchange side under FEMA: declaration, receipt, realisation, reporting. | FTP ch. 2; DIR para 2; R26 preamble (FEMA ss. 7, 8, 10(6), 47) |
| 3 | **IEC**: a **10-character** alphanumeric code; **same as the PAN** but separately issued by **DGFT** on an online application; no export or import of goods without an IEC unless specifically exempted (exempt categories and permanent IECs: HBP para 2.07); for services, IEC needed on the date of rendering services **to avail FTP benefits**; details to be **updated online every year in April–June** (confirmed even if no change); an IEC not updated, or with risks flagged and not addressed, is **de-activated**. | FTP 2.05(a)–(f) |
| 4 | **EDF for goods**: the exporter furnishes the EDF, with the **full export value**, to the **specified authority** at the time of export. Specified authority for goods: **Commissioner of Customs** (DTA), **Development Commissioner** (SEZ). At an **EDI port the shipping bill is the EDF** (deemed submitted). At a non-EDI port, Customs forwards the authenticated EDF to the AD. | R26 reg 2(1)(c), (f)(i); 3(1), 3(3) |
| 5 | **EDF for services (incl. software)**: within **30 days from the end of the month** in which the invoice was raised, to the specified authority: services other than software → **AD** (DTA) / DC (SEZ); software → **AD or STPI** (DTA) / DC (SEZ). One EDF may cover all services exported in a month; non-software services may file on or before the date of receipt of payment; AD may extend the period on a reasoned request. "Services" includes software (the old SOFTEX form for software is not named in the 2026 rules: the declaration is the EDF). | R26 reg 2(1)(e), (f)(ii)–(iii), Explanation; 3(2)(c)–(e) |
| 6 | **Deemed EDF for services** (2nd Amendment, effective 1 Oct 2026): a services exporter is **deemed to have filed the EDF** when it gives the AD the payment information for crediting its account: a **resident individual** → any amount; **anyone else** → if the inward remittance is **not more than ₹10 lakh** (or equivalent). | R26 reg 3(2)(a), (b); AM2 |
| 7 | **Who is not an exporter**: a **traveller** taking personal effects (accompanied or unaccompanied) is not treated as an exporter. This is the **only** carve-out in the 2026 regulations; the old MD's exempt list (trade samples, gifts up to a value, ship stores, goods for repair, etc.) is superseded with the MD. | R26 reg 3(1) second proviso; DIR para 4 |
| 8 | **Manner of receipt**: as the FEM (Manner of Receipt and Payment) Regulations 2023 say: through an authorised bank / authorised person; **Nepal and Bhutan → Indian rupees** (FCY allowed if Nepal Rastra Bank permits the Nepali importer); **ACU members** (other than Nepal, Bhutan) → through the **ACU mechanism** or as RBI directs, otherwise as for others; **all other countries → INR or any foreign currency**; also any manner in the Foreign Trade Policy. | R26 reg 4(1); MRP reg 3(2)(I) |
| 9 | **AD credits only after checking**: the AD credits the exporter for an export receipt only after satisfying itself the transaction is **genuine**, and **at the same time closes or updates the EDPMS entry**. | R26 reg 4(2) |
| 10 | **₹10 lakh simple closure**: where the shipping bill (goods) or invoice (services) is **up to ₹10 lakh**, the AD may close the EDPMS entry on the **exporter's declaration** that payment was realised in full or otherwise; or the exporter may give a **quarterly** declaration for **bulk closure**. Same for imports in IDPMS (bill of entry / invoice up to ₹10 lakh). | R26 reg 4(2) provisos |
| 11 | **Realisation period** (realise **and repatriate** the full value, set-off included): **goods: 9 months from the date of shipment**; **services: 9 months from the date of invoice**; **goods to a warehouse outside India: 9 months from the date of sale** from the warehouse; **project exports: as per the contract's payment terms**. **Invoiced and/or settled in INR: 12 months** (from shipment / invoice / sale from warehouse). No separate period for SEZ, EOU, STP or status-holder units in the 2026 rules. | R26 reg 5(1)(a)–(c) and first proviso |
| 12 | **Extension**: the **AD** may extend the realisation period on the exporter's request citing reasons, if satisfied (no RBI approval; no cap stated). The AD must have systems to **monitor and follow up** realisation. | R26 reg 5(1) second proviso, 5(2) |
| 13 | **The 9 → 15 → 9 history** (trap card): 2015 regs: 9 months · 13 Nov 2025 (FEMA 23(R)/(7)/2025-RB): **15 months**, advance-payment shipment 1 → 3 years · 5 June 2026 (23(R)/(8)/2026-RB): back to **9 months**, in force on publication · the 2026 regulations as notified on 13 Jan 2026 said **15 months / 18 months (INR)**; the 22 Sep 2026 amendment changed them to **9 / 12 months** **before** they came into force on 1 Oct 2026. So at the cut-off: **9 months, 12 for INR**. | R15 (Id 13467); R26 footnotes 8–10; `regulatory_updates.md` rows 29–31 |
| 14 | **Reduction in value** (replaces "write-off"): the AD may, on the exporter's reasoned request, allow **reduction** in the export value (under- or non-realisation), if satisfied; where the export value is **up to ₹10 lakh per shipping bill / invoice**, reduction (including non-realisation) may be allowed **on the exporter's declaration**. The old **self write-off** (5 % of the previous calendar year's realisation; **10 % for status holders**; AD 10 %) is gone with the MD. | R26 reg 6; OMD C.23 (superseded) |
| 15 | **Set-off**: the AD may allow export receivables to be set off against import payables **from / to the same overseas buyer or supplier, or with their overseas group or associate companies**, within the realisation period (or the extended period). (The old MD condition "same party, no group set-off" is outdated.) | R26 reg 7 |
| 16 | **Third-party receipts**: the AD may permit receipts and payments by a third party (not the exporter or importer) if satisfied with the bona fides. | R26 reg 8 |
| 17 | **Advance receipts for exports**: the exporter routes the **advance and any later proceeds through the same AD**; may switch AD after informing **both** ADs. Interest paid on an export advance must not exceed the **all-in-cost ceiling for trade credit** (FEM Borrowing and Lending Regs 2018). The 2026 regulations set **no fixed period** within which goods must be shipped against an advance (the 2015 rule was 1 year, 3 years from 13 Nov 2025); where no export is made and refund is not possible, the AD may close the advance entry in EDPMS on a reasoned request. | R26 reg 10(1), 10(4), 18(1)(i); R15 |
| 18 | **Unrealised exports, the self-acting caution**: if proceeds stay unrealised **beyond one year from the due date** (or extended date), the exporter may make further exports **only against full advance or an irrevocable LC**. Exporters already on RBI's **caution list on 30 Sep 2026** stay under that order until removed. (Old MD C.28: RBI caution-listed on the AD's recommendation; superseded for new cases.) | R26 reg 13 and proviso; OMD C.28 |
| 19 | **EDPMS** (Export Data Processing and Monitoring System; EDPMS / IDPMS include any other system RBI specifies). RBI's platform where ADs record and track every export till it is realised: **EDI shipping-bill data reaches it from Customs**; the AD enters a **non-EDI EDF within 5 working days** of receipt and a **services EDF within 5 working days** of receipt from the exporter; the AD enters **inward remittances** for all exports (and merchanting); it **monitors** outstanding entries and follows up; it **marks off** an export only after the value is realised. It began in **2014** (A.P. DIR 101 of 4 Feb 2014 and 109 of 28 Feb 2014, now superseded) and replaced the **XOS** statement of outstanding bills (XOS circulars, also superseded). All forex transactions are also reported in **FETERS**. | R26 reg 18(1)(a), (b), (e)–(g), (i), 18(2), footnote 7; DIR Annex A items 68, 69, 72, 73 |
| 20 | **AD policy and SOP**: every AD must have a separate Board-level internal **policy and SOP** for export, import and merchanting: documents, **timelines** and charges for each process; extension; reduction (under-, over-, non-realisation); advances; delegation; factoring. It needs an escalation and appeal route; charges reasonable; **no charge or penalty for a customer's regulatory delay or violation**; the policy and SOP's main features **on the AD's website**. (So the old fixed "21 days" to lodge documents is now the AD's own timeline.) | R26 reg 19; OMD C.5(iv), C.6(ii) (21 days, superseded) |
| 21 | **Powers to ADs**: cases before 1 Oct 2026 that needed RBI approval under the 2015 regs and the old MDs are now handled by ADs. | R26 reg 20 |
| 22 | **Merchanting (MTT)**: goods bought abroad and sold abroad, never entering India; the gap between the outward and inward legs **≤ 6 months** (AD may extend); outward only to the overseas seller, inward only from the overseas buyer (AD may allow a third party on reasons). Taught in A13; named only. | R26 reg 16 |
