# Fact sheet · C5.3 Risk measures: duration, modified duration and BPV (video C06)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1]–[F26]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text with paragraph number and full URL. Sister sheets: C05 (bond varieties, pricing, current yield, YTM, accrued interest) and C07 (treasury limits, market-risk capital, investment classification).


What this lesson must carry. A bond's interest-rate risk is measured first by Macaulay Duration, the present-value-weighted average time (in years) taken to recover a bond's purchase price [F1, F2]. Using the cash-flow table method, each future cash flow is discounted at the bond's yield to maturity (YTM), multiplied by its time period, summed, and divided by the bond's market price; for semi-annual bonds (such as Indian dated Government Securities), dividing the period count by 2 gives Macaulay Duration in years [F3]. Duration is always less than or equal to remaining maturity; only a zero-coupon bond (including G-sec STRIPS) has duration equal to its maturity [F4, F25]. Holding maturity constant, a higher coupon rate lowers duration because cash arrives sooner [F5]. To measure price sensitivity, Macaulay Duration is converted into Modified Duration (MD) = D ÷ (1 + y/n), where n is coupon frequency per year (n = 2 for semi-annual G-secs, n = 1 for annual bonds) [F6, F7]. Modified Duration approximates the percentage change in bond value for a 100-basis-point (1%) parallel shift in yield: %ΔP ≈ −MD × Δy, with the minus sign reflecting the inverse price-yield relationship [F6, F8, F9]. Because bond prices curve with yield (convexity), duration works cleanly only for small, parallel yield shifts; for large shifts on option-free bonds, actual price falls are smaller than duration predicts and price gains are larger [F10, F11]. Basis Point Value (BPV) or PV01 translates sensitivity into rupees: the change in bond or portfolio value for a 1 basis point (0.01%) yield change, computed as Market Value × MD × 0.0001 [F12, F13, F14, F15]. For a portfolio, Portfolio Modified Duration is the market-value-weighted average of individual bond MDs, and Portfolio BPV is the sum of individual BPVs [F19]. Treasuries set Board-approved internal BPV/PV01 and duration-wise limits [F16, F20], while RBI uses the standardised duration method for general market risk capital (15 time bands, 0.60%–1.00% assumed yield shifts, 5% vertical disallowance — retained in the Market Risk Directions 2026, issued 21 Sep 2026, effective 1 Apr 2027, "issued, coming") [F21]. Crucially, RBI's old regulatory ceiling capping gross PV01 of non-option rupee derivatives at 0.25% of net worth was deleted effective 19 October 2026 (RBI/2026-27/294) [F17, F18].
