# Fact sheet · A5.6 Factoring and forfaiting · A5.4 Gold Card · A5.2 Facilities for exporters (video A14)

Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-22. Rules as at the **31 Dec 2026** cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited
as [F3]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text by paragraph. A11's sheet is [A11 F14]; A11b's [A11b F22].

**What changed and what this lesson must carry.** The old Master Direction – Export of Goods and Services (OMD) had
fixed RBI paragraphs on forfaiting (A.12), export factoring on non-recourse basis (A.13), EEFC (A.6), DDA (A.5), cash
discount and reduction in invoice value (C.16, C.17: 25 % cap), agency commission (D.1), refund of export proceeds
(D.2) and write-off (C.23: 5 %, status holders 10 %). **All of it was superseded on 1 Oct 2026** by the FEM (Export
and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 and A.P. (DIR) Circular 20 [R26, DIR]. Today: factoring sits in
each AD's **internal policy and SOP** (reg 19(1)(f)); reduction in value is reg 6; EEFC and DDA live on in the FEM
(Foreign Currency Accounts) Regulations 2015 and the Master Direction – Deposits and Accounts; the Gold Card lives in
the Credit Facilities Directions 2025, ch. XV; factoring itself is governed by the Factoring Regulation Act 2011 (as
amended 2021) and RBI's 2025 directions; TReDS by RBI's TReDS Directions 2026. Old figures appear only as trap cards
(exam rule for old figures: PLAYBOOK §4a, A13 N018a).

## Points: factoring (A5.6)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Factoring business (legal definition, as amended 2021)**: acquisition **by way of assignment** of an assignor's **receivables**, **for a consideration**, for **collection** or for **financing** against the assignment (by loans, advances or otherwise). Excludes ordinary credit facilities by a **bank or NBFC** against the security of receivables. | FRA s.2(j) as amended |
| 2 | **Parties**: **assignor** = the seller who owns the receivable; **debtor** = the buyer who owes it; **factor** = the one who takes the assignment. **Receivables** = money owed by a debtor and not yet paid to the assignor **for goods or services** (incl. tolls / use of infrastructure). **Assignment** = transfer by agreement to a factor of an undivided interest, **in whole or in part**, incl. where assignor or debtor is **outside India**. | FRA s.2(a), 2(p) as amended |
| 3 | **Who may factor**: a **bank** (departmentally, or through a subsidiary) and the other bodies in FRA s.5 (statutory corporations, Government companies) **without** an FRA registration; an **NBFC-Factor** (registered under FRA s.3); an **NBFC-ICC** with total assets **₹1,000 crore+**, no public deposits, meeting NOF (granted CoR under the FRA). A CoR holder must start factoring within **6 months**. | NBM 36–41; UFS 43–44 |
| 4 | **NBFC-Factor principal business**: financial assets in factoring **≥ 50 %** of total assets **and** factoring income **≥ 50 %** of gross income. **NOF ₹10 crore** (glide path ₹5 cr → ₹7 cr by 31 Mar 2025 → **₹10 cr by 31 Mar 2027**). (The 2021 Act removed the 50 % threshold from the Act itself; RBI keeps it for NBFC-Factors by direction.) | NBM 42; NBR 39, 42; FRA 2021 s.3 |
| 5 | **Recourse**: banks may factor **with recourse, without recourse or with limited recourse**. Exposure booked: **with recourse → on the assignor** (seller); **without recourse → on the debtor** (buyer); **international factoring → on the import factor**; limited recourse → per the agreement. Without-recourse underwriting of debtor risk needs **Board-approved limits**. | UFS 10, 44(1), 44(2), 44(8); NBM 47, 51 |
| 6 | **Bank conditions**: thorough **credit appraisal of debtors**; only **genuine trade invoices**; factoring treated **on par with loans and advances** (prudential norms); bank and factor share information on **common borrowers** to avoid double financing (borrower's certificate of factored receivables; CERSAI checked); defaults reported to credit information companies. | UFS 44(3)–(7) |
| 7 | **NPA for NBFC-Factors**: a factored receivable overdue **> 90 days** (glide path from 180 days for those under ₹500 crore) is NPA, **whether with or without recourse**. | NBM 44–46 |
| 8 | **Export / import factoring (FEMA)**: in the 2026 regime each AD's internal policy and SOP must cover **export factoring and import factoring** (reg 19(1)(f)); an NBFC-Factor dealing in forex needs **FED authorisation** under FEMA. The old OMD A.13 conditions (non-recourse; **two-factor**: export factor in India + **import factor** abroad doing credit evaluation and collection; invoice notation to pay the import factor; export factor closes the bill in EDPMS; single-factor model using the correspondent bank's credit report; working capital assessed net of factored invoices) were superseded with the OMD; they describe how export factoring works and may still appear in questions. | R26 19(1)(f); NBM 53–54; OMD A.13 (superseded) |
| 9 | **Services a factor gives** (the classic bundle, described in the 1988 study group and textbooks; not an RBI list): **finance** (prepayment of part of the invoice), **sales-ledger management**, **collection**, **credit protection** (when without recourse), credit information on buyers. The **advance share** (often quoted as about 80 %) is a commercial term, **not** an RBI number: say "a factor typically advances…", never "RBI says". | Leads (practice); KAL |
| 10 | **TReDS** = a technology platform for **factoring of trade receivables through multiple financiers**; seller must be an **MSME** (MSMED Act); participants: sellers, buyers, financiers (anyone allowed to factor under the FRA), insurance companies, the Government's credit guarantee fund trust. Financiers bid; the **seller accepts the best bid**. Factoring units discounted on TReDS are **without recourse to the seller**; the buyer's obligation to pay on the due date is **unconditional** once accepted (no set-off for quality); the platform files the assignment with **CERSAI**; **reverse factoring** = the buyer uploads; **re-discounting** allowed; credit insurance (premium **not** on the seller; not a CRM) and CGTMSE-type guarantee allowed. Operator: Indian company, PSS Act authorisation, **net worth ₹25 crore** (existing platforms by 31 Mar 2028). | TRD 3, 9–10, 12–22 |
| 11 | **History**: RBI's **Study Group for Examining Introduction of Factoring Services in India, 1988**, chaired by **C. S. Kalyanasundaram** (the "factoring committee"). The Factoring Regulation Act came in **2011** (Act 12 of 2012); amended **2021** (wider NBFC entry; TReDS files with CERSAI for the factor; RBI regulation-making power, s.31A). TReDS guidelines **2014**; Directions **2026**. | KAL; FRA; TRD 28 |

## Points: forfaiting (A5.6)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | **What it is**: the forfaiter buys an exporter's **medium-term deferred receivable** (usually **bills of exchange or promissory notes**, or deferred payments under an **LC**) **without recourse** to the exporter: on payment the exporter is out of the risk. In India **EXIM Bank and AD Category-I banks** were permitted to forfait; commitment fee and other charges could be **remitted through an AD**, in advance or monthly. That OMD para was superseded on 1 Oct 2026 with nothing replacing it; forfaiting continues as an AD's ordinary trade-finance business. | OMD A.12 (superseded); DIR 4 |
| 13 | **Avalisation**: the importer's bank adds its **aval** (an unconditional guarantee written on the bill or note itself); or the receivable is under an LC / bank guarantee. The forfaiter looks to that bank, not to the exporter. | URF (frame); Leads (practice) |
| 14 | **Typical features** (market practice, not RBI figures): one **large transaction** (capital goods, projects); credit periods of **about 6 months to 5 years or more**; finance **up to 100 %** of the receivable; **fixed rate** discount; **does not use the exporter's bank limits**; turns a **credit sale into a cash sale**; removes the exporter's **credit, country (political / transfer), interest-rate and exchange** risk (the exporter gets the money now). | Leads (practice; EXIM Bank's own description: "non-recourse discounting of export receivables… does not affect the exporter's borrowing limits") |
| 15 | **Charges**: **commitment fee** (from the forfaiter's commitment till the paper is discounted; **payable even if the deal does not go through**, so not refunded), **discount** (interest for the period, deducted upfront), **documentation / other fees**. | Leads (practice; EXIM Bank) |
| 16 | **Rules**: ICC **URF 800** (in effect 1 Jan 2013), voluntary like UCP: they apply only when the agreement says so. The name: French *à forfait*, "outright, for a lump sum": the exporter sells outright, with no claim back. | URF |

## Points: Gold Card (A5.4)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 17 | **Eligibility**: all **creditworthy exporters with a good track record**, including **MSME**, per the bank's own criteria. **Not** for exporters on ECGC's **Specific Approval List (SAL)** or with **overdue bills over 10 % of the previous year's turnover**. | CF 365–367 |
| 18 | **Features**: better terms and rates than other exporters; simpler, faster processing; lower charges; **in-principle limits for 3 years** with **automatic renewal**; **stand-by limit of not less than 20 %** of the assessed limit for sudden orders (peak / off-peak levels for seasonal exporters); inventory norms relaxed for unanticipated orders; decisions in **25 days (fresh) / 15 days (renewal) / 7 days (ad hoc)**; **preference in PCFC**; may waive **collateral** and exemption from **ECGC's ECIB** (the bank's export-credit insurance) cover; extra services (ATM, internet banking, cards) at the bank's choice; rate **not more than** the bank's general export-credit rate; **foreign-currency credit cards** for urgent payments; priority over non-export borrowers for loans out of FCNR(B) funds; FC term loans from FCNR(B)/RFC funds in deserving cases. | CF 368–383 |
| 19 | **Line of credit** for established exporters: normally one year; for good track records **up to three years** with step-up / step-down. (Not part of the Gold Card text, nearby.) | CF 385–386 |
| 20 | **Origin**: the Exim Policy of **January 2004** asked RBI to frame a Gold Card scheme; RBI announced it in **2004** (May); the scheme now sits in CF ch. XV-H. | CF ch. XV-H; Leads (2004 year from the depth checklist and the old Master Circular; not re-read) |

## Points: status holders (A5.2)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 21 | **Who**: any exporter of goods, services or technology **with an IEC**, recognised by **DGFT** on export performance (FOB, in freely convertible currency or INR) in the **current and preceding three financial years** (**two** for **gems & jewellery**); performance needed in **each** of those preceding years. | FTP 1.25(b)–(d) |
| 22 | **Categories (USD million)**: **One Star 3 · Two Star 15 · Three Star 50 · Four Star 200 · Five Star 800**. **Double weightage** only for **One Star** (micro and small enterprises; ISO/BIS units; North-East, J&K, Ladakh units; fruits and vegetables etc.); no transfer of performance between IECs; re-exports not counted. | FTP 1.26–1.28 |
| 23 | **Privileges**: authorisations and customs clearance on **self-declaration**; input-output norms fixed on priority within **60 days**; **exemption from bank guarantees** under FTP schemes; **exemption from compulsory negotiation of documents through banks** (but money must still come through banking channels); **Two Star and above** may set up **export warehouses**; priority handling of consignments; Three Star+ manufacturers **self-certify origin**; **free-of-cost exports** for promotion up to **₹1 crore or 2 % of average annual realisation (3 years), whichever is lower** (pharma 2 %; UN/WHO health programmes 8 %); **mentoring duty**: trainees a year 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 (Two to Five Star). | FTP 1.29–1.30 |
| 24 | **Trap (old rule, asked Dec 2024)**: "a **status holder** may **self write-off** up to **10 %** of the previous year's realisation (others 5 %)": OMD C.23, **superseded**. Today: **reduction in realisation** by the AD on reasons; up to **₹10 lakh** per shipping bill / invoice on the **exporter's declaration** (reg 6), for every exporter. | OMD C.23; R26 reg 6; A11 F14 |

## Points: other facilities for exporters (A5.2)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | **Today's frame**: the AD's **policy and SOP** must cover documents, timelines and charges; **extension**; **adjustment (under-, over-, non-realisation)**; **advances**; delegation; **export and import factoring**; with escalation and appeal; no penalty for a customer's regulatory delay. | R26 reg 19 |
| 26 | **Agency commission** (old OMD D.1): AD could allow commission **by remittance or by deduction from the invoice value**, if **declared on the EDF/SOFTEX** (or reasons accepted with a valid agreement) and **after shipment**; not on exports for equity in an overseas JV/WOS or under the Rupee Credit Route (tea and tobacco up to 10 %). **Superseded**; today handled under the AD's policy (as an adjustment of value). | OMD D.1 (superseded); R26 reg 19(1)(c) |
| 27 | **Cash discount / reduction in invoice value** (old OMD C.16, C.17): cash discount for **prepayment of a usance bill** up to proportionate interest on the unexpired usance; other reductions up to **25 %** of invoice value (not floor-price goods, not caution-listed exporters, incentives surrendered); **no percentage cap** for exporters over 3 years in exports whose outstandings are ≤ 5 % of average annual realisation (C.17(ii)). **Superseded** by reg 6: AD may allow **reduction** on reasons; **≤ ₹10 lakh** per shipping bill / invoice on the exporter's declaration. | OMD C.16–C.17; R26 reg 6 |
| 28 | **Refund of export proceeds** (old OMD D.2): AD that realised the proceeds could refund for goods **re-imported for poor quality**, with incentives surrendered and an undertaking to **re-import within 3 months** (not needed where the goods were destroyed or auctioned abroad). **Superseded**; no fixed conditions in the 2026 rules (AD's policy). | OMD D.2 (superseded); DIR Annex A 40 |
| 29 | **EDF waiver limits by exporter type**: RBI's own paragraph (old OMD C.1, as substituted Nov 2025: free-of-cost exports exempt from EDF up to **2 %** of average annual realisation for all exporters; **₹1 crore or 2 %**, lower, for gems, jewellery and gold articles; **8 %** for health-programme supplies) went with the OMD on 1 Oct 2026. The **FTP 2023 para 1.29(h)** limit for **status holders' free-of-cost exports** is **still in force**: ₹1 crore or 2 % of average annual realisation (3 licensing years), whichever is lower, **excluding gems, jewellery, gold and precious-metal articles**; **pharma 2 %**; UN / WHO-PAHO / Government health programmes **up to 8 %**; no drawback or incentive on such supplies. The classes' gems-and-jewellery conflict is a real difference between these two texts (c1 expert M1, examiner M3). | OMD C.1 (superseded); FTP 1.29(h) |
| 30 | **EEFC account**: a resident may open with an AD in India; credit **100 %** of foreign-exchange earnings (export proceeds, **advance remittances** for exports, professional earnings; **not** loans or investments from abroad); **non-interest-bearing account** (RBI's MDA wording; the old OMD said "current account"); **no fund- or non-fund-based credit facilities against the balances**; rupee withdrawal allowed but **cannot be re-credited**; monthly accruals converted to rupees **by the last day of the next month**, after adjusting for approved uses and forward commitments; may repay **packing credit** to the extent exports have happened; may lend trade-related advances to overseas importers; ECGC claims paid in rupees are **not** an eligible credit. | FCA reg 4(A), Sch. I; MDA 3.1 |
| 31 | **Exporter's foreign currency account abroad** (FCA reg 5(CA)): to receive full export value and advances; funds used for **imports** or **repatriated** by the end of the **next month** after receipt (**three months** if the account is with a bank in an **IFSC**), after forward commitments. | FCA 5(CA) |
| 32 | **Diamond Dollar Account (DDA)**: firms dealing in rough / cut and polished diamonds and diamond or gold jewellery; **track record 3 years**, **average annual turnover ₹3 crore** in the preceding three licensing years; **up to 5 DDAs**, **US dollars only**, **current account, no interest**, subject to **CRR and SLR**; not for firms holding other foreign currency accounts (EEFC allowed). **Older books: 2 years**: the FEMA text says 3 years since the amendment of **29 Apr 2025** (OMD A.5 footnote 23); CF para 338 still prints 2 years: re-check at the freeze. | FCA Sch. II; application format (3 years after the 2025 amendment); CF 338–339 |

## Leads and exam evidence
- Asked: "Factoring Committee" (Dec 2023, A-EXIMP); "Status holder exporter can self write-off export bill" (Dec 2024);
  escrow (Jun 2025, A-EXIMP, Tier B, not in this lesson). Depth checklist A5.4, A5.6, A5.2 items 1–3, 5, 7.
- The depth checklist's "standby %" conflict is settled at **not less than 20 %** [F18]; the gems-and-jewellery EDF
  waiver conflict is a real difference between the old RBI paragraph and FTP 1.29(h) [F29].
- "Commitment fee is not refunded" and "factor advances about 80 %": practice, taught as "usually", never as RBI rules.
- Factoring "short term" (credit periods up to about 90–180 days): practice; RBI sets no tenor.
- Not in this lesson: ECGC (A15); EXIM Bank's own products and buyer's credit (A15b); BAF / EBR (A11b F23).
