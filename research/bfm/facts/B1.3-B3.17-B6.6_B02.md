## B1.3 The process and the framework
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The process: **identify, measure, monitor and control** the overall level of risks (RBI 1999). RBI's 2024 op-risk note spells the full gamut: identification, measurement and assessment, monitoring and control, mitigation, reporting to senior management and the Board. | RMS99 Introduction; ORGN def. (9) |
| 2 | RBI's broad parameters of a risk management function: organisational structure; a comprehensive risk measurement approach; Board-approved risk policies consistent with business strategy, capital strength, expertise and "overall willingness to assume risk"; prudential limits; strong MIS; effective controls and reporting; a risk management framework **independent of operational departments**; periodical review. | RMS99 Introduction |
| 3 | Building blocks of credit risk management: (a) policy and strategy, (b) organisational structure, (c) operations / systems. The Board approves and periodically reviews the credit risk strategy and policies; senior management implements them. | CRGN02 1.3, 1.4 |
| 4 | The four **risk responses**: **avoid, reduce, share (transfer), accept**. COSO 2004: "Management selects risk responses – avoiding, accepting, reducing, or sharing risk". | COSO04 p. 4 (Risk Response) |
| 5 | **Risk appetite**: "the aggregate level and types of risk an RE is willing to assume, decided in advance and within its risk capacity, to achieve its strategic objectives and business plan". **Risk tolerance**: "the variation around the prescribed risk appetite that the RE is willing to tolerate". | ORGN defs. (12), (13) |
| 6 | **Risk capacity** (FSB): "the maximum level of risk the financial institution can assume given its current level of resources before breaching constraints" set by regulatory capital and liquidity needs, the operating environment and obligations to depositors and others. Appetite sits within capacity. **Risk limits** allocate the appetite to business lines, risk types and concentrations. A **risk appetite statement** puts it in writing (qualitative and quantitative). | FSB13 s. II |
| 7 | The Board sets the risk appetite and approves the policies; the op-risk appetite and tolerance statement is reviewed by the Board or its committee, material changes approved by the Board. | ORGN para on the Board (risk appetite and tolerance statement); CRGN02 1.4; RMS99 2.1 |

## B1.3 Organisation: board, committees, CRO, three lines
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | The **Board** has the primary responsibility for understanding the risks and making sure they are managed; it sets risk limits by assessing risk and risk-bearing capacity. | RMS99 2.1; CRGN02 1.5 |
| 9 | **RMCB** (Risk Management Committee of the Board): the Board of a PSB shall constitute it with a **majority of non-executive directors**; the Board's chair may be a member only with risk-management expertise; it meets **at least once each quarter**, quorum three; at least half of those attending are independent directors, of whom at least one has risk-management expertise; it is **chaired by an independent director** who chairs neither the Board nor any other Board committee. | GOV25 para 21; para 58 applies it to private banks |
| 10 | The Risk Management Committee devises "the policy and strategy for integrated risk management" and coordinates the Credit Risk Management Committee (CRMC), the ALCO and other risk committees; its independence must be preserved. The RMC includes the CEO and the heads of the credit, market and operational risk management committees. | CRGN02 1.5; ALM25 para 23 |
| 11 | **CRMC**: headed by the Chairman / CEO / ED; heads of credit, treasury, the credit risk management department (CRMD) and the chief economist; implements the Board's credit risk policy, monitors credit risk bank-wide, decides delegation of credit powers, prudential limits, loan review mechanism etc. **CRMD**, independent of credit administration, measures, controls and manages credit risk bank-wide. | CRGN02 1.5, 1.5.2 |
| 12 | **ALCO**: headed by the CEO / CMD or an ED; members may include heads of investment, credit, treasury, international banking, risk management and economic research; it ensures adherence to the Board's risk tolerance and limits and implements the liquidity strategy, decides the maturity profile and funding mix. The **ALM support group** analyses, monitors and reports to the ALCO. Organisational set-up for liquidity risk: Board → RMC → ALCO → ALM support group. | ALM25 paras 21, 24–26, 28 |
| 13 | RBI 1999: the ALCO is "the top operational unit for managing the balance sheet"; an independent **middle office** tracks market risk on a real-time basis, separate from the treasury. | RMS99 8.1 |
| 14 | **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)**: a Board-approved policy defines the CRO's role; a senior official with risk qualifications; **direct reporting line to the MD&CEO / RMCB**; if reporting to the MD&CEO, the RMCB meets the CRO without the MD&CEO at least **quarterly**; **no reporting relationship with business verticals and no business targets**; **no dual hatting** (not CEO, COO, CFO, chief of internal audit or any other function). | GOV25 paras 29–31; para 59 applies them to private banks |
| 15 | CRO tenure: appointed for a **fixed tenure** with Board approval; transfer / removal before the tenure ends only with Board approval and reported to RBI's Department of Supervision (and to stock exchanges if listed). | GOV25 para 32 |
| 16 | **Credit risk management is separate from credit sanction.** If the CRO sits in a sanction committee as a decision maker, the CRO has a vote and all members are individually and severally liable; otherwise the CRO is an **adviser** and an invitee **without voting rights**; where there is no committee approach, the CRO can only be an adviser with no sanctioning power. | GOV25 para 33 |
| 17 | **Three lines of defence**: (1) **business unit management** identifies and manages the risks in its own products, processes and systems; (2) an **independent risk management function** (in op risk, the organisational operational risk function), which also typically includes the **compliance function**, gives an independent view and challenges the first line; (3) the **audit function** gives independent assurance to the Board, and its staff are not involved in running the risk processes. | ORGN para 12; def. (11) |
| 18 | So the risk is "owned" by the business line (first line); the risk department oversees and challenges; audit assures. | ORGN para 12 (first line "is responsible for identifying and managing the risks inherent in the products, services, activities, processes, and systems for which it is accountable") |

## B6.6 Integrated risk management (ERM)
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Why integrate: risks "are highly interdependent and events that affect one area of risk can have ramifications for a range of other risk categories", so top management should manage the overall level of risk. The global trend is to centralise risk management to see aggregate exposure, net exposures naturally and report easily. | RMS99 Introduction, 2.1 |
| 20 | Approach: the RMC devises the policy and strategy for integrated risk management across all risk exposures; a CRO independent of business; Board-set limits; MIS. Challenges (concept): data from every business line in one place, business pressure against limits, and a risk culture across the bank. | CRGN02 1.5; GOV25 paras 29–31; RMS99 2.2 (MIS "requires substantial upgradation"); concept for challenges |
| 21 | **COSO ERM (2004) definition**: "a process, effected by an entity's board of directors, management and other personnel, applied in strategy setting and across the enterprise, designed to identify potential events that may affect the entity, and manage risk to be within its risk appetite, to provide reasonable assurance regarding the achievement of entity objectives". | COSO04 p. 2 |
| 22 | COSO 2004: **four categories of objectives**: strategic, operations, reporting, compliance. | COSO04 p. 3 |
| 23 | COSO 2004: **eight components**: internal environment, objective setting, event identification, risk assessment, risk response, control activities, information and communication, monitoring. Risks are assessed for likelihood and impact, on an inherent and a residual basis. | COSO04 pp. 3–4 |
| 24 | COSO 2017 update (*Integrating with Strategy and Performance*): **five components**: governance and culture; strategy and objective-setting; performance; review and revision; information, communication and reporting; set out as **twenty principles**. The 2017 update does not replace COSO's separate Internal Control framework. | COSO17 pp. 2, 6 |
| 25 | COSO is a private-sector framework (Committee of Sponsoring Organizations); Basel is the bank supervisors' framework. Exam questions on "ERM components" refer to COSO. | COSO04 cover; concept |

## Measuring and testing
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 26 | Stress testing evaluates potential vulnerability to "unlikely but plausible events or movements in financial variables". Two broad categories: **sensitivity tests** = the impact of change in **one variable** (e.g. a large parallel shift in the yield curve, a big FX move, a large equity-index move); **scenario tests** = **simultaneous moves in a number of variables** based on a past event (**historical scenario**: natural disaster, market crash, loss of reserves) or a plausible event not yet seen (**hypothetical scenario**). They may be used separately or together. | ST07 Annex paras 1–2 |
| 27 | RBI revised the stress testing guidelines on 2 Dec 2013 to align with the Basel Committee's 2009 principles, to be adopted from 1 Apr 2014; banks must run at least the shocks RBI prescribes. | ST13 covering letter (search excerpt for the 2009 principles, 1 Apr 2014 and minimum shocks; annex not read) **(freeze: read annex)** |
| 28 | Stress-test vulnerabilities go to the Board, which ensures a plan of action; the Board / ALCO reviews limits after assessing interest-rate scenarios; RBI also requires sensitivity tests / scenario analysis in credit appraisal (infrastructure: delays, cost overruns) and stress testing of country risk. | ALM25 paras 14, 16; CRM25 paras 8, 53 |

| 28a | Three families of risk measures (concept, built in B03 and B09): **sensitivity** (how much value changes for a small move in one factor, e.g. per basis point), **volatility** (how much a value swings around its average: standard deviation), **downside potential** (the worst likely loss at a confidence level: VaR). RBI's market-risk guidance sets "Sensitivity and Value at Risk limits" measured daily for trading portfolios. | Concept; RBI *Guidance Note on Market Risk Management*, DOR.MRG.REC.300/14.10.001/2025-26, 28 Nov 2025, paras 5–6 (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/bs_viewcontent.aspx?Id=4788) |

## B3.17 Risk-based supervision
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 29 | RBS evaluates present **and future** risks, identifies incipient problems and allows prompt corrective action, against the older **compliance-based and transaction-testing approach (CAMELS)**, which is a point-in-time assessment. | HLSC12 Executive Summary |
| 30 | Compliance-based supervision looks at the bank's financial position at a point in time (**stock**); RBS is a dynamic process (**flow**), anticipating the risks the bank will face while executing its business plan. | HLSC12 para 2.6.21 |
| 31 | Under RBS, the **probability of failure and the likely impact of failure**, rather than the volume of business, decide the periodicity / intensity of on-site inspection; low risk / impact banks inspected about once in two to three years. | HLSC12 Executive Summary |
| 32 | The RBS rating reflects inherent risk and controls (not performance evaluation as under CAMELS), aiming at the overall probability of failure given risks, controls / governance and capital; the bank gets a risk mitigation plan (better controls, more capital, restructuring). Supervisor–bank communication is confidential. | HLSC12 Executive Summary |
| 33 | RBS depends on a robust off-site surveillance system and good data; it optimises supervisory resources. | HLSC12 Executive Summary |
| 34 | The 2012 HLSC recommended RBS for commercial banks; RBI then introduced it (SPARC, from 2013, per the IMF FSAP 2018, a lead). Teach "RBI moved to risk-based supervision after a 2012 committee"; no year on screen beyond 2012. **(freeze: an RBI text for the start year)** | HLSC12; IMF lead |

## Question blueprint
- **Define**: risk appetite, tolerance, capacity; ERM (COSO); RBS; sensitivity, scenario, stress test; three lines.
- **Purpose**: three lines; independent CRO; stress tests; RBS (target resources by risk).
- **Who / when / how much**: who sets appetite (Board); RMCB composition and quarterly meeting; ALCO head; CRO
  reporting line, fixed tenure, quarterly RMCB meeting without MD&CEO.
- **Compare**: sensitivity vs scenario; historical vs hypothetical; appetite vs tolerance vs capacity; COSO 2004 vs
  2017; RBS vs CAMELS (flow vs stock).
- **Exception**: CRO with no business targets, no dual hatting, adviser without vote in sanction; audit staff not in
  risk processes.
- **Calculate**: none (one sensitivity example in words).
- **Apply to a case**: which response, which committee, which line of defence, which test.
