# Fact sheet · C4.2 Liquidity adjustment facility (LAF) · C2.4 Domestic and global markets (video C02b)

What this lesson must carry. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) steers overnight money-market rates around the policy repo rate (5.50% from 7 October 2026; stance "calibrated tightening") inside a symmetric 50 basis-point LAF corridor, using the overnight Weighted Average Call Rate (WACR) as its single operating target. The floor of the corridor is the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) at 5.25% (repo minus 25 bps), which replaced the Fixed Rate Reverse Repo (FRRR) on 8 April 2022. SDF requires no collateral (enabled by the 2018 amendment to Section 17 of the RBI Act, 1934), has no upper ceiling on deposits (minimum ₹1 crore and multiples), and counts toward SLR (reported in Form VIII) but not CRR. The ceiling is the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) at 5.75% (repo plus 25 bps, equal to the Bank Rate), where eligible banks borrow overnight (minimum ₹1 crore) by dipping up to 2% of NDTL (outstanding at the end of the second preceding fortnight) into their stipulated SLR without seeking a default waiver. Both SDF and MSF run 7:00 PM to 11:59 PM daily (since 1 July 2025) with weekend/holiday reversals allowed (since 30 Dec 2023). Under RBI's Revised Liquidity Management Framework (30 September 2025), the old 14-day VRR/VRRR main operation was discontinued; transient liquidity is now managed primarily through 7-day VRR/VRRR and discretionary overnight-to-14-day auctions on e-Kuber, normally with at least one day's advance notice. VRR and VRRR are multiple-price auctions (minimum ₹1 crore): in a VRR (injection), bids at or below the repo rate are rejected and the highest bids win first; in a VRRR (absorption), bids at or above the repo rate are rejected and the lowest bids win first, with pro-rata allotment at the cut-off. Durable liquidity continues via OMOs, long-term VRR/VRRR (beyond a fortnight), and FX swap auctions. On 4 November 2026, RBI constituted the Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets (TCCFM), chaired by the Deputy Governor (FMRD), meeting at least quarterly. India's domestic markets connect to global markets through interest parity and FX swaps, FPI debt routes (General Route statutory caps of 6% Central G-Secs, 2% SGSs, 15% corporate debt; Fully Accessible Route (FAR) with zero ceiling on specified G-Secs; Voluntary Retention Route (VRR) whose standalone ₹2,50,000 crore cap was subsumed into General Route limits from 1 April 2026 while VRR remains an active 3-year retention channel; and RBI's 5 June 2026 withdrawal of General Route G-Sec short-term, security-wise, and concentration limits), AD Category-I banks' overseas borrowing ceiling (100% of unimpaired Tier I capital or USD 10 million, whichever is higher; borrowings above 50% require 12.0% CRAR and 3-year minimum maturity), and ADR/GDR conversion where the overseas holder bears exchange-rate risk. Bank treasuries classify domestic debt and equity under HTM, AFS, and FVTPL (incl. HFT), relying on recognised stock exchanges for active-market price discovery, liquidity, and 15-minute OTC corporate bond reporting. Globally, LIBOR and India's MIFOR ceased permanently (panel USD LIBOR and MIFOR after 30 June 2023; synthetic USD LIBOR on 30 September 2024), replaced by Alternative Reference Rates (ARRs) — SOFR (USD, secured, NY Fed), SONIA (GBP, unsecured, BoE), €STR (EUR, unsecured, ECB), TONA (JPY, unsecured, BoJ), and SARON (CHF, secured, SIX) — and FBIL Modified MIFOR (based on Adjusted SOFR + FBIL Forward Premia). Domestically, FBIL (regulated under RBI's FBA Directions, 2023, minimum net worth ₹5 crore for significant benchmarks) administers all 6 Significant Benchmarks (Overnight MIBOR, USD/INR Reference Rate, T-Bill Rates, G-Sec Valuation, SDL Valuation, Modified MIFOR), launched SORR (Secured Overnight Rupee Rate) on 7 July 2025, and publishes Overnight MIBOR at 12:45 PM (from 4 Aug 2025); MIBOR-OIS is actively used by non-banks as well as banks.
