What this lesson must carry. An option is a derivative contract giving the buyer (holder) the right, without any obligation, to buy (call option) or sell (put option) an underlying asset at a pre-agreed strike (exercise) price on or before a specified expiry date, in exchange for an upfront fee called the premium paid to the seller (writer), who accepts the binding obligation if exercised. Unlike forwards and futures where obligations are symmetrical, options are asymmetrical: the buyer's maximum loss is strictly capped at the premium paid, while the seller's maximum gain is capped at the premium received and the seller's loss risk is open-ended (unlimited on a naked short call; large, up to strike minus premium, on a short put). By exercise style, a European option can be exercised only on the expiration date, an American option on any day during the exercise period up to expiry, and a Bermudan option on specified dates; in India, all exchange-traded currency options (USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR, JPY-INR, and cross-currency EUR-USD, GBP-USD, USD-JPY), exchange-traded interest rate options, exchange-traded equity stock/index options (CE/PE), and RBI-permitted retail OTC FX and rupee interest rate options are European style. Moneyness compares spot price (S) against strike price (K): a call is In-the-Money (ITM) when S > K, At-the-Money (ATM) when S = K, and Out-of-the-Money (OTM) when S < K; a put is the mirror image — ITM when S < K, ATM when S = K, and OTM when S > K. An option's premium equals Intrinsic Value plus Time Value: intrinsic value is never negative (max(S − K, 0) for a call; max(K − S, 0) for a put), so ATM and OTM options carry zero intrinsic value and 100% time value, which decays to zero at expiry (making options eroding/depreciating assets). Under the Black-Scholes pricing framework used by Indian exchanges and RBI guidelines, the five core drivers of option premium are spot price (S), strike price (K), time to expiry (T), volatility of the underlying (σ), and interest rate (r) (for FX options, domestic and foreign interest rates); forward premium is not an independent driver. At expiry, payoff is max(S_T − K, 0) for a call and max(K − S_T, 0) for a put, whereas net profit accounts for premium (Buyer Profit = Payoff − Premium; Seller Profit = Premium − Payoff). In risk management, options hedge adverse moves while keeping favourable upside: a protective put (long underlying + long put) caps downside while leaving upside open, whereas a covered call (long underlying + short call) earns upfront premium income at the cost of capping upside above the strike. Under RBI's Market-makers in OTC Derivatives Directions, 2021, plain-vanilla European call/put options (and caps/floors) are "generic derivatives", whereas any other OTC derivative (such as barriers, digitals, or multi-component packages) is a "structured derivative" requiring enhanced due diligence and restricted to non-retail users (with leveraged derivatives prohibited). Finally, callable and putable bonds carry embedded options inside debt contracts, tested as a whole under SPPI in RBI's Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025.
