## B1.1 What risk is; risk, capital and return
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Risk is the uncertainty of an outcome: the chance that what actually happens differs from what was expected, above all the chance of loss. Banks earn by taking risk; they cannot remove it, only manage it. | Concept |
| 2 | RBI's grouping: banks face "various kinds of financial and non-financial risks viz., credit, interest rate, foreign exchange rate, liquidity, equity price, commodity price, legal, regulatory, reputational, operational, etc." The risks are "highly interdependent": an event in one area has ramifications for others. | RMS99 Introduction |
| 3 | **Expected loss (EL)**: the average level of losses a bank can reasonably expect; treated as a cost of doing business and managed through **pricing and provisioning**. **Unexpected loss (UL)**: losses above the expected level, whose timing and size cannot be known in advance; **capital** is needed to cover them ("it has a loss-absorbing function"). | IRB05 s. 3 |
| 4 | So the link: risk taken → capital needed (to absorb UL) → return must pay for both the expected loss and the capital. More risk needs more capital and should earn more return. | IRB05 s. 3; CRGN02 9.7–9.9; concept |
| 5 | **RAROC** (risk-adjusted return on capital) expresses an asset's or business's value-producing capacity as a ratio, so businesses of different risk can be compared; capital is allocated to a deal or business line, and "the higher the volatility of the returns, the more capital is allocated". A hurdle rate can set target pricing. (Taught in D07; one-line preview only.) | CRGN02 9.7–9.9 |

## B1.2 Why risk management
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | Top management should "improve the ability to identify, measure, monitor and control the overall level of risks undertaken". The four verbs are the risk management process (B02 builds on them). | RMS99 Introduction |
| 7 | Why: to protect depositors' money and the bank's capital; to keep earnings steady; to price each deal for its risk; to meet the regulator's rules; to survive shocks. Risk is managed, not removed. | Concept (B02 carries the framework) |

## B2.1 Identifying risk; B2.5 the definitions
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Risk is identified at the level of the single deal (transaction), the portfolio, and the bank as a whole. One loan carries several risks: "In addition to the risks related to creditworthiness of the counterparty, the banks are also exposed to interest rate, forex and country risks." | RMS99 3.1.1; concept for the levels |
| 9 | **Credit risk** (default risk): "inability or unwillingness of a customer or counterparty to meet commitments in relation to lending, trading, hedging, settlement and other financial transactions". It is "made up of transaction risk or default risk and portfolio risk"; portfolio risk "comprises intrinsic and concentration risk". | RMS99 3.1.2 |
| 10 | Credit risk also covers losses without a default: losses can "result from reduction in portfolio value arising from actual or perceived deterioration in credit quality" (downgrade / migration risk; a downgraded bond falls in price). | CRGN02 1.1 |
| 11 | Credit risk in an LC or guarantee: "funds may not be forthcoming from the constituents upon crystallization of the liability". | CRGN02 1.1 |
| 12 | **Counterparty credit risk**: "the risk that the counterparty to a transaction could default before the final settlement" of the transaction's cash flows; it is a **bilateral** risk of loss (the deal's value can move either way), unlike a loan's one-way credit risk. | CRE50.1 |
| 13 | **Settlement (principal) risk** in FX: a bank "pays away the currency being sold, but fails to receive the currency being bought"; the amount at risk can be the full value of the trade; "sometimes referred to as 'Herstatt Risk'". The failure of Bankhaus Herstatt in **1974** highlighted it. | FXS13 2.5, footnote, Annex para 6 |
| 14 | **Country risk**: "the possibility that a country will be unable to service or repay its debts to foreign lenders in a timely manner"; arises from cross-border lending and investment. **Transfer risk** "is the core risk under country risk": losses from restrictions on external remittances, so "an obligor may be able to pay in local currency, but may not be able to pay in foreign currency". **Sovereign risk**: lending to a government or taking its guarantee (immunity from legal process). | CRGN02 7.1 |
| 15 | Country risk element "should be explicitly recognised while assessing the counter-party risk"; banks reckon both funded and non-funded exposures. | CRM03 Annex (Scope) |
| 16 | **Market risk** (Basel): "the risk of losses in on- and off-balance sheet risk positions arising from movements in market prices". RBI 1999: market risk arises "from adverse changes in market variables, such as interest rate, foreign exchange rate, equity price and commodity price"; it "takes the form of" liquidity risk, interest rate risk, forex risk, commodity price risk and equity price risk. | MAR10.1; RMS99 7.1 |
| 17 | **Liquidity risk**: arises "from funding of long-term assets by short-term liabilities", making liabilities "subject to rollover or refinancing risk". Its three forms: **Funding risk** = "need to replace net outflows due to unanticipated withdrawal/non-renewal of deposits (wholesale and retail)"; **Time risk** = "need to compensate for non-receipt of expected inflows of funds, i.e. performing assets turning into non-performing assets"; **Call risk** = "due to crystallisation of contingent liabilities and unable to undertake profitable business opportunities when desirable". | RMS99 8.2.2, 8.2.3 |
| 18 | **Interest rate risk**: "potential impact on NII or NIM or Market Value of Equity (MVE), caused by unexpected changes in market interest rates". ALM99: "the risk where changes in market interest rates might adversely affect a bank's financial condition". | RMS99 9.2; ALM99 8.1 |
| 19 | **Gap or mismatch risk**: from assets, liabilities and off-balance-sheet items "with different principal amounts, maturity dates or repricing dates", exposing the bank to changes in the **level** of rates. | RMS99 9.3.1 |
| 20 | **Basis risk**: "The risk that the interest rate of different assets, liabilities and off-balance sheet items may change in different magnitude". Rates "seldom change by the same degree"; high where composite assets are funded by composite liabilities (Indian loan books). It can be favourable or unfavourable. | RMS99 9.3.2 |
| 21 | **Embedded option risk**: rate changes encourage prepayment of cash credit / demand / term loans, exercise of call / put options on bonds, and premature withdrawal of term deposits; the faster and bigger the rate change, the greater the risk to NII. | RMS99 9.3.3 |
| 22 | **Yield curve risk**: assets and liabilities priced off different benchmarks or maturities; "any non-parallel movements in yield curves would affect the NII". | RMS99 9.3.4 |
| 23 | **Price risk**: "occurs when assets are sold before their stated maturities"; bond prices and yields move inversely; "closely associated with the trading book". | RMS99 9.3.5 |
| 24 | **Reinvestment risk**: "Uncertainty with regard to interest rate at which the future cash flows could be reinvested". | RMS99 9.3.6 |
| 25 | **Net interest position risk**: when a bank has more earning assets than paying liabilities, a fall in market rates cuts NII (and a rise lifts it); "large float is a natural hedge". | RMS99 9.3.7 |
| 26 | The seven IRR types are named here; how they are measured (gap, duration, simulation) is taught in D05. | course plan |
| 27 | **Operational risk** (Basel, the definition in use): "the risk of loss resulting from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems or from external events. This definition includes legal risk, but excludes strategic and reputational risk." | OPE10.1 |
| 28 | Older RBI wording (1999): operational risk "is defined as any risk, which is not categoried as market or credit risk, or the risk of loss arising from various types of human or technical error". Teach the Basel definition as current; the 1999 line only as history. | RMS99 12.2 |
| 29 | **Compliance risk**: "the risk of legal or regulatory sanctions, material financial loss, or loss to reputation a bank may suffer as a result of its failure to comply with laws, regulations, rules, related self-regulatory organisation standards, and codes of conduct". | COMP05 para 3 |
| 30 | **Reputational risk**: "the risk arising from negative perception on the part of customers, counterparties, shareholders, investors, debt-holders, market analysts, other relevant parties or regulators" that can hurt the bank's ability to keep or win business and its "continued access to sources of funding". It is handled in Pillar 2 (ICAAP), and can lead to credit, liquidity, market and legal risk. | ENH09 paras 47–48 |
| 31 | **Strategic risk**: the risk of loss from wrong business decisions or poor carrying-out of them (entering the wrong market, a failed product, not responding to change). Basel's operational-risk definition **excludes** it (row 27). | Concept; OPE10.1 for the exclusion |
| 32 | **Systemic risk**: the risk that the failure or distress of one institution or market spreads (contagion) and disrupts the financial system as a whole. | Concept (B05 carries D-SIBs) |
| 33 | **Model risk**: "the risk of adverse outcomes of a model arising from, inter alia," model errors, misapplication and models that have become unsuitable over time. **Status: RBI draft guidance (June 2026)**; teach the idea, say "RBI has issued draft guidance". **(freeze: check whether finalised)** | MRM26 para 7(7) |
| 34 | **Climate-related risks**: **physical risk** = losses from more severe and frequent climate events: *acute* (floods, heatwaves, storms) and *chronic* (gradual shifts such as rising sea levels and temperatures); **transition risk** = risks of adjusting to a low-carbon economy: changes in climate policy and regulation, new technologies, shifts in customer preferences. These definitions are from RBI's **draft** disclosure framework (Feb 2024). Climate risk shows up through the usual risks (a flooded borrower is a credit loss). **(freeze: status of RBI's final climate-risk disclosure rules)** | CLIM24 (g), (l); concept for "through the usual risks" |

## B2.2–B2.3 The banking book and the trading book
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | Basel boundary: an instrument held for any of these purposes must be in the **trading book** when first recognised: (1) short-term resale; (2) profiting from short-term price movements; (3) locking in arbitrage profits; (4) hedging risks from (1)–(3). An instrument not held for these at inception goes to the **banking book**, which is the residual ("All other instruments"). Trading-book instruments must be free of legal impediments to selling or hedging. | RBC25.1, 25.3, 25.5, 25.7 |
| 36 | Indian investment categories: a bank classifies its investments as **HTM**, **AFS** and **FVTPL**; **HFT is a separate sub-category within FVTPL**; the category is decided at or before acquisition and documented. | INV25 para 33 |
| 37 | **HTM**: held to collect contractual cash flows till maturity, and cash flows are solely payments of principal and interest (SPPI). Carried **at cost**, **not marked to market** after initial recognition (premium / discount amortised). | INV25 paras 35, 48–49 |
| 38 | **AFS**: objective met "by both collecting contractual cash flows and selling securities", plus SPPI. Fair valued **at least quarterly**; net gains or losses go to the **AFS-Reserve**, not through profit and loss. | INV25 paras 38, 50, 51 |
| 39 | **FVTPL**: securities that do not qualify for HTM or AFS (e.g. most equity shares, mutual funds). Gains and losses go to the **profit and loss account**; non-HFT FVTPL fair valued at least quarterly. | INV25 paras 40, 56 |
| 40 | **HFT**: instruments held for short-term resale, profiting from short-term price movements, locking in arbitrage profits, or hedging those; **fair valued daily**, valuation change in profit and loss; only instruments with no legal impediment against selling or fully hedging. | INV25 para 41(1)–(3) |
| 41 | **Today's rule (since 1 Apr 2024)**: the trading book for capital is the **HFT** sub-category. Instruments under **HTM, AFS, FVTPL (non-HFT)** and investments in own subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates are part of the **banking book** and do not attract a market-risk capital charge. | TB24 paras 2–4, 7 and Annex 1 (freeze: open annex) |
| 42 | **Issued, coming (1 Apr 2027)**: the Market Risk Directions, 2026 keep the same boundary ("Trading book, for the purpose of capital adequacy, shall include all instruments that are classified as 'Held for Trading'"; all else is banking book with a credit-risk or counterparty-risk charge) and bring a new method for the market-risk charge (B07). | MR26 paras 2, 6 |
| 43 | Old rule, do not teach as current: before 1 Apr 2024 the trading book for capital was "HFT and AFS". | TB24 (the change); depth/C.md outdated list |
| 44 | Banking book vs trading book, side by side: **intent** (hold / collect vs trade for short-term gain); **accounting** (banking book mostly at cost or accrual, loans and HTM; trading book fair valued daily through profit and loss); **main risks** (banking book: credit, liquidity, interest rate risk in the banking book; trading book: market risk, plus counterparty); **capital** (banking book: credit-risk charge; trading book: market-risk charge). | Rows 35–42; concept for "main risks" |
| 45 | Accrual accounting records income as it is earned over time; mark-to-market (fair value) records the asset at today's market price, so price moves show up at once. | Concept; INV25 paras 48, 41(2) |

## B2.4 Off-balance-sheet exposures
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 46 | Off-balance-sheet items are commitments that are not yet assets or liabilities: guarantees, letters of credit, undrawn sanctioned limits, derivatives. They are **contingent**: they can **crystallise** into a funded exposure (an invoked guarantee, a devolved LC). | Concept; CRGN02 1.1 ("upon crystallization of the liability") |
| 47 | When they crystallise, they bring credit risk (the customer may not pay the bank back) and **call risk** (a sudden need for funds). | CRGN02 1.1; RMS99 8.2.3 |
| 48 | Market risk and interest rate risk also arise from off-balance-sheet positions (MAR10.1 "on- and off-balance sheet risk positions"; RMS99 9.3.1). How they become credit-equivalent amounts (CCFs) is taught in B06. | MAR10.1; RMS99 9.3.1 |

## Question blueprint (what the lesson must equip the learner for)
- **Define**: risk; EL vs UL; credit, counterparty, settlement (Herstatt), country, transfer, sovereign risk; market
  risk; liquidity risk and its three forms; interest rate risk and its seven types; operational, compliance,
  reputational, strategic, systemic, model, climate (physical / transition) risk; banking and trading book; HTM, AFS,
  FVTPL, HFT; off-balance-sheet item.
- **Purpose**: why manage risk; why capital (UL); why provisions and pricing (EL); why the HFT boundary matters.
- **Who / when / how much**: which book an item sits in today; since when (1 Apr 2024); what changes on 1 Apr 2027;
  HFT valued daily, AFS at least quarterly, HTM at cost.
- **Compare**: basis vs gap vs yield curve risk; funding vs time vs call risk; EL vs UL; banking vs trading book;
  accrual vs MTM; counterparty vs settlement risk; transfer vs sovereign risk.
- **Exception**: Basel op-risk definition includes legal but excludes strategic and reputational risk; a loss without
  default (downgrade); a borrower willing to pay but blocked (transfer risk); AFS is banking book for capital today.
- **Calculate**: none in this lesson (statistics in B03; capital sums from B06).
- **Apply to a case**: name every risk in a branch story; one loan, many risks.
