What this lesson must carry. Four referees govern foreign exchange in India as at 31 December 2026: the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the Central Government under FEMA 1999, Authorised Persons under the May 2026 regulations, and FEDAI as the market's self-regulatory body, alongside four global bodies (BIS/BCBS, ICC, SWIFT, World Bank). Exchange control began in India on 3 September 1939 under the Defence of India Rules as a wartime measure, received statutory backing in FERA 1947 (Act 7 of 1947), tightened into FERA 1973 (Act 46 of 1973, in force 1 January 1974, criminal prosecution for conserving forex), and shifted on 1 June 2000 to FEMA 1999 (Act 42 of 1999, enacted 29 December 1999) — a civil law to facilitate external trade and payments and promote orderly development of the forex market; RBI renamed its department to the Foreign Exchange Department (FED) on 31 January 2004. FEMA applies across India and to overseas branches/offices owned or controlled by a person resident in India (PRI) [s. 1(2)–(3)]. Its core structure runs in five blocks: ss. 3–8 (dealings only through APs, holding, current account, capital account — RBI for debt s. 6(2), Central Govt for non-debt s. 6(2A), export declarations, repatriation); ss. 10–12 (AP authorisation/revocation, customer declaration s. 10(5), RBI directions and AP penalty up to ₹10,000 + ₹2,000/day s. 11, RBI inspection s. 12); ss. 13–15 (penalties up to 3× quantifiable sum or ₹2 lakh + ₹5,000/day; s. 13(1A)–(1D) undisclosed foreign assets up to 5 years' prison; s. 14 civil imprisonment after 90 days; s. 15 compounding within 180 days); ss. 36–37A (Directorate of Enforcement investigation and non-compoundable seizure of equivalent Indian assets); and s. 46 (Central Govt Rules) vs s. 47 (RBI Regulations). FEMA residency (>182 days in preceding FY + intention test) differs from the Income-tax Act, 2025 s. 6 (effective 1 April 2026: ≥182 days in current tax year, 60/365 and 120-day rules, Deemed Resident, RNOR, POEM). Under the Compounding Rules, 2024 and RBI MD 04/2025-26 (updated 23 October 2026), applications cost ₹10,000 + 18% GST, run physically or via PRAVAAH, are disposed within 180 days, and paid within 15 days; RBI compounds all contraventions except s. 3(a) (AGM ≤ ₹60 lakh, DGM ≤ ₹2.5 crore, GM ≤ ₹5 crore, CGM > ₹5 crore) while ED compounds s. 3(a); the general ceiling is 300% of sum involved (a discretionary ₹2 lakh cap inserted April 2025 applies only to Row 5 non-reporting contraventions). Under the Overseas Investment Rules/Regulations, 2022 and MD OI (updated 1 April 2026), Indian entities face a 400% net worth financial commitment cap, obtain a UIN before remittance, and report via Form FC, Form OPI (60 days half-yearly), APR (31 December), and FLA Return (15 July); Indian banks/NBFCs remain subject to RBI conditions. Under FEM (Authorised Persons) Regulations, 2026 (FEMA 401/2026-RB, in force 6 May 2026), fresh authorisations (via PRAVAAH) are in three categories — AD Category-I (RBI-licensed banks: all permissible current & capital txns), AD Category-II (min net worth ₹10 crore, ₹50 crore annual turnover in 2 yrs; non-trade current account txns except gift/donation PLUS trade transactions up to ₹25 lakh per transaction), and AD Category-III (min net worth ₹2 crore; incidental/innovative forex activities). Fresh FFMC licences are discontinued, though existing FFMCs remain renewable (net worth ₹25L/₹50L; ₹10 crore turnover in 2 yrs); all licences are now perpetual (replacing old 1–5 year renewals). No fresh money-changing franchisees are allowed from 6 May 2026 and existing ones must wind down within 2 years (by 6 May 2028) ("issued, in progress"), replaced by Forex Correspondents (FxCs) appointed by AD Cat-I/II under a principal-agent model who can both purchase and sell travel forex. FEDAI (set up 1958, Section 8 non-profit, 100 members, formally recognised by RBI as an SRO on 14 January 2026) frames FEDAI Rules (10th Edition, updated 20 August 2026 — 8 Rules, not 10), publishes Month-End Revaluation Rates for 26 currencies (mandated by RBI for AS 11; FBIL took over daily Reference Rates in 2018 and daily NOOP Spot Rate on 6 April 2026), accredits voice forex brokers, trains staff, and advises RBI — but does not supervise banks and does not represent India at the ICC (ICC India does).
