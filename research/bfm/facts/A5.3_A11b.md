# Fact sheet · A5.3 export finance: packing credit to post-shipment (video A11b)

Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-21. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (May/June 2027 exam). Rows cited as [F3].
Our own words; we cite paragraph numbers. A11 owns the FEMA side (EDF, realisation period, EDPMS, reduction, set-off:
`A5.1-A5.5_A11.md`); A12 owns the discounting and crystallisation arithmetic; A14 owns Gold Card detail, EEFC, DDA.

**The big facts for this lesson:**
1. **The Master Circular is gone.** The rules that sat in the Master Circular "Rupee / Foreign Currency Export Credit and
   Customer Service to Exporters" (last edition RBI/2015-16/47, 1 Jul 2015) now sit in **Chapter XV "Export Credit"
   (paras 234–396)** of the **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Credit Facilities) Directions, 2025**
   (DOR.CRE.REC.73/07-01-001/2025-26, **28 Nov 2025**; in force on issue; "updated as on 1 Oct 2026"). Chapter XV carries
   **no amendment markers**: nothing in it has changed since 28 Nov 2025. Lessons cite "Credit Facilities Directions,
   para N", not the old circular.
2. **The 450-day relief has ended for new money.** Two separate sets of Trade Relief Measures Directions allowed up to
   **450 days** of pre- and post-shipment credit: the 2025 set (14 Nov 2025, US-tariff "global headwinds", listed sectors
   only) for credit **disbursed till 31 Mar 2026**, and the 2026 set (31 Mar 2026, West Asia crisis, all exporters) for
   credit **disbursed till 30 Jun 2026**. No later extension is in RBI's 2026 notification list (read to 11 Nov 2026).
   At the cut-off the normal ceilings apply: **360 days** pre-shipment, **365 days** post-shipment usance.
3. **There is no RBI "lower of FOB value and domestic cost" rule** in either the 2025 Directions or the 2015 Master
   Circular (both searched in full). It is a bank appraisal norm (lead: the classes, the IIBF book). Teach it as "how
   banks size a packing credit", never as an RBI rule (row 9).
4. **Interest Equalisation Scheme ended on 31 Dec 2024.** Its successor at the cut-off is the Government's **interest
   subvention under the Export Promotion Mission (EPM) – Niryat Protsahan** (DGFT Trade Notice 20/2025-26, 2 Jan 2026;
   RBI circular of 19 Jan 2026), a pilot for MSME exporters, run through **EXIM Bank from 1 Apr 2026** (row 41–43).

| 8 | **Need-based, not collateral-based**: export credit limits are assessed on need; credit shall **not be denied merely for want of collateral** if the exporter's performance justifies it. Pre- and post-shipment limits are **interchangeable**; peak/non-peak limits for seasonal goods; lines of credit normally for one year, up to **three years** for established exporters; additional export credit is met **even if it exceeds MPBF**; each bank to reach export credit of at least **10% of net credit** (a target in the text). | CF 385–389, 394 |
| 9 | **"Lower of FOB value and domestic cost" (the bank's sizing norm, not RBI text)**: the classes teach that packing credit is the lower of (a) the **FOB** value of the order (not CIF: freight and insurance are not working capital) and (b) the **domestic cost** (cost of production / domestic market value) of the goods, less the bank's margin. **No such sentence exists in CF Chapter XV or in MC15.** RBI's text only (i) speaks of **"packing credit in excess of FOB value"** as an exception for agro products, where PCFC covers only the **exportable portion** (316), and (ii) for services, says export credit must **not exceed the foreign exchange earned less margins and advances received** (257(7)). Teach the norm as bank practice with its logic (you cannot finance more than the goods cost you, or more than the buyer pays for the goods at the port). | CF 241, 257(7), 316; MC15 (searched); lead: classes |
| 10 | **Packing credit above the export value (agro cases)**: wastage (raw cashew): the excess may be extinguished by bills for the **by-product** (cashew shell oil) (241(2)); goods graded into exportable and non-exportable (tobacco, pepper, cardamom, cashew): PC covering the **non-exportable part sold at home** carries the **domestic advance rate from the date of advance** (241(4)); **HPS groundnut and de-oiled cakes**: PC up to the value of raw material even above the order, excess adjusted in cash or by sale of by-product oil **within 30 days** (241(6)). | CF 241 |
| 11 | **Running account facility**: normally PC needs an LC/firm order first, but for **seasonal raw materials** or **production time longer than the delivery schedule** the bank may give a running account **for any commodity**, without prior LC/order, only to exporters with a **good track record** and to **EOUs, FTZ/EPZ and SEZ units**; LCs/orders must be produced **within a reasonable time fixed by the bank**; bills are marked off on **FIFO** against the earliest PC, keeping each drawal within its sanction period or **360 days, whichever is earlier**; misuse → **withdrawn forthwith**; non-compliance → **not export credit ab initio**; **not for sub-suppliers**. | CF 243–246, 249(1) |
| 12 | **PCFC (pre-shipment credit in foreign currency)**: an **additional window** at internationally competitive rates, for **domestic and imported inputs**, **cash exports only**; rupee export credit rules apply mutatis mutandis. Exporter's three routes: rupee PC → rupee post-shipment or EBR; PCFC → EBR in foreign currency; rupee PC then converted to PCFC at the bank's discretion. | CF 294–296 |
| 13 | **PCFC currency**: any **convertible currency** (USD, GBP, JPY, EUR etc.), and it may differ from the invoice currency (PCFC in USD against a EUR order; the **cross-currency risk and cost are the exporter's**); allowed for **exports to ACU countries**. If the exporter has **suppliers' credit** for imported inputs, PCFC covers **domestic inputs only**. | CF 297, 301 |
| 14 | **PCFC funding**: banks may use **EEFC, RFC(D), FCNR(B)** balances, escrow and exporters' FC account balances; **lines of credit from overseas banks without prior RBI approval**; lines from other Indian banks if they cannot raise abroad and have **no branch abroad**; FC borrowed under FEMA 3(R)/2018 and **buy-sell swaps**, within the **Aggregate Gap Limit (AGL)**. | CF 298–302 |
| 15 | **PCFC interest**: bank-determined, using any widely accepted **Alternative Reference Rate (ARR)** in that currency (LIBOR is gone), per Board policy and the Interest Rates on Advances Directions. | CF 294, 303 |
| 16 | **PCFC period**: maximum **360 days**; extension on the same terms as rupee PC; if **no export within 360 days**, PCFC is **adjusted at the TT selling rate** and the bank may repay the foreign line without RBI permission. | CF 304–305 |
| 17 | **PCFC liquidation**: by proceeds of export documents **discounted/rediscounted under EBR** or by a **foreign currency loan (DP bills)**; by agreement also from **EEFC** or **rupee resources** to the extent exports have taken place; substitution of order/commodity if commercially necessary; on **cancellation** of the order the exporter **buys the foreign exchange (principal + interest) in the domestic market** through the bank (or the bank remits to the overseas lender); the running account is available for PCFC too (good track record, FIFO). | CF 315, 317–322 |
| 18 | **Rupee fluctuations**: limits are fixed in rupees, so when the rupee falls the FC component shrinks or the exporter is asked to pay down; banks should consider **denominating the FC component in foreign currency** or recomputing limits about **monthly**. | CF 306–311 |

## Numericals (solved twice; basic 8-function calculator)
### N1 · Packing credit limit and drawing power
An exporter has an order for 40,000 shirts, **CIF USD 2,80,000**. Freight is USD 14,000 and insurance USD 2,800.
The bank sizes packing credit at the **lower of FOB value and domestic cost of production**, converts at **₹88.00** (its
notional rate for the limit) and keeps a **10% margin**. Domestic cost of production is **₹2,20,00,000**. The sanctioned
PC limit is ₹2,10,00,000 and ₹1,20,00,000 is already drawn. How much more can the exporter draw?

**Method 1 (lower first, then margin)**
1. FOB = 2,80,000 − 14,000 − 2,800 = **USD 2,63,200**. Keys: `280000 − 14000 − 2800 =` → 263200.
2. FOB in rupees = 2,63,200 × 88 = **₹2,31,61,600**. Keys: `× 88 =` → 23161600.
3. Lower of ₹2,31,61,600 and ₹2,20,00,000 = **₹2,20,00,000** (domestic cost).
4. Less 10% margin = 2,20,00,000 × 90% = **₹1,98,00,000**. Keys: `22000000 × 90 %` → 19800000.
5. Drawing power = lower of ₹1,98,00,000 and the limit ₹2,10,00,000 = **₹1,98,00,000**.
6. Available = 1,98,00,000 − 1,20,00,000 = **₹78,00,000**. Keys: `19800000 − 12000000 =` → 7800000.

**Method 2 (margin on both, then lower)**: 2,31,61,600 × 90% = 2,08,45,440 (`23161600 × 90 %`); 2,20,00,000 × 90% =
1,98,00,000; lower = **₹1,98,00,000**; below the ₹2,10,00,000 limit; less 1,20,00,000 = **₹78,00,000**. Same answer
(a common % applied to both cannot change which is lower).

**Trap check**: CIF instead of FOB gives 2,80,000 × 88 = ₹2,46,40,000, still not the lower here, but if domestic cost
were ₹2,40,00,000 the CIF-user would pick ₹2,40,00,000 (wrong) against the correct FOB ₹2,31,61,600. Label in the
lesson: "the lower-of rule is the bank's sizing norm; RBI only says no export credit beyond the export value except
listed agro cases" (row 9).

### N2 · Liquidating packing credit, and what if the export never happens
Packing credit **₹50,00,000** is drawn on **1 Mar 2027** at the bank's PC rate **8.50% p.a.** (simple interest for the
sum; the bank actually applies it at monthly rests, IRA para 6(7)). The exporter ships and submits documents on
**29 Jul 2027**; the bill for **USD 62,500** is purchased at **₹87.40** and the proceeds liquidate the PC with its
interest. (a) What is credited to the exporter's current account? (b) If instead the order is cancelled, nothing is
exported, and the exporter repays from its own funds on day 200, at the bank's domestic rate **11.00%**, how much more
interest does it pay than at the PC rate (before any penal charges)?

**(a) Method 1 (yearly interest, then days)**
1. Days 1 Mar → 29 Jul 2027 (count the 1st out, the 29th in) = 30 (Mar) + 30 (Apr) + 31 (May) + 30 (Jun) + 29 (Jul)
   = **150 days**. Within 360 days: export credit status kept (CF 235).
2. Interest = 50,00,000 × 8.5% × 150 ÷ 365. Keys: `5000000 × 8.5 %` → 425000; `× 150 ÷ 365 =` → 174657.53 →
   **₹1,74,658**.
3. Bill proceeds = 62,500 × 87.40 = **₹54,62,500**. Keys: `62500 × 87.4 =` → 5462500.
4. Credit to current account = 54,62,500 − 50,00,000 − 1,74,658 = **₹2,87,842**. Keys: `5462500 − 5000000 − 174658 =`
   → 287842.

**(a) Method 2 (interest per day)**: 50,00,000 × 8.5 ÷ 100 ÷ 365 = ₹1,164.3836 a day (`5000000 × 8.5 ÷ 100 ÷ 365 =`);
× 150 = ₹1,74,657.53 → ₹1,74,658; 54,62,500 − 51,74,658 = **₹2,87,842**. Same.

**(b) Method 1**: domestic interest 50,00,000 × 11% × 200 ÷ 365 = ₹3,01,369.86 (`5000000 × 11 %` → 550000;
`× 200 ÷ 365 =`); PC-rate interest 50,00,000 × 8.5% × 200 ÷ 365 = ₹2,32,876.71; extra = **₹68,493.15 ≈ ₹68,493**.
**(b) Method 2**: rate gap 11 − 8.5 = 2.5%; 50,00,000 × 2.5% × 200 ÷ 365 (`5000000 × 2.5 %` → 125000; `× 200 ÷ 365 =`)
= **₹68,493.15**. Same. Teaching line: "no export → domestic rate **from the date of advance**, plus penal charges as
per the bank's Board policy" (CF 293); "documents after day 360 → not export credit **ab initio**" (CF 235, 292).

## Traps
- "Packing credit can be repaid only from that export's bill" (no: also EEFC or rupee funds to the extent exported; substitution of contract for good track record; CF 240, 242).
- "PC period is 180 days / 270 days" (no fixed period: the bank sets it; 360 days is the outer limit).
- "After 360 days it loses export status from day 361" (no: **ab initio**, from the date of advance).
- "No export → penal interest only for the overdue days" (no: domestic rate **from the date of advance**, plus penal charges).
- "450 days is the current limit" (no: relief for disbursals till 31 Mar 2026 (TR25) / 30 Jun 2026 (TR26); now 360/365).
- "The 2026 relief was only for the 20 tariff-hit sectors" (no: the sector list and 31-Aug-2025 standard test were TR25; TR26 covers any exporter of an eligible RE).
- "RBI says lower of FOB and domestic cost" (bank norm; RBI text has no such line). "CIF value for PC" (no: FOB).
- "Running account for everyone / sub-suppliers" (only good track record and EOU/FTZ/EPZ/SEZ; never sub-suppliers).
- "PCFC only in the invoice currency / only USD" (any convertible currency; cross-currency risk on the exporter).
- "PCFC priced on LIBOR" (ARR now). "PCFC unpaid at 360 days → TT buying" (TT **selling**).
- "Post-shipment usance credit up to 180 days" (180 is the **EBR** coverage; rupee post-shipment usance up to **365 days** from shipment).
- "NTP = time for goods to reach the port" (no: negotiation → receipt of proceeds; 25 days FC sight bills).
- "Advance against retention money for the services portion" (not allowed; supply portion only).
- "Interest Equalisation Scheme is running" (ended 31 Dec 2024; now EPM Niryat Protsahan subvention for MSMEs, via EXIM Bank from 1 Apr 2026).
- "ECGC post-shipment premium is charged to the exporter" (RBI: may be borne by the bank).
- "Export credit rules are in the Master Circular" (repealed; Credit Facilities Directions 2025, Ch. XV).
