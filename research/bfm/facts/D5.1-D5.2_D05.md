What this lesson must carry
Interest rate deregulation in India — starting in the early 1990s, largely completed by 1997, and finished on rupee deposits with savings-rate deregulation on 25 October 2011 — turned interest rate risk (IRR) into a core balance-sheet risk for every commercial bank. Under the operative RBI (Commercial Banks – Asset Liability Management) Directions, 2025 (28 November 2025), IRR is the risk that changes in market interest rates may adversely affect a bank's financial condition. It acts through six practical sources that bank officers encounter daily — gap (mismatch/repricing) risk, basis risk, embedded option risk, yield curve risk, price risk, and reinvestment risk — plus net interest position risk when earning assets exceed interest-paying liabilities (while BCBS SRP31 groups banking-book IRR into gap, basis, and option risk). To measure short-term earnings exposure, banks use Traditional Gap Analysis (TGA), grouping Rate Sensitive Assets (RSA), Rate Sensitive Liabilities (RSL), and off-balance-sheet items into 11 RBI time buckets (1–28 days up to over 15 years, plus Non-sensitive) by residual maturity or next repricing date, whichever is earlier. Items that do not reprice — such as cash, current deposits, fixed assets, capital, and reserves — sit in the Non-sensitive bucket, whereas non-maturity deposits (savings/current) and mortgages require empirical behavioural studies based on at least three years of data. The repricing Gap = RSA − RSL: a positive (asset-sensitive) gap boosts Net Interest Income (NII) when rates rise and cuts NII when rates fall, whereas a negative (liability-sensitive) gap cuts NII when rates rise and boosts NII when rates fall. Simple earnings sensitivity is ΔNII = Gap × Δr, and the resulting potential income loss over a one-year horizon under adverse rate scenarios is Earnings at Risk (EaR), capped by Board/RMC internal limits linked to NII. NII equals Interest Earned minus Interest Expended, and under RBI's IRACP Directions, 2025 (updated 1 October 2026), accrual income is allowed only on Standard loans: whenever an account turns NPA, all accrued unrealised interest must be reversed through P&L, directly cutting Interest Earned, NII, and Net Interest Margin (NIM) (and from 1 October 2026, unrealised pre-acquisition interest on Specified Non-Financial Assets also cannot be recognised as income). RBI's Statistical Tables and Bulletin — matching IIBF's standard CAIIB formula — define NIM = NII ÷ Average Total Assets × 100, whereas some international disclosures divide by Average Earning Assets (a key exam denominator trap). Finally, Gap Analysis is only the first rung on the techniques ladder (Gap → Duration → Simulation, static and dynamic): it misses intra-bucket repricing timing, basis risk, embedded options, non-parallel yield-curve shifts, non-interest income/expense volatility, and long-term economic value (MVE) impacts. As at 31 December 2026, RBI's ALM Directions, 2025 (requiring both TGA and DGA, with the 200 bps / 20% MVE Pillar 2 outlier test) remain the operative rule, while the BCBS-aligned IRRBB framework sits in RBI's Forthcoming Instructions Directions, 2025 ("issued, coming", effective date yet to be notified).

	Definition of Interest Rate Risk (IRR): Under the operative RBI ALM Directions, 2025, Interest Rate Risk means the risk where changes in market interest rates might adversely affect a bank's financial condition — impacting earnings (NII / NIM) in the short run and Market Value of Equity (MVE / Net Worth) in the long run.

	F3
	Banking book vs trading book focus: Assets in the trading book are held for short-term price/yield gains, making price risk the prime concern; the banking book holds relationship loans, deposits, and statutory investments generally held to maturity, making earnings (NII) and economic value (MVE) changes the prime concern.
	RMS99 Para 9.4.3 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0)
	F4
	Source 1 — Gap or Mismatch (Repricing) Risk: Arises from holding assets, liabilities, and off-balance-sheet items with different principal amounts, maturity dates (fixed rate), or repricing dates (floating rate), exposing the bank to unexpected shifts in market interest rates. BCBS SRP31 splits gap risk into parallel risk (uniform shift across curve) and non-parallel risk (differential shifts across tenors).
	RMS99 Para 9.3.1 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0); SRP31 Para 31.2 (https://www.bis.org/basel_framework/chapter/SRP/31.htm?inforce=20260101&published=20240716&tldate=20250103); FORTH25 Para 51(7) (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13137)
	F5
	Source 2 — Basis Risk: The risk that interest rates on different assets, liabilities, and off-balance-sheet items of similar tenors change by different magnitudes because they are priced off different interest rate indices (e.g., loans linked to T-Bill/repo funded by composite deposits). Favourable basis shifts expand NII; adverse basis shifts contract NII.
	RMS99 Para 9.3.2 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0); SRP31 Para 31.2 (https://www.bis.org.in/basel_framework/chapter/SRP/31.htm?inforce=20260101&published=20240716&tldate=20250103); FORTH25 Para 51(3) (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13137)
	F6
	Source 3 — Embedded Option Risk: Significant rate moves trigger customers or issuers to exercise options embedded in banking products — prepayment of loans when rates fall, premature withdrawal of term deposits when rates rise, or call/put options on bonds — always to the bank's disadvantage. Divided into automatic option risk (caps, floors, explicit options) and behavioural option risk (prepayments, early withdrawals).
	RMS99 Para 9.3.3 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0); SRP31 Para 31.2 (https://www.bis.org/basel_framework/chapter/SRP/31.htm?inforce=20260101&published=20240716&tldate=20250103); FORTH25 Para 51(12) (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13137)
	F7
	Source 4 — Yield Curve Risk: When assets and liabilities are priced using benchmarks of different maturities (e.g., short-term call/3-month deposit funding vs 5-year G-Sec/loan yields), non-parallel shifts (steepening, flattening, or twisting) in the yield curve adversely affect NII and portfolio value.
	RMS99 Para 9.3.4 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0)
	F8
	Source 5 — Price Risk: Occurs when fixed-income assets (such as bonds) are sold before their stated maturities; because bond prices and yields move inversely, a rise in yields causes a capital loss on sale. Closely associated with the trading book.
	RMS99 Para 9.3.5 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0)
	F9
	Source 6 — Reinvestment Risk: Uncertainty regarding the interest rate at which future cash flows (maturing principal, loan instalments, or periodic bond coupons) can be reinvested. When rates fall, maturing high-yield cash flows must be redeployed at lower yields, compressing NII.
	RMS99 Para 9.3.6 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0)
	F10
	Source 7 (Additional RBI category) — Net Interest Position Risk: When a bank has more earning assets than interest-paying liabilities (funded by non-interest-bearing float such as current accounts and equity), a downward shift in market interest rates lowers interest income without an offsetting fall in interest expense, reducing NII.
	RMS99 Para 9.3.7 (https://m.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=85&Mode=0)
	F11
	BCBS SRP31 Three Main Sub-Types of IRRBB: Globally, BCBS Basel Framework SRP31 (effective 1 Jan 2026) classifies IRRBB into three main sub-types — Gap risk (parallel and non-parallel), Basis risk, and Option risk (automatic and behavioural) — alongside Credit Spread Risk in the Banking Book (CSRBB) as a related risk.
	SRP31 Paras 31.2–31.3 (https://www.bis.org/basel_framework/chapter/SRP/31.htm?inforce=20260101&published=20240716&tldate=20250103)
