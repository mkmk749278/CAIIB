# Fact sheet · C5.1 Supervision and control of treasury · C5.2 Market risk and credit risk · C5.3 Risk measures: VaR on the desk · C2.3 Investment classification, valuation, and IFR (video C07)

Sister sheets: C01/C01b (integrated treasury & dealing room), B09/B09b (VaR methods & sums), C05/C06 (bond pricing, duration, BPV).


What this lesson must carry. Treasury risk worries bank management for three reasons: high leverage and large deal sizes handled by a few dealers, rapid market moves that can cause quick and irreversible losses, and the fact that unlike a loan (capped at principal plus interest), a trading or derivative loss can exceed the initial outlay. Banks fence this risk with three control types: organisational controls (clear functional separation of Front Office dealing, Mid-Office risk monitoring reporting independently to ALCO, and Back Office settlement, confirmation, MTM valuation, and accounting), exposure ceilings (counterparty, issuer, country, and the 5% individual broker cap per financial year excluding Primary Dealers), and position/stop-loss limits (deal-size, daylight and overnight open-position limits where daylight > overnight, NOOPL capped at 25% of total capital, AGL capped at 6 times total capital or replaced by PV01/VaR, stop-loss triggers, and NOP-INR capped at USD 100 million onshore deliverable end-of-day since March/April 2026). The Board of Directors (and its Risk Management Committee) sets NOOPL, AGL, and market-risk limits—prior RBI approval is not required, only immediate communication to RBI. On the desk, market risk covers interest-rate, exchange-rate, equity/price, and commodity risk (an FX loss is market risk, not credit risk), alongside liquidity risk; credit risk splits into pre-settlement (default/replacement) risk and settlement (Herstatt/time-zone) risk, mitigated by netting and Payment-versus-Payment (CLS). Corporate bond yields add a credit spread (at least 50 bps over G-sec for rated unquoted bonds) above sovereign yields. VaR estimates maximum expected loss over a holding period at a confidence level (95%–99% internally; 99% one-tailed, 10 trading days under Basel), validated by back-testing and supplemented by stress testing—it complements, never replaces, stop-loss and position limits. For market-risk capital, India uses the Standardised Duration Method (specific risk + general market risk across 15 time bands in 3 zones, 5% vertical disallowance for basis risk, and horizontal disallowances of 40%/30%/30% within zones, 40% adjacent, 100% zones 1–3). RBI's Minimum Capital Requirements for Market Risk Directions, 2026 (issued 21 Sep 2026) are "issued, coming 1 April 2027": Simplified Standardised Approach, trading book = all HFT, FX risk (including bullion/precious metals) on both books, and no capital credit for internal risk transfers from trading to banking book. Under the Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025 (updated 1 Oct 2026), commercial banks classify investments into HTM (SPPI + hold-to-maturity, carried at cost, no 25% cap, 5% annual sales limit with 7 exclusions), AFS (SPPI + collect and sell, quarterly fair value through AFS-Reserve), and FVTPL (residual, fair value through P&L, with HFT as a daily-MTM sub-category). Reclassification requires both Board and prior RBI DoS approval. From 22 Sep 2026, unquoted InvIT/REIT units are valued at disclosed NAV (or ₹1 if NAV is undisclosed or infrequently traded). From 18 May 2026, Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR) is discontinued for commercial banks (and LABs), with balances moved below the line to Statutory Reserve, General Reserve, or P&L; SFBs, Payments Banks, UCBs, RCBs, and RRBs still retain IFR on balance-sheet dates.
