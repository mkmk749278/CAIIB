What this lesson must carry. A derivative is a financial contract settled at a future date whose value derives from an underlying rate, price, or index, with no stand-alone intrinsic value (RBI Act 1934 s. 45U(a); SC(R)A 1956 s. 2(ac)). Bank treasuries use derivatives for three purposes: hedging / ALM, customer service, and proprietary trading / market-making — never for operational or legal risk. Under RBI directions, participants split into market-makers (who quote prices without needing an underlying risk: Scheduled Banks, SPDs, NBFC-UL, and specified AIFIs for Rupee IRDs; AD Cat-I banks and AD Cat-III SPDs for FX) and users, classified as retail or non-retail (corporate threshold: ₹500 crore net worth or ₹1,000 crore turnover); RBI is the regulator, neither a market-maker nor a user. OTC derivatives (including ETP trades) are customised with bilateral counterparty risk, whereas exchange-traded derivatives are standardised, cleared through a central counterparty (CCP), and protected by upfront initial margin, extreme loss margin, and daily cash mark-to-market (M2M) settlement on T+1. An AD Cat-I bank seeking trading/clearing membership of a SEBI-recognised currency derivatives segment needs ₹500 crore minimum net worth, minimum prescribed capital including Capital Conservation Buffer (replacing the old 10% CRAR wording), Net NPA <= 3%, and net profit for the preceding 3 financial years; otherwise it may participate as a client. Effective 10 October 2026 (A.P. (DIR) Circular No. 25), the threshold to hedge contracted FX exposures without proving the underlying dropped from USD 100 million to USD 5 million across all ADs (OTC) and across all recognised stock exchanges combined (ETCD), and rebooking cancelled INR FX derivatives is prohibited (rollover on maturity remains permitted). Currency futures started on NSE on 29 August 2008 (7 permitted pairs, up to 12 months, USD-INR lot USD 1,000, tick ₹0.0025, cash-settled in INR at the FBIL Reference Rate). From 2 May 2025, RBI permitted OTC Bond Forwards on dated G-Secs (excluding T-Bills) for non-retail users. Exchange-traded Interest Rate Futures (IRFs) (first launched June 2003; cash-settled 10-year G-Sec IRF launched January 2014; lot size ₹2,00,000 face value; FPI net long cap ₹5,000 crore) allow banks to hedge a bond portfolio against rising yields by selling (shorting) IRF.
