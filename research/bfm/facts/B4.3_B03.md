## Mean, variance, standard deviation
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mean** (arithmetic average) = sum of the values ÷ number of values (n). | Concept |
| 2 | **Deviation** = value − mean. Deviations always add up to zero, so we cannot average them directly. | Concept |
| 3 | **Variance** = average of the squared deviations: Σ(x − mean)² ÷ n. Its unit is the **square** of the data's unit (₹² , %²). | Concept |
| 4 | **Standard deviation (SD, σ)** = √variance; back in the data's own unit, so it is the usual measure of spread. A larger SD means bigger swings, i.e. more risk. | Concept |
| 5 | **Population vs sample**: dividing by n gives the population variance; dividing by n − 1 gives the sample variance (an estimate from a sample). Exam sums give a full set of values and usually expect ÷ n; use n − 1 only when the question says "sample". | Concept; exam convention (ITEMS show "variance calculation" sums; no recalled item states n − 1) |
| 6 | Shortcut: variance = (mean of the squares) − (square of the mean). Same answer; fewer subtractions. | Concept (checked in `check_b03.py`) |
| 7 | **Coefficient of variation (CV)** = SD ÷ mean: risk per unit of return, used to compare series with different means. | Concept |
| 8 | **Mean–variance choice**: with equal mean return, prefer the lower variance (SD); with equal SD, prefer the higher mean; otherwise compare CV (lower = less risk per unit of return). | Concept (ITEMS: "Mean Variance", Jun 2026 ×2) |

## The normal curve
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Many returns are taken to follow the **normal (bell) curve**, symmetric round the mean. About **68%** of outcomes lie within mean ± 1 SD, about **95%** within ± 2 SD (exactly 95% at ± 1.96 SD), about **99.7%** within ± 3 SD. | Concept |
| 10 | One tail: about **95%** of outcomes lie above mean − 1.65 SD, and about **99%** above mean − 2.33 SD. Exam questions give these z values; no tables are needed. | Concept (used in B09) |
| 11 | Basel's VaR standard uses "a 99th percentile, one-tailed confidence interval". | BCBS96 B.4(b) |

## Volatility over time
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | **Volatility** = the SD of returns over a period (daily, yearly). | Concept |
| 13 | Volatility grows with the **square root of time**, not with time: σ(T days) = σ(1 day) × √T, if daily moves are independent. Variance grows with T. | Concept |
| 14 | So: yearly = daily × √(trading days); n-day = yearly × √(n ÷ trading days in the year); 1-day = n-day ÷ √n. | Concept |
| 15 | The number of trading days is set by the question (commonly 250 or 252; sometimes 365). Use the number the question gives. | Concept; exam convention |
| 16 | Basel 1996: VaR uses a minimum holding period of **ten trading days**; banks "may use value-at-risk numbers calculated according to shorter holding periods scaled up to ten days by the square root of time"; VaR computed daily; observation period at least one year. | BCBS96 B.4(a)–(d) |
| 17 | RBI's market-risk guidance: sensitivity and Value at Risk limits for trading portfolios are measured daily. | MRGN25 paras 5–6 |
| 18 | √ on the allowed calculator: type the number, press √. For √(n ÷ D): n ÷ D = then √. | Concept (IIBF calculator: + − × ÷ % √) |

## Question blueprint
- **Define**: mean, deviation, variance, SD, CV, volatility.
- **Purpose**: why SD measures risk; why we square deviations.
- **Who / when / how much**: how many days the question assumes; which confidence goes with which z.
- **Compare**: variance vs SD units; population vs sample; which of two series is riskier; equal-mean choice.
- **Exception**: √T assumes independent daily moves; variance (not SD) grows with T.
- **Calculate**: SD by the table method; variance by the shortcut; σ√T both ways; mean ± k·SD ranges.
- **Apply to a case**: two branches' cash shortfalls; two portfolios' mean and SD.
