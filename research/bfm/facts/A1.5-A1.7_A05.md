## Points
| # | Testable point | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Value dates**: "ready" or "cash" = same day; "value next day" (tom) = next working business day; **spot = second succeeding working business day** after the contract date; a **forward** = any delivery beyond the spot date. | FEDAI 5.2 |
| 2 | The duration of a forward contract is counted **from the spot value date** at the time of the deal (a "one-month forward" runs one month from spot, not from today). | FEDAI 5.2 |
| 3 | Saturday is **not** a working business day for forex (except individuals' transactions under 1.2(DC)). | FEDAI 1.3 |
| 4 | **Known holiday** = known at least **3 working business days** before; any other is a **suddenly declared** holiday. | FEDAI 1.4 |
| 5 | If the fixed delivery date or the **last** date of the option period is a **known holiday** → delivery on the **preceding** working day. If a **suddenly declared holiday** → the **next** working day. | FEDAI 5.2 |
| 6 | **Option period**: if the delivery date is not fixed, the customer may choose an option period, **not beyond one month**; the contract must state the **first and last** dates (FEDAI's examples: 18 Jan to 17 Feb; 31 Jan to 29 Feb 2016). | FEDAI 5.2 |
| 7 | In every forward merchant contract (except NDDC) the **merchant** (buyer or seller) has the **option of delivery** within the option period. | FEDAI 5.5 |
| 8 | Contracts are for **definite amounts and periods**. | FEDAI 5.1 |
| 9 | **Forward rate = spot ± swap points.** Premium: the foreign currency is dearer forward → **add**. Discount: cheaper forward → **subtract**. Two-way points: if the first figure is **smaller** than the second (e.g. 0.20/0.24) it is a premium, add; if **larger** (0.24/0.20) a discount, subtract. | Market convention (textbook; label "convention") |
| 10 | The premium or discount comes from the **interest-rate differential** (covered interest parity): the currency with the **higher** interest rate trades at a forward **discount**. Rupee rates above dollar rates → USD at a premium against the rupee. It is **not** a forecast that the rupee will fall, and **not** a measure of volatility. | CIP (A00 sheet); concept |
| 11 | Annualised premium % = (forward − spot) ÷ spot × (12 ÷ months) × 100; approximately equal to the interest differential. Forward by parity: F = S × (1 + r_INR × t) ÷ (1 + r_USD × t). | Arithmetic |
| 12 | **Option period, the bank's favour** (exam and textbook convention, not FEDAI text): the bank quotes the rate for the date in the option period that is **best for the bank**. Purchase contract (bank buys): premium → only up to the **first** date (lower rate); discount → up to the **last** date. Sale contract (bank sells): premium → up to the **last** date; discount → only up to the **first** date. | Convention (label "convention") |
| 13 | **Bill buying for a usance export bill** (rule): purchased at the AD's bill buying rate or the contracted rate; **interest for the normal transit period and the usance period is recovered upfront**. | FEDAI 2.1(a) |
| 14 | Exam convention for the rate: spot bid + premium for (transit + usance), the period **rounded down to completed months when it is a premium** (bank buying, its favour) and **up when it is a discount**; then less the bill-buying margin. | Convention (label "convention") |
| 15 | **Normal transit period (NTP)**: the average time from negotiation/purchase/discount until the proceeds arrive (not the time for the goods to arrive). Foreign-currency bills on DP/sight basis, not under LC: **25 days**. Rupee bills not under LC: 20 days. Exports to a country under UN guidelines: max 120 days. Rupee bills under LC: 3 / 7 / 20 days by where reimbursement is given. | FEDAI 2.3(b)–(d) |
| 16 | Usance bills with a **fixed** due date (or reckoned from shipment / bill date): the actual due date is known, so NTP does **not** apply. | FEDAI 2.3(a) |
| 17 | An AD may apply a higher or lower NTP in exceptional cases (documented); finance beyond NTP at most **90 days from shipment**; no change in due date after purchase. | FEDAI 2.3(b) |
| 18 | **Overdue interest** if not paid within NTP (demand bills) or by the notional / actual due date (usance bills). | FEDAI 2.2(b) |
| 19 | Early realisation: interest for the unexpired period refunded; swap cost paid/recovered as for early delivery. | FEDAI 2.2(c) (detail in A06) |
| 20 | **Forward (OTC) vs futures (exchange-traded)**: a forward is a bilateral contract with a bank, any amount and date, settled by delivery, with counterparty risk. A currency future is a standard contract on an exchange (NSE, BSE; USD/INR lot **USD 1,000**), margins and daily mark-to-market, the clearing corporation guarantees settlement. | Concept; NSE contract specification; RBI–SEBI ETCD framework |

## Traps
- Adding a premium for a purchase up to the last date of the option period (bank's disfavour).
- "Premium means the rupee will surely fall" (it reflects the interest differential).
- "Premium reflects expected volatility" (no: interest differential).
- Reading 0.24/0.20 as a premium (falling points = discount).
- Counting a one-month forward from the deal date instead of the spot date.
- Known holiday → next day (wrong: preceding day).
- Rounding a part-month premium **up** in a purchase (bank would pay more than it should).
