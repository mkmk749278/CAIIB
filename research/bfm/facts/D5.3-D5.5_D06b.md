# Fact sheet · D5.3 Strategies for controlling interest rate risk · D5.4 Controls and supervision; sound practices · D5.5 RBI guidelines on IRRBB (video D06b)
Layer 2 (PLAYBOOK §4c). Written 2026-11-24. Rules as at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off (exam May/June 2027). Rows cited as [F1]–[F29]. Our own words; every row cites its primary text with exact paragraph numbers and full URLs. Sister sheets: D05 (sources of IRR, TGA, NII, NIM) and D06 (duration gap formula, MCLR, EBLR).


What this lesson must carry. A bank cannot eliminate both short-term earnings risk (NII / EaR) and long-term economic value risk (MVE / EVE) at the same time — managing interest rate risk is a deliberate trade-off, not a quest for a zero gap. To steer a gap, a bank uses on-balance-sheet strategies (changing investment maturities, lengthening or shortening deposit tenors, shifting loans between fixed and floating rates) or off-balance-sheet hedges under the RBI Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives Directions, 2025 (effective 1 March 2026): Interest Rate Swaps (IRS), Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs), Interest Rate Futures (IRFs), and options (caps, floors, collars, swaptions). A defensive bank keeps gaps near zero; an aggressive bank widens a positive gap when rates are expected to rise and a negative gap when rates are expected to fall. Banking-book HTM securities are held to collect contractual cash flows and cannot be actively traded: annual sales out of HTM are capped at 5% of opening carrying value without prior RBI DoS approval, though hedging an HTM bond with an IRS does not taint its HTM status. Crucially, the old 0.25% of net worth gross PV01 cap on non-option rupee derivatives was deleted on 19 October 2026 (RBI/2026-27/294). Under sound governance practices, the Board approves overall strategy, risk appetite, and limits (on EaR and MVE volatility); ALCO / senior management executes strategy and pre-approves DGA assumptions and behavioural studies; risk measurement and control are independent of position-taking; reports carry both summary and detail; and policies, assumptions, and models undergo at least annual review and independent audit vetted by the Audit Committee of the Board (ACB). As at 31 December 2026, the rule in force is Chapter VII of the Commercial Banks – Asset Liability Management Directions, 2025 (28 Nov 2025): banks run both TGA and DGA across global positions (separate for currencies >= 5% of global assets or liabilities), bucket into 11 time bands, study non-maturity deposits (NMDs) using at least 3 years of data reviewed annually in Q1, and face a Pillar 2 outlier trigger if banking-book economic value falls by more than 20% of MVE under a 200 bps parallel shock (an economic impact, not an accounting loss). RBI's BCBS-aligned IRRBB framework (17 Feb 2023, consolidated in Chapter V of Forthcoming Instructions Directions, 2025) is issued, coming — its effective date is not yet notified, though banks file quarterly interim ΔEVE/ΔNII returns to RBI DoR. Once effective, it will replace TGA/DGA with 6 shock scenarios for ΔEVE (INR: 250 bps parallel, 300 bps short, 200 bps long) and 2 parallel shocks for ΔNII, 19 time buckets, 10-year NMD studies with core/maturity caps (90%/5y, 70%/4.5y, 50%/4y), a stricter 15% of Tier 1 capital outlier test, and expanded Pillar 3 disclosures.

| Perfect zero risk is impossible: a bank cannot eliminate both economic value risk and earnings volatility at the same time. Locking capital into long fixed-rate G-secs stabilises earnings but creates EV risk; investing overnight stabilises EV but makes earnings fluctuate with market rates. Managing IRR is always a deliberate trade-off within Board limits. | SRP98 para 98.28 fn 4 | 2 |
| On-balance-sheet strategies: ALCO forms a view on future interest rates and steers the balance sheet by changing the maturity profile and mix of incremental assets and liabilities — shortening or lengthening investment maturities, adjusting deposit tenors (fixed vs floating, retail vs wholesale), and altering loan repricing terms. | ALM25 para 26(1)–(2) | 3 |
| Banking-book HTM securities cannot be actively traded: HTM securities are held to collect contractual cash flows (SPPI criterion) and are carried at cost (not MTM). Annual sales from HTM are capped at 5% of the opening carrying value of the HTM portfolio; any sale beyond 5% requires prior approval from RBI DoS (exceptions include RBI OMO/GSAP, government buybacks/switches, credit downgrade/default of non-SLR, resolution plans). | INV25 paras 35, 48, 70, 71 | 4 |
| AFS for ALM flexibility; strict reclassification: debt securities acquired for ALM where the bank needs flexibility to sell routinely before maturity must be placed in AFS, not HTM. Reclassifying between HTM, AFS, and FVTPL is rare and requires Board approval plus prior RBI DoS approval. | INV25 paras 39, FAQ 5, 66 | 5 |
| Defensive vs aggressive gap management: a defensive (risk-averse) bank aims for a near-matched book (small gap near zero) so NII stays steady regardless of rate moves. An aggressive bank deliberately opens or widens a positive gap (RSA > RSL, asset-sensitive) when it expects rates to rise, and widens a negative gap (RSL > RSA, liability-sensitive) when it expects rates to fall. | ALM25 paras 26(2), 255; SRP98 para 98.28 | 6 |
| 2×2 Strategy Matrix (expected rate move × gap sign): (a) Rates expected to rise + Negative gap (RSL > RSA) → bank loses NII if unhedged → defend: shift assets to shorter maturity / floating rate, lengthen fixed deposits, or enter a pay-fixed / receive-floating IRS (or sell IRF / buy cap). (b) Rates expected to rise + Positive gap (RSA > RSL) → bank gains NII → hold or widen if aggressive. (c) Rates expected to fall + Positive gap (RSA > RSL) → bank loses NII → defend: lock in longer fixed-rate assets/bonds, shorten deposit maturities, or enter a receive-fixed / pay-floating IRS (or buy IRF / buy floor). (d) Rates expected to fall + Negative gap (RSL > RSA) → bank gains NII → hold or widen if aggressive. | ALM25 paras 26(1)–(2), 255; IRD25 para 2.1(xviii) | 7 |
| Off-balance-sheet hedging defined (Rupee IRD Directions 2025): effective 1 March 2026, hedging is defined as undertaking a derivative transaction to reduce Rupee interest rate risk at the balance sheet level, portfolio level, or individual asset or liability level. | IRD25 paras 1.3, 2.1(xviii) | 8 |
| Off-balance-sheet hedging instruments: Scheduled Banks (eligible as market-makers and non-retail users) may hedge IRR using OTC Interest Rate Swaps (IRS), Forward Rate Agreements (FRAs), exchange-traded Interest Rate Futures (IRFs) (including money market futures), Interest Rate Options (caps, floors, collars), and swaptions. | IRD25 paras 2.1(ix)–(xvi), 4.1, 5.1(i), 5.3 | 9 |
| IRS hedging an HTM bond does not taint HTM: hedging a fixed-rate HTM bond with a fixed-to-floating IRS (or swapping a floating-rate note to fixed) is treated as a separate transaction; the bond remains in HTM, and the derivative follows ICAI's Guidance Note on Accounting for Derivative Contracts. | INV25 FAQ 17, para 109 | 10 |
