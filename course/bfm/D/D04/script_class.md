## Chapter 3 · Haircut, legal costs, guarantee cover, and interest suspense

N044 · Chapter three. First, quick recall: if an export account is sub-standard with fifty per cent E-C-G-C cover, do you deduct the cover?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Haircut, cover, and interest suspense" · 2 H: 3 s


N045 · No. Sub-standard fifteen per cent applies without any allowance for E-C-G-C cover or security. Cover enters once the account is doubtful or loss.
> 1 N: "Sub-standard: no ECGC/security deduction ✓" [F9]


N046 · Now meet our tougher case: Deccan Precision Works, an M-S-M-E loan in Doubtful-two with seventy-five per cent C-G-T-M-S-E cover.

> 1 N: "Gross ₹60L · Suspense ₹4L · Collateral ₹20L"


N048 · Many of you are thinking: count the four lakh Interest Suspense as part of our provision. Stop right there.
> 1 C: trap card "WRONG: Interest Suspense = provision · RIGHT: deduct from balance first" [F15]

N052 · Notice that trap: never apply C-G-T-M-S-E or E-C-G-C cover to the gross sixty lakh. Apply it only to the unsecured shortfall.

## Chapter 4 · After NPA: restructuring, IBC, and NARCL

N067 · Chapter four. First, recall: once a normal, non-restructured N-P-A pays part of its overdue interest, can you upgrade it to standard?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · After NPA: restructuring, IBC, NARCL" · 2 S: road from branch → Restructuring → IBC/NCLT → NARCL/ARC · 3 H: 3 s


N068 · Never. A normal N-P-A upgrades only when the borrower clears the entire arrears of interest and principal across all facilities.
> 1 N: "Normal upgrade: 100% arrears across all facilities ✓" [F20]


N069 · Now suppose the borrower cannot pay everything, so the bank restructures the loan under the twenty twenty-five Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions.
> 1 C: card "Road 1 · Restructuring (RSA Directions, 2025)" [F21]


N070 · First rule: a standard account is immediately downgraded to sub-standard upon restructuring; an N-P-A stays in its existing category and keeps ageing.
> 1 N: "Standard → immediate Sub-standard on restructuring" [F21]


N071 · When can a restructured account upgrade to standard? Not on day one of payment. It must pass the monitoring period with zero default.
> 1 C: card "Monitoring period: 10% of principal + capitalised interest repaid · min 1-year floor" [F22]


N072 · Monitoring period runs until at least ten per cent of outstanding principal plus capitalised interest is repaid, with a strict one-year floor.
> 1 B: underline "10% repaid · min 1 year" [F22]


N073 · For large exposures of one hundred crore or more, the facilities also need an investment-grade rating—triple-B-minus or better—at upgrade.
> 1 C: card "≥ ₹100 cr: 1 CRA (BBB- or better) · ≥ ₹500 cr: 2 CRAs" [F24]


N074 · One rating from one hundred crore, two ratings from five hundred crore. And M-S-M-E accounts below twenty-five crore follow a one-year specified period.
> 1 N: "MSME < ₹25 cr: 1-yr specified period (no overdue > 30 days)" [F23]


N075 · Specified period for others is twenty per cent repaid. Default before twenty per cent triggers a fresh plan and fifteen per cent extra provision.
> 1 C: card "Specified period = 20% repaid · post-upgrade default → fresh RP + 15% extra prov" [F25]


N076 · What if restructuring fails? Road two is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, twenty sixteen—the I-B-C.
> 1 C: card "Road 2 · IBC 2016 (CIRP before NCLT)" [F27]


N077 · Three parties may trigger C-I-R-P before the N-C-L-T: a financial creditor under section seven, operational creditor under section nine, or corporate debtor under section ten.
> 1 N: "Who files: FC (s. 7) · OC (s. 9) · CD (s. 10) · Default ≥ ₹1 cr" [F27]


N078 · On admission—targeted within fourteen days—section fourteen imposes a moratorium, halting recovery suits, asset sales, and SARFAESI enforcement.
> 1 C: card "s. 14 Moratorium: halts suits, asset transfers, SARFAESI" [F28, F30]


N079 · Control shifts from promoters to an Insolvency Professional and the Committee of Creditors, made of financial creditors—a true creditor-in-control model.
> 1 N: "Creditors in control: IRP/RP + CoC (financial creditors)" [F30]


N080 · Under section twelve, C-I-R-P runs for one hundred eighty days, with a mandatory three hundred thirty-day upper cap including extensions and litigation, else liquidation.
> 1 C: card "s. 12(3): 180 days · mandatory 330-day cap → else liquidation" [F29]


N081 · Note the twenty twenty-six I-B-C Amendment Act: it restricts withdrawals, extends C-o-C oversight into liquidation, and adds a creditor-initiated resolution route.
> 1 N: "IBC Amendment Act, 2026: CoC supervises liquidation · CIIRP" [F31]


N082 · Road three: selling legacy N-P-As above five hundred crore to N-A-R-C-L, India's bad bank, targeting about two lakh crore of stressed loans.
> 1 C: card "Road 3 · NARCL (PSBs 51%) + IDRCL (PSBs max 49%)" [F32, F34]


N083 · Public sector banks hold fifty-one per cent in N-A-R-C-L, which acquires the loans; I-D-R-C-L, majority private-lender owned, manages turnaround and recovery.
> 1 N: "NARCL acquires · IDRCL resolves" [F34]


N084 · How does N-A-R-C-L pay banks? Fifteen per cent in cash, and eighty-five per cent in tradable Security Receipts.
> 1 B: box "15% Cash + 85% Security Receipts (SRs)" [F32]


N085 · What separates N-A-R-C-L from a regular A-R-C? A sovereign Government of India guarantee up to thirty thousand six hundred crore rupees, valid five years.
> 1 C: card "GoI Guarantee: ₹30,600 cr · 5 yrs · covers SR shortfall on resolution/liquidation" [F33]


N086 · Regular A-R-Cs under twenty twenty-five Directions need three hundred crore net owned fund, five to eight years to realise, and no government guarantee.
> 1 C: card "NARCL vs Regular ARC: NOF ₹300 cr · 5–8 yr realisation · NO GoI guarantee" [F35] · 2 M: map node "Asset quality: after NPA"


N087 · Pause check for chapter four. Two quick questions.
> 1 S: pause card "Ch 4 Check (2 Qs)"


N088 · One: which I-B-C section lets a bank, as financial creditor, file for C-I-R-P, and what is the mandatory outer day cap?
