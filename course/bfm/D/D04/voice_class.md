B spent one year in sub-standard, then eighteen months in doubtful, so it sits in Doubtful-two, one to three years. C is a loss asset: identified by external auditors as uncollectible and not yet written off. Question two: provision for Borrower A. First, quick recall: if an export account is sub-standard with fifty per cent E-C-G-C cover, do you deduct the cover? Now meet our tougher case: Deccan Precision Works, an M-S-M-E loan in Doubtful-two with seventy-five per cent C-G-T-M-S-E cover. Gross balance is sixty lakh; Interest Suspense is four lakh. Fifty-six minus fourteen leaves forty-two lakh unsecured shortfall. Notice that trap: never apply C-G-T-M-S-E or E-C-G-C cover to the gross sixty lakh. Apply it only to the unsecured shortfall. Total provision: ten point five plus five point six equals sixteen point one zero lakh rupees. Now watch the loss-asset twist. Suppose the collateral after haircut and legal costs drops to just four lakh rupees. Ten per cent of the fifty-six lakh net balance is five point six lakh. Four lakh is below ten per cent. With valid seventy-five per cent C-G-T-M-S-E cover on fifty-six lakh—forty-two lakh covered—you provide one hundred per cent on the remaining fourteen lakh. One vital warning: never use Loss Given Default, or L-G-D, in an I-R-A-C-P provisioning sum. Secured portion eighteen lakh at twenty-five per cent for Doubtful-one equals four point five lakh. Total provision: nineteen point five zero lakh. If you got twenty point five lakh, you forgot to deduct Interest Suspense first. One thing to remember: suspense first, security second, cover third. Chapter four. First, recall: once a normal, non-restructured N-P-A pays part of its overdue interest, can you upgrade it to standard? Never. A normal N-P-A upgrades only when the borrower clears the entire arrears of interest and principal across all facilities. Now suppose the borrower cannot pay everything, so the bank restructures the loan under the twenty twenty-five Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions. First rule: a standard account is immediately downgraded to sub-standard upon restructuring; an N-P-A stays in its existing category and keeps ageing. When can a restructured account upgrade to standard? Not on day one of payment. It must pass the monitoring period with zero default. Monitoring period runs until at least ten per cent of outstanding principal plus capitalised interest is repaid, with a strict one-year floor. For large exposures of one hundred crore or more, the facilities also need an investment-grade rating—triple-B-minus or better—at upgrade. One rating from one hundred crore, two ratings from five hundred crore. And M-S-M-E accounts below twenty-five crore follow a one-year specified period.

Specified period for others is twenty per cent repaid. Default before twenty per cent triggers a fresh plan and fifteen per cent extra provision. What if restructuring fails? Road two is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, twenty sixteen—the I-B-C. Three parties may trigger C-I-R-P before the N-C-L-T: a financial creditor under section seven, operational creditor under section nine, or corporate debtor under section ten. On admission—targeted within fourteen days—section fourteen imposes a moratorium, halting recovery suits, asset sales, and SARFAESI enforcement. Control shifts from promoters to an Insolvency Professional and the Committee of Creditors, made of financial creditors—a true creditor-in-control model. Under section twelve, C-I-R-P runs for one hundred eighty days, with a mandatory three hundred thirty-day upper cap including extensions and litigation, else liquidation. Note the twenty twenty-six I-B-C Amendment Act: it restricts withdrawals, extends C-o-C oversight into liquidation, and adds a creditor-initiated resolution route. Road three: selling legacy N-P-As above five hundred crore to N-A-R-C-L, India's bad bank, targeting about two lakh crore of stressed loans. Public sector banks hold fifty-one per cent in N-A-R-C-L, which acquires the loans; I-D-R-C-L, majority private-lender owned, manages turnaround and recovery. How does N-A-R-C-L pay banks? Fifteen per cent in cash, and eighty-five per cent in tradable Security Receipts. What separates N-A-R-C-L from a regular A-R-C? A sovereign Government of India guarantee up to thirty thousand six hundred crore rupees, valid five years. Regular A-R-Cs under twenty twenty-five Directions need three hundred crore net owned fund, five to eight years to realise, and no government guarantee. Pause check for chapter four. Two quick questions. One: which I-B-C section lets a bank, as financial creditor, file for C-I-R-P, and what is the mandatory outer day cap? Section seven for a financial creditor, and three hundred thirty days including extensions and legal proceedings under section twelve. Two: in the N-A-R-C-L structure, who holds majority ownership in N-A-R-C-L versus I-D-R-C-L? Public sector banks hold fifty-one per cent in N-A-R-C-L, while private-sector lenders hold the majority in I-D-R-C-L. Mixed check before we close. A Doubtful-two loan has Interest Suspense and E-C-G-C cover. Which is deducted first, and can it upgrade after one instalment? Deduct Interest Suspense first, then realisable security, then E-C-G-C cover. And it never upgrades on one instalment—entire arrears must be cleared. Now look at the blank balance-sheet map.
