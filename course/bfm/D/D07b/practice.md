Part A · Multiple-Choice Questions (Concept, Comparison, Exception & Case)
1. In the RAROC formula, which two errors are the commonest mistakes made by candidates?
(a) Subtracting operating costs and dividing by economic capital
(b) Omitting expected loss from the numerator and dividing by total loan amount instead of economic capital
(c) Using after-tax profit and comparing RAROC with the hurdle rate
(d) Measuring unexpected loss over a one-year horizon at a chosen confidence level
2. Why is Return on Equity (ROE) considered an inadequate stand-alone measure of bank performance compared with RAROC?
(a) ROE requires confidential internal economic capital models that analysts cannot access
(b) ROE cannot be computed from audited bank balance sheets and profit-and-loss accounts
(c) ROE is not risk-sensitive and can be artificially inflated by higher financial leverage
(d) ROE already deducts the full opportunity cost of equity capital before showing net income
3. Under RBI's Interest Rate on Advances Directions (updated 1 October 2025), which statement on loan pricing and cost of equity is CORRECT?
(a) Credit risk premium under MCLR ignores expected losses and depends solely on loan tenor
(b) Under EBLR, banks may raise an existing borrower's credit risk premium every quarter regardless of credit quality
(c) Banks are prohibited from using CAPM to estimate their cost of equity in lending benchmarks
(d) Credit risk premium reflects default risk based on a rating model considering expected losses and collaterals, and banks may use CAPM for cost of equity
4. Two trading desks at an illustrative bank each earn ₹8 crore net profit in the same year, using the same 99% confidence level. Desk Alpha holds lower volatility-scaled exposure than Desk Beta. Under RAPM:
(a) Both desks performed equally because their accounting profit is identical
(b) Desk Beta outperformed Desk Alpha because higher volatility always creates higher shareholder value
(c) Desk Alpha achieved higher RAPM because it generated the same profit using less risk capital
(d) RAPM cannot compare two trading desks unless they trade the exact same asset class
5. How does Earnings at Risk (EaR) under RBI's ALM Directions, 2025 differ from RAROC?
(a) EaR measures potential 1-year Net Interest Income loss in rupees from interest-rate scenarios, whereas RAROC measures percentage risk-adjusted return on allocated economic capital across risks
(b) EaR measures credit default loss over 10 years, whereas RAROC measures only 1-day trading-desk VaR
(c) EaR is expressed as a percentage of Tier 1 capital, whereas RAROC is expressed strictly in rupees
(d) EaR and RAROC are two names for the same Economic Value Added calculation

   8. CAPM, WACC & EVA (No Options): An illustrative bank has a risk-free rate of 6.50%, expected market return of 12.50%, and equity beta of 1.10. Its ₹800.00 crore invested capital is funded 50% by equity and 50% by debt carrying an 8.00% pre-tax cost at a 25.00% corporate tax rate. If its Net Operating Profit After Tax (NOPAT) is ₹90.00 crore, compute its Cost of Equity (CAPM), WACC (%), and EVA (₹ crore).
Answers (with One-Line Reasons)
   1. b — Expected loss must be subtracted in the numerator and economic capital (not loan size) belongs in the denominator [F5, F6, F7].
   2. c — ROE ignores asset risk and can be boosted artificially by higher financial leverage without creating economic value [F11].
   3. d — RBI's Interest Rate on Advances Directions base credit risk premium on rating models/EL/collaterals and explicitly allow CAPM for cost of equity [F15, F22].
   4. c — Lower volatility-scaled exposure requires less risk capital, producing higher RAPM for the same rupee profit [F17, F18, F19].
   5. a — EaR tracks 1-year NII sensitivity to interest-rate shocks under ALM, whereas RAROC measures risk-adjusted return on economic capital [F27, F28].
   6. 20.00% · Accept — (9.00 − 6.20 − 0.40) ÷ 12.00 × 100 = 2.40 ÷ 12.00 × 100 = 20.00%, which exceeds the 18.00% hurdle rate [F5, F9].
   7. Risk Capital = ₹19.80 crore · RAPM = 22.73% — Risk Capital = 1.65 × 120 × 10% = ₹19.80 crore; RAPM = 4.50 ÷ 19.80 × 100 = 22.7273% → 22.73% [F18, F19, F20].
   8. Ke = 13.10% · WACC = 9.55% · EVA = +₹13.60 crore — Ke = 6.50 + 1.10 × 6.00 = 13.10%; WACC = 0.50 × 13.10 + 0.50 × 8.00 × 0.75 = 9.55%; EVA = 90.00 − (800 × 9.55%) = +₹13.60 crore [F21, F23, F24].
