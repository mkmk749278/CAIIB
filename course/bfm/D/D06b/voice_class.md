From two classes back: if rate-sensitive liabilities exceed rate-sensitive assets, is the gap positive or negative? Negative. Chapter one. Strategies for controlling interest rate risk. First, let's break a common myth. Many of you think a safe bank should push its interest rate gap down to exact zero. That is impossible. Locking funds long stabilises earnings yet exposes economic value; staying overnight does the reverse. So banks never chase zero risk. They choose a deliberate trade-off inside Board-approved limits. To steer the gap, banks use two toolkits: on the balance sheet, and off it. On the balance sheet, A-L-C-O shifts the maturity and repricing mix of loans, deposits, and investments. Wait. Can treasury actively trade banking-book bonds every month to adjust duration? No. Held-to-maturity securities are carried at cost to collect cash flows, not marked to market. Because balance-sheet shifts move slowly, banks also hedge off the balance sheet with Rupee interest rate derivatives. Under R-B-I's Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives Directions, effective March twenty twenty-six, hedging covers the balance sheet, portfolio, or individual items. Banks use interest rate swaps, forward rate agreements, exchange-traded interest rate futures, caps, floors, collars, and swaptions. Hedging an H-T-M bond with an interest rate swap does not taint its H-T-M classification. Now, write this down — a major trap. On nineteen October twenty twenty-six, R-B-I deleted paragraph thirty-seven of the Miscellaneous Directions immediately. Next: defensive versus aggressive management. A defensive bank keeps its gap near zero so earnings stay steady either way. An aggressive bank takes a view: expecting rates up, it widens a positive gap; expecting rates down, a negative gap. Chapter two. Controls and sound practices. Recall first: can a bank actively trade H-T-M bonds to manage duration? No, non-exempt sales are capped at five per cent. Now let's climb the governance ladder. R-B-I's twenty twenty-five A-L-M Directions set four tiers: the Board, Risk Management Committee, A-L-C-O, and the A-L-M Support Group. The Board owns ultimate oversight. It approves overall strategy, policies, risk appetite, and internal prudential limits. Specifically, the Board or Risk Management Committee sets limits on Earnings at Risk and Market Value of Equity volatility. A-L-C-O, headed by the C-E-O, C-M-D, or Executive Director, executes strategy and ensures limit compliance. A-L-C-O also gives prior approval for duration gap yields, assumptions, bucketing, and behavioural studies. Now a key question. Should the treasury desk taking interest rate positions also measure and report its own risk? Never. Many assume dealers know their book best, so they should score it. That creates a direct conflict of interest. Sound practice separates trading, settlement, monitoring, and accounting. The independent risk unit reports directly to senior management and the Board. Three more controls complete the sound-practices card. First: pre-approval. New products need a pre-acquisition review and test phase. Major hedging initiatives also need prior approval, with dedicated risk limits for derivatives. Second: escalation. Positions exceeding, or likely to exceed, Board limits must be escalated promptly with clear corrective rules. Third: reporting and review. Board reports must carry both aggregate summaries and supporting detail — limits, assumptions, stress tests, and audits. [seriously] One thing to remember from chapter two: the Board sets strategy and limits, A-L-C-O executes, and an independent function measures and audits. Chapter three. R-B-I guidelines on Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book. Recall: how often must material I-R-R assumptions be reviewed? At least annually. Now let's define I-R-R-B-B clearly. I-R-R-B-B is the current or prospective risk to capital and earnings from adverse rate moves affecting the banking book. Earnings perspective tracks short-term Net Interest Income or E-a-R; economic value tracks long-term present value of equity. Now let's clear up a big confusion. Some classes claim Basel's six-shock I-R-R-B-B framework is already mandatory in India. Not yet. Here is the exact position as at thirty-one December twenty twenty-six. In force today is Chapter Seven of R-B-I's twenty twenty-five Asset Liability Management Directions. Banks must run both Traditional Gap Analysis and Duration Gap Analysis globally, and separately for currencies at five per cent or more. Positions go into eleven time buckets. Behavioural studies for non-maturity deposits use at least three years of data, reviewed in quarter one. Today's standardised shock is two hundred basis points parallel. Under Pillar Two, a bank is an outlier if banking-book M-V-E falls over twenty per cent. That drop in M-V-E is an economic impact, not an accounting loss, because the banking book is not marked to market. Let's test this on our calculator at Apex Bank. Shock is plus two hundred basis points, or zero point zero two. Step one: weight liabilities. Nine thousand divided by ten thousand is zero point nine zero; times one point five zero equals one point three five. Step two: modified duration gap. Two point five zero minus one point three five equals plus one point one five years. Step three: change in equity. One point one five times ten thousand times zero point zero two equals minus two hundred thirty crore. Step four: two hundred thirty divided by one thousand times one hundred equals twenty-three per cent. Above twenty per cent — outlier. Common trap: skipping the zero point nine zero weight gives duration gap one, a twenty per cent drop, missing the outlier trigger. Your turn. Zero point seven five times ten thousand times zero point zero two is one hundred fifty crore. Divided by one thousand: fifteen per cent. Safe.
