## D01 · The bank's balance sheet and P&L (split proposed: D01 + D01b)
- **Goal**: read a bank's balance sheet and P&L in the Third Schedule forms: what sits where, why each item behaves
  as it does, and which head an income or expense goes under.
- **Sub-topics**: D1.1 (all). (D1.2, D1.3 and C7 move to D01b if the split is accepted.)
- **Question kinds**: define (each item; contingent liability) · purpose (why the forms are fixed by law) · who/when
  (s. 29: who prepares, which form) · compare (sources vs uses; call vs notice vs term money; interest earned vs other
  income; interest expended vs operating expenses) · exception (overdue deposits stay deposits; refinance is a
  borrowing; debentures are investments) · apply to a case (place ten items; classify ten P&L lines).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: AFM, final accounts of banking companies (Form A/B). Refresher: the two forms on one screen.
- **Builds on / leads to**: B13, C01 / D01b, D02, D05.
- **Evidence**: D-ALM: sources and applications of funds (Dec 2023); FP1 Q44, Q47, Q53.
- **Opening**: a bank's annual report on a phone screen: "Where does the money come from and where does it go?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think deposits are assets of the bank; others put RBI refinance under deposits.
- **Picture**: two shelves: capital, reserves, deposits, borrowings, other liabilities (left); cash and RBI balances,
  bank balances and call money, investments, advances, fixed assets, other assets (right); contingent items as a
  dotted shelf below (off the balance sheet).
- **Chapters**: 1 The law and the forms: s. 29, Form A and Form B, the schedule map for both statements (~3 min) ·
  2 Each item and why it behaves so: cost, stability, risk, earning or non-earning; call/notice/term money; contingent
  items (~4 min) · 3 Placement traps (~2 min) · 4 The P&L heads drill (~3 min). ~12–13 min.
- **Numericals**: none (classification drills instead).
- **Traps**: sources vs uses; overdue term deposit (still a deposit); refinance from NABARD/EXIM and RBI borrowing
  (borrowings); bills payable, interest accrued, inter-office adjustment (other liabilities); debentures (investments,
  not advances); advances shown by security; provisions and contingencies are not operating expenses.
- **Practice**: 3 (one placement, one P&L head, one statement-type).
- **Map nodes**: Components, Form A/B, P&L heads.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: (1) one-screen map of the Third Schedule: s. 29, Form A, Form B, schedule
  numbers for balance sheet and P&L; (2) P&L classification drill (interest earned vs other income; interest expended
  vs operating expenses; provisions kept apart); (3) placement traps card (overdue deposits, refinance, bills payable,
  debentures, advances by security); (4) call/notice/term money by tenor, one line, link C02; (5) one line per item on
  why it behaves as it does; (6) contingent (off-balance-sheet) items as part of the picture, link B06. **6 additions.**
- **Fallback if no split**: chapters 1–3 here shrink to ~6 min (schedule map on the PDF page *and* on screen for
  30 s; P&L drill cut to five lines) and D01b's content follows as chapters 4–7 (~22 min total): too long; we
  recommend the split.
- **Facts to verify**: BR Act 1949 s. 29 and Third Schedule (current forms, schedule numbers and notes); RBI master
  direction on financial statements – presentation and disclosures (P&L heads; where deposit insurance premium,
  depreciation, profit on sale/revaluation of investments go); call/notice/term money tenors in RBI money-market
  directions.

## D01b · ALM, ALCO, treasury and transfer pricing (proposed new video, from D01)
- **Goal**: explain what ALM is for and why it matters now, how ALCO and the treasury run it under board-approved
  policies, and split a bank's margin with funds transfer pricing.
- **Sub-topics**: D1.2, D1.3, C7.1, C7.2 (closes partial row), C7.4, C7.5 (closes partial row).
- **Question kinds**: define (ALM; economic equity ratio; FTP) · purpose (objectives of ALM; why FTP) · who/when
  (ALCO members and decisions; board approves policies, reviewed every year; treasury executes) · compare (price
  matching vs liquidity matching; ALCO decides vs board approves; positive vs negative gap) · exception (why a bank
  does not close every mismatch) · calculate (three-way FTP split) · apply to a case (mixed-risk mini case: which
  risks are present).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: IE&IFS / PPB, how banks earn the spread (NII) and deregulation of interest rates.
  Refresher: "a bank borrows short and lends long" with one picture.
- **Builds on / leads to**: D01, B13, C01, C08 / D05, D06, D06b.
- **Evidence**: D-ALM: ALCO and ALM (Jun 2023 three questions, Jul 2024 ×3); FP1 Q44, Q47, Q53.
- **Opening**: rates fall; borrowers prepay, depositors stay locked in at old rates, and the branch's margin
  shrinks. "Whose job was it to see this coming?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think ALM is only about liquidity; others think the treasury should remove every
  mismatch.
- **Picture**: the two shelves (D01) with ALM as the hub joining assets, liabilities, income and expense; ALCO as a
  meeting table between the shelves; the treasury as the bank's single pool of funds; FTP as three coloured slices of
  one margin.
- **Chapters**: 1 What ALM is and why it matters now: objectives (stable NII/NIM, equity value, risk within limits);
  rate deregulation, new products, embedded options, regulation; price matching vs liquidity matching; ALM as
  co-ordinated balance-sheet management; economic equity ratio; liquidity risk turning into rate risk; gap sign and
  where a fixed-rate item sits (~4 min) · 2 ALCO and the treasury: who sits, what it decides (incl. deposit and loan
  pricing; deposit rates now published in advance each business day); the treasury executes, hedges the residual
  risk and gives pricing input; derivatives in ALM in one picture (IRS for a repricing gap, FRA/IRF for a future gap,
  currency swap for a foreign-currency mismatch; products taught in C08) (~4 min) · 3 The policy environment: the five
  policies (ALM, investment, liquidity, derivatives, transfer pricing) and one line each on contents; board approval,
  annual review; FIMMDA/FEDAI codes (~2 min) · 4 Transfer pricing: components of the transfer rate; worked three-way
  split; who carries which risk after FTP; mixed-risk mini case (~4 min). ~14 min.
- **Numericals**: 1 (three-way FTP split: deposit unit, lending unit, treasury; own numbers).
- **Traps**: ALCO decides vs board approves; FTP leaves credit risk with the lending unit and moves rate and
  liquidity risk to the treasury; positive gap = assets more than liabilities in the bucket; "perfect matching" is
  neither possible nor desirable (the term premium is income).
- **Practice**: 3 (one FTP split, one "who decides", one mini case).
- **Map nodes**: ALM (objectives, why now), ALCO, Treasury, Policies, FTP.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: (1) "why ALM matters now" list; (2) price matching vs liquidity matching;
  (3) ALM objectives in one picture; (4) ALM as the hub of asset, liability, income and expense decisions (syllabus
  D1.3 wording); (5) economic equity ratio (definition only); (6) mixed-risk mini case (maturity mismatch, embedded
  options, rate cut squeezing NIM); (7) liquidity risk becoming rate risk; (8) gap-sign card; (9) bucket placement of a
  fixed-rate item; (10) treasury's ALM role: fund pool, executes ALCO decisions, residual-risk hedging, pricing input;
  (11) why not eliminate every mismatch; (12) derivatives in ALM (IRS, FRA/IRF, currency swaps; link C08);
  (13) worked three-way FTP split; (14) components of the transfer rate (market rate, hedging cost, cost of
  reserves); (15) "who bears which risk after FTP" trap; (16) the five policies with one line of contents each;
  (17) board approval and annual review; (18) FIMMDA/FEDAI codes; (19) daily advance publication of deposit rates
  (from 1 Oct 2026; current rule, light). **19 additions.**
- **Facts to verify**: ALM Directions 2025 (ALM purpose, ALCO composition and role, board-approved ALM/liquidity
  policy, time buckets); investment-portfolio directions (board-approved investment policy); derivative directions
  (board-approved policy, bank's own balance-sheet hedging); interest rate on deposits directions as amended (daily
  advance publication from 1 Oct 2026; same rate across branches; bulk-deposit differential); FTP: any text in the ALM

## D07 · Profit planning, risk aggregation and capital allocation (split proposed: D07 + D07b)
- **Goal**: explain how a bank plans profit at an acceptable risk, why it holds capital for unexpected loss, and how
  capital is aggregated and allocated across business lines.
- **Sub-topics**: D6.1, D6.2 (and the concept half of D6.3: economic vs regulatory capital).
- **Question kinds**: define (economic capital, EL, UL, hurdle rate) · purpose (why profit planning is not profit
  maximisation; why capital by business line) · compare (EL vs UL; economic vs regulatory capital; accounting profit
  vs value created) · exception (concentration raises capital even when profit is unchanged) · apply to a case (which
  of the six variables moved).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: AFM, ratio analysis (ROA, ROE) and cost of capital; IE&IFS, banking reforms since 1991.
- **Builds on / leads to**: B04 (ICAAP), B05, B10 (EL, UL) / D07b.
- **Evidence**: D-RAROC (Jun 2023, Jul 2024 definition, Jun 2025); profit-planning statements (syllabus).
- **Opening**: two branches report the same profit; one did it with treasury gains in a falling-rate year. "Which
  profit would you plan on next year?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think the plan is maximum profit; others think regulatory capital and economic capital are
  the same number.
- **Picture**: six levers (three income: interest, fees, treasury; three expense: interest, staff, other operating)
  on the P&L shelf from D01; a loss curve with EL (covered by pricing/provisions) and UL (covered by capital).
- **Chapters**: 1 Profit planning: the six variables pulling against each other; profit at acceptable risk;
  treasury income is volatile; heavy G-sec holding lowers yield; NPAs hit income and capital (~4 min) · 2 The
  landscape card: post-1991 reforms (Narasimham), differentiated banks (SFBs, payments banks), DFIs incl. NaBFID,
  PSB mergers (~2 min) · 3 EL vs UL; economic vs regulatory capital; risk measured by variability (~3 min) · 4 Risk
  aggregation and capital allocation by business line; concentration/correlation effect; link to ICAAP (~3 min).
  ~12 min.
- **Numericals**: 1 (simple aggregation: two business lines' capital with and without diversification, numbers
  given; no statistics beyond the basic calculator).
- **Traps**: accounting profit ≠ value created; EL is not covered by capital; NaBFID takes no public deposits (verify).
- **Practice**: 3.
- **Map nodes**: Profit planning, EL/UL, economic capital, aggregation.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: (1) six-variable picture; (2) "profit at acceptable risk" and treasury-income
  volatility; (3) G-sec yield and the NPA double hit; (4) landscape card (reforms, SFB/PB, DFIs/NaBFID, mergers);
  (5) EL vs UL picture with provisions vs capital; (6) concentration/correlation effect; (7) link to ICAAP (B04).

- **Fallback if no split**: one ~22-minute L+N video; too long. We recommend the split.

  1998); BCBS ICAAP/aggregation texts (definitions only).

## D07b · RAROC, risk-adjusted performance and EVA: the sums (proposed new video, from D07)
- **Goal**: compute RAROC and decide against the hurdle rate; compare two desks with equal profit but different
  risk; compute EVA with a CAPM cost of equity and a weighted cost of capital.
- **Sub-topics**: D6.3.
- **Question kinds**: define (RAROC, RAPM, EVA, hurdle rate) · compare (ROA/ROE vs RAROC; EaR vs RAROC) · calculate
  (RAROC; project choice; two-desk RAPM; CAPM; WACC; EVA) · apply to a case (accept or reject; price a loan so it
  clears the hurdle).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: AFM, CAPM and WACC. Refresher: the CAPM line and a WACC weight table in 30 seconds.
- **Builds on / leads to**: D07, B03 (SD), B09 (VaR multiple) / DR.
- **Evidence**: D-RAROC (Jun 2023, Jul 2024 definition, Jun 2025); FP2 Q42 (two projects), Q43.
- **Opening**: two loans with the same interest rate but different risk. Which one earns more for the bank?
- **Misconceptions**: some divide by the loan amount instead of economic capital; others forget expected loss.
- **Picture**: a scale with return on one side and capital at risk on the other (from D07's loss curve).
- **Chapters**: 1 The three-step method (measure risk, allocate capital at a confidence level and horizon, measure
  performance) and RAROC = (revenue − cost − EL) ÷ economic capital; hurdle rate; ROA/ROE do not adjust for risk
  (~4 min) · 2 Project choice and risk-based loan pricing (pricing that ignores EL and the capital charge erodes
  capital) (~3 min) · 3 Two desks, equal profit, different volatility: risk capital as a given multiple × notional ×
  volatility (~3 min) · 4 EVA = profit after tax − capital × cost of capital, with CAPM and WACC; EaR vs RAROC in one
  line (~4 min). ~14 min.
- **Numericals**: 5 (RAROC; project choice; two-desk RAPM; CAPM + WACC; EVA). The confidence multiplier (z for 99%,
  etc.) is always given in the question: no scientific calculator is allowed. Keys shown on the 8-function calculator.
- **Traps**: denominator; EL subtracted; hurdle comparison; equal profit ≠ equal performance; EVA uses capital ×
  cost of capital, not interest only.
- **Practice**: 3, no options.
- **Map nodes**: RAROC, RAPM, EVA.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: (1) three-step RAROC method; (2) two-desk RAPM numerical; (3) EVA numerical
  with CAPM and WACC; (4) ROA/ROE vs RAROC card; (5) EaR vs RAROC line; (6) risk-based loan pricing line. **6
  additions.**
- **Facts to verify**: standard finance definitions (RAROC, CAPM, WACC, EVA); confidence-level multipliers from a
  statistics text (given in questions); no RBI number.
