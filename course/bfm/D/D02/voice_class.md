From last class, who approves the bank's ALM policy, and who executes it? The Board approves, ALCO decides, and the treasury executes. Earlier still, on which shelf of the balance sheet do loans sit? The asset shelf, which is why loan quality is called asset quality. This topic appeared in six of seven recent exam cycles, with twenty-nine recalled questions. Today you will classify any loan, from crop loans to credit cards, and book NPA income right. Easy win first: a term loan EMI is sixty days overdue. Is it NPA today? India's IRACP norms began on the Narasimham Committee recommendations, replacing the old subjective Health Code system. A non-performing asset, or NPA, is an asset, including a leased asset, that has ceased generating income. An amount is overdue if unpaid on the due date fixed by the bank. Term loans and discounted bills turn NPA when principal or interest stays overdue beyond ninety days. Many think strong collateral or a rich guarantor prevents NPA. That is wrong. Classification rests strictly on the record of recovery, never on security or borrower net worth. Classification is borrower-wise, not facility-wise. One failed loan turns every facility and security of that borrower NPA. Narrow exceptions: bills discounted under LC, PACS on-lending, Central Government guarantees, and select deposit-backed loans. Watch two traps. Loans against pledged ornaments or government securities are not exempt, even with full margin. Central Government guaranteed loans turn NPA only on repudiation; State Government guaranteed loans follow ninety days. In a consortium, each bank classifies by its own recovery; if the lead bank withholds your share, it is NPA. Now income recognition: accrual basis applies only to Standard assets; NPAs follow actual cash receipt. Even an overdue Central Government guaranteed loan cannot accrue interest; the exemption never covers income recognition. On turning NPA, reverse all unrealised past interest through P&L, and record future interest in a Memorandum account. Never book interest paid out of a fresh loan to the same borrower; that is evergreening. One thing to remember: record of recovery decides NPA, borrower-wise, and NPA interest is cash-only. Chapter Two: early warning before day ninety. Recall first: where do banks record uncollected interest on an NPA? In a Memorandum account, excluded from Gross Advances. Immediately on default, banks flag stress as Special Mention Accounts, or SMA. For non-revolving loans: SMA-0 is up to thirty days; SMA-1, thirty-one to sixty; SMA-2, sixty-one to ninety. Classic twist for revolving cash credit and overdraft: does CC have an SMA-0 bucket? No. Revolving facilities skip SMA-0 and start at SMA-1 when continuous excess over limit or DP crosses thirty days. SMA covers every loan, including retail loans, except agricultural advances governed by crop seasons. Flagging runs during the automated day-end process, and the classification date is that exact calendar date. Let us work RBI's exact date sum. An EMI due thirty-one March stays unpaid at day-end. Recall from Chapter Two: which weekday is the weekly CRILC default report due? Every Friday by close of business, or the prior working day on a holiday. Back to Raghav's three folders. Long-duration crops, over one year like sugarcane, turn NPA after one overdue crop season. Next, Meena Hardware's cash credit. Refresher: sanctioned limit is the ceiling; drawing power comes from stock minus margin. An OD or CC turns NPA when out of order, meaning any one of three tests fails. Test one: balance stays continuously above limit or drawing power for ninety days. Meena Hardware stayed at nineteen lakh above eighteen lakh DP for one hundred one days, so it is NPA. Many think staying under the limit keeps an OD Standard. Tests two and three break that belief. Out-of-order rules apply to every overdraft, including personal and interest-only overdrafts. Now working-capital irregularity rules. Question two: an ad hoc limit sanctioned one October is still unreviewed on ten April. Status? NPA, because it crossed one hundred eighty days without review or renewal. Question three: an NPA borrower pays all overdue interest but leaves one principal EMI unpaid. Upgrade allowed? No; entire arrears of interest and principal must be cleared across all facilities. One thing to remember: crops use seasons, CC has three tests, unreviewed limits hit one-eighty days, upgrades need full arrears.

Chapter Four: NPA ageing. Quick recall: how old can a stock statement be before drawing power turns irregular? Three months; older than three months makes drawing power irregular. An NPA ages through three categories: sub-standard, doubtful, and loss. Sub-standard means NPA for twelve months or less. After twelve months in sub-standard, it turns doubtful: D1 up to one year, D2 one to three, D3 over three. Loss means loss identified by bank, auditors, or RBI inspection, not yet wholly written off. Normally NPAs step down one rung at a time. Security erosion is the trapdoor that skips rungs. Realisable security below fifty percent of the last assessed value moves the NPA straightaway to Doubtful. Realisable security below ten percent of loan outstanding ignores security and moves it straightaway to Loss. Quick sum: loan outstanding is one hundred lakh; last assessed security was eighty lakh; fresh value is thirty-five lakh. Press thirty-five divided by eighty equals zero point four three seven five, or forty-three point seven five percent: straightaway Doubtful. Against outstanding, thirty-five divided by one hundred is thirty-five percent, above ten percent, so not Loss. Common mistake: dividing by outstanding for the fifty-percent test. Fifty percent uses last assessed security value. Your turn: loan fifty lakh, last security forty lakh, fresh value four lakh. Doubtful or Loss? Press four divided by fifty equals zero point zero eight, or eight percent of outstanding: straightaway Loss. One issued-coming update: RBI issued Expected Credit Loss, or ECL, Directions on twenty-seven April twenty-twenty-six, effective one April twenty-twenty-seven. ECL uses three stages — Stage one twelve-month loss, Stage two lifetime loss past thirty days, Stage three NPA — keeping current NPA rules. On thirty-one December twenty-twenty-six, current IRACP rules still govern; treat ECL strictly as issued, coming. Pause check, Chapter Four. Question one: an NPA is fourteen months old with intact security. Category? Doubtful D1, having crossed twelve months in sub-standard. Question two: are RBI's ECL Directions in force on thirty-one December twenty-twenty-six? No; they take effect on one April twenty-twenty-seven. One thing to remember: sub-standard lasts twelve months, erosion under fifty or ten percent skips steps, ECL starts April twenty-twenty-seven. Final mixed check: paddy loan one season overdue, credit card ninety-five days unpaid, and security at seven percent of loan. Answers: paddy is Standard, needing two seasons; card is NPA; seven percent security is straightaway Loss. Picture the blank Asset Quality branch on our Balance Sheet mind map. Name its three nodes from memory. Income recognition with SNFA, SMA with CRILC, and NPA classes with special cases and erosion. You can now classify any loan under asset classification, compute day-end SMA dates, and apply NPA income rules. Provisioning rates come next class, and restructured-account upgrades follow in the case-study class. Homework tonight: draw the three nodes from memory, and find the SMA-2 date for an EMI due thirty June. Question for next class: once Meena Hardware turns sub-standard, how much profit must the bank set aside on day one? That's how banks draw the line between standard and bad loans. Rest well, and I'll see you in the next class.
