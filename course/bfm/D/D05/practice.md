Part A · No-Options Numericals (Keyed-In Format)
1. A commercial bank has a cumulative 1-year repricing gap of +₹1,200 crore (RSA > RSL). If market interest rates fall by 40 basis points across all maturities, compute the expected change in Net Interest Income (ΔNII, in ₹ crore, with sign, to 2 decimals).
2. An illustrative bank reports Opening Total Assets of ₹11,500 crore and Closing Total Assets of ₹12,500 crore. Gross Interest Earned for the year is ₹1,020 crore, Interest Expended is ₹675 crore, and ₹45 crore of accrued interest on accounts turning NPA remains unrealised and must be reversed. Compute the bank's Net Interest Margin (NIM) under RBI's Statistical Tables / IIBF standard formula (in %, to 2 decimals).
3. In the 0–3 month horizon, a bank reports: 1–28 days RSA = ₹1,450 crore, RSL = ₹1,900 crore; 29 days–3 months RSA = ₹2,300 crore, RSL = ₹1,650 crore. It also holds Cash of ₹250 crore and Current Deposits of ₹400 crore. Compute the cumulative 0–3 month Repricing Gap (in ₹ crore, with sign).

Part C · "Which Source of IRR Is This?" (4-Item Case Set)
Identify the primary source of Interest Rate Risk in each branch/treasury situation:
1. Situation A: A bank funds its 1-year floating corporate loans (linked to 364-day T-Bill yields) using 1-year wholesale deposits (linked to the policy repo rate). Both reprice on the same date, but the T-Bill yield rises 20 bps while the repo-linked deposit rate rises 50 bps.
2. (a) Gap / Mismatch Risk (b) Basis Risk (c) Yield Curve Risk (d) Reinvestment Risk
3. Situation B: Market interest rates jump by 150 bps in six months. Retail customers prematurely close their old 5.5% fixed deposits (paying a small penalty) to re-book at 7.0%, sharply raising the bank's funding cost before contractual maturity.
4. (a) Embedded Option Risk (b) Price Risk (c) Basis Risk (d) Net Interest Position Risk
5. Situation C: A bank funds 10-year fixed-rate infrastructure bonds using 6-month money-market borrowings. During a monetary tightening cycle, 6-month rates rise by 125 bps while 10-year yields remain unchanged, flattening the yield curve and squeezing spreads.
6. (a) Reinvestment Risk (b) Embedded Option Risk (c) Yield Curve Risk (d) Price Risk
7. Situation D: A bank's treasury holds a 7.50% Government Security in its trading book and sells it three years before maturity after market yields climb to 8.25%, realising an immediate capital loss.
8. (a) Reinvestment Risk (b) Basis Risk (c) Price Risk (d) Net Interest Position Risk
________________
Answers (One-Line Reasons)
1. −₹4.80 crore — ΔNII = (+1,200) × (−0.40%) = −₹4.80 crore; a positive (asset-sensitive) gap loses NII when rates fall [F18, F20; numericals Q1].
2. 2.50% — Average Total Assets = (11,500 + 12,500) ÷ 2 = ₹12,000 cr; Net Interest Earned = 1,020 − 45 = ₹975 cr; NII = 975 − 675 = ₹300 cr; NIM = 300 ÷ 12,000 × 100 = 2.50% [F23, F25; numericals Q2].
3. +₹200.00 crore — Cash and Current Deposits are non-sensitive and excluded; cumulative RSA 3,750 − cumulative RSL 3,550 = +₹200 crore [F15, F17; numericals Q3].
4. (b) — Operative ALM Directions 2025 require both TGA and DGA across 11 buckets by earlier of maturity/repricing [F13, F14, F28]; TGA misses basis/options [F27], IRRBB is "issued, coming" [F32], and Cash/Current Deposits are non-sensitive [F15].
5. (b) Basis Risk — Instruments of similar tenor priced on different indices move by unequal magnitudes [F5].
6. (a) Embedded Option Risk — Depositors exercise early-withdrawal (behavioural) options against the bank when rates rise [F6].
7. (c) Yield Curve Risk — Non-parallel flattening across different maturity benchmarks (6-month vs 10-year) erodes spread [F7].
8. (c) Price Risk — Selling a bond prior to maturity after yields rise crystallises a capital loss [F8].
