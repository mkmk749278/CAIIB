Answers with one-line reasons citing [F#] appear at the end.
1. (Define / Compare) Which statement correctly distinguishes the three books of an integrated bank treasury under RBI's 2025 Investment Portfolio and ALM framework? (a) The ALM book holds strategic subsidiary stakes, while the trading book holds mandatory CRR balances (b) The ALM book manages statutory reserves, HQLA, and balance-sheet hedges; the merchant book handles client FX and hedging deals; the trading book holds HFT and proprietary positions marked to market daily (c) The merchant book covers interbank call money, while the trading book covers only HTM government securities (d) All three books are combined into HTM so that mark-to-market volatility never touches profit and loss
2. (Exception / Trap) Under RBI's Commercial Banks – Classification, Valuation and Operation of Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025 (updated 1 October 2026), which of the following positions is NOT permitted for a scheduled commercial bank's treasury? (a) Short-selling a liquid Central Government dated security within the 2% / ₹500 crore security limit (b) Holding equity investments in a wholly owned subsidiary in a separate sui generis category at acquisition cost (c) Taking a short-sale position in listed corporate equity shares on a recognised stock exchange (d) Classifying debt securities held for ALM liquidity flexibility under the AFS category
3. (Apply to a case / Trap) A Bengaluru manufacturing client approaches your AD Category-I bank to remit USD 400,000 towards annual royalty and technical collaboration fees to its Japanese technology partner. How is this transaction classified under FEMA 1999, and what does India's globalisation framework imply? (a) Capital-account transaction; freely permitted because globalisation eliminated all FEMA controls (b) Current-account transaction governed by the FEM (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000; globalisation links markets without removing regulation (c) Capital-account transaction permitted only under the ECB route (d) Prohibited transaction under Schedule I of the Current Account Transactions Rules
4. (Compare / Activity sorting) A treasury dealer borrows USD 2 million for 6 months, converts it at spot to INR, places the rupees in a 6-month money-market instrument, and simultaneously buys USD 6-month forward to lock in a positive spread above funding cost. Which treasury activity is this? (a) Speculation, because the dealer carries full unhedged exchange-rate risk for 6 months (b) Merchant business, because the trade is booked for a corporate exporter's spread (c) Covered interest arbitrage, locking in a near-risk-free return from a forward-rate divergence from parity (d) Strategic subsidiary investment held in the sui generis book
5. (Who / When / How much) An AD Category-I bank holds surplus foreign currency funds in its New York Nostro account. Under RBI's Master Direction – Risk Management and Inter-Bank Dealings (updated 1 October 2026), which overseas deployment is permitted within Board-approved limits? (a) Leaving the surplus idle in the Nostro current account to earn mandatory overnight interest from the correspondent (b) Investing in overseas money-market instruments or foreign sovereign debt with residual maturity under 1 year, rated at least AA(-) by S&P/Fitch or Aa3 by Moody's (c) Investing in 5-year unrated corporate bonds of an overseas private issuer (d) Short-selling US technology equities on an overseas exchange
6. (Purpose / Market developments) Which statement accurately reflects India's globalised treasury market infrastructure and 2025–2026 RBI framework as at 31 December 2026? (a) Interbank Forex Forward contracts settle bilaterally without any central counterparty requirement (b) RBI discontinued the Investment Fluctuation Reserve (IFR) w.e.f. 18 May 2026, raised daily minimum CRR to 99% from 16 October 2026, and constituted the Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets (TCCFM) on 4 November 2026 (c) Commercial banks can deal in FCY/INR only strictly between 9:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. IST, with no beyond-hours dealing allowed (d) Credit Default Swaps (CDS) and Total Return Swaps (TRS) on corporate bonds remain prohibited under RBI directions
7. (Keyed-in numerical — Overseas FC Borrowing Ceiling) An Indian AD Category-I bank has unimpaired Tier I capital of ₹6,000 crore. It currently has ₹2,400 crore in general overseas foreign-currency borrowings and ₹1,000 crore in overseas borrowings specifically for financing export credit. What is the maximum additional general overseas foreign-currency borrowing (in ₹ crore) it can raise without prior RBI approval?
8. (Keyed-in numerical — Covered Interest Arbitrage) A treasury desk borrows USD 400,000 for 6 months at 4.50% p.a., converts at Spot ₹84.0000, invests the rupees for 6 months at 7.50% p.a., and locks in a 6-month Forward rate of ₹84.9000 to repay principal and interest. Calculate the exact net arbitrage profit in rupees (₹).

Answers and One-Line Reasons
1. (b) — The ALM book manages reserves, HQLA, and balance-sheet hedges; the merchant book serves client FX/hedging; and the trading book holds daily-MTM HFT and proprietary positions [F9].
2. (c) — Under RBI's 2025 Investment Portfolio Directions Para 41(4)(ii), short positions are not allowed except in permitted derivatives and Central Government dated securities, barring equity short sales [F10, F11].
3. (b) — Royalties and technical collaboration fees are current-account transactions under the FEM (Current Account Transactions) Rules, 2000, and globalisation does not remove FEMA/RBI regulation [F21, F25].
4. (c) — Simultaneous borrowing, spot conversion, investment, and forward cover to lock in a rate-gap spread with near-zero market risk is covered interest arbitrage [F17, F19].
5. (b) — Under RBI MD RMIBD Part C Para 4(ii), ADs may invest FC surpluses in overseas money-market or foreign sovereign debt of residual maturity under 1 year rated at least AA(-) (S&P/Fitch) or Aa3 (Moody's); Nostro accounts earn no interest unless swept [F36].
6. (b) — IFR was discontinued 18 May 2026 [F12], daily minimum CRR rose to 99% from 16 October 2026 [F3], and TCCFM was constituted 4 November 2026 [F28] (while CCIL clearing is mandatory [F27], beyond-hours FX dealing is allowed [F34], and CDS/TRS are permitted under 2026 Directions [F29]).
7. ₹3,600 crore — Ceiling is 100% of unimpaired Tier I (₹6,000 crore); export-credit borrowing (₹1,000 crore) is excluded, leaving ₹6,000 − ₹2,400 = ₹3,600 crore (note: borrowing past 50% = ₹3,000 crore triggers Board policy, 12.0% CRAR, and 3-year min maturity) [F30, F31, F32].
8. ₹1,35,900 (or ₹1,35,900.00) — USD repayment = 400,000 × 1.0225 = USD 409,000 (₹3,47,24,100 at ₹84.9000 forward); INR maturity = (400,000 × 84) × 1.0375 = ₹3,48,60,000; net profit = ₹3,48,60,000 − ₹3,47,24,100 = ₹1,35,900 [F17, F18].
