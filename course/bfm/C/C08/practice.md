# C08 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Our own questions, written from C08_fact_sheet and C08_numericals. All names, rates, prices, and amounts are illustrative. Rules as at 31 December 2026. Answers and one-line reasons at the end.


1. Which of the following risks can a bank treasury hedge directly using financial derivative contracts?
   * (a) Operational risk from dealer error
   * (b) Legal risk from an unenforceable master agreement
   * (c) Interest-rate and foreign-exchange market risk
   * (d) Reputational risk from a regulatory penalty
2. Under RBI's Master Direction – Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives Directions, 2025, which of the following entities is not eligible to act as a market-maker?
   * (a) A Scheduled Commercial Bank
   * (b) A Standalone Primary Dealer (SPD)
   * (c) An NBFC – Upper Layer (NBFC-UL)
   * (d) The Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
3. Effective 10 October 2026, what is the maximum notional position a user may hold across all recognised stock exchanges combined in INR exchange-traded currency derivatives without having to establish the existence of underlying exposure?
   * (a) USD 5 million equivalent
   * (b) USD 10 million equivalent
   * (c) USD 50 million equivalent
   * (d) USD 100 million equivalent
4. An Authorised Dealer Category-I bank wants to become a trading/clearing member of the currency derivatives segment of a SEBI-recognised stock exchange. It has net worth of ₹650 crore, maintains minimum prescribed capital including Capital Conservation Buffer, and has Net NPA of 2.4%, but posted a net loss two years ago. What is its status?
   * (a) Eligible as a trading/clearing member because net worth exceeds ₹500 crore
   * (b) Eligible as a trading/clearing member because Net NPA is below 3%
   * (c) Not eligible as a trading/clearing member; may participate only as a client
   * (d) Completely barred from participating in exchange-traded currency futures
5. Which of the following statements about OTC Forward Contracts in Government Securities (Bond Forwards) under RBI's February 2025 Directions (effective 2 May 2025) is incorrect?
   * (a) Eligible users are limited to non-retail users
   * (b) Treasury Bills are included as eligible underlying securities
   * (c) Physically settled bond forwards must settle through CCIL
   * (d) Government securities held to cover a short bond forward remain eligible for SLR
6. When did exchange-traded currency futures commence trading on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) in India, and how are INR currency futures settled?
   * (a) 29 August 2008; cash-settled in INR at the FBIL Reference Rate
   * (b) 29 August 2008; physically settled in US dollars via CCIL
   * (c) 1 January 2014; cash-settled in INR at the FBIL Reference Rate
   * (d) 1 October 2008; physically settled in US dollars via NSE Clearing
7. (Keyed-in numerical — no options) An exporter sells (shorts) 30 lots of USD/INR currency futures (1 lot = USD 1,000, illustrative) at ₹84.8000. On the last trading day, the FBIL Reference Rate is ₹84.3500. Compute the exporter's cash gain in rupees (to 2 decimal places).
8. (Keyed-in numerical — no options) A bank treasury shorts 100 lots of 10-year G-Sec Interest Rate Futures (1 lot = ₹2,00,000 face value = 2,000 units, illustrative) at ₹102.10 to hedge a bond portfolio. Yields rise and the IRF closes at ₹101.40. Compute the cash gain on the IRF position in rupees (to 2 decimal places).
Answers and One-Line Reasons
Q.
	Correct Answer
	Explanation / Citation
	1
	(c)
	Derivatives hedge identifiable market risks (interest-rate, currency, price); legal risk is managed via standard agreements (e.g., ISDA) and operational risk via internal controls ([F5]).
	2
	(d)
	RBI is the regulator, neither a market-maker nor a user; Scheduled Banks, SPDs, NBFC-UL, and specified AIFIs are eligible IRD market-makers ([F7]).
	3
	(a)
	A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 25 (10 Oct 2026) cut the no-proof-of-underlying threshold from USD 100 million to USD 5 million across all recognised stock exchanges combined ([F15]).
	4
	(c)
	Trading/clearing membership requires net profit in the preceding 3 financial years alongside ₹500 crore net worth, minimum capital incl. CCB, and Net NPA <= 3%; banks failing any norm may participate as clients ([F14]).
	5
	(b)
	Under RBI's 2025 Bond Forward Directions, "Government Security" expressly excludes Treasury Bills; only dated G-Secs qualify ([F24, F25]).
	6
	(a)
	Currency futures started on NSE on 29 August 2008 (Jan 2014 was cash-settled 10-yr G-Sec IRF) and are cash-settled in INR at the FBIL Reference Rate ([F20, F22, F27]).
	7
	₹13,500.00
	Seller gains when rate falls: (84.8000 − 84.3500) x 30 x 1,000 = 0.4500 x 30,000 = ₹13,500.00 ([F22, F23]).
	8
	₹1,40,000.00
	Short IRF gains when bond price falls: (102.10 − 101.40) x 2,000 x 100 = 0.70 x 2,00,000 = ₹1,40,000.00 ([F28, F30]).
