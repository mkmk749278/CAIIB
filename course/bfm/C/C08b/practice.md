# C08b · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Our own questions, written strictly from C08b_fact_sheet and C08b_numericals. All figures are illustrative. Answers with one-line reasons at the end.


1. A cash-settled OTC interest rate derivative that locks a fixed interest rate against a floating reference rate on a notional principal for a single future period is a:
(a) Interest Rate Swap (b) Forward Rate Agreement (c) Currency Swap (d) Overnight Indexed Swap
2. Which statement accurately distinguishes an Interest Rate Swap (IRS) from a full Cross-Currency Swap (CCS)?
(a) Both exchange principal at inception and maturity (b) Neither exchanges principal (c) An IRS never exchanges notional principal, whereas a full CCS usually exchanges principal in two currencies (d) An IRS is exchange-traded while a CCS is OTC
3. A swap in which both counterparties exchange floating interest payments linked to two different floating benchmarks in the same currency is called a:
(a) Zero-coupon swap (b) Basis swap (c) Overnight Indexed Swap (d) Principal-only swap
4. Under the Master Direction – RBI (Rupee Interest Rate Derivatives) Directions, 2025 (effective 1 March 2026), which entity is NOT eligible to act as a market-maker in Rupee IRDs?
(a) Scheduled Commercial Bank (b) Standalone Primary Dealer (c) NBFC – Upper Layer (d) NBFC – Base Layer
5. A resident manufacturing company has audited net worth of ₹600 crore and turnover of ₹850 crore. Under RBI's 2025 Rupee IRD Directions, it is eligible to be classified as a:
(a) Non-retail user (b) Retail user only (c) Market-maker (d) Excluded participant
6. Which statement about corporate access to MIBOR-OIS and MMIFOR swaps under RBI's 2025 Rupee IRD Directions is correct?
(a) Only banks may enter MIBOR-OIS swaps (b) Any resident may enter MIBOR-OIS (retail for hedging only), whereas MMIFOR swaps are restricted to hedging only for all users (c) Non-retail corporates may use MMIFOR swaps for trading (d) Retail corporates cannot enter any IRS
7. Since 4 August 2025, FBIL computes Overnight MIBOR from actual call money trades on CCIL's NDS-CALL platform during:
(a) 9:00 AM to 10:00 AM, published at 10:45 AM (b) 9:00 AM to 12:00 Noon, published by 12:45 PM (c) 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, published at 5:30 PM (d) 3:00 PM to 5:00 PM, published at 6:30 PM
8. A bank holds long-term fixed-rate home loans funded by 6-month floating-rate deposits and expects RBI to raise policy rates. To hedge its negative repricing gap using an IRS, the bank should:
(a) Receive fixed and pay floating (b) Pay fixed and receive floating (c) Enter a principal-only currency swap (d) Buy an interest rate future
9. An Indian corporate with a 5-year USD external commercial borrowing wants to hedge only the final USD principal repayment at maturity while leaving semi-annual USD interest payments unhedged. Which swap fits?
(a) Coupon-only swap (b) Full cross-currency swap (c) Principal-only swap (d) Basis swap
10. (Keyed-in numerical) Bank A enters a ₹50 crore notional rupee IRS (Actual/365, 182 days), paying 6.40% p.a. fixed and receiving floating. Floating resets at 7.10% p.a. Calculate the net settlement amount (in ₹, to 2 decimals) and state who pays whom.
11. (Keyed-in numerical) A bank buys a 3×6 FRA (91 days, Actual/365) on ₹20 crore notional at 6.50% p.a. On the fixing date, the 3-month reference rate fixes at 7.30% p.a. Calculate the discounted settlement amount (in ₹, to 2 decimals) and state who pays whom.
12. (Keyed-in numerical) A treasury uses USD 1,000,000 (cost 4.50% p.a.) in a 90-day USD/INR FX swap (Actual/365) at spot ₹85.0000 and 90-day forward ₹85.4500 to raise rupees. Calculate the exact annualised rupee funding cost (% p.a., to 4 decimals).
Answers (one-line reasons)
   1. b: An FRA is a cash-settled OTC derivative locking one future interest period on a notional principal [F2].
   2. c: IRS exchanges only net interest in one currency with no principal exchange; a full currency swap exchanges principal and interest in two currencies [F4, F30].
   3. b: A basis swap exchanges one floating benchmark stream against another floating benchmark stream in the same currency [F6].
   4. d: Eligible Rupee IRD market-makers are Scheduled Banks, SPDs, NBFC-ULs, and five AIFIs (EXIM, NABARD, NHB, SIDBI, NaBFID) — not NBFC-BL [F8].
   5. a: Resident users with minimum net worth of ₹500 crore OR minimum turnover of ₹1,000 crore qualify as non-retail users [F10].
   6. b: Any resident may enter MIBOR/SORR swaps (retail for hedging only), while MMIFOR IRDs may be offered to users only for hedging [F11].
   7. b: From 4 August 2025, FBIL widened Overnight MIBOR to the first 3 hours (9:00 AM–12:00 Noon) of NDS-CALL trades, published by 12:45 PM [F18].
   8. b: Paying fixed and receiving floating produces floating swap inflows that offset rising floating deposit costs [F24, F25].
   9. c: A Principal-Only Swap (POS) exchanges only the principal at maturity, removing principal FX risk while leaving coupon FX risk open [F31].
   10. ₹17,45,205.48, paid by Bank B to Bank A: 50,00,00,000 × (7.10% − 6.40%) × 182 ÷ 365 = ₹17,45,205.48 [F4, F7; numericals Q1].
   11. ₹3,91,773.83, paid by FRA seller to FRA buyer: Undiscounted ₹3,98,904.11 ÷ (1 + 7.30% × 91 ÷ 365) = ₹3,98,904.11 ÷ 1.0182 = ₹3,91,773.83 [F3; numericals Q2].
   12. 6.6709% p.a.: INR raised ₹8,50,00,000; INR outflow USD 1,011,095.89 × 85.45 = ₹8,63,98,143.84; (13,98,143.84 ÷ 8,50,00,000) × (365 ÷ 90) × 100 = 6.6709% [F36; numericals Q3].
