# C01b · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Eight original exam-style questions (6 MCQs + 2 keyed-in numericals) written strictly from C01b_fact_sheet (rules as at 31 December 2026) and C01b_numericals. All bank names and figures are illustrative. Options use realistic exam traps (Mid Office reporting to Treasury Head, RBI approving NOOPL case by case, FEDAI vs FBIL April 2026 shift, Alert List non-exhaustiveness, Primary Dealer exemption, direct vs brokered turnover). Answers with one-line reasons citing [F#] appear at the end.
1. At a commercial bank's integrated treasury, the Head of Treasury supervises both the forex dealing room and the deal confirmation unit, while Mid Office limit-breach alerts are routed through the Head of Treasury before reaching ALCO. Under RBI's Internal Control Guidelines and Governance Directions, 2025, this structure represents: (a) An efficient specialised-branch model permitted for AD Category-I banks (b) A structural control failure violating functional segregation and independent risk reporting (c) Acceptable practice provided the bank conducts an annual system audit (d) Permissible only for inter-bank deals settled outside CCIL
2. Which of the following statements regarding Foreign Exchange Exposure Limits under RBI's Master Direction – Risk Management and Inter-Bank Dealings (updated 1 October 2026) and RBI A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 24 (27 March 2026) is CORRECT as at 31 December 2026? (a) RBI individually approves each bank's Net Overnight Open Position Limit (NOOPL) before it takes effect (b) A bank's overnight limit is kept larger than its daylight limit to absorb offshore volatility (c) The bank's Board fixes NOOPL (up to 25% of total capital) and communicates it to RBI, while onshore deliverable NOP-INR must stay within USD 100 million at day end (d) Under the shorthand method, net long currency positions are netted against net short currency positions to arrive at Overall NOP
3. Effective 6 April 2026, which body publishes the daily USD/INR spot rate used by banks for Net Overnight Open Position (NOOP) calculation, and which body continues to publish month-end revaluation rates? (a) FEDAI publishes both the daily NOOP spot rate and month-end revaluation rates (b) FBIL publishes the daily USD/INR spot rate for NOOP, while FEDAI continues to publish month-end revaluation rates (c) CCIL publishes the daily USD/INR spot rate for NOOP, while FIMMDA publishes month-end revaluation rates (d) RBI publishes both the daily NOOP spot rate and month-end revaluation rates directly
4. During a concurrent audit of a commercial bank's forex back office, the auditor finds (i) an unreconciled debit entry in a USD Nostro account pending for 2 years and 4 months, and (ii) an unreconciled credit entry pending for 3 years and 2 months. Under RBI's Commercial Banks – Financial Statements Directions, 2025, what treatment is mandatory? (a) Provide 50% for the debit entry and transfer the credit entry to Profit & Loss Account (b) Make 100% provision for the debit entry (> 2 years) and transfer the credit entry (> 3 years) to a Blocked Account reckoned for CRR/SLR (c) Net off the debit entry against the credit entry and provide 100% only for the net debit (d) Transfer both entries to General Reserve after obtaining Board approval
5. Which of the following statements about forex dealing risks and RBI's Alert List of unauthorised forex trading platforms is INCORRECT? (a) Herstatt Risk (settlement principal risk) is mitigated by settling through Payment-versus-Payment (PVP) mechanisms (b) Legal risk in OTC forex derivatives is mitigated through master agreements such as ISDA, IFEMA, and ICOM (c) If an overseas online forex trading app does not appear on RBI's Alert List, a resident individual may safely remit margin money to it under LRS (d) Dealing rooms must preserve voice recordings for at least 2 months, or longer if a dispute has been raised
6. Under RBI's Commercial Banks – Classification, Valuation, and Operation of Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025 (Para 92(8)), the 5% annual upper contract limit per individual approved broker does NOT apply to dealings through: (a) SEBI-registered corporate debt brokers (b) Stockbrokers who are members of NSE or BSE (c) Primary Dealers (d) Voice brokers accredited by FEDAI
7. (Keyed-in numerical) A commercial bank's annual securities turnover is ₹2,000 crore, comprising ₹1,200 crore routed through approved brokers and ₹800 crore executed directly with counterparties without any broker. What is the maximum aggregate annual contract limit (in ₹ crore) for a single approved non-PD broker?
8. (Keyed-in numerical) A commercial bank has Tier I capital of ₹1,200 crore and Tier II capital of ₹300 crore. Calculate the maximum Aggregate Gap Limit (AGL) (in ₹ crore) that its Board may approve under RBI's Master Direction – Risk Management and Inter-Bank Dealings.
Answers (one-line reasons)
1. (b) — Front, Mid, and Back Office must be functionally segregated, dealers/front-office bosses cannot supervise confirmations, and Mid Office/risk reporting must stay independent of the Head of Treasury [F4, F5, F8, F9, F11].
2. (c) — The bank's Board fixes NOOPL (max 25% of total capital) and communicates it to RBI via CIMS/email, while RBI/2025-26/252 caps onshore deliverable NOP-INR at USD 100 million at day end (effective 10 April 2026); daylight > overnight and shorthand never nets longs against shorts [F14, F16, F17, F18, F19].
3. (b) — From 6 April 2026, FBIL took over publication of the daily USD/INR spot rate for NOOP calculation, while FEDAI continues publishing month-end revaluation rates [F22].
4. (b) — Under RBI Financial Statements Directions, 2025 Para 20(3)–(5), unreconciled Nostro debits > 2 years attract 100% provision and unreconciled Nostro credits > 3 years move to a Blocked Account reckoned for CRR/SLR (no set-off) [F31].
5. (c) — RBI's Alert List is non-exhaustive (absence never implies authorisation), and LRS remittances for margin/margin calls to overseas forex platforms are strictly prohibited under FEMA [F34, F36, F39].
6. (c) — Under Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025 Para 92(8), the 5% individual broker cap explicitly exempts dealings through Primary Dealers [F24].
7. ₹60 crore — The 5% broker cap applies only to total transactions routed through brokers (1200 × 5% = ₹60 crore), excluding direct non-brokered deals [F24].
8. ₹9,000 crore — Total capital = ₹1,200 cr + ₹300 cr = ₹1,500 cr; maximum Board AGL = 6 × total capital (1500 × 6 = ₹9,000 crore) [F21].
