What share of the notified amount is reserved for retail non-competitive bidders in G-Secs and T-Bills? Up to five percent within the notified amount. For dated G-Secs, one retail bid is capped at two crore rupees. Two exceptions. State Governments, eligible Provident Funds, and designated foreign central banks bid in T-Bill N-C-B outside the notified amount, uncapped. New F-R-Bs are spread-based. Second pair: Uniform Price versus Multiple Price. In Uniform Price, every winning bidder pays the same cut-off price. Under Multiple Price, Bank A pays nine point eight five crore; Bank B pays nine point eight four crore — one lakh less. Under Uniform Price, both would pay nine point eight four crore. Indian money-market bills use Actual over 365 — always 365 days in the denominator, never 360. Discount is rear-ended: you earn return on what you paid, so divide by purchase price P, never by 100. Final chapter: C-P and C-D. Quick recall: what day-count basis do money-market discount bills follow? Actual over 365. Commercial banks, R-R-Bs, and Small Finance Banks issue C-Ds for 7 days to 1 year, and may buy back after 7 days. Two: no, A-I-F-I buyback is barred, though banks may buy back after 7 days. One thing to remember: one formula prices T-Bills, C-M-Bs, C-P, and C-D, and 7 days is the buyback floor for C-P and Bank C-Ds. Mixed check across the whole class — three rapid questions. Dated bond pricing, accrued interest, and Y-T-M come later in this module, where we will recall these auction rules once. Homework: a bank issues a 146-day C-D of ₹10 lakh at 7.50%, illustrative.
