# C05 · Practice Questions ("Bonds: the family, price and yield")
7 original CAIIB BFM questions (4 MCQs + 3 keyed-in numericals) covering all blueprint question kinds (define, purpose, who/when/how much, compare, exception, calculate, apply to a case). Every answer and trap traces to C05_fact_sheet ([F#]). All names and numbers are illustrative.
Q1 · MCQ (Compare / Regulatory Trap — Investment Classification)
Under RBI's Commercial Banks – Classification, Valuation and Operation of Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025 (as at 31 December 2026), which statement is correct?


* A. Commercial banks classify investments into three equal top-level categories: HTM, AFS, and HFT, and must maintain an Investment Fluctuation Reserve of 2% of AFS + HFT.
* B. Commercial banks classify investments into HTM, AFS, and FVTPL, with HFT as a separate sub-category within FVTPL, and IFR has been discontinued effective 18 May 2026.
* C. All SLR securities must be classified under HTM, while all corporate bonds must be classified under AFS or HFT.
* D. Convertible debentures may be classified under HTM if the bank intends to hold them until conversion.


Answer: B


Reason: Under INV25 Paras 33 & 105 (and Second Amendment Directions, 18 May 2026), categories are HTM, AFS, and FVTPL (with HFT inside FVTPL), classification depends on business objective plus SPPI (not SLR status), convertible bonds fail SPPI, and IFR was discontinued on 18 May 2026 [F12, F20, F21, F23].
Q2 · MCQ (Who / When / Exception — STRIPS & SLR Status)
A commercial bank holds four securities in its treasury portfolio: (i) a fixed-coupon 10-year GoI dated security (SLR-eligible, transferable), (ii) a fixed-coupon State Government Security with 8 years residual maturity and ₹2,500 crore outstanding (SLR-eligible, transferable), (iii) a GoI Special Oil Bond, and (iv) a AAA-rated listed corporate bond. Which of these qualify for both SLR maintenance and STRIPS?


* A. Only (i)
* B. (i) and (ii) only
* C. (i), (ii), and (iii) only
* D. (i), (ii), and (iv) only


Answer: B


Reason: Both SLR-eligible transferable fixed-coupon GoI dated securities and (since 12 June 2025) fixed-coupon State Government Securities with residual maturity ≤ 14 years and minimum outstanding ₹1,000 crore are SLR-eligible and strippable; GoI Special Oil Bonds and corporate bonds are non-SLR [F8, F13, F14, F15].
Q3 · MCQ (Exception / Operational Rule — Demat, ZCBs & Embedded Options)
Which of the following actions by a commercial bank's treasury desk violates RBI's Investment Portfolio Directions, 2025?


* A. Holding a privately placed corporate bond in dematerialised form with NSDL.
* B. Buying a 7-year corporate zero-coupon bond where the issuer has not created a sinking fund in liquid securities.
* C. Charging broken-period interest paid on a G-Sec purchase to the Profit & Loss Account instead of capitalising it.
* D. Valuing an unquoted rated corporate bond at 75 bps above the equivalent-maturity Central G-Sec YTM.


Answer: B


Reason: Banks are barred from investing in long-term corporate ZCBs or low-coupon bonds unless the issuer builds a sinking fund invested in liquid securities such as G-Secs (INV25 Para 90(4)); demat holding, expensing broken-period interest, and a ≥ 50 bps mark-up are all compliant [F6, F18, F31, F32].
Q4 · MCQ (Apply to a Case — Yield Curve, Embedded Options & Price-Yield Order)
An 8.50% coupon bond with face value ₹1,000 is currently trading at ₹940. Meanwhile, the government yield curve is inverted (1-year yield 7.10%, 10-year yield 6.60%). Which statement is correct?


* A. For this bond, Coupon Rate > Current Yield > YTM; and an inverted curve means long-term yields exceed short-term yields.
* B. For this bond, YTM > Current Yield > Coupon Rate; and an inverted curve means short-term yields exceed long-term yields.
* C. If this bond were callable, falling interest rates would cause its price to rise without limit above its call price.
* D. Because the bond is trading at a discount, its current yield equals its YTM.


Answer: B


Reason: For a discount bond (P < FV), YTM > Current Yield > Coupon Rate, and an inverted (negative) yield curve has short-term yields higher than long-term yields; a callable bond's price upside is capped when rates fall because the issuer can redeem early [F9, F26, F28, F33].
Q5 · Keyed-in Numerical (Calculate — 3-Year Annual Bond Price)
An illustrative 3-year annual coupon bond has face value ₹1,000, coupon rate 7.00% p.a., and market YTM 10.00% p.a. Calculate its fair market price (in ₹, rounded to 2 decimal places).


Answer: ₹925.39 (925.3944)


Workings & Keys:


* 70 ÷ 1.1 = 63.6364 · 70 ÷ 1.1 ÷ 1.1 = 57.8512 · 1070 ÷ 1.1 ÷ 1.1 ÷ 1.1 = 803.9068
* Sum: 63.6364 + 57.8512 + 803.9068 = ₹925.3944 → ₹925.39
* Cross-check (PVAF): 70 × 2.486852 + 1000 × 0.751315 = 174.0796 + 751.3148 = ₹925.3944.


Reason: Bond price is the sum of present values of all future cash flows discounted at YTM [F24, F26].
Q6 · Keyed-in Numerical (Calculate — Accrued Interest & Total Consideration on G-Sec)
A bank buys ₹2,00,00,000 (₹2 crore) face value of illustrative 6.60% GS 2034 at a clean price of ₹98.50 per ₹100. Exactly 90 days have elapsed since the last coupon date under the 30/360 convention. Calculate the total consideration payable (in ₹).


Answer: ₹2,00,30,000 (Dirty price ₹100.15 per ₹100)


Workings & Keys:


* Accrued interest per ₹100: 6.60 × 90 ÷ 360 = ₹1.6500
* Dirty price: 98.50 + 1.65 = ₹100.1500
* Total consideration: 20000000 × 100.15 ÷ 100 = ₹2,00,30,000
* Cross-check: Clean ₹1,97,00,000 + AI ₹3,30,000 = ₹2,00,30,000.


Reason: Indian dated G-Secs use the 30/360 day-count convention and settle at dirty price = clean price + accrued interest [F29, F30].
Q7 · Keyed-in Numerical (Calculate — YTM Approximation for a Premium Bond)
An illustrative ₹1,000 face value bond paying a 9.00% annual coupon (₹90) has 4 years to maturity and trades at a premium price of ₹1,040. Compute its approximate YTM (in %, rounded to 2 decimal places).


Answer: 7.84% (7.8431%)


Workings & Keys:


* Annual capital loss: (1000 − 1040) ÷ 4 = −₹10
* Numerator: 90 − 10 = ₹80; Denominator: (1000 + 1040) ÷ 2 = ₹1,020
* 80 ÷ 1020 × 100 = 7.8431% → 7.84%
* Cross-check: 2 × (4 × 90 + 1000 − 1040) ÷ (4 × 2040) × 100 = 640 ÷ 8160 × 100 = 7.8431%.


Reason: For a premium bond, annual capital loss reduces YTM below both coupon rate (9.00%) and current yield (8.65%) [F26, F28].
