## C01 · Integrated treasury: what it is, why it pays, and the world it trades in
- **Goal**: explain what an integrated treasury is and does, why it became a profit centre, and how globalisation and
  the forex market shape its work.
- **Sub-topics**: C1.1 (define, purpose, compare: integrated vs separate desks) · C1.2 (define, compare: capital vs
  current account, exception: partial convertibility, apply to a case: which flow is this?) · C1.3 (purpose, compare:
  arbitrage vs trading vs speculation vs merchant, calculate: covered interest arbitrage) · C2.1 market features
  (define, compare: convertible vs partly convertible) · C2.4 (overseas borrowing ceiling: who/how much; rest in C02b).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: IE&IFS (financial markets; balance of payments, capital vs current account) and PPB (what a
  bank does); 30-second refresher on the current/capital account split before ch3.
- **Evidence**: C-TPROD seen 6/7: integrated treasury functions (Dec 2023 ×2, Dec 2025 "treasury office", case); FP1
  Q67–70 (Q70 globalisation).
- **Opening**: 9:00 a.m. at a bank's treasury: forex, money market and bond dealers at one desk. "Why put them together?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think treasury only parks spare cash; others think globalisation means RBI stepped back.
- **Picture**: the desk from above with three desks joined into one; a bridge from the bank's branches (deposits,
  loans) to the markets; a world map with arrows for capital flows.
- **Chapters** (~15 min):
  1. From separate desks to one treasury: the bridge between the core book and the markets; **drivers of
     integration** on one card (cash across currencies, interest arbitrage, access to global funds via FDI/FPI/ECB,
     corporate demand for hedging and advice, risk management, technology, reserve requirements); **functions list**
     (CRR/SLR management, global cash management, merchant and advisory services, profit from forex, money and
     securities, risk management; treasury clearance of availability and cost before a foreign-currency loan); the
     **three roles** (liquidity, proprietary trading, risk management) and the **three books** (ALM, merchant,
     trading) with one example each.
  2. Treasury as a profit centre: from cost centre to profit centre after the 1990s reforms; **four reasons treasury
     profit is attractive** (low credit risk, leverage, low operating cost, volume over margin; little capital);
     **sources of income** as bars (merchant spread, money-market deployment, investment income, trading gains;
     strategic stakes are not for trading); **covered interest arbitrage worked once** (own numbers, link A05
     interest parity) and why it disappears between two fully convertible currencies; **sorting card**: arbitrage /
     merchant / speculation / trading.
  3. Globalisation and the treasury: **meaning**, "not deregulation", India's **partial** capital-account
     convertibility; **sorting card** capital-account routes (FDI, FPI, ECB, ADR/GDR/FCCB, cross-border M&A) vs
     current-account items (royalty, technical fees); the **three impacts** on treasury (rates and the rupee follow
     global rates and flows; new infrastructure and regulators: CCIL, depositories, SEBI, IRDAI; wider products:
     rupee derivatives, IRFs, CDS); portfolio flows add volatility; **banks' overseas borrowing and investment
     ceiling** linked to Tier 1 (one line; recalled in C02b).
  4. The forex market the treasury trades in: **features card** (global, no floor, screen-based, round the clock,
     two-way quotes, narrow spread = liquidity); free vs partly convertible INR; where FC surplus may be placed
     (interbank, rated overseas money-market paper, within limits); **nostro balances earn nothing** unless a sweep
     is agreed. Quiz.
- **Numericals**: 1 (covered interest arbitrage: borrow, convert, invest, cover forward; profit or none).
- **Traps**: royalty and technical fees are current account, not capital; globalisation ≠ deregulation (FP1 Q70);
  arbitrage vs speculation (arbitrage carries almost no market risk); banks may not short-sell equities (verify);
  three books vs three roles.
- **Practice**: 3 in video (one sorting, one arbitrage, one globalisation); `practice.md` covers every kind above.
- **Map nodes**: The desk: integration, functions, profit centre, globalisation, forex market.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: 15 from depth/C (C1.1 ×4: drivers, functions, three roles, three books ·
  C1.2 ×4: meaning/partial convertibility, route sorting, three impacts, overseas ceiling · C1.3 ×4: four reasons,
  income sources, arbitrage sum, activity sorting · C2.1 ×2: market features, FC surplus and nostro) + 1 from depth/A
  (A1.4 item 4: integrated treasury's scope incl. CRR/SLR, liquidity, ALM, transfer pricing, derivatives). The other
  depth/C C1.4 items (3) and depth/A A1.4 items (6) are in C01b.
- **Facts to verify**: FEMA 1999 s.6 and the current/capital account rules (FEM (Permissible Capital Account
  Transactions) Regulations; FEM (Current Account Transactions) Rules 2000); banks' overseas FC borrowing and
  investment limit (RBI MD on risk management and inter-bank dealings, as consolidated 2025); short sale rules (RBI
  short-sale directions for G-secs; investment directions); FC advance funding and nostro/FC surplus deployment (same
  MD); forward-rate conventions for the arbitrage (FEDAI Rules).

## C01b · Organising the treasury and the dealing room *(proposed split from C01)*
- **Goal**: explain how a treasury is organised, who does what in front, mid and back office and why they are
  separated, and how dealers are controlled.
- **Sub-topics**: C1.4 (define, compare: department vs specialised branch; front vs mid vs back; apply to a case:
  segregation failure) · C5.1 dealer level (who/how much: limits; compare: daylight vs overnight; exception) · A1.4
  dealing-room operations (taught here, linked from A21).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: PPB (internal control and audit basics); A01 (buying and selling a currency) for funds vs
  currency position.
- **Evidence**: front/mid/back office (Jun 2023); FP1 Q84 (functional segregation); dealing room case, daylight
  limit, forex hold (A-DEALROOM, Dec 2025, Jun 2026); NOP-INR cap (Mar 2026).
- **Opening**: a dealer books a big deal at 3 p.m.; the same manager runs his confirmations. "What could go wrong?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think the back office only does paperwork; others think the mid office makes trades.
- **Picture**: the desk from above with three rings (front: deal; mid: watch risk; back: settle and account); the
  dealing-room clock for daylight and overnight limits.
- **Chapters** (~14 min):
  1. Two ways to organise: **department vs specialised branch** (own GL and P&L, may be an AD, settles directly),
     two-column card; head of treasury at senior level; **support units** (investment cell, accounts, systems,
     connectivity: SWIFT, RTGS/NEFT).
  2. Front, mid, back: who does what (front deals and may bid in primary auctions, never settles; mid monitors risk,
     limits, stop loss, M2M valuation, MIS to top management/ALCO, **reports outside the treasury head**; back
     confirms with the counterparty independently, settles on value date, accounts, reconciles, flags breaches);
     **segregation case**: dealer and back office under one manager, mid-office reports ignored → control failure.
     **Qualities of a dealer** (decisions under stress, discipline, owning mistakes) in one line.
  3. The dealing room's controls: **funds position vs currency position** (overbought/oversold = long/short; net
     position worked once); deal-size limit, open-position limits (currency-wise and aggregate), **daylight vs
     overnight** (daylight larger), NOP and the rupee NOP cap, stop loss (intro; full treatment C07); **internal
     controls**: functional separation, access control, counterparty limits, periodic revaluation at FEDAI rates,
     nostro reconciliation.
  4. Risks in forex dealing (operational, exchange, credit, settlement/Herstatt, liquidity, systemic, country,
     sovereign, gap, legal) on one card; **RBI Alert List** of unauthorised forex platforms (one line). Quiz.
- **Numericals**: 1 small (net currency position from a day's deals).
- **Traps**: who confirms (back office, independently) and who values (mid/back) vs the dealer; mid office must not
  report to the treasury head; daylight limit > overnight limit; NOP vs daylight limit; who sets dealer limits (the
  board/management, within RBI rules; not RBI case by case).
- **Practice**: 3 in video (one segregation case).
- **Map nodes**: The desk: organisation, three offices, dealer limits, forex risks.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: 3 from depth/C (C1.4: department vs branch; segregation case; who confirms and
  values) + 6 from depth/A A1.4 (dealer qualities; funds vs currency position; internal controls list; forex risk
  list; RBI Alert List; dealing-room malpractices folded into the segregation case).
- **Facts to verify**: RBI investment-portfolio directions (2023, now in the 2025 consolidated MD: segregation of
  front, mid, back office); RBI MD on risk management and inter-bank dealings (dealer limits, NOP; NOP framework
  revamp 2026, date to verify); NOP-INR within USD 100 million at day end (RBI/2025-26/252); FEDAI rules on
  revaluation rates and dealing-room conduct; RBI Alert List page.

## C02 · Money-market instruments
- **Goal**: tell every money-market instrument apart (tenor, issuer, who may invest, the fine print RBI sets).
- **Sub-topics**: C2.2 (define, who/when/how much: tenors, issuers, minimum sizes, ratings; compare: CP vs CD, call
  vs notice vs term; exception: CD no premature closure, accommodation bills barred; apply to a case: a CP issue).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: IE&IFS (money market and its instruments); 30-second refresher: repo is a loan against
  securities with a buy-back.
- **Evidence**: CP case (Jun 2026 ×2), CD ("A3-rated company can issue", Jun 2026), notice money FP1 Q74, repo items.
- **Opening**: a bank is short Rs 500 crore for one night; a company has spare cash for three months.
- **Misconceptions**: some think CDs are issued by companies; others think every interbank loan is risk-free.
- **Picture**: a ladder of tenors (overnight to one year) with each instrument on its rung (designed here; C02b adds
  the corridor beside it).
- **Chapters** (~14 min):
  1. Call, notice, term money: tenors; **prudential limits** on borrowing and lending for banks (as % of capital
     funds, fortnight average and single day) and PDs (% of NOF); counterparty risk.
  2. Repo and TREPS: market repo and reverse repo; **repo fine print**: eligible securities (incl. municipal debt),
     securities of related entities barred, first leg T+0 or T+1; TREPS (once CBLO).
  3. CP and CD: issuers, minimum size, tenor, rating; **CP fine print**: IPA must be a scheduled bank, buyback only
     after a minimum period, dual rating → the lower one counts, LLP/trust eligibility; why credit risk is low; **CD
     fine print**: banks and FIs only, demat, at discount, no premature closure, no buyback by the issuer.
  4. The rest of the shelf: **bill rediscounting** (tenor band, genuine trade bills only, accommodation bills
     barred, interest front-ended) and **OMOs** in one line (RBI buys to inject, sells to absorb; link C02b). Quiz.
- **Traps**: notice money tenor; CP vs CD issuers; CP buyback timing; CD premature closure (not allowed); related-party
  securities in repo; dual rating uses the lower.
- **Practice**: 3 in video (one CP case).
- **Map nodes**: Money market: instruments.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: 5 (C2.2: CP/CD fine print; call-money prudential limits; repo fine print; OMO
  line; bill rediscounting).
- **Facts to verify**: RBI Call, Notice and Term Money Markets Directions 2021; Commercial Paper and NCD (up to one
  year) Directions 2024; Certificate of Deposit Directions 2021; Repurchase Transactions (Repo) Directions 2018 as
  amended Nov–Dec 2025 (municipal debt, T+0/T+1); bill rediscounting directions; the 2025 consolidated MD names for
  each.

## C02b · RBI's liquidity tools and domestic vs global markets *(proposed split from C02)*
- **Goal**: explain how RBI steers short-term rates through the LAF corridor, and how India's money and debt markets
  link to global markets and benchmarks.
- **Sub-topics**: C4.2 (define, purpose, who/how much: rates and SLR dip, compare: SDF vs reverse repo, apply to a
  case: which window will a bank use?) · C2.4 (purpose, compare: domestic vs global markets, who/how much: FPI
  routes, apply to a case: an FPI or ADR flow).
- **JAIIB prerequisite**: IE&IFS (monetary policy tools: repo, reverse repo, OMO; money supply); A05 interest parity.
- **Evidence**: liquidity corridor FP1 Q81; SOFR (Jun 2026); repo items; MPC questions (C04 list).
- **Opening**: on a Thursday evening the call rate shoots above the repo rate. "Who will step in, and with what?"
- **Misconceptions**: some think the SDF needs collateral; others think the 14-day auction is still RBI's main tool.
- **Picture**: the corridor as a road beside the C02 tenor ladder: SDF at the floor, repo in the middle, MSF at the
  ceiling; the call rate (WACR) as a car that RBI keeps near the middle lane.
- **Chapters** (~13 min):
  1. The LAF corridor: repo and SDF/MSF at the cut-off; **WACR as the operating target**; corridor width; **MSF**:
     overnight, banks may dip into SLR up to a % of NDTL, at a margin over repo; **trap: SDF (no collateral)
     replaced fixed-rate reverse repo as the floor** (2022).
  2. The liquidity framework **as revised Oct 2025**: main operation and VRR/VRRR tenors overnight to 14 days;
     **auction logic** (who gets funds: the higher bids in a VRR); durable liquidity tools (OMO, long-term repo, forex
     swaps); RBI's Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets (one line).
  3. Domestic and global markets: the links: interest parity and FX swaps (from C01), **FPI debt routes** (general
     route, FAR; VRR folded into the general route from 1 Apr 2026; G-sec concentration limit withdrawn 2026), ADR/GDR
     price link and FX risk on the holder; banks' overseas borrowing ceiling (recall of C01); domestic debt and
     equity markets in the treasury's product set; stock exchanges' role (price discovery, liquidity).
  4. Benchmarks: FBIL publishes MIBOR and others; LIBOR's end and the ARRs (SOFR etc.). Quiz.
- **Traps**: SDF without collateral; old "fixed-rate reverse repo as floor"; the old 14-day main operation; old LAF
  access limits as % of NDTL (pre-2020 design); LIBOR as if current; VRR as a separate FPI route after 1 Apr 2026.
- **Practice**: 3 in video (one "which window" case).
- **Map nodes**: Money market: corridor, liquidity framework, global links, benchmarks.
- **v3 additions (depth checklist)**: 8 (C4.2: Oct 2025 framework; WACR target; MSF SLR dip; VRR auction logic;
  SDF-replaced-reverse-repo trap · C2.4: domestic–global links incl. FPI routes and ADR/GDR; overseas ceiling recall;
  LIBOR end and FBIL benchmarks).
- **Facts to verify**: RBI Revised Liquidity Management Framework (Oct 2025); policy rates as at 31 Dec 2026 (repo

- **Chapters** (~15 min):

  3. Monetary policy: MPC, repo (link C02b), M3; **money multiplier** and the liquidity effect of a CRR change (one
     picture).
  4. Case (5 linked questions incl. cost of non-deployment and a daily shortfall).
- **Numericals**: 1 case (5 linked sums) + 1 daily-minimum shortfall at 99% with penal interest.
- **Traps**: old vs new fortnight; CRR 3% (not the older figure some classes still say); daily minimum 99% (not 90%);

- **Map nodes**: Reserves and payments: NDTL, CRR, SLR, MPC.
