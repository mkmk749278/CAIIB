# C02 · Numericals (c1, 2026-11-24)
All names, amounts, and figures are illustrative. Checked by Python (two independent methods: exact Decimal and 12-digit basic calculator key presses); all match. Basic 8-function IIBF calculator (+ − × ÷ % √) only.


Rules used:


* SFB Call & Notice Money borrowing cap: 100% of capital funds (fortnightly daily average), 125% on any single day [F6].
* Primary Dealer Call & Notice Money borrowing cap: 225% of previous-FY NOF (fortnightly daily average) [F9].
* Co-operative Bank Call, Notice & Term Money combined borrowing cap: 2.0% of previous-FY aggregate deposits [F8].
* Market repo minimum haircut on Municipal Debt Securities: 2% of market value [F25].
* Commercial Paper minimum denomination ₹5 lakh [F31]; individual + HUF primary subscription cap 25% of total issue [F36].
Q1 · Small Finance Bank Call & Notice borrowing ceiling (Ch 1, worked in full)
* Data: Small Finance Bank (illustrative) with Capital Funds = ₹800 crore.
* Fortnightly daily average (100%): 800 × 100 % = → ₹800.00 crore.
* Any single day (125%):
   * Method 1 (key presses): 800 × 125 % = → ₹1,000.00 crore.
   * Method 2 (check): 125% = 100% + 25% = 100% + one-fourth → 800 ÷ 4 = 200; 800 + 200 = → ₹1,000.00 crore. Both match.
* Common wrong answer: Applying 125% to the fortnightly average (overstates the average limit by ₹200 crore) or assuming an SCB also faces this 100%/125% cap (SCBs excluding SFBs/PBs moved to Board-approved limits on 8 June 2023 [F5]).
Q2 · Primary Dealer Call & Notice borrowing ceiling (Ch 1, faded / your turn)
* Data: Primary Dealer (illustrative) with Net Owned Fund (NOF) at previous FY end = ₹400 crore.
* Fortnightly daily average (225% of NOF):
   * Method 1 (key presses): 400 × 225 % = → ₹900.00 crore.
   * Method 2 (check): 400 × 2.25 = → ₹900.00 crore (or 400 × 2 = 800, plus 400 ÷ 4 = 100, total ₹900.00 crore). Both match.
* Common wrong answer: Using 125% (the bank single-day cap) → ₹500 crore.
Q3 · Market repo borrowing against Municipal Debt Securities (Ch 2, worked)
* Data: Eligible Municipal Debt Securities of market value ₹200 crore, RBI minimum haircut 2% [F25].
* Maximum first-leg borrowing:
   * Method 1 (key presses): Haircut = 200 × 2 % = ₹4.00 crore; Borrowing = 200 − 4 = → ₹196.00 crore.
   * Method 2 (check): Net value = 100% − 2% = 98% → 200 × 98 % = → ₹196.00 crore. Both match.
* Common wrong answer: Applying 1.5% haircut (that is for CPs/CDs, giving ₹197.00 crore) or forgetting the haircut (₹200.00 crore).
Q4 · Commercial Paper individual + HUF cap and lot count (Ch 3, your turn)
* Data: Company (illustrative) issues a ₹40 crore Commercial Paper.
* Maximum primary subscription by all individuals + HUFs (25% cap):
   * Method 1 (key presses): 40 × 25 % = → ₹10.00 crore.
   * Method 2 (check): 40 ÷ 4 = → ₹10.00 crore. Both match.
* Number of ₹5 lakh minimum lots in ₹10 crore:
   * ₹10 crore = ₹1,000 lakh.
   * Method 1 (key presses): 1000 ÷ 5 = → 200 lots.
   * Method 2 (check): 200 × 5 = 1000 lakh = ₹10.00 crore. Both match.
