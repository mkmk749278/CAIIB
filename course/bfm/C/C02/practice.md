# C02 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Our own questions, written from C02_fact_sheet. Sums verified in C02_numericals. All figures illustrative. Options use real exam traps (old figures and confusable neighbours). Answers with one-line reasons at the end.


1. Unsecured interbank borrowing or lending for a period of 10 days falls under:
(a) Call Money (b) Notice Money (c) Term Money (d) Market Repo
2. Under the operative Call, Notice and Term Money Directions (updated 8 June 2023), the borrowing limit in Call and Notice Money for a Scheduled Commercial Bank (excluding SFBs and Payment Banks) is:
(a) 100% of capital funds fortnightly average and 125% on any day (b) 225% of Net Owned Fund (c) internal Board-approved limit within DoR inter-bank liability limits (d) 2.0% of previous-year aggregate deposits
3. A Primary Dealer has Net Owned Fund (NOF) of ₹400 crore at the end of the previous financial year. Its maximum fortnightly daily average borrowing in Call and Notice Money is:
(a) ₹500 crore (b) ₹400 crore (c) ₹900 crore (d) ₹800 crore
4. An Urban Co-operative Bank has aggregate deposits of ₹1,500 crore at the end of the previous financial year. Its combined borrowing ceiling across Call, Notice and Term Money is:
(a) ₹30 crore (b) ₹15 crore (c) ₹150 crore (d) ₹300 crore
5. Under the Repurchase Transactions (Repo) Directions, 2025, which of the following is strictly prohibited as collateral for a repo borrowing?
(a) State Development Loans (b) Municipal Debt Securities (c) listed corporate bonds issued by the borrower's subsidiary (d) Commercial Papers issued by an unrelated listed company
6. A bank borrows in market repo against eligible Municipal Debt Securities with a market value of ₹200 crore, applying the RBI minimum haircut. The maximum first-leg borrowing is:
(a) ₹200 crore (b) ₹197 crore (c) ₹196 crore (d) ₹190 crore
7. TREPS (Triparty Repo Dealing System), operated by Clearcorp with CCIL as Central Counterparty and Tri-Party Agent, replaced which earlier money-market instrument on 5 November 2018?
(a) CBLO (b) NDS-CALL (c) CROMS (d) BRDS
8. Under the Commercial Paper and NCD Directions, 2024 (effective 1 April 2024), an issuer may buy back its Commercial Paper only after a minimum period of:
(a) 30 days from date of issue (b) 15 days from date of issue (c) 7 days from date of issue (d) 90 days from date of issue
9. Which of the following statements about Commercial Paper under the 2024 Directions is CORRECT?
(a) Dual credit rating is mandatory for CP issues of ₹1,000 crore or more (b) Any scheduled bank (including a co-operative bank) may act as IPA (c) Total primary subscription by all individuals and HUFs cannot exceed 25% of the issue (d) Call and put options are permitted with Board approval
10. A company rated 'A3' wants to issue a money-market instrument for 90 days. Which statement is correct?
(a) It can issue a Certificate of Deposit of minimum ₹5 lakh (b) It can issue a Commercial Paper if all its fund-based facilities from banks, AIFIs and NBFCs are Standard (c) It can issue a Commercial Paper even if one NBFC loan is substandard (d) It can issue a Certificate of Deposit through a Scheduled Commercial Bank IPA
11. Compare buyback rules for Certificates of Deposit as at 31 December 2026:
(a) Neither banks nor AIFIs may buy back their own CDs before maturity (b) Both banks and AIFIs may buy back their own CDs after 30 days (c) Issuing banks may buy back CDs after 7 days (offered to all investors at market price), whereas AIFIs are prohibited from buying back their own CDs (d) Issuing banks may buy back CDs at any time without a minimum holding period
12. Under the Commercial Banks – Credit Facilities Directions, 2025 (Chapter X) and RBI's Bill Rediscounting framework, which of the following is eligible for rediscounting?
(a) Genuine commercial trade usance bills backed by a DUPN of 15 to 90 days (b) Accommodation bills drawn between group entities (c) Services-sector usance bills (d) Discounted bills pledged as collateral in a market repo
Answers (one-line reasons)
   1. b — Notice Money covers unsecured interbank funds for 2 to 14 days [F2].
   2. c — Since 8 June 2023, SCBs (excluding SFBs & PBs) fix internal Board-approved limits within DoR IBL limits; 100%/125% is the old trap [F5].
   3. c — PDs are capped at 225% of previous-FY NOF: 400 × 225% = ₹900 crore [F9; numericals Q2].
   4. a — Co-operative Banks are capped at 2.0% of previous-FY aggregate deposits: 1500 × 2% = ₹30 crore [F8; numericals HW].
   5. c — Borrowing against own securities or securities of a related entity (holding/subsidiary/associate) is strictly barred [F21].
   6. c — Municipal Debt Securities carry a minimum 2% haircut (200 × 98% = ₹196 crore); 1.5% is for CPs/CDs [F25; numericals Q3].
   7. a — CCIL discontinued CBLO and operationalised TREPS on 5 November 2018 [F28].
   8. c — The 2024 CP Directions cut the buyback lock-in from 30 days (old 2017 rule) to 7 days [F34].
   9. c — Individual + HUF primary CP subscription is capped at 25% [F36]; dual rating was dropped in 2024 [F32], IPA must be a Scheduled Commercial Bank [F33], and options are barred [F35].
   10. b — Companies issue CPs (not CDs) [F30, F37], and all fund-based facilities from banks/AIFIs/NBFCs must be Standard [F30].
   11. c — Issuing banks may buy back CDs after 7 days (CD Directions 2021 Para 5(e)), whereas AIFIs cannot (AIFI Directions 2025 Para 18) [F40].
   12. a — Only genuine trade bills (DUPN 15–90 days) qualify [F41, F42]; accommodation bills, services bills, and repo against discounted bills are barred [F42, F43].
