## Chapter 1 · Call, Notice, and Term Money

N009 · Chapter one. The money market covers short-term funds up to one year. First, the unsecured interbank ladder.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Call, Notice, and Term Money" · 2 M: root node "Money market: instruments"

N010 · Three rungs sit here by tenor. Call Money is unsecured borrowing or lending overnight.
> 1 S: ladder rung 1 "Call · overnight (1 day)" [F1]

N011 · Notice Money is unsecured funds for two to fourteen days, inclusive. Term Money is fifteen days up to one year.
> 1 S: rung 2 "Notice · 2–14 days" [F2] · 2 S: rung 3 "Term · 15 days–1 year" [F3] · 3 B: underline "unsecured" [F12]

N012 · Back to our opening question. Who may use these three rungs? Only banks and Primary Dealers.
> 1 C: card "SCBs (excl. LABs), PBs, SFBs, RRBs, Co-op Banks, PDs" [F4]

N013 · Every eligible entity can both borrow and lend; there is no lenders-only group today.
> 1 N: "all eligible = both borrow & lend" [F4]

N016 · Wrong. Because there is no collateral, the lender carries direct counterparty credit risk.
> 1 B: strike thought bubble · 2 N: "unsecured → counterparty credit risk" [F12]

N017 · That is why RBI sets prudential borrowing limits, and one key limit changed on eight June twenty-twenty-three.
> 1 S: trap card "Old vs Current · SCB Call/Notice cap" [F5]

N018 · Old notes quote one hundred per cent average and one twenty-five single day for all banks.
> 1 N: trap left "Old: SCBs 100% avg / 125% day" [F5]

> 1 N: trap right "Current: SCBs = Board-approved within IBL" [F5]

N020 · Small Finance Banks keep one hundred per cent average and one twenty-five single day in Call and Notice.
> 1 C: limits table "SFBs (Call/Notice): 100% avg · 125% day of capital funds" [F6]

N021 · Payment Banks and Regional Rural Banks face that same cap across all three segments combined.
> 1 C: limits table "PBs & RRBs (all 3): 100% avg · 125% day" [F7]

## Chapter 2 · Market Repo and TREPS

N039 · Chapter two. Recall first: if Deccan Bank borrows overnight without collateral, which rung did it use?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Market Repo and TREPS" · 2 C: recall "overnight, unsecured?" · H: 3 s

N040 · Call Money. Now suppose Deccan Bank pledges government securities for cheaper funds. That is Market Repo.
> 1 N: "Call Money ✓" · 2 S: ladder rung 4 "Repo & TREPS · 1d–1yr (secured)" [F17, F23]

N041 · Under Section forty-five U of the RBI Act, a repo is borrowing by selling securities with an agreement to repurchase them.
> 1 C: names-first card "Repo = sell & buy back (borrowing)" [F17]

N042 · The buy-back price includes interest. Mirror rule: one side's repo is always the counterparty's reverse repo, which is lending.
> 1 N: "Borrower = Repo · Lender = Reverse Repo" [F18]

N043 · Market repos follow RBI's November twenty-twenty-five Repo Directions, excluding RBI's L-A-F and M-S-F windows.
> 1 N: "Repo MD 2025 (11 Nov 2025) · excl. LAF/MSF" [F19]

N044 · Tenor is one day to one year. First leg settles on T plus zero or T plus one, always Delivery versus Payment.
> 1 N: "Tenor: 1d–1yr · Leg 1: T+0 or T+1 · DvP" [F23, F24]

N045 · Eligible collateral includes Central and State G-secs, listed corporate bonds, C-Ps, C-Ds, eligible Debt E-T-Fs, and one new entrant.
> 1 C: collateral card "G-Secs · Listed Corp Bonds · CP/CD · Debt ETFs" [F20]

N046 · Since eleven November twenty-twenty-five, Municipal Debt Securities also qualify for repo, with a minimum two per cent haircut.
> 1 N: "+ Municipal Debt Securities (11 Nov 2025, min 2% haircut)" [F20, F25] · 2 G: "Municipal Debt"

N047 · Watch the big repo trap. Can a bank borrow in repo by pledging listed bonds of its own subsidiary?
> 1 N: thought bubble "pledge subsidiary's bonds?" · H: 3 s

N048 · Never. No participant may borrow against its own securities or a related entity's securities — holding, subsidiary, or associate.
> 1 B: strike thought bubble · 2 C: rule card "Own or related-entity securities BARRED" [F21]

N049 · Participants are regulated entities excluding individuals and H-U-Fs, listed corporates, and five A-I-F-Is, including NaBFID.
> 1 N: "Regulated entities, listed corporates, 5 AIFIs (+NaBFID)" [F22]

N050 · Calculator check. Deccan Bank pledges two hundred crore of Municipal Debt Securities at the two per cent minimum haircut.
> 1 S: calculator panel "Municipal bonds ₹200 cr · haircut 2%" [F25]

N051 · Two hundred times two per cent is four crore; two hundred minus four gives one ninety-six crore.
> 1 S: keys "200 × 2 % = 4 · 200 − 4 = 196" · 2 N: "Max borrowing = ₹196 cr"

N052 · Check: two hundred times ninety-eight per cent is one ninety-six. C-Ps and C-Ds carry one-point-five per cent.
> 1 N: "Check: 200 × 98% = 196 · CP/CD min = 1.5%" [F25]

N053 · O-T-C repos report within fifteen minutes: G-secs to C-R-O-M-S, others to F-T-R-A-C. G-sec repo borrowings are C-R-R and S-L-R exempt.
> 1 N: "≤ 15 min: CROMS / F-TRAC · G-sec repo CRR/SLR exempt" [F26]

N054 · Now meet TREPS: the Triparty Repo Dealing System. Here a third party manages collateral between borrower and lender.
> 1 C: names-first card "TREPS = Triparty Repo Dealing System" [F27]

N055 · Clearcorp runs the screen, while C-C-I-L acts as Central Counterparty and Tri-Party Agent.
> 1 S: triangle "Borrower ↔ CCIL (CCP + Agent) ↔ Lender" [F27]

N056 · TREPS replaced the old C-B-L-O on five November twenty-eighteen, with a five-lakh minimum lot against eligible government securities.
> 1 N: "TREPS replaced CBLO (5 Nov 2018) · min ₹5 lakh" [F28] · 2 M: node "Repo & TREPS"

N057 · Pause check. One. Can a listed firm borrow in repo against its subsidiary's bonds?

## Chapter 3 · Commercial Paper and Certificate of Deposit

N061 · Chapter three. Recall first: which new security became eligible for market repo in November twenty-twenty-five?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Commercial Paper and Certificate of Deposit" · 2 C: recall "new repo security Nov 2025?" · H: 3 s

N062 · Municipal Debt Securities. Now let us match Kaveri Textiles with two sibling instruments: C-P and C-D.
> 1 N: "Municipal Debt ✓" · 2 S: ladder rung 5 "CP & CD · 7d–1yr (demat)" [F31, F38]

N063 · Smash a favourite exam trap first. "An A-three rated company can issue a C-D." True or false?
> 1 N: thought bubble "A3 company issues CD?" · H: 3 s

N064 · False. Companies never issue C-Ds. Banks and A-I-F-Is issue C-Ds; companies issue Commercial Paper.
> 1 B: strike thought bubble · 2 C: split card "CP = corporates · CD = banks & AIFIs" [F30, F37]

N065 · Under the twenty-twenty-four Directions, a C-P is an unsecured demat promissory note issued at a discount.
> 1 N: "CP: unsecured, demat, at discount" [F29]

N066 · Tenor is seven days to one year, minimum five lakh; primary settlement within T plus four days.
> 1 N: "CP: 7d–1yr · min ₹5 lakh · primary ≤ T+4" [F31]

N067 · Issuers: companies, N-B-F-Cs, InvITs, R-E-I-Ts, A-I-F-Is, plus co-ops and L-L-Ps with hundred-crore net worth.
> 1 C: issuer card "Companies, NBFCs, InvITs, REITs, AIFIs; Co-ops/LLPs ≥ ₹100 cr NW" [F30]

N068 · General trusts are not listed, and every fund-based facility from banks, A-I-F-Is, and N-B-F-Cs must be Standard.
> 1 N: "Facilities (banks/AIFIs/NBFCs) = Standard" [F30]

N069 · Minimum rating is A-three. Watch this twenty-twenty-four trap: old notes teach dual rating above one thousand crore, taking the lower rating.
> 1 S: trap card "CP Dual Rating (≥ ₹1,000 cr)" [F32]

N070 · That rule belonged to the superseded twenty-seventeen Directions. RBI dropped it in twenty-twenty-four; only the A-three minimum stands.
> 1 B: strike "2017 dual rating" · 2 N: "2024 MD: min A3 only" [F32]

N071 · Every C-P needs an Issuing and Paying Agent, or I-P-A, which must now be a Scheduled Commercial Bank.
> 1 N: "IPA = Scheduled Commercial Bank (2024)" [F33]

N072 · The I-P-A checks documents, routes redemptions by three P-M with no grace period, and reports on F-T-R-A-C.
> 1 N: "IPA verifies · funds by 3 PM · no grace" [F33]

N073 · Second twenty-twenty-four trap: C-P buyback. Old notes say thirty days; today buyback is allowed after seven days.
> 1 S: trap card "CP buyback: Old 30 days → Current 7 days" [F34]

N074 · Buyback goes to all investors on identical terms at market price, through the I-P-A, and extinguishes immediately.
> 1 N: "Buyback ≥ 7 days · all investors · extinguish" [F34]

N075 · Proceeds fund current assets and operating expenses, certified by C-E-O or C-F-O within three months.
> 1 N: "End-use: current assets/opex · CEO/CFO cert ≤ 3 mo" [F35]

N076 · Call or put options, underwriting, and co-acceptance are barred. Related parties cannot buy C-Ps, and default triggers a six-month ban.
> 1 N: "No options · no related-party buyers · 6-mo default ban" [F35, F36]

N077 · Standard status, A-three rating, short tenor, I-P-A checks, and the default ban keep C-P risk low.
> 1 N: "Low credit risk = 5 regulatory filters" [F36]

N078 · Write this down: total primary C-P subscription by all individuals and H-U-Fs combined cannot exceed twenty-five per cent.
> 1 B: box "Individuals + HUFs ≤ 25% of primary CP" [F36]

N079 · Your turn. Kaveri Textiles issues forty crore of C-P. Maximum allocation to individuals and H-U-Fs, and how many five-lakh lots?
> 1 S: data "CP ₹40 cr · 25% cap · lot ₹5 lakh" [F31, F36] · H: 15 s

N080 · Ten crore, or two hundred lots. Forty times twenty-five per cent; one thousand lakh divided by five.
> 1 S: keys "40 × 25 % = 10 · 1000 ÷ 5 = 200" · 2 N: "₹10 cr = 200 lots ✓"

N081 · Now Certificates of Deposit. Only Scheduled Commercial Banks, Regional Rural Banks, and Small Finance Banks issue bank C-Ds.
> 1 C: CD card "Banks: SCBs, RRBs, SFBs · AIFIs under 2025 MD" [F37]

N082 · Bank C-Ds run seven days to one year; A-I-F-I C-Ds run one to three years.
> 1 N: "Tenor: Banks 7d–1yr · AIFIs 1–3 yrs" [F37, F38]

N083 · Minimum five lakh, not one lakh. Demat only, discount or fixed or floating, attracting C-R-R and S-L-R.
> 1 N: "CD: min ₹5 lakh · demat · CRR/SLR applies" [F38, F39]

N084 · Banks cannot lend against C-Ds unless RBI permits. Unilateral premature withdrawal is barred, with no grace period on repayment.
> 1 N: "No loans against CD · no premature withdrawal" [F39, F40]

N085 · Here is tonight's sharpest trap. Many teachers say issuers can never buy back C-Ds. Watch the split.
> 1 S: trap card "CD Buyback: Banks vs AIFIs" [F40]

N086 · Issuing banks may buy back C-Ds after seven days, offered to all investors at market price. A-I-F-Is are strictly barred from CD buyback.
> 1 N: "Banks: allowed ≥ 7 days · AIFIs: PROHIBITED" [F40] · 2 M: node "CP & CD"
