## Findings

**H1 · High · IRM "approach" not taught as a list (B6.6; FP1 Q36) · N091–N096, N113, N152.**
- Evidence: the syllabus heading is "Integrated risk management: necessity, challenges, approach". Plan v3 (6) and
  depth B6.6 [both teachers] give the four-part approach: strategy, organisation, process, systems. The recalled
  FP1 Q36 asks "The process of Integrated Risk Management consists of". The script covers necessity (N092) and
  challenges (N095), but has no labelled "approach" and no list for the learner to pick from four options. Fact
  row 20 names the parts (RMC policy, independent CRO, Board limits, MIS), but not as one list.
- Fix: after N095, add one beat (two lines, a four-tile card): "How do banks do it? Four parts. Strategy and policy,
  set by the Board and the risk committee. Organisation: the committees and an independent C-R-O. Process: identify,
  measure, monitor, control. Systems: data and M-I-S." Write it as a fact row with sources (CRGN02 1.3–1.5, GOV25
  29–31, RMS99 Introduction and 2.2) and label it "concept: the four-part approach". Add it to the ch4 pause check or
  the mixed round, to map node 4, and to N152.

**M1 · Medium · Monitoring and control: no audit layers and no periodic review (plan v3 (4); depth B1.3) · N017,
N033, N076.**
- Evidence: the plan and the depth checklist ask for limits, periodic review, and the audit layers: internal audit,
  concurrent audit and RBI inspection. The third line (N076) says only "audit". A statement MCQ ("concurrent audit is
  a part of…") would go unanswered.
- Fix: one line at N076 ("Audit here means internal audit, concurrent audit at big branches, and above them R-B-I's
  own inspection"), backed by a fact row from RBI's concurrent audit circular. Add "reviewed periodically" to N017,
  from RMS99 "periodical review". If the author keeps audit layers for a later lesson, record that in the ack and in
  `coverage.csv`.

**M2 · Medium · Risk pricing and the six building blocks missing (plan v3 (9); plan chapter 1; depth B1.3).**
- Evidence: the plan promises "the six building blocks" in chapter 1 and "risk-pricing components as one card". The
  script gives RBI's 1999 parameters (N017) and the three credit building blocks (N018) instead. Pricing (cost of
  funds, operating cost, expected loss, capital charge) appears nowhere, so a question asking which is NOT a
  building block, or what a loan price must cover, is unanswered.
- Fix: add one card after N019, worded as our own concept with a fact row: "A loan's price must cover four things:
  cost of funds, operating cost, expected loss, and a charge for the capital it uses. More in B10." Either name the
  six-block list once (organisation, identification, measurement, pricing, monitoring and control, mitigation) or
  record in the ack why RBI's own lists replace it.

**M3 · Medium · Mixed round too easy and not interleaved (checklist 12, 30) · N142–N147.**
- Evidence: Q1 (N142) repeats N019. Q2 (N143) is N025 with "robberies" for "cash in transit". Q3 repeats N060. No
  item tests the true confusables: capacity vs appetite, the three lines, RBS stock vs flow, or historical vs
  hypothetical against sensitivity. Estimated success is about 90–95% against the 80% target. A00's review rated the
  same issue Medium.
- Fix: replace Q1 and Q2 with two harder items, for example:
  - "An internal auditor is asked to run the branch KYC review for a month, then audit it. Fine?" (No: third-line
    staff do not run risk processes; about 75%.)
  - "R-B-I inspects Bank X every year and the larger Bank Y once in three years. Possible?" (Yes: the frequency
    follows the chance and impact of failure, not size; about 70%.)
  - Keep Q4 and Q5.

**L1 · Low · N097.** Say "the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission" once. The exam's
options use the full name. Add it to fact row 25.

**L2 · Low · N116.** Name the family: "three kinds of risk *measures*: sensitivity, volatility, downside potential".
The Jul 2024 stem is "…are part of", so the word "measurement" must be heard and seen. Put it on the cue.

**L3 · Low · N120 or N125.** Add "and banks may use them separately or together" (F26, ST07 Annex). It is the
statement-type hook.

**L4 · Low · N047–N048.** Add the quorum of three and "the Board's Chair can be a member only with risk expertise"
(GOV25 para 21(2)–(3), verified today). Both are classic statement-MCQ distractors.

**L5 · Low · N051.** Name the operational one: "the O-R-M-C, Operational Risk Management Committee". The plan and
depth list it, and the cue branch says only "operational".

**L6 · Low · N058, N135.** Two small gaps:
- The CRO's appointment rule is missing: a senior official with risk qualifications, whose role a Board-approved policy
  defines (F14).
- RBS's use of stress tests and the governance assessment is not linked. One clause in N135 would do it, from the HLSC
  report or a lead, once verified.

**L7 · Low · practice.md Q6.** The distractor "reverse stress test" is not taught and has no fact row (checklist 24).
Either swap it for "risk limit", or add a row once the ST13 annex is read at the freeze.

**L8 · Low · housekeeping.** Two small fixes:
- Header line 34 lists the music bed as out for "N153–N170", but the script ends at N158. Correct the range.
- N098: "COSO defines" should read "COSO defined ERM in two thousand four".
- Also note that the house, dial and walls analogy has no stated limit (checklist 20). One clause would cover it, for
