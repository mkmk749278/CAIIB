# B02 · Review v1 · beginner (independent session, 2026-11-21)

**Verdict: REWORK.** Two High findings, both on terms the learner will confuse in the exam. "Risk committee" is used
for both the Board's committee (R-M-C-B) and the executive one. The lesson also promises "three kinds of what-if
test" but teaches one family with two members. Nine Medium findings follow. Most are one or two lines each. Chapter
2 needs one added check, and one needs a 30-second JAIIB refresher. After the fixes the lesson should reach ADOPT:
the story, the dial, the walls and the chapter checks work well for a beginner.

## Findings

### High

**H1 · "Risk committee" names two different committees** (N042, N045, N050, N051, N068, N093). N045 and N068 call
the R-M-C-B "the Board's risk committee". N050 then introduces "the risk management committee" (the executive one),
and N051 and N093 say only "the risk committee". A beginner hears one name for two bodies. N093 is the worst: "the
risk committee devises the policy for integrated risk management". Which one? The brief flags exactly this pair
(RMCB / RMC). **Fix**: give the executive committee one fixed name and acronym at N050. For example: "Below the
Board's committee sits an executive one, the Risk Management Committee, R-M-C. Executives, led by the C-E-O. Not
directors." Then use "R-M-C-B" or "R-M-C" every time after that, never the bare "risk committee". Add a one-line
contrast card ("RMCB = directors, Board level · RMC = executives, CEO") and one quick question. For example: "The
committee whose members include the C-E-O and the heads of the risk committees. R-M-C-B or R-M-C?" Put this contrast
into map branch 2 (N150) and `map_nodes.md`.

**H2 · Three kinds of test, or two?** (N003, N116–N123, N155, header "Picture"). N003 promises "the three kinds of
what-if test". The header draws three test dials (sensitivity, scenario, stress). In the script, N116 puts three
gauges in the yard for something different: the measures (sensitivity, volatility, downside). N119–N120 then teach
that stress tests have **two** kinds, and N155 says "the two kinds of stress test". So the learner meets
sensitivity/scenario/stress with three different counts, and "sensitivity" in two roles (see M8). **Fix**:
(a) Change N003 to something like "…and the what-if tests: the stress test and its two kinds". (b) Draw the family
tree as the only picture of the tests: stress at the top, sensitivity and scenario below. (c) Keep the three
measure gauges in a separate place, labelled "measures". (d) Update the header picture line to match.

### Medium

**M1 · Three similar lists in a row, with no check** (N015–N019). The learner gets three lists from three R-B-I
notes, one straight after another: the chain (five links), the 1999 "main pieces" (five blocks) and the 2002
building blocks (three bricks). Nothing marks which one matters, and no pause question or map recall ever returns to
the chain or the building blocks (N149 skips them). The learner will mix them up. **Fix**: say which one to keep
("the chain is the process; the bricks are what holds it up") and add one quick question after N019 ("What comes
after monitor and control in the chain?"). Or cut the 1999 list to one line ("and a risk function independent of
the operating departments").

**M2 · COSO shown before it is taught** (N024 cue "COSO: avoid · reduce · share · accept"). COSO is not spoken until
N097. The screen shows an acronym the learner has never heard, and the voice never mentions it. **Fix**: drop the
note at N024 and show it at N103 (the cue already brings back the four boxes there). Or say "These four come from a
framework called COSO. We meet it in chapter four."

**M3 · "Limits" means two things in four lines** (N030, N033). N030 defines capacity as the most risk before the bank
"breaks its capital or liquidity limits". N033 then defines "risk limits" as slices of the appetite, which sit
inside capacity. A beginner cannot tell whether capacity is set by risk limits. **Fix**: at N030, say "…before it
breaks its capital or liquidity requirements, the rules it must meet". Keep "limits" only for the slices (N033).
N032 already says "capital needs".

**M4 · The plan's JAIIB refresher is missing: executive, non-executive and independent directors** (N046–N048). The
plan asks for "how a bank is governed: 30 seconds". The script uses "non-executive directors" (N046) and
"independent directors" (N047, N048) as if the learner knew them. "Mostly outsiders" makes the two sound the same.
Then "at least half are independent" sounds like a separate rule. **Fix**: add a refresher of about 30 seconds
before N045. For example: "Quick refresher from JAIIB. Executive directors work full time in the bank, like the M-D
and C-E-O. Non-executive directors don't. An independent director is a non-executive with no ties to the bank.
Every independent director is non-executive, but not every non-executive is independent." Add a cue for it.

**M5 · Chapter 2 is too dense for one check** (N041–N070; 5.8 min, and over 6 once M4 is added). It brings in about
twelve new names and rules: RMCB (4 rules), RMC, CRMC, CRMD, ALCO, ALM support group, middle office, and the CRO
(reporting line, quarterly meeting, no targets, no dual hatting, tenure, sanction role). There is only one quick
question before the chapter check (N049). **Fix**: split it at N057/N058 into "2 · The committee tree" and
"3 · The C-R-O" (and renumber the chapters). Give the tree its own 2-question check, for example "Who heads the
C-R-M-C?" and "R-M-C-B or R-M-C: which has the C-E-O as a member?" (this also covers H1).

**M6 · The C-R-O in the sanction committee reads as a contradiction** (N062–N064, N090–N091). N062 says "credit risk
management is kept separate from credit sanction". N063 then says the C-R-O may vote. "Only in a set role" is vague,
and "liable" is hard for a learner with English as a second language. The recall at N091 ("only as a set decision
maker") repeats the same fog. **Fix**: N063: "Yes, in one of two roles, fixed in advance. One: a full member with a
vote, and then the C-R-O answers for the decision like every other member. Two: an adviser, invited, with no vote."
N064 stays as it is. N091: "Only as a full voting member, sharing the responsibility. Otherwise an adviser, no vote."

**M7 · COSO objectives and components are never told apart, and "reporting" sits in both** (N100–N109). N100 says
"two lists" but never says what an objective is or what a component is. The 2017 component N107 is "Information,
communication and **reporting**". So the check at N109 ("is 'reporting' an objective or a component?") can trap a
careful learner. The brief flags this pair. **Fix**: at N100 add a short contrast: "Objectives are what the firm
wants to achieve. Components are the parts of the risk process that get it there." In N110's answer add: "In two
thousand four, reporting is an objective. The component is information and communication."

**M8 · "Sensitivity" in two senses, five lines apart** (N116–N117 and N121). In N117 sensitivity is a measure ("a
small move in one factor"). In N121 it is a stress test ("a big shift" in one variable). The learner is not told
these are related, or how they differ. **Fix**: one line after N121: "Same idea as the sensitivity measure, one
factor at a time. But in a stress test the move is big and unlikely." (This also helps H2.)

**M9 · The trap question contradicts itself** (N146). "A test **replays** a sharp downturn that hasn't happened yet."
In N126 "replays" was the word for the historical case, so this question teaches the wrong signal. **Fix**: "A test
imagines a sharp downturn that has never happened, with many variables moving together. Which kind of test?"
