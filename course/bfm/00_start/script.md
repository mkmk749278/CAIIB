## Opening

N01 · Let me start with a story. It's made up, but I'm sure you know someone like this. An officer, let's call him a

N02 · He studied every evening for three months. He read the whole book, chapter by chapter, giving every chapter

N03 · Four marks short. So here's my question to you. Was his problem effort, or aim? Just think about it for a second.

N04 · I know what many of you are thinking. "Effort. He should have studied more." And a few of you are thinking,

N05 · The answer is aim. Three months of evenings is a lot of effort. But B-F-M doesn't ask every chapter equally.
Some topics come in almost every exam. Some come once in three years. If you give them the same time, you lose marks
you could have had. So today, we fix the aim.

## Chapter 1 · The paper

N06 · First, let's look at the paper itself. B-F-M, Bank Financial Management, is one of the compulsory CAIIB papers.

N07 · One hundred questions. One hundred marks. Two hours. All objective, online.

N08 · And here's something people forget. Some questions come as case studies. A small story, a bank's figures, and
then three, four, five questions on the same story. So one case can carry five marks.

N09 · And some numerical questions have no options at all. You calculate, and you type the answer. No guessing. So
your sums have to be exact.

N10 · Now, the good news. There's no negative marking. A wrong answer costs you nothing. So never leave a question
blank. Ever.

N11 · To pass, you need fifty out of a hundred. Or, if you clear all your papers in one attempt, at least forty-five
in each and fifty percent on average. Either way, think of fifty as your target, and sixty as your safe zone.

N12 · One more rule, and this one is a trap. Which rules does the exam follow? R-B-I changes rules all the time. So
I-I-B-F fixes a date. For exams from March to August, it's the rules up to the thirty-first of December before. For
exams from September to February, it's the rules up to the thirtieth of June.

N13 · So for the May-June twenty twenty-seven exam, everything in this course is taught as it stood on the
thirty-first of December, twenty twenty-six. And every lesson tells you that date on the first screen. If a rule was
announced but starts later, I'll say so clearly: "issued, coming".

N14 · Okay. Everyone with me so far? Good. Quick stretch if you need it. Now the important part.

## Chapter 2 · Where the marks are

N15 · How do we know which topics matter? Not by guessing. After every exam, candidates share what they were asked.
We collected those recollections from seven exams, June twenty twenty-three to June twenty twenty-six. Four hundred
and fifty-three questions and topics.

N16 · Then we tagged every one to the official I-I-B-F syllabus and counted. In how many exams did this topic come?
How often? Was it a sum or a case study?

N17 · Here's what came out. Look at the four maps on your screen. Module A, International Banking, about thirty-nine
percent of what was recalled. Module B, Risk Management, about thirty-five percent.

N18 · Module C, Treasury, about sixteen percent. And Module D, Balance Sheet Management, about eleven.

N19 · Now be careful here, because this is where people slip. These are shares of what people remembered, not of
marks. People remember sums and strange facts. They forget plain theory.

N20 · We checked one full memory-based paper from December twenty twenty-five, all hundred questions. Treasury had
twenty-seven of them, not sixteen. Mostly plain theory. So, Treasury is bigger in the exam than in people's memory.
Don't skip it.

N21 · Next, the topics. We sorted them into three tiers. Tier A means it comes in almost every exam. These you must
master. Tier B, know them well. Tier C, know the basics.

N22 · And here's the list that should guide your evenings. Tier A, twelve topics. Forex sums. Capital adequacy sums.
The family of risks. The Basel framework. N-P-A and provisioning. Bonds, duration and VaR.

N23 · Letters of credit and UCP. Treasury products like T-Bills. Export and import finance. N-R-I accounts. L-R-S.
And credit risk.

N24 · Forex sums and capital adequacy sums came in all seven exams we checked. Seven out of seven. So if you're
short of time, you know where to start.

N25 · And the mix? Most exams are about sixty to seventy percent theory, and thirty to forty percent numericals. In
some shifts, close to half were sums or cases. So you can't pass B-F-M on theory alone, and you can't pass it on sums
alone.

## Chapter 3 · Your calculator

N26 · Now the calculator. This one matters more than people think.

N27 · I-I-B-F allows only a simple battery calculator. Up to eight functions: plus, minus, multiply, divide, percent,
square root, and the two tax keys. Twelve digits at most.

N28 · A scientific calculator is not allowed. It's treated as unfair means. So please, don't practise on your phone's
scientific calculator. Practise on the exact kind you'll carry.

N29 · Some of you are thinking, "But then how do I do powers? One point zero five to the power four?" Simple.
Multiply it by itself, step by step. One point zero five, into one point zero five, into one point zero five, into
one point zero five. Many basic calculators also repeat the last multiply each time you press equals. Try it on yours
tonight, so you know how your own calculator behaves.

N30 · And the square root key is your friend. When we scale volatility from one day to ten days, it's one key.

N31 · One more habit we'll use all through the course. When you add or take off a margin, use a multiplier. To take
off zero point one five percent, multiply by zero point nine nine eight five. To add zero point one percent, multiply
by one point zero zero one.

N32 · Why? Because the percent key behaves differently on different calculators, and a multiplier is the same on
every one of them. In every sum in this course, you'll see the exact keys on screen. Press along with me.

## Chapter 4 · How to use this course

N33 · Last part. How to use these classes.

N34 · The course follows the four modules in order. A first, the money crossing the bank's counter. Then B, what can
go wrong. Then C, the treasury desk. Then D, the balance sheet where it all shows up. Each module leans on the one
before, so go in order the first time.

N35 · Each module has one mind map. The same layout every time, so you remember where a topic lives. It grows a
little in every class. At the end of the module, you'll see it complete.

N36 · Every class starts with a real situation from a branch, then the rule as the answer. Every sum is worked once
fully, then once with a few steps left for you, then once all on your own.

N37 · When I ask a question, pause the video and answer it first. Out loud if you can. That small effort is what
makes you remember. Watching is not studying. Answering is.

N38 · And each module comes with a sheet: the map, the formulas with an example each, the traps, and one page for the
night before the exam.
