# A20 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Eight original exam-style questions (6 MCQs + 2 keyed-in numericals) written strictly from A20_fact_sheet (rules as at 31 December 2026) and A20_numericals. All bank names, company names, and figures are illustrative. Options use real exam traps (old RBI regulator, 2019 start-date myth, DICGC/LOLR confusion, old 1-month exporter FCA rule, standard <50% FPI cap). Answers with one-line reasons citing [F#] appear at the end.
1. Under the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 and FEM (IFSC) Regulations, 2015, which statement accurately describes an IFSC Banking Unit (IBU) operating at GIFT City, Gandhinagar? (a) It is treated as a person resident in India under FEMA and exempt from all domestic Indian laws (b) It is treated as a person resident outside India for FEMA purposes, while domestic Indian laws continue to apply unless specifically exempted (c) The Central Government may approve up to three IFSCs inside a single Special Economic Zone (d) It is regulated jointly by RBI for banking and SEBI for derivatives
2. Which set of requirements applies to an Indian scheduled commercial bank setting up an IFSC Banking Unit (IBU) as a branch in GIFT City under the IFSCA (Banking) Regulations, 2020 (as amended)? (a) Licence from RBI under s. 23 of the BR Act, USD 50 million capital, and mandatory CRR/SLR (b) Licence from IFSCA, minimum USD 20 million regulatory capital maintained at parent bank level, No Objection Letter from the home regulator, and a parent liquidity undertaking (Letter of Comfort) (c) Licence from IFSCA, minimum USD 10 million capital held in INR, and mandatory DICGC insurance (d) Licence from Ministry of Commerce, USD 20 million capital, and RBI Lender of Last Resort liquidity backstop
3. Under the IFSCA (Banking) Regulations, 2020 (as amended up to July 2024), which of the following statements regarding IBU accounts and operations is CORRECT? (a) Cash transactions up to USD 10,000 per day are permitted in individual foreign currency accounts (b) IBUs may transact in 11 specified foreign currencies and may participate in the Mumbai domestic call money market (c) Individuals may hold current, savings, or term deposits in 15 specified foreign currencies, whereas non-individuals may hold current or term deposits only, with cash transactions strictly prohibited (d) IBUs are barred from opening any INR account even for defraying their own administrative expenses
4. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), what tax holiday is available to an eligible Unit of an International Financial Services Centre under Section 147 (formerly Section 80LA)? (a) 50% deduction of eligible income for 5 consecutive tax years (b) 100% deduction of eligible income for any 10 consecutive tax years out of 15 years at the option of the assessee (c) 100% deduction for the first 5 years and 50% for the next 5 years, with no option to choose the window (d) Complete permanent tax exemption with no sunset or time limit
5. Under SEBI's 2024 framework for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) based in GIFT City IFSC and regulated by IFSCA, what is the maximum aggregate contribution permitted from NRIs, OCIs, and Resident Indian individuals in the corpus of an eligible IFSC FPI? (a) Below 25% of the corpus (b) Below 50% of the corpus (c) Up to 74% of the corpus (d) Up to 100% of the corpus, subject to Option A (constituent PAN/KYC disclosures) or Option B (blind-pool diversification conditions)
6. Following RBI's regulatory updates of October 2025 (FEMA 10(R)(7)/2025-RB) and October 2026 (A.P. DIR Circular No. 27), which pair of rules applies as at 31 December 2026? (a) Exporter FCA in IFSC: 1-month repatriation window; AD Cat-I with IBU may offer INR NDDCs to non-residents without purpose restriction (b) Exporter FCA in IFSC: 3-month repatriation window; AD Cat-I with IBU may offer INR NDDCs to non-residents ONLY for hedging (c) Exporter FCA in IFSC: 6-month repatriation window; AD Cat-I with IBU is completely banned from offering INR NDDCs (d) Exporter FCA in IFSC: 3-month repatriation window; AD Cat-I with IBU may offer INR NDDCs to non-residents for both hedging and speculation
7. (Keyed-in numerical) An IBU holds USD 15,000,000 in corporate term deposits, USD 3,500,000 in NRI individual savings deposits, and USD 2,500,000 in resident individual LRS term deposits outstanding at the end of the previous working day. Calculate today's mandatory daily Retail Deposit Reserve Ratio (RDRR) in USD.
8. (Keyed-in numerical) An IBU's parent bank has Tier 1 capital of USD 1,500 million. The IBU currently has loans of USD 65 million to Company X and USD 45 million to its group affiliate Company Y. Calculate the maximum additional loan (in USD million) the IBU may sanction to a third company in the same borrower group without breaching the IBU borrower-group exposure ceiling.

Answers and reasons
1. (b) — Under FEMA 339/2015-RB Reg 3, an IFSC unit is treated as a person resident outside India for FEMA, while all domestic Indian laws continue to apply unless specifically exempted [F1, F2, F4, F14].
2. (b) — Under IFSCA (Banking) Regulations, 2020 Reg 3(3), setting up an IBU branch requires an IFSCA licence, USD 20 million minimum capital maintained at parent level, a home-regulator NOC, and a parent liquidity undertaking/Letter of Comfort [F15, F24, F25].
3. (c) — Under Reg 12(1)–(2) and the July 2024 Amendment, IBUs operate in 15 specified foreign currencies with cash strictly prohibited; individuals may hold current, savings, or term accounts, while non-individuals hold current or term accounts only [F30, F31, F32].
4. (b) — Under Section 147(1)(b) & (2)(b) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly s. 80LA(1A)), an IFSC unit receives a 100% deduction for any 10 consecutive tax years out of 15 years at its option [F9].
5. (d) — SEBI's 2024 framework allows GIFT City IFSC-based FPIs regulated by IFSCA to accept up to 100% aggregate NRI/OCI/RI contribution under Option A or Option B (vs the standard <50% cap) [F38].
6. (b) — Notification FEMA 10(R)(7)/2025-RB (6 Oct 2025) set a 3-month repatriation window for exporters' FCAs in IFSCs, and A.P. DIR Circular No. 27 (12 Oct 2026) restricted INR NDDCs by AD Cat-I banks with an IBU to non-residents strictly to hedging [F40, F41].
7. USD 180,000 — Only individual deposits count toward RDRR (3,500,000 + 2,500,000 = 6,000,000; corporate USD 15M is exempt); 6,000,000 × 3% = USD 180,000 [F26, F27].
8. USD 40 million — Group exposure ceiling is 10% of parent Tier 1 capital (1,500 × 10% = USD 150 million); existing group exposure is 65 + 45 = USD 110 million; headroom is 150 − 110 = USD 40 million [F29].
