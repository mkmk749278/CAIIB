# A08 · Practice (c3, 2026-11-21). Every item is taught in the lesson.

Our own items. Names, places and amounts are illustrative. Each answer cites the UCP 600 article (via the fact sheet
`research/bfm/facts/A4.1-A4.8_A08.md`, row in brackets) or marks "practice" where UCP has no article.

## Story questions (name it / who bears it)

**P1.** A Chennai importer buys the same quantity of PVC resin every month for a year. Instead of opening twelve
credits, his bank opens one credit of USD 40,000 that reinstates itself each month. This is a:
(a) transferable credit (b) **revolving credit** (c) back-to-back credit (d) standby credit
*Reason: an amount that reinstates by time or value is a revolving credit (practice) [F17].*

**P2.** A Rotterdam buyer's credit carries a red clause. The nominated bank in Tiruppur advances USD 10,000 to the
exporter before shipment; she never ships. Who finally bears the loss?
(a) the nominated bank (b) the advising bank (c) **the issuing bank, which recovers from the applicant** (d) ECGC
*Reason: the issuing bank undertakes to repay the red-clause advance, at the applicant's request and risk (practice) [F17].*

**P3.** A Mumbai trader holds a Dubai credit that does **not** say "transferable". His bank opens a fresh credit in
favour of his Coimbatore supplier, on the strength of the Dubai credit. This is a:
(a) transferred credit (b) **back-to-back credit** (c) red clause credit (d) confirmed credit
*Reason: a credit not stated to be transferable cannot be transferred (Art. 38(b)) [F14]; a second, separate credit is back-to-back (practice) [F16].*

**P4.** The Dubai credit **is** transferable, and the trader wants to split it between two mills. Which is correct?
(a) any credit may be split, if the issuing bank agrees later
(b) **it is possible only if the credit allows partial drawings or shipments**
(c) each mill may transfer its part again to its own supplier
(d) the unit price may be raised for each mill
*Reason: transfer to more than one second beneficiary needs partial drawings/shipments allowed; no onward transfer; price may only be reduced (Art. 38(d), (g)) [F14].*

## Concept questions

**P5.** An amendment raises the amount and extends the expiry. The beneficiary accepts the amount and refuses the
new expiry. Result?
(a) the higher amount takes effect (b) **the amendment as a whole stands rejected** (c) it takes effect unless she
objects within five banking days (d) the applicant chooses which part applies
*Reason: partial acceptance of an amendment is a rejection; a deemed-acceptance clause is disregarded (Art. 10(e), (f)) [F11].*

**P6.** Under which credit does the bank undertake to pay on a future date **without** accepting a bill of exchange?
(a) sight payment (b) acceptance (c) **deferred payment** (d) negotiation
*Reason: deferred payment = incur a deferred payment undertaking and pay at maturity; acceptance needs a draft (Art. 2) [F4].*

**P7.** A credit is available with an Indian bank by negotiation. That bank has not confirmed it. Which is correct?
(a) it must negotiate, as the nominated bank
(b) it is liable once it receives and forwards the documents
(c) **it has no obligation to negotiate unless it expressly agrees and tells the beneficiary**
(d) as advising bank it guarantees payment
*Reason: a non-confirming nominated bank has no obligation; examining and forwarding documents does not make it liable (Art. 12(a), (c)); an advising bank gives no undertaking (Art. 9(a)) [F10, F12].*

**P8.** A standby letter of credit:
(a) is governed only by URR 725
(b) is paid when the goods arrive at the port
(c) **works like a guarantee, is paid on a statement of default, and may be issued subject to ISP98 or UCP 600**
(d) is revocable unless it states otherwise
*Reason: UCP 600 applies to standby credits to the extent applicable (Art. 1); ISP98 is ICC Publication 590 [F1, F18]; credits are irrevocable (Art. 3) [F3].*
