## Three things I'd remember
1. Recourse decides who carries the risk. Factoring: many small invoices, short, with or without recourse, part paid
   now, ledger and collection included; banks need no registration, NBFC-Factors do; TReDS for MSME sellers, without
   recourse.
2. Forfaiting: one big deferred deal, avalised notes, always without recourse, fixed rate, up to 100 %; commitment fee
   usually not refunded.
3. Gold Card is the bank's (3-year limits, standby ≥ 20 %, not for SAL or overdues > 10 %); status holder is DGFT's
   (3 / 15 / 50 / 200 / 800 USD m); EEFC pays no interest and nothing is lent against it.

## Findings

### Medium

**M1 · N021, N034 (and N037) · The 360-day year is never explained, and there is no error-analysis line.** A12 and
A13 drilled ÷ 365. Here "a three-sixty-day year" appears with no reason. A beginner will remember 365 from two lessons
of practice. The commonest wrong answers are ÷ 365 and interest on the full invoice instead of the advance.
- N021, after "a three-sixty-day year": add new line **N021a** · "Why three sixty? Dollar interest counts a year as
  three hundred sixty days. Rupee interest, as in the last two classes, uses three sixty-five."
  Cue: `C: card "USD (and most foreign currencies): ÷ 360 · ₹: ÷ 365 (A12, A13)" [numericals note]`.
- N037, append: "If you got a hundred eighty-seven thousand three hundred seventy-five, you divided by three
  sixty-five. Dollars use three sixty." (200,000 × 6.4 % × 360 ÷ 365 = 12,624.66 → 187,375.34.)
- N022, append: "Interest is on the advance, forty-eight thousand, not on sixty." (60,000 × 7 % × 90 ÷ 360 = 1,050
  is the trap answer; show it crossed out.)

**M2 · N020–N022 · The factoring sum asks three things in one hold, and never says what is asked.** N021 opens with
the answer to N020, then gives the fee and the interest terms, then a 10 s hold, but no question: fee? interest?
balance? Fee + interest + subtraction is about 20 key presses. Split:

  the advance, ninety days, a three-sixty-day year." (then N021a from M1) · no hold.
- N021b · "When the buyers pay, the factor keeps its advance, fee and interest. What does she get then?" · H: 15 s.
- N022 · "Fee, three hundred. Interest, eight hundred forty. She gets ten thousand eight hundred sixty more."
