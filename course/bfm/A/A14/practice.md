1. Under the Factoring Regulation Act 2011 (as amended), "factoring business" means:
   (a) any loan by a bank against the security of book debts (b) acquisition of receivables by assignment, for a

   (d) buying goods for resale on credit

   (d) the import factor's bank
3. A bank factors a seller's receivables **without recourse**. Its exposure is reckoned on: (a) the assignor (b) the
   debtor (c) the bank's own capital (d) the seller's working-capital bank
4. In **international** factoring where the import factor has taken the credit risk, exposure is on: (a) the exporter
   (b) the overseas buyer (c) the import factor (d) ECGC
5. Which of these may undertake factoring **without** a certificate of registration under the Factoring Regulation
   Act? (a) any NBFC-ICC (b) a bank (c) a partnership firm (d) an NBFC-MFI

7. The minimum net owned fund for an NBFC-Factor by 31 March 2027 is: (a) ₹2 crore (b) ₹5 crore (c) ₹7 crore
   (d) ₹10 crore
8. On TReDS: (a) any company may be the seller (b) factoring units are discounted with recourse to the seller
   (c) the seller must be an MSME and discounting is without recourse to the seller (d) only one financier may bid
9. The 1988 RBI study group on introducing factoring in India was chaired by: (a) M. Narasimham (b) C. S.
   Kalyanasundaram (c) Y. V. Reddy (d) U. K. Sinha
10. Forfaiting is best described as: (a) short-term, with recourse, many invoices (b) medium-term, without recourse,
    usually one large deal backed by avalised bills or an LC (c) a packing-credit scheme (d) an ECGC cover

    (c) a commitment fee (d) the forfaiter's discount
12. Which is **NOT** usually true of forfaiting? (a) fixed-rate discount (b) without recourse to the exporter
    (c) includes sales-ledger management (d) can finance up to 100 % of the receivable
13. A forfaiter commits to buy an exporter's notes; the export contract is cancelled. The commitment fee is usually:
    (a) refunded in full (b) refunded less 10 % (c) not refunded (d) adjusted against the discount
14. Factoring: invoices USD 80,000; advance 80 %; fee 0.5 % of invoices; interest 6 % a year on the advance for 90
    days (360-day year). What does the factor pay on collection?
15. Forfaiting: a note of USD 300,000 due in 180 days; discount 7.2 % a year (360-day year). What does the exporter
    receive now?
16. Commitment fee 0.75 % a year on USD 2,000,000 for 60 days (360-day year). How much?
17. The Gold Card scheme is **not** available to an exporter: (a) in the MSME sector (b) on ECGC's Specific Approval
    List (c) with overdue bills of 6 % of the previous year's turnover (d) with a 3-year track record

    (d) 5 years

    days (c) 25 / 10 / 5 days (d) 15 / 7 / 3 days
21. The rate of interest under the Gold Card scheme must be: (a) at least 1 % below MCLR (b) not more than the bank's
    general rate for export credit (c) fixed by RBI (d) the repo rate

    (b) USD 50 million (c) USD 200 million (d) USD 800 million

    only (d) none
24. Which is **NOT** a status-holder privilege? (a) exemption from compulsory negotiation of documents through banks
    (b) exemption from bank guarantees under FTP schemes (c) receiving export proceeds outside banking channels

    the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off this is: (a) true for status holders only (b) true for all exporters at 5 % (c) superseded:

26. Which is true of an EEFC account? (a) it pays interest like a savings account (b) the bank may give an overdraft
    against it (c) it is a non-interest current account and no credit facility is allowed against its balance

    be reported to RBI (d) attract TCS

    (a) the end of the next month (b) three months (c) six months (d) nine months
29. A Diamond Dollar Account: (a) any currency, interest-bearing (b) US dollars only, current account, up to 5 accounts
    (c) only for importers of gold (d) up to 10 accounts

    (a) factoring with recourse (b) forfaiting (c) TReDS (d) packing credit

    invoice value (d) the agency commission
