# A06 practice (c2, 2026-11-21; from the examiner review draft)

Own questions; all quotes, points, margins and interest rates illustrative. Rates to 4 decimals (round half up);
rupees to paise. Basic 8-function calculator; key presses shown. Purchase / sale always from the **bank's** side.
Margins as in the lesson: TT selling +0.12% (`× 1.0012`), TT buying −0.08% (`× 0.9992`). Points: 1m 0.18 / 0.20
(premium, add).

**P1 (rate, MCQ).** A forward **sale** contract is cancelled at the customer's request one month before maturity.
The bank applies:
(a) spot TT selling (b) spot TT buying (c) **one-month forward TT buying** (d) the contract rate
Answer (c): a sale contract is cancelled by the bank buying back (TT buying), and before maturity at the forward TT
rate (FEDAI 6.4(ii)(b), (c)). (a) is the contract-side trap; (b) forgets "before maturity".

**P2 (cancellation on maturity, no options).** Exporter's purchase contract USD 25,000 @ 85.80, cancelled on
maturity; spot 86.10 / 86.12. Exchange difference?
Answer **exporter pays ₹10,582.50**: `86.12 × 1.0012 =` 86.223344 → 86.2233; `86.2233 − 85.80 = × 25000 =`.
(TT buying 86.0311 gives ₹5,777.50: the contract-side trap.)

**P3 (the lesson's homework, no options).** Sum one again (purchase contract USD 1,00,000 @ 86.05, cancelled on
maturity), but spot is 85.90 / 85.92. Who pays, and how much?
Answer **the bank pays the exporter ₹2,690.00**: `85.92 × 1.0012 =` 86.023104 → 86.0231; `86.05 − 86.0231 = ×
100000 =`. The bank sells back below the price it bought at, so the difference is hers.

**P4 (early delivery, MCQ).** Exporter's purchase contract USD 50,000 @ 86.00, due in one month, delivered today.
Interbank spot 85.60 / 85.62; 1m points 0.18 / 0.20; interest on outlay 9% a year. Rupees paid to her:
(a) ₹43,00,000.00 (b) ₹42,89,000.00 (c) **₹42,88,850.00** (d) ₹43,11,150.00
Answer (c): swap `85.62 + 0.20 = − 85.60 = × 50000 =` ₹11,000 cost; outlay `86.00 − 85.60 = × 50000 =` ₹20,000;
interest `20000 × 0.09 ÷ 12 =` ₹150; `4300000 − 11000 − 150 =`. (a) ignores both; (b) forgets interest; (d) adds them.

**P5 (who keeps the gain, MCQ).** A forward contract matures; the customer sends no instructions and simply forgot.
The bank cancels it two working days later; the difference is ₹9,000 in his favour. The bank:
(a) pays ₹9,000 (b) pays ₹9,000 less swap cost (c) **pays nothing, and recovers any swap cost from him**
(d) must first rebook the contract
Answer (c): after maturity the customer is not entitled to a gain but bears any loss; the bank may pass a gain only if
the delay was beyond his control, on record (FEDAI 6.4(iv)). Forgetting is not beyond his control.

**P6 (rollover on maturity, no options).** Exporter's purchase contract USD 30,000 @ 86.10 matures today; she asks
for a one-month rollover. Spot 85.70 / 85.72. (a) Amount now? (b) New contract rate?
Answer **(a) she receives ₹8,313.00**: `85.72 × 1.0012 =` 85.822864 → 85.8229; `86.10 − 85.8229 = × 30000 =`.
**(b) 85.8113**: a new purchase at one-month forward TT buying, `85.70 + 0.18 = × 0.9992 =` 85.811296.
Never the old 86.10 (rollover only at the current rate, FEDAI 6.3); 86.0231 is forward TT selling, the wrong side.

**P7 (Oct 2026 rules, MCQ).** Which statement is **incorrect** as at 31 Dec 2026?
(a) A user may hedge up to USD 5 million without proving the underlying, across all ADs
(b) The AD takes an undertaking that the exposure is not hedged with another AD
(c) **A contract cancelled in November 2026 may be rebooked with another AD at the current rate**
(d) A rollover on the maturity date is still allowed
Answer (c): RBI's 10 Oct 2026 directions bar rebooking a cancelled INR forex derivative with any AD; (a), (b), (d)
are correct (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 25).

**P8 (exceptions, MCQ).** A Government order bans the import a forward sale contract was booked for. The contract:
(a) becomes void, and nothing is payable (b) is cancelled by the bank on maturity, gain passed
(c) **still stands; the customer must apply to cancel it, and the exchange difference is settled**
(d) is extended automatically
Answer (c): FEDAI 6.4(iii): impossibility, even by Government order, does not make the contract void.
