## Verdict: ADOPT WITH FIXES

Every rule, article number, weekday and conclusion in the script is right except the framing of Q5 and the ISP98
"three to seven" contrast, which can mislead on an exam option. One fact-sheet row (F5) and one trap line contradict the
(correct) script and must be fixed so later lessons and the PDF do not inherit them.

## Findings

### High
None.

### Medium

1. **N033–N035 (Q5) does not answer its own question "Who bears that?"** What the script says is correct: a confirming
   bank's without-recourse undertaking (Art. 8(a)(ii)) covers only a complying presentation, so documents that do not
   comply fall outside the confirmation. But the learner never hears the answer an IIBF option list wants: **the
   Singapore bank bears it**, because Chennai, having refused in time, owes it nothing; reimbursement under Art. 7(c)
   is only for a complying presentation. Then, secondarily, whether it can claw back from the seller depends on how it
   paid (under reserve, or against her indemnity, or its negotiation agreement with recourse); if it paid outright
   without reserve, it is generally left with the loss. Fix N035 (or add a line), e.g.: "These didn't. So Chennai owes
   Singapore nothing: reimbursement is only for complying documents. Singapore carries it, unless it paid her under
   reserve or against her indemnity; then it can claim the money back from her. That's why checkers take insurance dates
   seriously." Card N035: "discrepant docs → no reimbursement (Art. 7c) · Singapore bears it · recourse to seller only
   if paid under reserve / indemnity / its agreement".

2. **Fact sheet F5 and the trap list contradict the script (and the rule).** F5 says the nominated bank recovers from the
   beneficiary "unless it confirmed (confirming bank negotiates without recourse)". For **discrepant** documents that is
   wrong: Art. 8(a)(ii) is conditional on a complying presentation, so confirmation gives no shield to either side.
   Trap line "A confirming bank that negotiated can recover from the beneficiary (no: without recourse)" has the same gap.
   Fix F5: "…the nominated bank is not reimbursed (Art. 7(c) covers complying presentations only). Its recourse to the
   beneficiary is a matter of its terms with her (under reserve / indemnity / with-recourse negotiation; practice, not
   UCP). A confirming bank's 'without recourse' (Art. 8(a)(ii)) protects the beneficiary **only for a complying
   presentation**." Fix the trap: "A confirming bank that negotiated **complying** documents can recover from the
   beneficiary (no: without recourse)." N057 already uses "complying" correctly; keep it.

3. **N052 "I-S-P ninety-eight gives three to seven" misstates Rule 5.01.** ISP98 sets no window of three to seven; the
   rule is notice "within a time which is not unreasonable", with two deemed points: within 3 business days is deemed
   not unreasonable, beyond 7 is deemed unreasonable; days 4–7 turn on the facts. "Gives three to seven" invites a
   learner to tick "7 business days allowed" on an exam. N051 is right; fix N052: "U-C-P six hundred: a maximum of five
   banking days. I-S-P ninety-eight: a reasonable time; three is safe, more than seven is late." Box B: "UCP ≤ 5
   banking days" / "ISP: reasonable · ≤ 3 safe · > 7 late". (Also note on screen: "banking days" vs "business days".)

4. **Q2 leaves a trap open: documents reach Chennai on 3 June, after the 21-day limit (2 June).** A careful learner (and
   an IIBF distractor: "documents are stale; the issuing bank may refuse") will ask whether that is late. It is not: the
   beneficiary presented to the nominated (confirming) bank on 31 May, within Art. 14(c) and expiry; the issuing bank's
   own five days run from its receipt of the forwarded documents (Art. 14(b), 15(c), 7(c)); the transit time is not the
   beneficiary's presentation. Add to N026 or N019 one line, e.g. "And 3 June is after the second? Doesn't matter: she
   presented to the Singapore bank on the thirty-first. Chennai's five days start when the documents reach it." Same
   point gives a better answer to the plan's trap list.

### Low

5. **N024 "Only a court can stop payment, and only for clear fraud."** Fair simplification, but Indian case law says
   "established fraud of which the bank has notice, or irretrievable injustice" (F3, A08-F22). Suggest "only for
   established fraud, or an injustice that can't be undone". Optional: since the "used, not new" story could sound like
   fraud (an invoice saying "new"), add "and a rumour on the phone is not established fraud", or make the complaint a
   plain quality one ("poor finish, wrong tolerance").

6. **N032 / close: the cure route is shut, and saying so adds an exam point.** Under F4 the beneficiary could correct and
   re-present, but here the 21 days ended on 2 June, so she cannot. One clause in N032 ("and she can't re-present: her
   twenty-one days ended on the second") closes the case cleanly.

7. **N047 "The mills are the end of the line."** Correct for onward transfer; Art. 38(d) still allows transfer back to the
   first beneficiary (not a "subsequent" beneficiary). Not needed in voice; add "(transfer back to the first beneficiary
   is allowed)" in the PDF / trap card note.

8. **N050 card** cites ISP 4.17 only; the "does not investigate" half is Rule 1.06 (and 1.08). Card: "ISP98 1.06:
   documentary, independent · 4.17: statement · dated · signed → complies".

9. **N019 arithmetic**: say the step: "Twelve plus twenty-one is thirty-three; May has thirty-one days, so the second of
   June." Matches the calculator-and-steps rule.
