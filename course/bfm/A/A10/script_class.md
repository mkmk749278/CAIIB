## Chapter 1 · Read the case

N005 · Chapter one. Here's the case. Read along with me.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Read the case" · 2 S: case card slides in, left third (illustrative)

N006 · Anand, an importer in Chennai, buys a C-N-C lathe from a seller in Singapore. Eighty thousand dollars, C-I-F Chennai.
> 1 S: case line 1 "Applicant: Anand, Chennai · CNC lathe · USD 80,000 · CIF Chennai"

N007 · His bank in Chennai opens an irrevocable L-C, subject to U-C-P six hundred. It's available by negotiation with a bank in Singapore.
> 1 S: case line 2 "Issuing bank: Chennai · UCP 600 · available by negotiation, Singapore"

N008 · At the Chennai bank's request, the Singapore bank adds its confirmation.
> 1 S: case line 3 "Confirming and negotiating bank: Singapore"

N009 · The L-C asks for an original bill of lading, an invoice, and insurance for a hundred and ten per cent. Latest shipment, Friday the fourteenth of May. Expiry, Tuesday the fifteenth of June.
> 1 S: case line 4 "B/L (original) · invoice · insurance 110% · latest shipment Fri 14 May · expiry Tue 15 Jun 2027"

N010 · Before the questions, a thirty-second checklist. Do it on every case. One: is it subject to U-C-P six hundred?
> 1 C: checklist card, five empty boxes · 2 B: tick box 1 "UCP 600? ✓"

N011 · Two: name every bank, and its job. Three: how is it paid? Four: the three dates. Five: what exactly is asked?
> 1 B: boxes 2–5 fill "banks and jobs · how paid · three dates · what is asked?" [F13]

N012 · Quick one. In this case, who is the beneficiary?
> 1 G: the case card · H: 3 s

N013 · The Singapore seller. The one who gets paid under the L-C. And Anand is the applicant.
> 1 S: the relay; four figures labelled "Anand (applicant) · Chennai bank (issuing) · Singapore bank (confirming, negotiating) · seller (beneficiary)"

N014 · Now the rest of the case. The seller ships on Wednesday the twelfth of May. The bill of lading shows that date on board.
> 1 S: case line 5 "on board: Wed 12 May"

N015 · She presents the documents to the Singapore bank on Monday the thirty-first of May. It finds them complying, pays her, and sends them to Chennai.
> 1 S: case line 6 "presented Mon 31 May · Singapore bank negotiates, pays, forwards"

N016 · They reach Chennai on Thursday the third of June. The Chennai checker spots one thing: the insurance is dated thirteenth of May. The day after shipment.
> 1 S: case line 7 "reach Chennai Thu 3 Jun · insurance dated 13 May" · 2 B: circle "13 May"

N017 · And Anand phones his bank. He's heard the lathe is a used one, not new. He says, don't pay.
> 1 S: case line 8 "Anand: 'used machine; don't pay'"

N018 · Pause check for chapter one. Before any question: was her presentation in time? Shipment twelfth of May, presentation thirty-first.
> 1 S: the A09 calendar strip, May 2027 · H: 8 s

N019 · Yes. Twelve plus twenty-one is the second of June, and expiry is later. The thirty-first is fine. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: run the checklist before you read the options.
> 1 S: strip 12 → 2 Jun circled; 31 May ticked [A09-F4] · 2 S: finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 2 · Five questions

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. What's the first box on the checklist?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Five questions" · H: 3 s

N021 · Is it subject to U-C-P six hundred. Now, the questions. Pause before each, and answer first.
> 1 N: "UCP 600 ✓" · 2 S: the case card returns, left third

N022 · Question one. Anand says the lathe is used. Must the Chennai bank stop payment?
> 1 S: Q1 card · H: 8 s

N023 · No. Many of you would say yes; it's his money. But the L-C is separate from the sale, and banks deal in documents, not goods.
> 1 C: card "Q1: no · Art. 4 credit ≠ contract · Art. 5 documents, not goods" [F3]

N024 · His remedy is against the seller, under the sale contract. Only a court can stop payment, and only for clear fraud.
> 1 N: "remedy: the sale contract · court: fraud exception only" [F3, A08-F22]

N025 · Question two. The documents reached Chennai on Thursday the third. By when must the bank decide? No holidays.
> 1 S: Q2 card · 2 S: the A09 clock · H: 8 s

N026 · Friday is day one. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. Thursday the tenth of June is day five.
> 1 S: the clock fills Fri 4, Mon 7, Tue 8, Wed 9, Thu 10, one per word [A09-F3]

N027 · Question three. Is the insurance date a real discrepancy?
> 1 S: Q3 card · H: 8 s

N028 · Yes. Insurance must be dated no later than shipment, unless it shows cover from shipment. Here it's a day late, and shows no such cover.
> 1 C: card "Q3: discrepant · Art. 28: dated ≤ shipment, or cover effective from shipment" [A09-F15]

N029 · Question four. The bank asks Anand if he'll waive the discrepancy. He takes four days to reply. Does the bank's clock stop while it waits?
> 1 S: Q4 card · H: 8 s

N030 · No. Asking for a waiver doesn't extend the five days. It's the bank's choice to ask, and the bank's decision after.
> 1 C: card "Q4: clock keeps running · Art. 16(b): bank's own judgement" [F4]

N031 · So by Thursday the tenth, it sends one notice: refused, the discrepancy, and "documents held pending waiver".
> 1 N: "one notice by day 5 · 'held pending waiver'" [F6]

N032 · And if Anand refuses to waive? Then the bank won't pay.
> 1 N: "no waiver → no payment"

N033 · Question five. Who bears that? The Singapore bank already paid the seller. Can it recover from her?
> 1 S: Q5 card · H: 8 s

N034 · Not under its confirmation. A confirming bank negotiates without recourse, but only for documents that comply.
> 1 C: card "Q5: confirmation covers complying documents only (Art. 8)" [F5, A08-F9]

N035 · These didn't. So its right to recover from her depends on its own terms with her. That's why checkers take insurance dates seriously.
> 1 N: "discrepant docs: outside the confirmation · recourse per its agreement" [F5]

N036 · Now flip one fact. Suppose the insurance was fine. Anand still says, don't pay. What happens?
> 1 S: the insurance line turns green · H: 3 s

N037 · The documents comply, so Chennai must honour. It reimburses Singapore. Anand pays his bank, and argues with the seller.
> 1 C: card "complying → must honour (Art. 15) · issuing bank reimburses the nominated bank (Art. 7c)" [F1, F2]

N038 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Quality dispute: can the bank stop payment?
> 1 S: card "quality dispute → stop?" · H: 3 s

N039 · No. Two, the twist. Who decides whether to accept a waiver: Anand, or his bank?
> 1 N: "no ✓" · H: 3 s

N040 · His bank. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: the bank decides on paper, the buyer argues on goods.
> 1 N: "the issuing bank ✓" · 2 S: the case card; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 3 · Two variants

N041 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Complying documents: must the issuing bank honour?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Two variants" · H: 3 s

N042 · Yes. Now, the exam often keeps the same case and changes one thing. Two favourite changes.
> 1 N: "must honour ✓" · 2 S: the case card, two new tabs "variant 1 · variant 2"

N043 · Variant one. A Mumbai trader holds a transferable L-C for a hundred thousand dollars. Partial shipments allowed.
> 1 S: variant 1 card "Mumbai trader · transferable LC · USD 100,000 · partial shipments allowed" (illustrative)

N044 · He wants to pass forty thousand to a Surat mill, and forty-five thousand to a Ludhiana mill. Allowed?
> 1 S: two arrows "Surat 40,000 · Ludhiana 45,000" · H: 8 s

N045 · Yes. Two second beneficiaries are fine, because partial drawings or shipments are allowed. And his bank must agree to transfer.
> 1 C: card "several second beneficiaries → only if partial drawings / shipments allowed · bank must consent" [F7]

N046 · Now Ludhiana wants to pass its part to its own cotton supplier. Allowed?
> 1 S: a third arrow, dashed, "Ludhiana → cotton supplier?" · H: 3 s

N047 · No. A credit is transferred only once. The mills are the end of the line.
> 1 C: trap card "onward transfer ✗ · once only" [F7]

N048 · Variant two. Anand's bank issues a standby L-C under I-S-P ninety-eight, for fifty thousand dollars, backing his payment to another supplier.
> 1 S: variant 2 card "standby LC · ISP98 · USD 50,000 · backs Anand's payment" (illustrative)

N049 · Anand doesn't pay. The supplier sends a signed, dated statement: payment is due, because Anand has defaulted. Does the bank check whether he really defaulted?
> 1 S: a statement "payment due: applicant has defaulted · signed · dated" · H: 8 s

N050 · No. A standby is documentary and independent. A signed, dated statement of default is enough, if the standby asks for nothing more.
> 1 C: card "ISP98 4.17: statement of default · dated · signed → complies" [F8, F9]

N051 · One timing difference. If the bank refuses under I-S-P ninety-eight, three business days is safe; beyond seven is too late.
> 1 C: card "ISP98 5.01: ≤ 3 business days safe · > 7 unreasonable · vs UCP 600: max 5 banking days" [F10]

N052 · Many mix these up. U-C-P six hundred gives a hard five. I-S-P ninety-eight gives three to seven.
> 1 B: box "UCP 5" and "ISP 3–7" side by side

N053 · Pause check for chapter three. One. Transferable L-C: how many times?
> 1 S: card "transferred how many times?" · H: 3 s

N054 · Once. Two, the twist. Does a standby issuer investigate the default?
> 1 N: "once ✓" · H: 3 s

N055 · No; it checks the statement. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: change one fact, then rerun the checklist.
> 1 N: "no: documents only ✓" · 2 S: the variants; finished parts dim to grey
