## Chapter 1 · Read the case

N005 · Chapter one. Here's the case. Read along with me.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Read the case" · 2 S: case card slides in, left third (illustrative)

N006 · Anand, an importer in Chennai, buys a C-N-C lathe, a computer-run machine tool, from Mei, a seller in Singapore. Eighty thousand dollars, C-I-F Chennai.
> 1 S: case line 1 "Applicant: Anand, Chennai · CNC lathe · USD 80,000 · CIF Chennai · Beneficiary: Mei, Singapore"

N007 · His bank in Chennai opens an irrevocable L-C, subject to U-C-P six hundred. It's available by negotiation with a bank in Singapore.
> 1 S: case line 2 "Issuing bank: Chennai · UCP 600 · available by negotiation, Singapore"

N008 · At the Chennai bank's request, the Singapore bank adds its confirmation.
> 1 S: case line 3 "Confirming and negotiating bank: Singapore"

N009 · The L-C asks for an original bill of lading, an invoice, and insurance for a hundred and ten per cent. Latest shipment, Friday the fourteenth of May. Expiry, Tuesday the fifteenth of June.
> 1 S: case line 4 "B/L (original) · invoice · insurance 110% · latest shipment Fri 14 May · expiry Tue 15 Jun 2027" · 2 S: date strip; "14 May" and "15 Jun" land on it as spoken

N009a · Why insurance from Mei? Under C-I-F, the seller insures, so the policy is one of her documents. Take a look at the card.
> 1 N: "CIF → seller insures · ≥ 110% of CIF value" [A09-F15] · H: 5 s

N010 · Before the questions, a thirty-second checklist. Do it on every case. One: is it subject to U-C-P six hundred? Yes.
> 1 C: checklist card, five empty boxes · 2 B: tick box 1 "UCP 600 ✓"

N011 · Two: name every bank, and its job. Quick one. Who is the beneficiary?
> 1 B: box 2 "banks and jobs" · H: 3 s

N012 · Mei, the one who gets paid under the L-C. Anand is the applicant. Chennai issues, Singapore confirms and negotiates.
> 1 S: the relay; four figures "Anand (applicant) · Chennai bank (issuing) · Singapore bank (confirming, negotiating) · Mei (beneficiary)" · 2 B: tick box 2

N013 · Three: how is it paid? By negotiation. Singapore buys her documents and pays her early.
> 1 B: tick box 3 "negotiation, Singapore" [A08-F7]

N013a · Now the rest of the case. Mei ships on Wednesday the twelfth of May. The bill of lading shows that date on board.
> 1 S: case line 5 "on board: Wed 12 May" · 2 S: "12 May" lands on the strip

N013b · She presents the documents to the Singapore bank on Monday the thirty-first of May. It finds them complying, pays her, and sends them to Chennai.
> 1 S: case line 6 "presented Mon 31 May · Singapore negotiates, pays, forwards" · 2 S: "31 May" lands on the strip

N013c · They reach Chennai on Thursday the third of June. The Chennai checker spots one thing: the insurance is dated the thirteenth of May. The day after shipment, with no cover from shipment.
> 1 S: case line 7 "reach Chennai Thu 3 Jun · insurance dated 13 May" · 2 B: circle "13 May" · 3 S: "3 Jun" lands on the strip

N013d · And Anand phones his bank. He's heard the lathe is a cheaper model than he ordered. He says, don't pay. Take a look at the card.
> 1 S: case line 8 "Anand: 'cheaper model; don't pay'" · H: 5 s

N014 · Box four: the three dates. Latest shipment, fourteenth of May. Expiry, fifteenth of June. And shipment plus twenty-one. Box five, what's asked: that's chapter two.
> 1 B: box 4 "14 May · 15 Jun · 12 May + 21" · box 5 "→ ch2"

N015 · Pause check for chapter one. One. Did she ship in time?
> 1 S: card "shipped by 14 May?" · H: 3 s

N016 · Yes, the twelfth is before the fourteenth. Two, the twist. Was her presentation on the thirty-first in time?
> 1 N: "shipment ✓" · H: 8 s

N017 · Twelve plus twenty-one is thirty-three. May has thirty-one days, so that's the second of June. The thirty-first is fine.
> 1 S: strip 12 May → 2 Jun circled; 31 May ticked [A09-F4]

N018 · And Chennai got them on the third, after the second. Stale? No. She presented in time to the nominated bank. That's what counts.
> 1 N: "presented to the nominated bank 31 May ✓ · reached the issuing bank later: not stale" [A09-F4]

## Chapter 2 · Five questions

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. What's the first box on the checklist?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Five questions" · H: 3 s

N021 · Is it subject to U-C-P six hundred. Now, the questions. Pause before each, and answer first.
> 1 N: "UCP 600 ✓" · 2 S: the case card returns, left third

N022 · Question one. Anand says the lathe is a cheaper model. Must the Chennai bank stop payment?
> 1 S: Q1 card; case line 8 at full size · H: 8 s

N023 · No. Many of you would say yes; it's his money. But the L-C is separate from the sale, and banks deal in documents, not goods.
> 1 C: card "Q1: no · Art. 4 credit ≠ contract · Art. 5 documents, not goods" [F3]

N024 · Some of you are thinking: isn't a wrong machine fraud? His word isn't enough. Only a court order stops payment, and only for established fraud.
> 1 N: "his word ✗ · court order, established fraud only" [F3, A08-F22]

N024a · His real remedy is against Mei, under the sale contract.
> 1 N: "remedy: the sale contract" [F3]

N025 · Question two. The documents reached Chennai on Thursday the third. By when must it decide? The bank shuts on weekends.
> 1 S: Q2 card · 2 S: the A09 clock · H: 8 s

N026 · Friday is day one. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. Thursday the tenth of June is day five.
> 1 S: the clock fills Fri 4, Mon 7, Tue 8, Wed 9, Thu 10, one per word [A09-F3]

N027 · Question three. Is the insurance date a real discrepancy?
> 1 S: Q3 card; case line 7 at full size · H: 8 s

N028 · Yes. Insurance must be dated no later than shipment, unless it shows cover from shipment. Here it's a day late, with no such cover.
> 1 C: card "Q3: discrepant · Art. 28(e): dated ≤ shipment, or cover from shipment" [A09-F15]

N028a · So now the story joins up. Anand can't refuse over the goods. But the insurance gives his bank a paper reason.
> 1 N: "goods ✗ · paper ✓"

N029 · Question four. The bank asks Anand if he'll waive, that is, accept the insurance anyway. He takes a week to reply. Does the bank's clock stop?
> 1 S: Q4 card · H: 8 s

N030 · No. Asking for a waiver doesn't extend the five days. The bank chooses to ask, and the bank decides after.
> 1 C: card "Q4: clock keeps running · Art. 16(b): the bank's own judgement" [F4]

N031 · So if he hasn't said yes by Thursday the tenth, it sends one notice to Singapore: refused, the discrepancy, and documents held pending waiver.
> 1 N: "one notice by day 5, to the presenter" [F6]

N031a · Held pending waiver means: we're keeping the papers. If Anand later says yes, we may pay.
> 1 N: "held pending waiver = kept; may still pay if he waives" [F6]

N031b · And if the bank misses Thursday? It can't refuse later. It must pay, discrepancy or not.
> 1 N: "notice late → precluded → must pay" [F5, A09-F11]

N032 · Can Mei send a corrected policy instead? No. Her twenty-one days ended on the second of June, though expiry is later.
> 1 N: "re-present ✗ · 21 days ended 2 Jun" [F16]

N033 · Question five. Anand refuses to waive, so Chennai won't pay. Singapore already paid Mei. Who bears the loss?
> 1 S: Q5 card · H: 8 s

N034 · Singapore. Chennai pays back only for complying documents. And Singapore's confirmation covers only complying documents too.
> 1 C: card "Q5: the paying bank bears it · Art. 7(c), 8: only complying documents are reimbursed / confirmed" [F5]

N035 · Can Singapore claim from Mei? Only if it paid her with recourse, or under reserve. That's why checkers take insurance dates seriously.
> 1 N: "recover from Mei: only if paid with recourse / under reserve (practice)" [F5]

N036 · Now flip one fact. Suppose the insurance was fine. Anand still says, don't pay. What happens?
> 1 S: the insurance line turns green · H: 3 s

N037 · The documents comply, so Chennai must honour, that is, pay. It reimburses Singapore. Anand pays his bank, and argues with Mei.
> 1 C: card "complying → must honour (Art. 15) · issuing bank reimburses the nominated bank (Art. 7c)" [F1, F2]

N037a · And how does Chennai protect itself? A margin and a limit from Anand. And the bill of lading made out to its order, so it controls the lathe until he pays.
> 1 C: card "issuing bank's safeguards: limit · cash margin · B/L to its order · insurance" [F14]

N037b · One more risk: local law. U-C-P is only part of the contract, so a court order or a sanction can still stop payment.
> 1 N: "legal risk: local law and courts over UCP" [F15]

N038 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Quality dispute: can the bank stop payment?
> 1 S: card "quality dispute → stop?" · H: 3 s

N039 · No. Two, the twist. Who decides whether to accept a waiver: Anand, or his bank?
> 1 N: "no ✓" · H: 3 s

N040 · His bank. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: the bank decides on paper, the buyer argues on goods.
> 1 N: "the issuing bank ✓" · 2 S: the case card; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 3 · Two variants

N041 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Complying documents: must the issuing bank honour?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Two variants" · H: 3 s

N042 · Yes. Now, the exam often keeps the case and changes one thing. Two favourite changes.
> 1 N: "must honour ✓" · 2 S: the case card, two new tabs "variant 1 · variant 2"

N043 · Variant one. A Mumbai trader holds a transferable L-C for a hundred thousand dollars. Partial shipments allowed.
> 1 S: variant 1 card "Mumbai trader · transferable LC · USD 100,000 · partial shipments allowed" (illustrative)

N044 · He wants to pass forty thousand to a Surat mill, and forty-five thousand to a Ludhiana mill. Allowed?
> 1 S: two arrows "Surat 40,000 · Ludhiana 45,000" · H: 8 s

N045 · Yes. Two second beneficiaries are fine, because partial drawings or shipments are allowed. And his bank must agree to transfer.
> 1 C: card "several second beneficiaries → only if partial drawings / shipments allowed · bank must consent" [F7]

N046 · Now Ludhiana wants to pass its part to its own cotton supplier. Allowed?
> 1 S: a third arrow, dashed, "Ludhiana → cotton supplier?" · H: 3 s

N047 · No. A credit is transferred only once. The mills are the end of the line, though a part may come back to the trader.
> 1 C: trap card "onward transfer ✗ · once only · back to the first beneficiary ✓" [F7]

N048 · Variant two. Anand's bank issues a standby L-C under I-S-P ninety-eight, for fifty thousand dollars, backing his payment to another supplier.
> 1 S: variant 2 card "standby LC · ISP98 · USD 50,000 · backs Anand's payment" (illustrative)

N049 · Anand doesn't pay. The supplier sends a signed, dated statement: payment is due, because Anand has defaulted. Does the bank check whether he really defaulted?
> 1 S: a statement "payment due: applicant has defaulted · signed · dated" · H: 8 s

N050 · No. A standby is documentary and independent. A signed, dated statement of default is enough, if the standby asks for nothing more.
> 1 C: card "ISP98 1.06, 4.17: statement of default · dated · signed → complies" [F8, F9]

N051 · And if the bank refuses the claim? It must tell the supplier in time. Under I-S-P ninety-eight, a business day is a day the bank is normally open.
> 1 N: "business day = bank normally open" [F17]

N052 · Within three business days is always in time. Beyond seven is always too late. In between, it depends on the facts.
> 1 C: card "ISP98 5.01: ≤ 3 business days: timely · > 7: too late · 4–7: depends" [F10]

N053 · Many mix this with U-C-P. U-C-P six hundred gives a hard five banking days. Don't pick "seven allowed" for I-S-P.
> 1 B: box "UCP: max 5" and "ISP: ≤ 3 safe · > 7 late" side by side

N053a · One more I-S-P difference. If the bank is closed on the standby's last day, she gets thirty calendar days after it reopens.
> 1 N: "ISP98 3.14: closed on last day → +30 calendar days after reopening · UCP: Art. 29 / 36" [F11]

> 1 S: card "transferred how many times?" · H: 3 s

N055 · Once. Two, the twist. Does a standby issuer investigate the default?
> 1 N: "once ✓" · H: 3 s

N056 · No; it checks the statement. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: change one fact, then rerun the checklist.
