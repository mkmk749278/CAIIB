## Case

A Hosur importer buys auto parts worth **USD 60,000, CIF Chennai**, from a seller in Busan. His bank in Hosur issues
an irrevocable credit subject to UCP 600, **available by sight payment at the issuing bank**, advised (not confirmed)
by a bank in Busan. The credit calls for an original bill of lading, a commercial invoice and an insurance policy for
110% of the CIF value. Latest shipment **Mon 5 Apr 2027**; expiry **Fri 30 Apr 2027**. The goods are shipped on board
**Thu 1 Apr 2027**. The documents reach the issuing bank on **Mon 19 Apr 2027**. The insurance policy covers
**USD 60,000**.

**P1.** By when must the issuing bank give notice if it refuses?
(a) Fri 23 Apr (b) **Mon 26 Apr** (c) Sat 24 Apr (d) Fri 30 Apr
*Reason: five banking days following the day of presentation: Tue 20, Wed 21, Thu 22, Fri 23, Mon 26 (Art. 14(b))
[A09-F3].*

**P2.** Was the presentation within time?
(a) no, the 21 days ran out on 20 April
(b) **yes: 1 April + 21 days = 22 April, and expiry is 30 April**
(c) no, documents must be presented within 21 banking days
(d) yes, only the expiry date matters
*Reason: Art. 14(c), the earlier of shipment + 21 calendar days and expiry [A09-F4]. Note: under a sight credit
available with the issuing bank, presentation is made there.*

**P3.** The insurance policy is:
(a) compliant, as it covers the full invoice value
(b) **discrepant, because cover must be at least 110% of the CIF value, here USD 66,000**
(c) compliant, as CIF puts insurance on the seller
(d) discrepant only if the applicant objects
*Reason: the credit asks for 110% (and Art. 28(f)(ii) sets 110% if silent) [A09-F15]; 60,000 × 1.10 = 66,000.*

**P4.** Suppose instead the insurance policy covers USD 66,000 and every document complies. The importer says the parts
are of poor quality and asks his bank not to pay. The bank should:
(a) refuse payment, since the applicant has asked
(b) **honour, deciding on the documents alone; quality is a matter of the sale contract**
(c) pay only after inspecting the goods
(d) refer the matter to the advising bank
*Reason: Arts. 4, 5, 15; only a court order, on established fraud, can restrain payment [F1, F3].*

**P5.** The Busan advising bank:
(a) must pay the seller if the issuing bank refuses
(b) **has no undertaking to pay; it checks the apparent authenticity of the credit it advises**
(c) negotiates without recourse
(d) is liable for the discrepancy
*Reason: Art. 9: an advising bank that is not a confirming bank advises without any undertaking [A08-F9].*

## Variants

**P6.** A transferable credit for USD 90,000 prohibits partial shipments. The first beneficiary wants to transfer
USD 40,000 to one supplier and USD 45,000 to another. This is:
(a) allowed, as the total is within the credit amount
(b) **not allowed: transfer to more than one second beneficiary needs partial drawings or shipments to be allowed**
(c) allowed if the issuing bank agrees later
(d) allowed once each supplier accepts
*Reason: Art. 38(d) [F7].*

**P7.** A standby under ISP98 asks for "the beneficiary's statement that the applicant has failed to pay", with no
other wording set. The beneficiary presents a signed statement saying payment is due because the applicant has failed
to pay, but **leaves it undated**. The issuer may:
(a) pay, because it does not look into the default
(b) **refuse, because a dated statement is required**
(c) pay after asking the applicant whether he paid
(d) refuse, because it must verify the default
*Reason: ISP98 Rule 4.17: the statement must represent that payment is due because the drawing event occurred, be
dated, and be signed by the beneficiary [F9]. The issuer never investigates the default itself (Rule 1.06) [F8].*

**P8.** Under ISP98, notice of dishonour given on the **eighth** business day after presentation is:
(a) timely, as the standby has not expired
(b) **unreasonable, so the issuer is precluded from relying on the discrepancies it would have given**
(c) timely, as ISP98 allows up to ten business days
(d) timely if the applicant agreed
*Reason: Rule 5.01: within 3 business days is not unreasonable, beyond 7 is unreasonable; Rule 5.03: failure to give
timely notice precludes the issuer [F10].*

## Waiver and loss

**P9.** In the case above (the USD 60,000 policy), the issuing bank asks the importer for a waiver on Tue 20 Apr. He
agrees on Tue 27 Apr. The bank had sent no notice by Mon 26 Apr. Which is correct?
(a) the bank could still refuse on 27 Apr, as it was waiting for the applicant
(b) **the bank is precluded from claiming the discrepancy, as no notice went by the fifth banking day (Mon 26 Apr)**
(c) the five days restarted when the bank asked for the waiver
(d) the bank must refuse, as the waiver came late
*Reason: seeking a waiver does not extend the five banking days; a bank that gives no notice in time cannot claim
non-compliance (Art. 16(b), (f)) [F4, F5].*

**P10.** A nominated bank that has **not** confirmed the credit negotiates discrepant documents and pays the
beneficiary. The issuing bank refuses within time. Who finally bears the loss, in the usual case?
(a) the issuing bank, as the nominated bank acted on its nomination
(b) the applicant
(c) **the nominated bank first; it normally recovers from the beneficiary, having negotiated with recourse**
(d) the advising bank
*Reason: the issuing bank reimburses only a complying presentation (Art. 7(c)); a non-confirming negotiating bank
normally keeps recourse to the beneficiary (practice) [F5].*
