## Chapter 1 · Two regulators and the I-E-C

N007 · Chapter one. Two bodies watch every export. Quick one. Who decides what India may export, and what help exporters get?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Two regulators and the I-E-C" · H: 3 s

N008 · D-G-F-T, the Directorate General of Foreign Trade, under the Commerce Ministry. It runs the trade side, through the Foreign Trade Policy, the F-T-P.
> 1 S: road sign 1 "DGFT: trade · FTP 2023" [F1]

N009 · And the money side? R-B-I, under FEMA, the foreign exchange law. R-B-I authorises banks to deal in foreign exchange: authorised dealers, A-Ds. Banks follow both.
> 1 S: road sign 2 "RBI: foreign exchange · FEMA · AD banks follow both" [F1]

N010 · Her first paper: the I-E-C, Importer-Exporter Code. Ten characters, issued by D-G-F-T, online.
> 1 C: card "IEC: 10 characters · DGFT · online" [F2]

N011 · And here's a neat trick. Her I-E-C is the same as her firm's PAN. One PAN, one I-E-C.
> 1 B: write "= PAN" on the card on "PAN" · 2 N: "one PAN – one IEC" [F3]

N012 · Many of you think an I-E-C needs renewal. It doesn't. It's permanent, unless cancelled. But she must update it online every April to June, or it is deactivated.
> 1 C: trap card "IEC renewal ✗ · permanent unless cancelled ✓ · update every Apr–Jun or deactivated" [F3]

N013 · A few don't need one. Government departments, and people moving goods for personal use, not trade. A service exporter needs one only to claim F-T-P benefits.
> 1 N: "IEC exempt: Govt departments · personal use · small border trade · services: only for FTP benefits" [F2, F4]

N014 · Pause check for chapter one. One. Who issues the I-E-C?
> 1 S: card "IEC issued by?" · H: 3 s

N015 · D-G-F-T. Two, the twist. Her I-E-C is five years old. Must she renew it?
> 1 N: "DGFT ✓" · H: 3 s

N016 · No. She updates it each year; it never expires. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: D-G-F-T for trade, R-B-I for the money; the I-E-C comes first.
> 1 N: "update, not renew ✓" · 2 S: the road; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 2 · The E-D-F

N017 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Her I-E-C is the same as what?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The E-D-F" · H: 3 s

N018 · Her firm's PAN. Now she ships. She must declare the full export value, on the Export Declaration Form, the E-D-F.
> 1 N: "PAN ✓" · 2 S: the road reaches "shipment"; a form "EDF: full export value" [F6]

N019 · Quick one. For goods, does the E-D-F go to Customs, or to her bank?
> 1 G: two doors "Customs" · "bank" · H: 3 s

N020 · Customs. The shipping bill is the form she files with Customs to send goods out. At most ports it's electronic, and the E-D-F is part of it.
> 1 N: "goods: EDF to Customs · electronic port: inside the shipping bill" [F6, F7] · 2 N: "shipping bill = the Customs export form"

N021 · From a Special Economic Zone, an S-E-Z, it goes to the zone's officer, the Development Commissioner.
> 1 N: "SEZ unit: Development Commissioner" [F7]

N022 · Services, like consulting? The E-D-F goes to her bank, within thirty days from the end of the month of the invoice.
> 1 N: "services EDF: to the AD · 30 days from the end of the invoice month" [F7, F8]

N023 · Software? To her bank, or to S-T-P-I, Software Technology Parks of India. It used to be a separate form, SOFTEX. Now it's the same E-D-F.
> 1 N: "software: EDF to the AD or STPI" [F7, F10] · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: SOFTEX form, certified by STPI"

N024 · One exception. A traveller carrying personal belongings abroad is not an exporter here. No E-D-F.
> 1 N: "traveller's personal effects: not an export" [F6] · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: exempt list (trade samples, gifts ≤ ₹5 lakh, goods for repair or testing…)" [F11]

N025 · And a later change, back-dated to the first of October. For services, the E-D-F is deemed filed, treated as filed, when she gives her bank the payment details.
> 1 C: card "deemed EDF (services): from 1 Oct 2026 (notified 2 Nov 2026)" [F9]

N026 · For a resident individual, like a freelancer, any amount. For anyone else, if the money received is up to ten lakh rupees.
> 1 B: box "resident individual: any amount" · 2 N: "others: inward remittance ≤ ₹10 lakh" [F9]

N027 · Pause check for chapter two. One. For goods, who receives the E-D-F?
> 1 S: card "goods EDF → ?" · H: 3 s

N028 · Customs. Two, the twist. A freelance designer, a resident individual, receives twenty-five lakh rupees for a service. Must she fill an E-D-F?
> 1 N: "Customs ✓" · H: 3 s

N029 · No. It's deemed filed, for any amount. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: goods to Customs, services to the bank, and small service receipts are deemed filed.
> 1 N: "deemed filed ✓" · 2 S: the road; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 3 · Money in

N030 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Software: to whom does the E-D-F go?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Money in" · H: 3 s

N031 · Her bank, or S-T-P-I. Now the documents go to her bank. Who sets the time limit for that?
> 1 N: "AD or STPI ✓" · 2 S: the road reaches "documents to the AD" · H: 3 s

N032 · Her own bank. The new rules fix no number of days. Each bank's own policy and procedure lists the documents, the timelines and the charges.
> 1 C: card "documents to the AD: timelines in the bank's internal policy and SOP (R26 reg 19)" [F13] · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: 21 days" [F13]

N033 · How may the money come in? Through a bank. From most countries, in rupees or any foreign currency.
> 1 S: the road reaches "money in" · 2 N: "through an authorised bank · INR or any foreign currency" [F14]

N034 · From Nepal and Bhutan, in rupees. From the Asian Clearing Union, a group of Asian central banks that settle with each other, through the A-C-U.
> 1 N: "Nepal, Bhutan: INR · ACU members: ACU mechanism" [F14]

N035 · Before her bank credits her account, it checks the payment is genuine. Then it closes the export entry at once. That system is in chapter five.
> 1 N: "AD credits only after checking genuineness · closes the EDPMS entry at the same time" [F18]

N036 · Now, say the Milan buyer pays an advance before shipment. Through which bank must the export papers then go?
> 1 S: an arrow "advance" before the shipment point · H: 3 s

N037 · The same bank that received the advance. If she moves banks, she tells both, and the new one takes over.
> 1 C: card "advance: same AD for the advance and the realisation; may switch after informing both (R26 reg 10)" [F20]

N038 · And there's no fixed time to ship against the advance now. Any interest she pays the buyer is capped.
> 1 N: "no fixed shipment period · interest ≤ trade-credit ceiling" [F20] · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: ship within 1 year, then 3" [F21]

N039 · And if someone other than the buyer pays her? Her bank may allow it, if satisfied the deal is genuine.
> 1 N: "third-party payment: the AD may allow, if bona fide (R26 reg 8)" [F19]

N040 · Pause check for chapter three. One. Payment from Bhutan: in which currency?
> 1 S: card "Bhutan → ?" · H: 3 s

N041 · Rupees. Two, the twist. Within how many days must she hand her documents to the bank?
> 1 N: "INR ✓" · H: 3 s

## Chapter 4 · The realisation clock

N043 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Who may allow a payment from a third party?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · The realisation clock" · H: 3 s

N044 · Her bank. Now the big one. Realisation means the export money actually comes back to India. Repatriation, the same idea. By when?
> 1 N: "her bank ✓" · 2 S: a clock appears above the road · H: 3 s

N045 · Nine months. For goods, from the date of shipment. For services, from the date of the invoice.
> 1 C: card "realise within 9 months · goods: from shipment · services: from invoice (R26 reg 5)" [F22] · 2 B: circle "9"

N046 · If she invoices in rupees instead of dollars, twelve months.
> 1 N: "INR invoiced or settled: 12 months" [F25]

N047 · Goods sent to a warehouse abroad, to be sold later? Nine months from the date they're sold. A project export, like building a plant abroad, follows its contract.
> 1 N: "warehouse: 9 months from the date of sale · project exports: per contract" [F23, F24]

N048 · Is there a different clock for an S-E-Z unit, or a status holder, an exporter D-G-F-T recognises for big export volumes? No. Same rule for all.
> 1 N: "SEZ / EOU / status holders: same rule" [F26]

N049 · Let's try it. She ships on the twentieth of January twenty twenty-seven, invoiced in dollars. Realise by when? And if invoiced in rupees?
> 1 S: calendar strip "ship: 20 Jan 2027" · H: 8 s

N050 · By the twentieth of October twenty twenty-seven. In rupees, by the twentieth of January twenty twenty-eight. That last day is the due date.
> 1 S: the clock runs 20 Jan → 20 Oct 2027; a second hand to 20 Jan 2028 [F33] · 2 N: "due date = last day to realise"

N051 · And if the buyer is slow? Her bank may extend the period, if satisfied with her reasons. The rules set no cap; the bank's policy guides it.
> 1 C: card "extension: the AD, on reasons · no cap in the rules · no RBI reference (R26 reg 5)" [F27] · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: 6 months at a time" [F28]

N052 · Many of you remember a longer period from older notes. For a May or June twenty twenty-seven exam, the answer is nine.
> 1 B: box "9" · 2 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: 9 → 15 (from 14 Nov 2025) → 9 (5 Jun 2026)" [F32]

N053 · What if money is still not home a year after the due date, or the extended date? Then she may export only against full advance, or an irrevocable L-C.
> 1 C: card "unrealised > 1 year after the due date (or extended date) → further exports only against full advance or irrevocable LC (R26 reg 13)" [F29]

N054 · Older notes mention R-B-I's caution list. Since twenty twenty, R-B-I listed an exporter only on the bank's recommendation, for example when she couldn't be traced.
> 1 N: "caution list (since Oct 2020): RBI, on the AD's recommendation" [F40]

N055 · Listed exporters ship only against advance or an L-C. Those on the list on the thirtieth of September twenty twenty-six stay on it, until removed.
> 1 N: "listed → advance or LC only · on the list on 30 Sep 2026 → stays until removed (R26 reg 13 proviso)" [F30, F40]

## Chapter 5 · EDPMS, reduction, set-off

N059 · Chapter five. First, from chapter four. Invoiced in rupees: how many months?
> 1 S: chapter title "5 · EDPMS, reduction, set-off" · H: 3 s

N060 · Twelve. Now, how does anyone know which exports are still unpaid? E-D-P-M-S. The Export Data Processing and Monitoring System, R-B-I's platform since March twenty fourteen.
> 1 N: "12 ✓" · 2 C: card "EDPMS: RBI's export monitoring system · since 1 Mar 2014" [F35]

N061 · For goods, when does her entry appear? Quick one. When she ships, or when the money comes?
> 1 S: the road with a database strip under it · H: 3 s

N062 · When she ships. Customs sends the shipping bill data. The money, when it comes, closes the entry.
> 1 N: "goods: entry opens from Customs data at shipment · closes on realisation" [F36, F37] · 2 S: the strip lights at "shipment", closes at "money in"

N063 · For services, and paper forms from the few non-electronic ports, her bank makes the entry, within five working days. The old half-yearly statement, X-O-S, is gone.
> 1 N: "services and non-EDI EDFs: the AD enters within 5 working days · XOS (export outstanding statement) discontinued" [F36, F39]

N064 · Small bills got simpler. If a shipping bill or invoice is up to ten lakh rupees, her bank may close it on her own declaration. Even quarterly, in bulk.
> 1 C: card "shipping bill / invoice ≤ ₹10 lakh → close on the exporter's declaration · or quarterly, in bulk" [F38]

N065 · Now, the buyer pays less, say for a quality claim. Her bank may allow a reduction in the export value, if satisfied with her reasons.
> 1 C: card "reduction in realisation: the AD allows (R26 reg 6)" [F41]

N066 · And if the bill itself is worth up to ten lakh, her declaration alone is enough. Even if nothing at all was paid.
> 1 N: "bill ≤ ₹10 lakh → on her declaration, even for full non-realisation" [F41]

N067 · Older notes call this write-off: five per cent on her own, ten for status holders, ten by the bank. That was the old rule.
> 1 C: grey card "before 1 Oct 2026: write-off: self 5% · status holder self 10% · AD 10%" [F42]

N068 · Last idea: set-off. She also buys dyes from the same Milan buyer. Can her bank net what she's owed against what she owes?
> 1 S: two arrows, shirts out, dyes in, same buyer · H: 3 s

N069 · Yes. With the same buyer or supplier, or its group or associate companies abroad, within the realisation period. The set-off counts as realisation.
> 1 C: card "set-off: same overseas buyer / supplier or its group / associates · within the realisation period · counts as realisation (R26 reg 7)" [F43]
