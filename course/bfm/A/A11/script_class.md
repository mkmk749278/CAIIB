## Chapter 1 · Two regulators and the first papers

N007 · Chapter one. Two bodies watch every export. Quick one. Who decides what India may export, and what help exporters get?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Two regulators and the first papers" · H: 3 s

N008 · D-G-F-T, the Directorate General of Foreign Trade, under the Commerce Ministry. It runs the trade side, through the Foreign Trade Policy, the F-T-P.
> 1 S: road sign 1 "DGFT: trade · FTP 2023" [F1]

N009 · And the money side? R-B-I, under FEMA, the foreign exchange law. Banks act for R-B-I as authorised dealers, A-Ds. And they follow both.
> 1 S: road sign 2 "RBI: foreign exchange · FEMA · AD banks follow both" [F1]

N010 · Her first paper: the I-E-C, Importer-Exporter Code. Ten characters, issued by D-G-F-T, online.
> 1 C: card "IEC: 10 characters · DGFT · online · = the firm's PAN" [F2]

N011 · And here's a neat trick. Her I-E-C is the same as her firm's PAN. One PAN, one I-E-C.
> 1 B: underline "= PAN" · 2 N: "one PAN – one IEC" [F3]

N012 · Many of you think an I-E-C needs renewal. It doesn't. It's valid for good. But she must update it online every April to June, or it is deactivated.
> 1 C: trap card "IEC renewal ✗ · permanent ✓ · update every Apr–Jun or deactivated" [F3]

N013 · A few don't need one. Government departments, and people moving goods for personal use, not trade.
> 1 N: "IEC exempt: Govt departments · personal use · small border trade" [F4]

N014 · Now she ships. She must declare the full export value, on the Export Declaration Form, the E-D-F.
> 1 S: the road reaches "shipment"; a form "EDF: full export value" [F6]

N015 · For goods, she gives it to Customs. At an electronic port, it's part of the shipping bill itself. From an S-E-Z unit, to its Development Commissioner.
> 1 N: "goods: Customs (EDF in the shipping bill at EDI ports) · SEZ: Development Commissioner" [F6, F7]

N015a · One exception. A traveller carrying personal belongings abroad is not an exporter here. No E-D-F.
> 1 N: "traveller's personal effects: not an export" [F6]

N016 · And services, like software? The E-D-F goes to her bank, within thirty days from the end of the month of the invoice.
> 1 N: "services EDF: to the AD (software: AD or STPI) · 30 days from the end of the invoice month" [F7, F8]

N017 · Since November, small service exporters needn't fill it at all. Telling the bank the payment details counts as filing. A resident individual, any amount. Anyone else, up to ten lakh rupees.
> 1 C: card "deemed EDF (services): resident individual · any amount; others · inward remittance ≤ ₹10 lakh" [F9] · 2 B: box "individual: any amount"

N018 · Pause check for chapter one. One. Who issues the I-E-C?
> 1 S: card "IEC issued by?" · H: 3 s

N019 · D-G-F-T. Two, the twist. A software firm, a company, receives eight lakh rupees for a service. Must it file an E-D-F?
> 1 N: "DGFT ✓" · H: 3 s

N020 · No. It's deemed filed, because the remittance is within ten lakh. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: D-G-F-T for trade, R-B-I for the money; I-E-C first, E-D-F at shipment.
> 1 N: "deemed filed ✓" · 2 S: the road; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 2 · Money in

N021 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Her I-E-C is the same as what?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Money in" · H: 3 s

N022 · Her firm's PAN. Now the documents go to her bank. Within how many days? Many notes say twenty-one.
> 1 N: "PAN ✓" · 2 S: the road reaches "documents to the AD" · H: 3 s

N023 · Not any more. The new rules set no fixed days. Each bank's board-approved policy lists the documents, the timelines and the charges.
> 1 C: card "documents to the AD: timelines in the bank's Board-approved policy (R26 reg 19)" [F13] · 2 C: stamp card "before 1 Oct 2026: within 21 days" [F13]

N024 · How may the money come in? Through a bank. From most countries, in rupees or any foreign currency.
> 1 S: the road reaches "money in" · 2 N: "through an authorised bank · INR or any foreign currency" [F14]

N025 · From Nepal and Bhutan, in rupees. From Asian Clearing Union members, through the A-C-U system.
> 1 N: "Nepal, Bhutan: INR · ACU members: ACU mechanism" [F14]

N026 · And before her bank credits her account, it checks the payment is genuine. Then it closes the export entry at once. We'll meet that system in chapter four.
> 1 N: "AD credits only after checking genuineness · closes the EDPMS entry at the same time" [F18]

N027 · Now, say the buyer pays an advance before shipment. Quick one. Can she take it through one bank, and send the export papers through another?
> 1 S: an arrow "advance" before the shipment point · H: 3 s

N028 · No. The advance and the later export go through the same bank. She may switch, after telling both banks.
> 1 C: card "advance: same AD for the advance and the realisation (R26 reg 10)" [F20]

N028a · And if someone other than the buyer pays her? Her bank may allow it, if satisfied the deal is genuine.
> 1 N: "third-party payment: the AD may allow, if bona fide (R26 reg 8)" [F19]

N029 · Many of you learnt: ship within one year of an advance, later three. The new rules set no time limit. Only the same bank, and a cap on any interest paid on it.
> 1 C: trap card "shipment against advance: before 1 Oct 2026, 1 year → 3 years · now no fixed period · interest ≤ trade-credit ceiling" [F20, F21]

N030 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Payment from Bhutan: in which currency?

N031 · Rupees. Two, the twist. An exam option says, documents to the bank within twenty-one days. True at the cut-off?

N032 · No. That was the old rule. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: money through a bank, and an advance stays with one bank.

## Chapter 3 · The realisation clock

N033 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Who now sets the days for submitting documents?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · The realisation clock" · H: 3 s

N034 · Her own bank, in its policy. Now the big one. Realisation means the full export value comes back to India. By when?
> 1 N: "the AD's policy ✓" · 2 S: a clock appears above the road · H: 3 s

N035 · Nine months. For goods, from the date of shipment. For services, from the date of the invoice.
> 1 C: card "realise within 9 months · goods: from shipment · services: from invoice (R26 reg 5)" [F22] · 2 B: circle "9"

N036 · If she invoices in rupees instead of dollars, twelve months.
> 1 N: "INR invoiced or settled: 12 months" [F25]

N037 · Goods sent to a warehouse abroad, to be sold later? Nine months from the date they're sold. Project exports follow the contract's payment terms.
> 1 N: "warehouse: 9 months from the date of sale · project exports: per contract" [F23, F24]

N038 · Is there a different clock for an S-E-Z or an export-oriented unit? No. Same rule for every exporter.
> 1 N: "SEZ / EOU / status holders: same rule" [F26]

N039 · Let's try it. She ships on the twentieth of January twenty twenty-seven, invoiced in dollars. Realise by when? And if invoiced in rupees?
> 1 S: calendar strip "ship: 20 Jan 2027" · H: 8 s

N040 · By the twentieth of October twenty twenty-seven. In rupees, by the twentieth of January twenty twenty-eight.
> 1 S: the clock runs 20 Jan → 20 Oct 2027; a second hand to 20 Jan 2028 [F33]

N041 · And if the buyer is slow? Her bank may extend the period, if satisfied with her reasons. No cap, and no need to go to R-B-I.
> 1 C: card "extension: the AD, on reasons · no cap · no RBI reference (R26 reg 5)" [F27] · 2 C: stamp card "before 1 Oct 2026: up to 6 months at a time; beyond 1 year, limits or RBI" [F28]

N042 · Now the trap card of the year. Nine months became fifteen in November twenty twenty-five. Then back to nine in June twenty twenty-six. And nine in the new rules.
> 1 C: trap card "9 → 15 (13 Nov 2025) → 9 (5 Jun 2026) → R26: 9 · INR 12 (R26's first text said 15 / 18; changed before it began)" [F32]

N043 · Many of you still say fifteen. For a May or June twenty twenty-seven exam, the answer is nine.
> 1 B: cross out "15" · box "9"

N044 · What if money is still not home a year after the due date? Then she may export only against full advance, or an irrevocable L-C.
> 1 C: card "unrealised > 1 year after the due date → further exports only against full advance or irrevocable LC (R26 reg 13)" [F29]

N045 · Older notes talk of an R-B-I caution list for such exporters. Those already on it on the thirtieth of September twenty twenty-six stay under that order, until removed.

## Chapter 4 · EDPMS, reduction, set-off

N049 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Invoiced in rupees: how many months?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · EDPMS, reduction, set-off" · H: 3 s

N050 · Twelve. Now, how does anyone know which exports are still unpaid? E-D-P-M-S. The Export Data Processing and Monitoring System, R-B-I's platform since March twenty fourteen.
> 1 N: "12 ✓" · 2 C: card "EDPMS: RBI's export monitoring system · since 1 Mar 2014" [F35]

N051 · When does her entry appear in it? Quick one. When she ships, or when the money comes?
> 1 S: the road with a database strip under it · H: 3 s

N052 · When she ships. Customs sends the shipping bill data. The money, when it comes, closes the entry.
> 1 N: "entry opens: Customs data at shipment · closes: on realisation" [F36, F37] · 2 S: the strip lights at "shipment", closes at "money in"

N053 · Banks enter the forms Customs can't, within five working days. And the old half-yearly statement, X-O-S, is gone, folded into E-D-P-M-S.
> 1 N: "AD enters non-EDI and services EDFs: 5 working days · XOS discontinued" [F36, F39]

N054 · Small bills got simpler. Up to ten lakh rupees per shipping bill or invoice, her bank may close the entry on her own declaration. Even quarterly, in bulk.
> 1 C: card "≤ ₹10 lakh per shipping bill / invoice → close on the exporter's declaration · or quarterly, in bulk" [F38]

N055 · Now, the buyer pays less, say for a quality claim. Her bank may allow a reduction in the export value, if satisfied. Up to ten lakh, on her declaration alone.
> 1 C: card "reduction in realisation: the AD allows · ≤ ₹10 lakh per shipping bill / invoice on declaration (R26 reg 6)" [F41]

N056 · Older notes call this write-off, with five and ten per cent limits. That was the old rule. Don't pick those for this exam.
> 1 C: stamp card "before 1 Oct 2026: write-off 5% (self) · 10% (status holders, AD)" [F42]

N057 · Last idea: set-off. She also buys dyes from the same Rotterdam buyer. Can her bank net what she's owed against what she owes?
> 1 S: two arrows, shirts out, dyes in, same buyer · H: 3 s

N058 · Yes. Set-off is allowed with the same buyer or supplier, or their group companies abroad, within the realisation period.
> 1 C: card "set-off: same overseas buyer / supplier or its group · within the realisation period (R26 reg 7)" [F43]

N059 · Pause check for chapter four. One. What opens an export entry in E-D-P-M-S?
> 1 S: card "EDPMS entry opens on?" · H: 3 s

N060 · The shipping bill from Customs. Two, the twist. An option says, status holders may self-write-off ten per cent. Current?
> 1 N: "Customs data ✓" · H: 3 s

N061 · No. Today it's a reduction the bank allows. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter four: E-D-P-M-S opens at shipment, and closes when the money comes.
