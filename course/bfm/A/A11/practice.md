## Declaring and receiving

**P1.** An Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) is:
(a) issued by RBI to every AD customer (b) **a 10-character code, the same as the PAN, issued by DGFT, updated
online every April–June** (c) issued by Customs at the first shipment, valid for life (d) required only for exports
above ₹10 lakh
*Reason: FTP 2023 para 2.05: 10 characters, same as PAN, issued separately by DGFT online; annual update in
April–June; an IEC not updated is de-activated [F3].*

**P2.** A private limited company in Pune exports consultancy services; the remittance for one invoice is ₹7.5
lakh. Under the 2026 regulations:
(a) it must file an EDF with Customs within 21 days (b) it must file SOFTEX with STPI (c) **it is deemed to have
filed the EDF when it gives its AD the payment information** (d) services need no declaration at all
*Reason: a person other than a resident individual is deemed to have filed the EDF where the inward remittance is
not more than ₹10 lakh (reg 3(2)(b), 2nd Amendment) [F6]. Services declare to the AD, not Customs [F5]. SOFTEX was
the old form for software; consultancy is not software, and under the 2026 rules every declaration is the EDF [F5].*

**P3.** Which statement on receipt of export proceeds is correct?
(a) an exporter to Bhutan must be paid in US dollars (b) **an exporter to Germany may be paid in rupees or any
foreign currency, through an authorised bank** (c) the AD credits the exporter first and checks genuineness within
30 days (d) an advance and the balance may come through different ADs without telling either
*Reason: countries outside the ACU → INR or any foreign currency (MRP reg 3(2)(I)(a)(iii)) [F8]; Nepal and Bhutan
→ INR [F8]; the AD credits only after checking genuineness and closes EDPMS at the same time (reg 4(2)) [F9]; advance
and proceeds through the same AD unless both are told (reg 10(1)) [F17].*

## The realisation clock

**P4.** Goods are shipped on 12 February 2027, invoiced in US dollars. The full value must be realised and
repatriated by:
(a) 12 May 2028 (15 months) (b) **12 November 2027** (c) 12 February 2028 (d) 11 August 2027
*Reason: 9 months from the date of shipment (reg 5(1)(a), as amended 22 Sep 2026) [F11, F13]. (a) uses the
Nov 2025 – Jun 2026 figure; (c) is the INR-invoice period.*

**P5.** An exporter ships carpets to its own warehouse in Dubai on 1 March 2027. They are sold from the warehouse on
15 June 2027, invoiced in rupees. The realisation deadline is:
(a) 1 December 2027 (b) 15 March 2028 (c) **15 June 2028** (d) 1 March 2028
*Reason: for warehouse exports the clock runs from the date of sale; INR-invoiced trade gets 12 months (reg 5(1)(b)
and first proviso) [F11].*

**P6.** Who may extend the period of realisation of export proceeds at the cut-off of 31 Dec 2026?
(a) RBI only (b) DGFT (c) **the AD bank, on the exporter's request with reasons** (d) nobody; the period is fixed
*Reason: reg 5(1) second proviso [F12].*

## EDPMS and the money that doesn't come

**P7.** A shipping bill for ₹6 lakh is fully paid. The quickest way the AD may close the EDPMS entry is:
(a) only after RBI approval (b) **on the exporter's declaration that payment has been realised, or a quarterly
bulk declaration** (c) by XOS statement (d) it closes by itself after nine months
*Reason: reg 4(2) proviso: entries up to ₹10 lakh may be closed on the exporter's declaration, or quarterly in bulk
[F10]. XOS was replaced by EDPMS [F19].*

**P8.** An exporter's bill of USD 50,000 is still unpaid 14 months after the last day to realise it. Under the
2026 regulations:
(a) RBI will caution-list it on the AD's recommendation (b) **it may export further only against full advance or an
irrevocable LC** (c) it may self write-off up to 10 % of last year's realisation (d) nothing changes until the AD
reports it
*Reason: reg 13: unrealised beyond one year from the due date (or extended date) [F18]. RBI's caution list for new
cases and self write-off went with the old MD [F14, F18].*

**P9.** A Tiruppur exporter owes USD 20,000 for fabric to her buyer's German sister company; the buyer owes her USD
60,000, still within the realisation period. Her AD:
(a) cannot allow set-off within a group (b) **may allow set-off of the USD 20,000** (c) needs RBI approval (d) may
allow it only for an Asian Clearing Union country
*Reason: reg 7: set-off with the same overseas buyer or supplier, or its group or associate companies, within the
realisation period [F15].*

**P10.** A non-EDI EDF reaches the AD. It must be entered in EDPMS within:
(a) 21 days (b) **5 working days** (c) 30 days from the end of the month (d) the AD's own SOP timeline
*Reason: reg 18(1)(a) [F19]. 30 days from month-end is the services exporter's own filing time [F5].*

**P11.** Under the 2026 rules, the time within which an exporter gives the shipping documents to the AD is set by:
(a) RBI at 21 days (b) **the AD's Board-approved policy and SOP** (c) FEDAI (d) DGFT
*Reason: reg 19(1)(a) [F20]; the 21 days was the old Master Direction (superseded 1 Oct 2026).*
