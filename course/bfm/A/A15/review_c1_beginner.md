## Verdict: ADOPT WITH FIXES
**High 0 · Medium 9 · Low 12.**

A well-built lesson with a strong story: the honest buyer behind Country Z's closed gate makes transfer risk easy to
picture, and the closing "fourth name" (ECGC) pays off the opening. Every chapter has a recall opener, a two-question
pause check (easy, then a twist) and a "one thing to remember" line; the "ECGC insures the shirts" and "ECGC pays 100 %"
misconceptions are committed and broken; the 1 % threshold your-turn ("Did you jump to twenty-five?") is the best
error-analysis line in the module so far; the map is filled from memory; all sums are right. What stops a plain
ADOPT: two lines over 25 words (M1); the opening's "the buyer" belief is never broken and the "fourth name" does not
match three buttons (M2); **"restricted" means two different things eight lines apart** (M3); **"same percentages"
hides that the old labels carry different percentages** (M4); "a quarter" means both 0.25 % and 25 % in one minute
(M5); funded + non-funded vs "net funded" is left tangled (M6); ECGC's "commercial / political" words are never tied to
chapter one's "buyer risk / country risk" (M7); the letters E-C-G-C, S-S-P and B-W-P are used without their names
(M8); chapter one is about 1.6 minutes and has no misconception, while chapter two carries most of the lesson (M9).

## Three things I'd remember
1. Each risk has an owner: buyer risk → exporter, seller risk → importer; transfer risk is one part of country risk.
2. ECGC grades countries A1 to D (seven); RBI makes banks provide on net funded exposure only at 1 % of assets or
   more: 0.25 / 0.25 / 5 / 20 / 25 / 100 / 100, short-term a quarter of that.
3. ECGC insures the payment, not the goods, and never all of it: SCR whole turnover 90 %, SSP and buyers' policy 80 %.

## Findings

### Medium

**M1 · N035, N051 · two lines over 25 words** (spec gate; checklist #28).
- N035 (26) → "First, a Board-approved country risk policy, with exit plans. It's needed where net funded exposure to a
  country is one per cent of assets or more." (25) Or split after "exit plans."
- N051 (26) → split: N051 "One hundred per cent. Now, who protects our exporter?" · N051b "Export credit insurance. It
  pays a share of the loss when an export on credit isn't paid."

**M2 · N006–N007, N072–N073 · the opening belief "the buyer" is never broken, and the count of names is off.** The
screen in N006 shows four labels (buyer, exporter, her bank, someone else); N007 shows two buttons and a third face
down, then says "a fourth name joins in"; N072 turns "the face-down one" (the third button) and N073 says "That's the
fourth name." By ear and eye I count three, not four. And the class never says aloud that the buyer does *not* carry
the loss (he is willing; his government stops the money), nor what happens with no insurance (the exporter carries all
of it, which ch1 N019 actually answers without linking back).
- N007 → "Some of you say the buyer. Others, the exporter. Keep your answer. By the end of class, a third name joins
  in." (and N073 "That's the third name.") Or show three face-up buttons plus one face down and keep "fourth".
- After N019 add: "Remember my opening? Without insurance, that's our answer: the exporter. Not the buyer. He was
  willing; his government stopped the money." (21 words)
- N073 → "With the standard policy: E-C-G-C pays ninety per cent. Our exporter bears ten. Not the buyer at all. That's
  the third name."

**M3 · N033 vs N041 · "restricted" means two things, eight lines apart.** N033 teaches "restricted cover" (ECGC's
case-by-case political cover); N041 says older books call the top grades "restricted". Heard once, I will merge them
("restricted = the D countries"), and an exam option using either word will catch me. N033 is also an ECGC cover rule
taught before I know what an ECGC cover is (ch3).
- Move N033 to chapter three, after N058 (political risks): "For most countries, E-C-G-C's political cover is open.
  For a few very risky ones, it's restricted: case by case." and add "Different from the old grade name 'restricted'."
  Or drop it from the voice and keep it as a PDF card (practice has no item on it). Either way chapter two loses 22
  words.

**M4 · N041 · "Same percentages" hides a real trap.** The percentages stay rung by rung, but the names moved: in older
books "moderate" is 5 % and "high" 20 %; today "moderate" (B2) is 20 % and "high" (C2) is 100 % [F14]. A learner told
"same percentages" may pair today's word with the old figure. Also "the top grades" is unclear: the ladder has A1 at
the top.
- N041 → "Older books call the two riskiest grades 'restricted' and 'off-credit', with a two per cent threshold."
- N041a (new) → "The seven percentages are the same, rung by rung. But the names moved: today, 'high' means a hundred
  per cent." (21) Keep the trap card; add "old 'high' 20 % · today 'high' (C2) 100 %".

**M5 · N038, N040, N045 · "a quarter" means 0.25 % and then 25 %.** N038 "A-one and A-two, a quarter per cent";
N040 "short-term ... may carry a quarter of it"; N045 "then a quarter of that". By ear these sound like the same
number.
- N038 → "A-one and A-two, point two five per cent. B-one, five per cent."
- N040 → "...Short-term exposure, under a hundred eighty days, may carry just twenty-five per cent of that provision."
- N079 (recap) → "provisions from point two five to a hundred."

**M6 · N035–N036, N038 · funded + non-funded vs "net funded".** N035 says the 1 % test is on net funded exposure;
N036 then says "Exposure counts both: funded ... and non-funded"; N038 says provisions are on net funded. I can't tell
which number goes into the 1 % test, and "net" (of what?) is never said.
- N036 → "For watching a country, count everything: funded, like loans; non-funded, like L-Cs and guarantees. From
  Indian and foreign branches." (20)
- N036a (new) → "But the one per cent test and the provisions use net funded exposure only. Loans, not L-Cs." (17) If the
  fact sheet gives what "net" is net of, add it on the card.

**M7 · N057–N058, N074 · ECGC's words not tied to chapter one's.** Chapter one taught "buyer risk, or credit risk" and
"country risk"; chapter three suddenly says "commercial risks" and "political risks", and the mixed check N074 asks
"commercial or political?". Two names per concept, no bridge (checklist #7).
- Before N057 add: "E-C-G-C uses two words. Commercial risk: the buyer's side, chapter one. Political risk: the
  country's side, chapter two." (19) Better still, plant the two words once in chapter one (see M9).

**M8 · N026, N054, N067, N081 · letters without their names.** I have heard "E-C-G-C" since A11b and still don't know
what it stands for; N054 gives only the 1957 name. N081 recaps "S-S-P and B-W-P, eighty", but the voice never said
those letters, and "B-W-P" doesn't even match "Export Specific Buyers Policy".
- N054 → "India's insurer is E-C-G-C: the Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India. Set up in nineteen fifty-seven
  as E-R-I-C, the Export Risks Insurance Corporation." (24)
- N067 → "...Just chosen buyers? The Export Specific Buyers Policy, called B-W-P, buyer-wise. Both pay eighty per cent."
  and say "the Specific Shipment Policy, S-S-P" once in N067 or N071; or in N081 say the full names.
- N026: "E-C-G-C, India's government-owned insurer of export payments, grades every country." (avoids "export credit
  insurer" before N051 explains it).

**M9 · Chapter one ≈ 1.6 min with no misconception; chapter two ≈ 5.0 min and very dense** (checklist #4, #10).
Chapter one is under the 3-minute floor and has no "many of you think" line; chapter two carries about fourteen new
points. Suggested balance, no new facts:
- Turn N017 into the chapter's misconception: "Many of you think: an old, honest buyer, so no risk. Is that right?"
