Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Grade C-two: what provision? One hundred per cent. Now, who protects our exporter? Export credit insurance. It pays a share of the loss when an export sold on credit isn't paid, for commercial or political reasons. Many of you think E-C-G-C insures the shirts. Does it? No. It insures the payment. Shirts lost at sea are for the marine insurer. E-C-G-C is the Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India. It began in nineteen fifty-seven as E-R-I-C, the Export Risks Insurance Corporation. It's fully owned by the Government of India, under the Commerce Ministry, and registered with I-R-D-A-I, the insurance regulator. Three jobs. Policies to exporters. Covers to banks, so exporters get finance. And insurance for Indian investment abroad. Note the words. To an exporter, a policy. To a bank, a cover. What does the policy cover? Commercial risks: the buyer goes insolvent, or simply doesn't pay within four months of the due date. Or the buyer refuses to accept the goods. Political risks: the transfer is blocked, a war, a new import ban, or a voyage diverted at extra cost. How much political cover for each country? For most, open cover: no limit. For riskier ones, restricted: a yearly limit, or case by case. Not covered: quality disputes, the exporter's own fault, exchange-rate losses, and damage to goods. And two sly ones. The buyer fails to get his own import permit: not covered. The collecting bank or agent defaults: not covered. Now the standard policy: the Shipments Comprehensive Risks Policy, S-C-R. For exports above five crore rupees a year, on short credit. Short means a credit period up to a hundred eighty days. Longer credit is for E-C-G-C's medium-term policies. Whole turnover: every shipment goes under the policy. She can't insure only the risky buyers. Now my question. How much of a loss does the standard policy pay? Ninety per cent. Not a hundred. She keeps ten, so she still chooses buyers carefully. That's risk-sharing. Our Country Z loss: eighty thousand dollars. What does E-C-G-C pay, and what does she bear? Seventy-two thousand from E-C-G-C. Eight thousand she bears. Her side of the deal: declare every shipment by the fifteenth of the next month, and report bills overdue by thirty days. And the cover starts at shipment. Before shipment, say the buyer cancels? Not under the standard policy. Exams call this pre-shipment and post-shipment risk. The rest of the shelf, one line each. Exports up to five crore: the Small Exporters Policy, S-E-P, with a shorter wait. And richer cover: ninety-five per cent for commercial losses, a hundred for political. The one policy on this shelf that can pay in full. Very small exporters, up to one crore: M-E-P, the micro exporters' policy. Large ones, twenty lakh premium or more: the Export Turnover Policy. Just one shipment? The Specific Shipment Policy, S-S-P. Just chosen buyers? The Export Specific Buyers Policy, B-W-P, buyer-wise. Both pay eighty per cent. Many shipments to one buyer: the Buyer Exposure Policy. Many buyers: the Multi-Buyer Exposure Policy. Premium on the most E-C-G-C will pay, not per shipment. And policies for consignment exports, services, software, and construction projects abroad. Pause check for chapter three. One. E-C-G-C's original name? Export Risks Insurance Corporation. Two, the twist. An exporter wants to insure just one risky shipment. Can she get it under S-C-R? No. S-C-R is whole turnover. One shipment: S-S-P. [seriously] One thing from chapter three: E-C-G-C insures payment, and usually not all of it. Back to our opening. Country Z blocks the money. Who carries the loss? With the standard policy: E-C-G-C, ninety per cent. Our exporter, ten. That's the third name. Quick mix. One. A war breaks out in the buyer's country. Commercial or political risk? Political. Two. A bank's net funded exposure to a D country, all long-term, is two per cent of its assets. Provision? One hundred per cent. Three. Who decides a country's grade for E-C-G-C cover: R-B-I or E-C-G-C? E-C-G-C. Now the map, from memory. First node: risks in trade. Buyer risk to the exporter, seller risk to the importer; commercial and political; transport to the cargo insurer. Second: country risk. Transfer risk inside it; seven grades, go by the letter; one per cent, net funded; point two five to a hundred. Third: E-C-G-C. Nineteen fifty-seven, E-R-I-C; Government-owned; policies to exporters, covers to banks. Fourth: policies. S-C-R, whole turnover, ninety per cent; S-S-P and B-W-P, eighty; S-E-P, up to a hundred. That's the map. Homework. A loss of fifty thousand dollars under the Specific Shipment Policy. What does E-C-G-C pay? Next class, her bank asks: and who covers my packing credit? E-C-G-C's covers for banks, claims, and EXIM Bank. [warmly] That's risk, named and shared. Take it easy tonight, and I'll see you in the next class.
