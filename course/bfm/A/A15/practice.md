# A15 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-22)

Our own questions, written from the fact sheet `research/bfm/facts/A7.1-A7.5_A15.md`. Sums checked by
`tools/check/country_risk.py`. All figures illustrative. Answers at the end.

1. An Indian exporter ships on D/A terms. The risk that the buyer does not pay is suffered mainly by: (a) the
   importer (b) the exporter (c) the collecting bank (d) the carrier
2. An importer pays 100 % in advance. The risk that the goods never arrive is called: (a) buyer risk (b) seller risk
   (c) transfer risk (d) sovereign risk
3. Goods are damaged at sea. The loss is normally covered by: (a) ECGC's standard policy (b) marine / cargo insurance
   (c) RBI (d) the LC issuing bank
4. A buyer is solvent and willing to pay, but its government does not allow the money to be converted and sent out.
   This is: (a) transfer risk, a part of country risk (b) commercial risk (c) exchange risk (d) legal risk
5. Which statement is correct? (a) country risk and transfer risk mean the same (b) every country-risk loss is a
   transfer-risk loss (c) transfer risk is one kind of country risk (d) a war is a transfer risk
6. ECGC's country risk classification has: (a) five categories (b) six (c) seven, A1 to D (d) ten
7. In ECGC's current legend, category **D** means: (a) insignificant risk (b) moderate risk (c) high risk (d) very high
   risk
8. ECGC publishes its country lists: (a) once in five years (b) quarter by quarter, with single-country revisions in
   between (c) only when RBI asks (d) daily
9. Under RBI's directions a bank must have a Board-approved country risk management policy, internal country rating
   and country-risk provisions for countries where its net funded exposure is at least: (a) 0.5 % (b) 1 % (c) 2 %
   (d) 5 % of total assets
10. Until a bank has its own internal country rating it may use: (a) any coaching institute's list (b) ECGC's
    seven-category classification (c) IMF's World Economic Outlook only (d) no classification at all
11. A bank's own rating of a country must be: (a) better than an international agency's (b) not better than an
    international rating agency's rating of that country (c) the same as ECGC's always (d) approved by RBI
12. Country-risk provision on a **B2** (moderate risk) country is: (a) 0.25 % (b) 5 % (c) 20 % (d) 25 %
13. Country-risk provision on **C2** and **D** countries is: (a) 25 % (b) 50 % (c) 75 % (d) 100 %
14. Total assets ₹2,00,000 crore; net funded exposure to a B2 country ₹3,000 crore, of which ₹1,000 crore under
    180 days. Country-risk provision (with the short-term relief): (a) ₹600 crore (b) ₹450 crore (c) ₹400 crore
    (d) nil
15. Same bank: exposure to a C1 country ₹1,500 crore. Country-risk provision: (a) ₹375 crore (b) ₹150 crore (c) nil,
    as exposure is below 1 % of assets (d) ₹1,500 crore
16. Country-risk provision is NOT required on exposure to: (a) a D country (b) India, the home country (c) the host
    country of the bank's foreign branch (d) an A2 country
17. ECGC was set up in 1957 under the name: (a) Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India (b) Export Risks
    Insurance Corporation (c) Export-Import Bank of India (d) National Export Insurance Account
18. ECGC is: (a) a department of RBI (b) wholly owned by the Government of India, under the Ministry of Commerce &
    Industry (c) a private insurer (d) a subsidiary of EXIM Bank
19. ECGC's product given to a bank (not to an exporter) is called a: (a) policy (b) cover / guarantee (c) LC
    (d) bill of entry
20. Which is a **political** risk under ECGC's standard policy? (a) buyer's insolvency (b) buyer does not pay within
    four months of the due date (c) a new import ban in the buyer's country (d) a quality dispute
21. Which is NOT covered by ECGC's standard policy? (a) war in the buyer's country (b) exchange-rate fluctuation
    (c) buyer's insolvency (d) delay in transfer of payment by the buyer's government
22. ECGC's Shipments Comprehensive Risks (Standard) Policy normally pays: (a) 100 % of the loss (b) 90 % (c) 75 %
    (d) 50 %
23. Loss USD 80,000 under SCR. ECGC pays: (a) USD 80,000 (b) USD 72,000 (c) USD 64,000 (d) USD 8,000
24. The SCR policy is a "whole turnover" policy. This means: (a) only the exporter's largest buyer is covered
    (b) all the exporter's shipments must be covered (LC-backed shipments may be excluded) (c) only one shipment is
    covered (d) only pre-shipment risk is covered
25. An exporter wants cover for one particular contract only. The suitable ECGC policy is: (a) SCR (b) Specific
    Shipment Policy (c) Export Turnover Policy (d) Small Exporters Policy
26. The Export Turnover Policy suits: (a) micro exporters (b) large exporters paying at least ₹20 lakh premium a year
    (c) exporters with one buyer (d) importers
27. Under the standard policy, the exporter declares shipments: (a) yearly (b) monthly, by the 15th of the next month
    (c) only when a claim arises (d) to RBI
28. Under ECGC's standard policy, cover starts: (a) when the order is received (b) from the date of shipment
    (c) when packing credit is drawn (d) when the bill is paid
29. Commercial risk of "protracted default" under the standard policy means the buyer has not paid within: (a) one
    month (b) four months of the due date (c) one year (d) 180 days of shipment
30. Older books call the two riskiest grades "restricted" and "off-credit" and use a 2 % threshold. Today's RBI text:
    (a) the same (b) "high" and "very high", threshold 1 %, same percentages (c) abolished country provisions
    (d) 50 % for both
