## Chapter 1 · Reading a quote

N005 · Chapter one. Every quote is a pair of currencies. Euro-dollar one point zero eight five zero means one euro costs one point zero eight five zero dollars.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Reading a quote" · 2 S: a balance scale: "1 EUR" on the left, "1.0850 USD" on the right [F1]

N006 · The currency on the left is the base. Always one unit. The one on the right is the quote currency. It tells you the price.
> 1 N: left pan "base (1 unit)" · right pan "quote currency (the price)" [F1]

N007 · Quick one. In dollar-rupee eighty-five point six zero, which is the base?
> 1 S: scale "1 USD = 85.60 INR" · H: 3 s

N008 · The dollar. One dollar costs eighty-five point six zero rupees. That's a direct quote: so many rupees for one unit of foreign currency. India quotes directly.
> 1 N: "base = USD ✓" · 2 C: card "direct = ₹ per 1 (or 100) foreign units · India's merchant quotes (FEDAI 5.7)" [F2]

N009 · Turn it round, and you get an indirect quote: foreign currency for a hundred rupees. A hundred, divided by eighty-five point six. One point one six eight two dollars per hundred rupees.
> 1 S: keys 100 ÷ 85.60 = · 2 C: card "indirect = foreign units per ₹100 (USD 1.1682 per ₹100)" [F2]

N009a · With two sides, flip and swap. The indirect bid is a hundred divided by the direct ask. The indirect ask is a hundred divided by the direct bid.
> 1 S: "85.60 / 85.62 → USD 1.1680 / 1.1682 per ₹100" · 2 B: crossed arrows "÷ ask → bid · ÷ bid → ask" [F2]

N009b · Careful. Here the rupee is the base. So the bank buys dollars at the higher figure, one point one six eight two. Buy high, sell low.
> 1 N: "indirect: bank buys USD at 1.1682, sells USD at 1.1680 → buy high, sell low (for the foreign currency)" [F2, F12]

N010 · And remember the two sides. The bid is where the bank buys the base currency. The ask is where it sells it.
> 1 N: "bid: bank buys the base · ask: bank sells the base" [F3]

N011 · Now the word for today. A cross rate is the rate between two currencies, fixed through a third. Usually the dollar.
> 1 C: card "cross rate = rate between two currencies through a third (usually USD)" [F4]; second line "FBIL crosses its EUR, GBP, JPY rates through USD/INR · JPY per 100" [F5] · 2 B: box "through a third"

## Chapter 2 · Cross rates: multiply or divide

N014 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. In euro-dollar, which currency is the base?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Multiply or divide" · H: 3 s

N015 · The euro. Now look where the dollar sits. In euro-dollar, it's on the right. In dollar-rupee, it's on the left. Opposite sides.
> 1 N: "base = EUR ✓" · 2 S: two scales side by side; the USD glows right in one, left in the other [F7]

N016 · Rule one. When the dollar sits on opposite sides, multiply. The dollars cancel, and you're left with euro-rupee.
> 1 C: card "USD on opposite sides → MULTIPLY" [F7] · 2 S: "EUR→USD × USD→INR = EUR→INR"

N017 · And multiply like with like. Bid with bid. Ask with ask. Small times small gives the smallest answer. That's the bid.
> 1 B: box "bid × bid · ask × ask" [F7, F9]

N018 · Press along. Euro-dollar: bid one point zero eight five zero, ask one point zero eight five five. Dollar-rupee: bid eighty-five point six zero, ask eighty-five point six two.
> 1 S: quote cards (illustrative)

N019 · Bid: one point zero eight five. The last zero needn't be keyed. Times eighty-five point six, equals. Ninety-two point eight seven six zero.
> 1 S: keys 1.085 × 85.60 = · H: 6 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "EUR/INR bid 92.8760"

N020 · Ask: one point zero eight five five, times eighty-five point six two, equals. Ninety-two point nine four zero five.
> 1 S: keys 1.0855 × 85.62 = · H: 6 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "EUR/INR ask 92.9405"

N021 · Many of you will multiply the euro-dollar bid by the dollar-rupee ask. That gives ninety-two point eight nine seven seven. Wrong. Bid goes with bid.
> 1 C: trap card "bid × ask = 92.8977 ✗ · bid × bid = 92.8760 ✓" [F7]

N021a · A cross rate's spread, in percent, is about the two spreads added. So a cross costs a little more.
> 1 N: "EUR/USD 0.046% + USD/INR 0.023% ≈ EUR/INR 0.069%"

N022 · Your turn. Pound-dollar: bid one point two six five zero, ask one point two six six zero. Same dollar-rupee. Find pound-rupee, bid and ask. Pause.
> 1 S: card "GBP/USD 1.2650 / 1.2660 · USD/INR 85.60 / 85.62" (illustrative) · H: 15 s

N023 · Bid, one point two six five, times eighty-five point six. One hundred eight point two eight four zero. Ask, one point two six six, times eighty-five point six two. One hundred eight point three nine four nine.
> 1 B: boxes "GBP/INR 108.2840 / 108.3949"

N024 · Now rule two. Look at dollar-rupee and dollar-yen. Dollar-yen: bid one hundred forty-nine point five zero, ask one hundred forty-nine point six zero. The dollar is the base in both. Same side.
> 1 S: two scales, USD on the left in both [F8]

N025 · When the dollar sits on the same side in both, divide. Here, rupees per dollar, divided by yen per dollar, gives rupees per yen.
> 1 C: card "USD on the SAME side (base in both, or quote in both) → DIVIDE" [F8] · 2 S: "(INR per USD) ÷ (JPY per USD) = INR per JPY"

N026 · And here the sides cross. For the bid, divide the rupee bid by the yen ask. For the ask, divide the rupee ask by the yen bid.
> 1 B: crossed arrows "bid ÷ ask · ask ÷ bid" [F8]

N027 · Why? The bid must be the smallest answer you can make. So put the smaller rupee figure on top, and the bigger yen figure below.
> 1 S: a fraction "85.60 (small) / 149.60 (big) → smallest = bid" · 2 B: circle "small" and "big" [F8, F9]

N027a · Small on top, big below: the smallest answer. That's the bid. The ask is the other way round: big on top, small below.
> 1 S: second fraction "85.62 (big) / 149.50 (small) → biggest = ask"

N028 · Press along. Eighty-five point six, divided by one forty-nine point six, times a hundred, for per hundred yen, equals. Fifty-seven point two one nine three.
> 1 S: keys 85.60 ÷ 149.60 × 100 = · H: 8 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "JPY/INR bid 57.2193 per 100"

N029 · Ask: eighty-five point six two, divided by one forty-nine point five, times a hundred, equals. Fifty-seven point two seven zero nine.
> 1 S: keys 85.62 ÷ 149.50 × 100 = · H: 8 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "JPY/INR ask 57.2709 per 100"

N030 · Many of you divided bid by bid, and ask by ask. That gives fifty-seven point two five seven five, and fifty-seven point two three two six.
> 1 C: trap card "bid ÷ bid 57.2575 · ask ÷ ask 57.2326" [F9]

N030a · Look. That bid is above that ask. That can't be. So always check: is my bid below my ask?
> 1 B: circle "57.2575 > 57.2326 ✗" · 2 B: box "check: bid < ask" [F9]

N030b · Same side works on the right too. Euro-dollar and pound-dollar: the dollar is the quote currency in both. Divide, and cross the sides, for euro-pound.
> 1 S: "EUR/GBP bid 1.0850 ÷ 1.2660 = 0.8570 · ask 1.0855 ÷ 1.2650 = 0.8581" (illustrative) [F8]

N031 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Euro-dollar and dollar-rupee: multiply or divide?
> 1 S: card "EUR/USD & USD/INR → ?" · H: 3 s

N032 · Multiply. The dollar sits on opposite sides. Two. Dollar-rupee and dollar-yen?
> 1 N: "multiply ✓" · H: 3 s

N033 · Divide. The dollar is on the same side. Three, the twist. For the yen-rupee bid, which yen figure do you divide by?
> 1 N: "divide ✓" · H: 3 s

N034 · The yen ask, the bigger one. So the bid comes out smaller.

## Chapter 3 · The chain rule

N036 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Bid times bid, or bid times ask?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · The chain rule" · H: 3 s

N037 · Bid times bid. Now, sometimes a question gives three or four links. That's the chain rule. Think of rings, each holding the next.
> 1 N: "bid × bid ✓" · 2 S: a chain of rings [F10]

N038 · How many rupees for one pound? On another day's rates: one pound is one point one seven euro. One euro is one point zero eight five dollars. One dollar is eighty-five point six zero rupees.
> 1 S: rings fill: "1 GBP = 1.17 EUR" · "1 EUR = 1.085 USD" · "1 USD = 85.60 INR" (illustrative, a different day's rates)

N039 · See how each ring starts with the currency the last one ended in? Pound, euro, dollar, rupee.
> 1 B: underline the joining currencies EUR, USD [F10]

N040 · So multiply along the chain. One point one seven, times one point zero eight five, times eighty-five point six, equals. One hundred eight point six six four nine.
> 1 S: keys 1.17 × 1.085 × 85.60 = · H: 8 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "₹108.6649 per GBP"

N040a · Sometimes a ring starts with a number other than one, like a hundred yen. Then the full rule: multiply the right-hand numbers, and divide by the left-hand ones.
> 1 C: card "chain: × every right-hand number · ÷ every left-hand number" [F10, F11]

N040b · Your turn. One pound is one point one seven euro. Zero point nine two one seven euro is one dollar.
> 1 S: rings "1 GBP = 1.17 EUR" · "0.9217 EUR = 1 USD" (illustrative)

N040c · One dollar is one forty-nine point five yen. A hundred yen is fifty-seven point two two rupees. Rupees for one pound? Pause the video if you need.

N040d · Right side: one point one seven, times one forty-nine point five, times fifty-seven point two two. Then divide by zero point nine two one seven, and by a hundred.

> 1 B: box "₹108.5889 per GBP" · 2 C: trap card "× 0.9217 → 92.2496 ✗" [F10]
