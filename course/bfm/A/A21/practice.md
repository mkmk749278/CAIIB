# A21 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Eight original exam-style questions (6 MCQs + 2 keyed-in numericals) written strictly from A21_fact_sheet (rules as at 31 December 2026) and A21_numericals. All names and figures are illustrative. Options use realistic exam traps (1947 vs 1939 exchange control start, pre-2026 AD-II ban on trade, 10 vs 8 FEDAI Rules, FEDAI supervising / ICC representation, flat ₹2 lakh compounding cap myth). Answers with one-line reasons citing [F#] are at the end.


1. Exchange control was first introduced in India on 3 September 1939 under which legal instrument?
(a) The Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947
(b) The Defence of India Rules
(c) The Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
(d) The Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973
2. Under FEMA, 1999, which pair correctly states the rule-making and regulation-making authorities?
(a) Central Government makes Regulations under s. 46; RBI makes Rules under s. 47
(b) Central Government makes Rules under s. 46; RBI makes Regulations under s. 47
(c) RBI makes both Rules and Regulations under s. 47
(d) Directorate of Enforcement makes Rules under s. 46; Central Government makes Regulations under s. 47
3. Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Authorised Persons) Regulations, 2026 (in force 6 May 2026), which statement about AD Category-II is CORRECT?
(a) AD Category-II entities remain prohibited from undertaking any foreign trade transactions
(b) AD Category-II licences must be renewed every 1 to 5 years
(c) AD Category-II may handle permissible non-trade current account transactions (excluding gift and donation) and foreign trade transactions up to ₹25 lakh per transaction
(d) Only scheduled commercial banks may apply for AD Category-II authorisation
4. Under the 2026 Authorised Persons framework and updated RBI Master Direction on Money Changing Activities, what is the status of money-changing franchisees and Forex Correspondents (FxCs)?
(a) Fresh franchisees may be appointed by FFMCs, and FxCs may only purchase foreign currency
(b) No fresh franchisees are permitted from 6 May 2026, existing franchisees must wind down within 2 years (by 6 May 2028), and AD Cat-I / AD Cat-II may appoint FxCs to both buy and sell travel forex
(c) Existing franchisees are automatically converted into AD Category-III entities
(d) Only FFMCs may appoint Forex Correspondents under a principal-agent model
5. Which of the following statements about FEDAI as at 31 December 2026 is INCORRECT?
(a) FEDAI was formally recognised by RBI as a Self-Regulatory Organisation (SRO) on 14 January 2026
(b) FEDAI publishes Month-End Revaluation Rates for 26 currencies on the last working day of each month for uniform AS 11 valuation
(c) FEDAI Rules (10th Edition, updated 20 August 2026) contain 8 Rules and leave member banks free to determine their own charges
(d) FEDAI exercises statutory supervisory inspection powers over banks and formally represents India at the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC)
6. Which international body is incorrectly matched with its headquarters?
(a) Bank for International Settlements (BIS) & BCBS — Basel, Switzerland
(b) International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) — Paris, France
(c) SWIFT — La Hulpe, Belgium
(d) World Bank (IBRD / World Bank Group) — Geneva, Switzerland
7. (Keyed-in numerical) Under RBI Master Directions on Compounding of Contraventions under FEMA (updated 23 October 2026), a company applies to compound a quantifiable contravention of ₹85,00,000 (not falling under the discretionary Row 5 non-reporting cap). What is the statutory maximum compounding ceiling in rupees (300% of sum involved)?
8. (Keyed-in numerical) Under the Foreign Exchange (Compounding Proceedings) Rules, 2024, an applicant submits a compounding application attracting the statutory fee of ₹10,000 plus 18% GST. Calculate the total payable application fee in rupees.
Answers and one-line reasons
   1. (b) — Exchange control began in India on 3 September 1939 as a temporary wartime measure under the Defence of India Rules [F1].
   2. (b) — Under FEMA, the Central Government makes Rules under s. 46 and RBI makes Regulations under s. 47 (both laid before Parliament for 30 days) [F13].
   3. (c) — Under FEMA 401/2026-RB Reg. 7 (effective 6 May 2026), AD Category-II receives perpetual authorisation and may handle permissible non-trade current account transactions (other than gift and donation) plus foreign trade transactions up to ₹25 lakh per transaction [F22, F25].
   4. (b) — Under FEMA 401/2026-RB Regs. 10–16 and MD Money Changing (updated 29 Sep 2026), no fresh franchisees are allowed from 6 May 2026, existing ones must wind down by 6 May 2028, and AD Cat-I/II (not FFMCs) may appoint FxCs to both purchase and sell travel forex [F26, F27].
   5. (d) — FEDAI does not supervise banks (RBI supervises and inspects ADs under FEMA ss. 10–12) and does not represent India at the ICC (ICC India / FICCI does) [F11, F32].
   6. (d) — The World Bank (World Bank Group: IBRD, IDA, IFC, MIGA, ICSID) is headquartered in Washington, D.C., USA, not Geneva [F38].
   7. ₹2,55,00,000 — General compounding ceiling is 300% of the sum involved (8500000 × 300 % = ₹2,55,00,000; the ₹2 lakh cap is discretionary for Row 5 non-reporting cases only) [F16].
   8. ₹11,800 — Compounding application fee is ₹10,000 + 18% GST (10000 × 1.18 = ₹11,800) [F14].
