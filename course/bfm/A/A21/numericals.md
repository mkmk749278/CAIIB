Sum 1 (Worked) · FEMA s. 13(1) Maximum Penalty on Quantifiable Contravention (N068–N071)
* Data: An Indian firm (illustrative) commits a quantifiable FEMA contravention of ₹40,00,000 (₹40 lakh) that continues for 10 days after the first day. Find the maximum statutory penalty under FEMA s. 13(1) [F12].
* Method 1 (IIBF calculator key presses):
   * Principal penalty: 4000000 × 3 = → ₹1,20,00,000
   * Continuing penalty: 10 × 5000 = → ₹50,000
   * Total maximum penalty: 12000000 + 50000 = → ₹1,20,50,000
* Method 2 (In ₹ lakh):
   * 40 × 3 = 120 lakh; 10 × 0.05 = 0.50 lakh; 120 + 0.50 = ₹120.50 lakh (= ₹1,20,50,000). Both methods match.
* Common wrong answer (trap): Applying the ₹2,00,000 ceiling (wrong — ₹2 lakh applies only when the contravention amount is not quantifiable).
Sum 2 (Faded) · FEMA s. 11(3) Maximum Penalty on an Authorised Person (N072–N073)
* Data: An Authorised Person fails to comply with an RBI direction and continues the default for 15 days after the first day. Find the maximum penalty RBI may impose under FEMA s. 11(3) [F11].
* Method 1 (IIBF calculator key presses):
   * Continuing component: 15 × 2000 = → ₹30,000
   * Add base component: 30000 + 10000 = → ₹40,000
* Method 2 (Base first, then add 15 increments of ₹2,000):
   * Base ₹10,000 + 10 days (₹20,000) + 5 days (₹10,000) = 10000 + 20000 + 10000 = ₹40,000. Both methods match.
* Common wrong answer (trap): Using the s. 13(1) continuing rate of ₹5,000/day (10000 + 75000 = ₹85,000) instead of s. 11(3)'s ₹2,00,00/day.
Sum 3 (Your Turn) · AD Category-II Average Annual Forex Turnover Check (N074–N075)
* Data: An FFMC functioning for 3 years posts annual forex turnover of ₹44 crore in Year 1 and ₹58 crore in Year 2 (excluding inward remittances processed). Does it meet the 2026 turnover threshold to apply for AD Category-II? [F21]
* Method 1 (IIBF calculator key presses):
   * 44 + 58 = 102 ÷ 2 = → ₹51.00 crore (≥ ₹50 crore → Eligible on turnover, subject to ₹10 crore minimum positive net worth).
* Method 2 (Deviation from ₹50 crore target):
   * Year 1 shortfall: 44 − 50 = −6 crore; Year 2 surplus: 58 − 50 = +8 crore; Net surplus = +2 crore → Average = 50 + (2 ÷ 2) = ₹51.00 crore (≥ ₹50 crore → Eligible). Both methods match.
Homework Sum · ODI Financial Commitment Ceiling (N108)
* Data: An Indian manufacturing company (illustrative) has a net worth of ₹120 crore as per its last audited balance sheet. What is its maximum permissible total financial commitment for ODI under the automatic route? [F17]
* Method 1 (IIBF calculator % key):
   * 120 × 400 % = → ₹480.00 crore
* Method 2 (Multiplier 4):
   * 120 × 4 = → ₹480.00 crore. Both methods match.
