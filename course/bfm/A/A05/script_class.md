## Chapter 1 · Value dates

N006 · Chapter one. Every forex deal has two dates. The day you agree it, and the day the money moves. That second day is the value date.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Value dates" · 2 S: calendar strip; "deal date" and "value date" pins [F1]

N007 · Cash, or ready, means the money moves the same day. Value next day, or tom, means the next working day.
> 1 N: "cash / ready = same day · tom = next working day" [F1]

N008 · And spot means the second working day after the deal. That's the market's normal date.
> 1 B: box "spot = 2nd working day" [F1]

N009 · Quick one. A deal on Thursday, the third of December. When is spot? Remember, Saturday is not a forex working day.
> 1 S: calendar "Thu 3 Dec 2026" (illustrative) · H: 3 s

N010 · Monday, the seventh. Friday is day one, the weekend is skipped, Monday is day two.
> 1 N: "Fri = 1 · Sat, Sun skipped · Mon 7 Dec = spot ✓" [F3]

N011 · Anything later than spot is a forward. And a forward's months are counted from the spot date, not the deal date.
> 1 C: card "forward = beyond spot · months counted from the spot date" [F1, F2]

N012 · So a one-month forward from that Thursday's deal falls on the seventh of January.
> 1 S: arrow on the strip "7 Dec → 7 Jan 2027" [F2]

N013 · What if that day is a holiday? A known holiday, known three working days ahead, moves delivery back to the day before. A sudden one moves it forward, to the next working day.
> 1 C: card "known holiday → preceding working day · sudden holiday → next working day" [F4, F5]

N014 · One more word. A forward with a bank is O-T-C, over the counter. Any amount, any date, made for you.
> 1 S: two columns open: "forward (OTC)" [F20]

N015 · A currency future is the opposite. Standard size, traded on an exchange, margins every day. The exchange's clearing house stands behind it.
> 1 S: second column "future: exchange · USD 1,000 lots · daily margin · clearing guarantee" [F20]

N016 · Pause check for chapter one. One. Spot is which day after the deal?
> 1 S: card "spot = ?" · H: 3 s

N017 · The second working day. Two. A known holiday on the delivery date. Earlier day or later day?
> 1 N: "2nd working day ✓" · H: 3 s

N018 · Earlier, the working day before. Three, the twist. A one-month forward is counted from which date?

N019 · From spot. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: spot is two working days, and forwards count from spot.

## Chapter 2 · Premium and discount

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. A forward with a bank: exchange or over the counter?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Premium and discount" · H: 3 s

N021 · Over the counter. Now, the price. A forward rate is the spot rate, plus or minus some points.
> 1 N: "OTC ✓" · 2 C: card "forward = spot ± points" [F9]

N022 · If the dollar costs more later, it's at a premium. You add. If it costs less later, it's at a discount. You take away.
> 1 B: arrow up "premium → add" · arrow down "discount → subtract" [F9]

N023 · How do you tell from the screen? Look at the two point figures. Small then big, it's a premium. Big then small, a discount.
> 1 S: two cards "0.36 / 0.39 → premium, add" · "0.24 / 0.20 → discount, subtract" [F9]

N024 · Press along. Spot eighty-five point six zero, eighty-five point six two. Two-month points, zero point three six, zero point three nine.
> 1 S: quote card "spot 85.60 / 85.62 · 1m 0.18/0.20 · 2m 0.36/0.39 · 3m 0.54/0.58" (illustrative)

N025 · Small then big, so add. Bid: eighty-five point six, plus point three six. Eighty-five point nine six. Ask: eighty-five point six two, plus point three nine. Eighty-six point zero one.
> 1 S: keys 85.60 + 0.36 = · 85.62 + 0.39 = · H: 6 s · 2 B: box "2m forward 85.96 / 86.01"

N026 · Now the big question. Why is the dollar dearer later? Many of you think the market expects the rupee to fall.
> 1 S: thought bubble "the rupee will fall?" · H: 3 s

N027 · Not quite. The premium comes from interest rates. Rupee deposits pay more than dollar deposits. The forward rate evens that out.
> 1 C: card "premium = interest-rate gap, not a forecast" [F10]

N028 · Think of it this way. If the forward were the same as spot, you'd convert to rupees, earn more interest, and convert back at no cost. Free money. The premium closes that door.
> 1 S: two piggy banks "₹ 6.5%" · "$ 4.5%" (illustrative); a door closing [F10]

N029 · Let's see it. Spot eighty-five point six zero. Rupee interest six and a half percent, dollar four and a half. Three months.
> 1 S: data card "S 85.60 · ₹ 6.5% · $ 4.5% · 3 months" (illustrative)

N030 · Eighty-five point six, times one point zero one six two five. Divided by one point zero one one two five. Eighty-six point zero two three two.
> 1 S: keys 85.60 × 1.01625 ÷ 1.01125 = · H: 8 s · 2 B: box "3m forward 86.0232" [F11]

N031 · That's a premium of about two percent a year. The same as the interest gap. So the higher-interest currency, the rupee, sits at a discount.
> 1 N: "≈ 2% a year = 6.5% − 4.5%" [F11] · 2 C: trap card "premium = forecast ✗ · = volatility ✗ · = interest gap ✓" [F10]

N032 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Points zero point two four, zero point two zero. Premium or discount?
> 1 S: card "0.24 / 0.20 → ?" · H: 3 s

N033 · Discount. Big then small, so take away. Two. Which currency trades at a forward discount, the higher-interest one or the lower?

N034 · The higher-interest one. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: small then big, add; and the premium is the interest gap.

## Chapter 3 · The option period

N035 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Zero point three six, zero point three nine. Add or take away?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · The option period" · H: 3 s

N036 · Add. Now, a customer often doesn't know the exact day the money will come. So F-E-D-A-I lets him pick a window. That's the option period.
> 1 N: "add ✓" · 2 S: calendar strip with a shaded window [F6]

N037 · The window is at most one month. The contract states the first and the last date. And the customer chooses the day of delivery inside it.
> 1 C: card "option period ≤ 1 month · first & last dates in the contract · customer chooses the day" [F6, F7]

N038 · But which rate does the bank quote for a whole window? The rule of thumb: the date in the window that's best for the bank.
> 1 S: the "bank's favour" arrow appears over the window [F12]

N039 · So, if the bank is selling dollars at a premium, it charges the premium to the last date. The highest rate.
> 1 B: arrow to the window's end "sale + premium → last date" [F12]

N040 · If the bank is buying dollars at a premium, it gives the premium only to the first date. The lowest rate.
> 1 B: arrow to the window's start "purchase + premium → first date" [F12] · 2 N: "(exam convention)"

N041 · Our importer. The bank sells him dollars, delivery any day in the second month. Premium, so which date? Pause.
> 1 S: importer card "bank sells USD · option: 2nd month" · H: 5 s

N042 · The last date, end of month two. Two-month ask, eighty-six point zero one. Plus the T-T selling margin, point one two percent.
> 1 B: box "2m ask 86.01" · 2 S: margin card "TT selling + 0.12%" (illustrative)

N043 · Eighty-six point zero one, times one point zero zero one two. Eighty-six point one one three two. That's his rate.
> 1 S: keys 86.01 × 1.0012 = · H: 6 s · 2 B: box "86.1132"

N044 · For fifty thousand dollars: forty-three lakh, five thousand, six hundred sixty rupees. Fixed today, whatever the rupee does.

## Chapter 4 · Usance bills

N051 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Bank selling, premium. First date or last?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · Usance bills" · H: 3 s

N052 · Last. Now, a usance bill. The exporter gives his buyer sixty days to pay, after sight. The bank buys the bill today and waits for the money.
> 1 N: "last ✓" · 2 S: an export bill "USD 20,000 · 60 days sight" (illustrative)

N053 · How long does it wait? The usance, plus the normal transit period. For a foreign currency bill, not under a letter of credit, F-E-D-A-I sets that at twenty-five days.
> 1 C: card "normal transit period: FC bill, not under LC = 25 days" [F15] · 2 S: strip "25 days transit + 60 days usance"

N054 · Sixty plus twenty-five, eighty-five days. Premium, and the bank is buying. Round up to three months, or down to two?
> 1 S: "85 days → ? months" · H: 3 s

N055 · Down, to two completed months. Rounding up would give the customer more premium than he has earned.
> 1 B: box "85 days → 2 months (premium, round down)" [F14] · 2 N: "(exam convention)"

N056 · Two-month bid, eighty-five point nine six. Less the bill buying margin, point one five percent. Times point nine nine eight five. Eighty-five point eight three one one.
> 1 S: keys 85.96 × 0.9985 = · H: 6 s · 2 B: box "85.8311"

N057 · Twenty thousand dollars, so seventeen lakh, sixteen thousand, six hundred twenty-two rupees. And the bank recovers interest for those eighty-five days upfront.
> 1 S: keys 20000 × 85.8311 = · 2 B: box "₹17,16,622.00" · 3 N: "interest for transit + usance: recovered upfront" [F13]

N058 · One exception. If the bill has a fixed due date, the bank already knows when it's paid. Then the transit period doesn't apply.
> 1 C: card "fixed due date → no transit period" [F16]

N059 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter four: usance plus transit, round in the bank's favour, then the margin.
