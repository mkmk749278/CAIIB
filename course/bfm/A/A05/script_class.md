## Chapter 1 · Value dates

N006 · Chapter one. Every forex deal has two dates. The day you agree it, and the day the money moves. That second day is the value date.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Value dates" · 2 S: calendar strip; "deal date" and "value date" pins [F1]

N007 · Cash, or ready, means the money moves the same day. Value next day, or tom, means the next working day.
> 1 N: "cash / ready = same day · tom = next working day" [F1]

N008 · And spot means the second working day after the deal. That's the market's normal date.
> 1 B: box "spot = 2nd working day" [F1]

N009 · Quick one. A deal on Thursday, the third of December. When is spot? Remember, Saturday is not a forex working day.
> 1 S: calendar "Thu 3 Dec 2026" (illustrative) · H: 3 s

N010 · Monday, the seventh. Friday is day one, the weekend is skipped, Monday is day two. A holiday in between is skipped the same way.
> 1 N: "Fri = 1 · Sat, Sun skipped · Mon 7 Dec = spot ✓ · holidays skipped too" [F3]

N011 · Anything later than spot is a forward. And a forward's months are counted from the spot date, not the deal date.
> 1 C: card "forward = beyond spot · months counted from the spot date" [F1, F2]

N012 · So a one-month forward from that Thursday's deal falls on the seventh of January.
> 1 S: arrow on the strip "7 Dec → 7 Jan 2027" [F2]

N013 · What if that day is a holiday? A known holiday, known three working days ahead, moves delivery back to the working day before. A sudden one moves it forward, to the next working day.
> 1 C: card "known holiday → preceding working day · sudden holiday → next working day (merchant contracts, FEDAI 5.2)" [F4, F5]

N014 · A forward with a bank is O-T-C, over the counter. Any amount, any date.
> 1 S: two columns open: "forward (OTC): any amount · any date · settled by delivery" [F20]

N015 · A currency future is standard, traded on an exchange. You keep a deposit there, topped up daily. The clearing corporation guarantees every deal.
> 1 S: second column "future: exchange (since 2008) · USD 1,000 lots · daily deposit (margin) · clearing corporation guarantee" [F20] · 2 N: "futures margin = a deposit, not the bank's rate margin"

## Chapter 2 · Premium and discount

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. A forward with a bank: exchange or over the counter?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Premium and discount" · H: 3 s

N021 · Over the counter. Now, the price. A forward rate is the spot rate, plus or minus some points. Here, points are just rupees. Zero point three six is thirty-six paise.
> 1 N: "OTC ✓" · 2 C: card "forward = spot ± points" [F9] · 3 N: "points = ₹ amounts: 0.36 = 36 paise"

N022 · If the dollar costs more later, it's at a premium. You add. If it costs less later, it's at a discount. You take away.
> 1 B: arrow up "premium → add" · arrow down "discount → subtract" [F9]

N023 · How do you tell from the screen? Look at the two point figures. Small then big, it's a premium. Big then small, a discount.
> 1 S: two cards "0.36 / 0.39 → premium, add" · "0.24 / 0.20 → discount, subtract" [F9]

N024 · Press along. Spot eighty-five point six zero, eighty-five point six two. Two-month points, zero point three six, zero point three nine.
> 1 S: quote card "spot 85.60 / 85.62 · 1m 0.18/0.20 · 2m 0.36/0.39 · 3m 0.54/0.58" (illustrative); the card stays up to N047

N025 · Small then big, so add. Bid: eighty-five point six, plus point three six, equals. Eighty-five point nine six. Ask: eighty-five point six two, plus point three nine, equals. Eighty-six point zero one.
> 1 S: keys 85.60 + 0.36 = · H: 6 s (after "equals") · 2 S: keys 85.62 + 0.39 = · H: 6 s · 3 B: box "2m forward 85.96 / 86.01"

N026 · Now last class's question. Why is the dollar dearer later? Many of you think the market expects the rupee to fall.
> 1 S: thought bubble "the rupee will fall?" · H: 3 s

N027 · Not quite. The premium comes from interest rates. Rupee deposits pay more than dollar deposits. The forward rate evens that out.
> 1 C: card "premium = interest-rate gap, not a forecast" [F10]

N028 · Say you borrow dollars at four and a half percent. Change them to rupees, and earn six and a half.
> 1 S: two piggy banks "$ 4.5%" · "₹ 6.5%" (illustrative) [F10]

N028a · Change back at the same rate, and that's two percent free. The premium closes that door. Buying dollars back costs more, and eats the two percent.
> 1 S: a door closing; "+2% − 2% = 0" [F10]

N029 · Let's see it. Spot eighty-five point six zero. Rupee interest six and a half percent, dollar four and a half. Three months.
> 1 S: data card "S 85.60 · ₹ 6.5% · $ 4.5% · 3 months" (illustrative)

N029a · Three months is a quarter year. A quarter of six and a half percent is one point six two five percent. So one rupee grows to one point zero one six two five.
> 1 S: keys 6.5 ÷ 4 = 1.625 · 2 N: "₹ growth 1.01625"

N029b · A quarter of four and a half is one point one two five percent. One dollar grows to one point zero one one two five.
> 1 N: "$ growth 1.01125"

N029c · Forward equals spot, times the rupee growth, divided by the dollar growth.
> 1 C: card "F = S × (1 + ₹ rate × t) ÷ (1 + $ rate × t)" [F11]

N030 · Eighty-five point six, times one point zero one six two five, divided by one point zero one one two five, equals. Eighty-six point zero two three two.
> 1 S: keys 85.60 × 1.01625 ÷ 1.01125 = · H: 12 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "3m forward 86.0232" [F11]

N031 · Dollar at a premium means the rupee, the higher-interest currency, is at a discount.
> 1 C: trap card "premium = forecast ✗ · = volatility ✗ · = interest gap ✓" [F10]

N031a · How big a premium, per year? Forward minus spot, divided by spot. Then times twelve over the months, times a hundred.
> 1 C: card "annual % = (F − S) ÷ S × 12/months × 100" [F11]

N031b · Eighty-six point zero two three two, minus eighty-five point six. Divided by eighty-five point six, times four, times a hundred, equals. One point nine eight. The gap.
> 1 S: keys 86.0232 − 85.60 = ÷ 85.60 × 4 × 100 = · H: 8 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "≈ 1.98% a year ≈ 6.5% − 4.5%"

N031c · Exams also run it backwards, with no options. Premium two point four percent a year, two months. Two point four, divided by twelve, times two.
> 1 S: label "No options · 4 decimals" · card "spot 83.50 · premium 2.4% p.a. · 2 months" (illustrative) · H: 8 s

N031d · Point four percent. So eighty-three point five, times one point zero zero four. Eighty-three point eight three four zero.
> 1 S: keys 2.4 ÷ 12 × 2 = · 83.50 × 1.004 = · 2 B: box "83.8340" · 3 C: trap card "× 1.024 = 85.5040 ✗ (not cut to 2 months)"

N031e · One condition. The premium equals the interest gap exactly only for freely convertible currencies. For the rupee, exporters' and importers' demand also moves it.
> 1 C: card "premium = interest gap exactly → only for freely convertible currencies" [F10] · 2 N: "C01: how dealers trade the gap"

## Chapter 3 · The option period

N035 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Zero point three six, zero point three nine. Add or take away?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · The option period" · H: 3 s

N036 · Add. Now, like our importer, a customer often doesn't know the exact day. So he picks a window: the option period.
> 1 N: "add ✓" · 2 S: calendar strip with a shaded window [F6]

N037 · The window is at most one month. The contract states the first and the last date. And the customer chooses the day of delivery inside it.
> 1 C: card "option period ≤ 1 month · first & last dates in the contract · customer chooses the day" [F6, F7]

N037a · "Second month" means from the end of month one to the end of month two. "Third month", from the end of month two to the end of month three.
> 1 S: the strip marked "1m · 2m · 3m"; the 2nd-month window shaded between the 1m and 2m pins

N038 · But which rate does the bank quote for a whole window? The date in it that's best for the bank. Selling, it wants the most rupees. Buying, it wants to pay the fewest.
> 1 S: the "bank's favour" arrow appears over the window · 2 N: "sell → highest ₹ · buy → lowest ₹" [F12]

N039 · So, if the bank is selling dollars at a premium, it charges the premium to the last date. The highest rate.
> 1 B: arrow to the window's end "sale + premium → last date" [F12]

N040 · If the bank is buying dollars at a premium, it gives the premium only to the first date. The lowest rate. That's the exam convention, not a F-E-D-A-I line.
> 1 B: arrow to the window's start "purchase + premium → first date" [F12] · 2 N: "(exam convention)"

## Chapter 4 · Usance bills

N051 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Bank selling, premium. First date or last?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · Usance bills" · H: 3 s

N052 · Last. Now, a usance bill: a bill with time to pay. The buyer pays sixty days after sight, after he first sees the bill. The bank buys the bill today.
> 1 N: "last ✓" · 2 S: an export bill "USD 20,000 · 60 days sight" (illustrative)

N052a · The dollars come in later, so the bank prices them for that later date. That's a forward rate.
> 1 N: "dollars later → forward rate"

N053 · How long does the bank wait? First the bill travels abroad, and the money comes back. That's the normal transit period.
> 1 S: strip "bill travels → buyer sees it → 60 days → money home"

N053a · For a foreign currency bill not under a letter of credit, F-E-D-A-I sets it at twenty-five days. For a bill payable after sight, the bank adds it to the usance.
> 1 C: card "normal transit period: FC bill, not under LC = 25 days (FEDAI 2.3) · after-sight bill: transit + usance" [F15]

N054 · Sixty plus twenty-five, eighty-five days. Premium, and the bank is buying. Round up to three months, or down to two?
> 1 S: "85 days → ? months" · H: 3 s

N055 · Down, to two completed months. Rounding up would give more premium than he has earned. Exam convention again.
> 1 B: box "85 days → 2 months (premium, round down)" [F14] · 2 N: "(exam convention)"

N055a · At a discount, it's the other way. Round up to the next month. A bigger discount gives a lower rate, again in the bank's favour.
> 1 C: card "bank buys · premium → round down · discount → round up" [F14]

N056 · Two-month bid, eighty-five point nine six. Less the bill buying margin, point one five percent. Times point nine nine eight five, equals. Eighty-five point eight three one one.
> 1 S: keys 85.96 × 0.9985 = · H: 6 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "85.8311"

N057 · Times twenty thousand, equals. Seventeen lakh, sixteen thousand, six hundred twenty-two rupees. And the bank recovers interest for those eighty-five days upfront.
> 1 S: keys × 20000 = · H: 5 s (after "equals") · 2 B: box "₹17,16,622.00" · 3 N: "interest for transit + usance: recovered upfront" [F13]

N058 · One exception. If the bill has a fixed due date, the bank already knows when it's paid. Then the transit period doesn't apply.
> 1 C: card "fixed due date → no transit period" [F16]

N059 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter four: usance plus transit, round in the bank's favour, then the margin.
> 1 S: the strip; finished parts dim to grey
