## Chapter 1 · The rule book

N006 · Chapter one. First, from last class. Banks deal in goods, or in documents?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · The rule book" · H: 3 s

N007 · Documents. And the rule book for those documents is U-C-P six hundred. Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits, from the I-C-C in Paris.
> 1 C: card "UCP 600 · ICC · in force 1 July 2007" [F1]

N008 · Many of you think it is a law. It isn't. It applies only when the L-C itself says it is subject to U-C-P six hundred.
> 1 C: trap card "UCP is law ✗ · applies when the LC says so ✓" [F1]

N009 · How many articles? Thirty-nine. The one before it, U-C-P five hundred, had forty-nine.
> 1 B: circle "39" on the card · 2 N: "UCP 500 (1993): 49" [F1]

N010 · You don't need all thirty-nine. Think of them as seven shelves.
> 1 S: a bookshelf, seven shelves, empty

N011 · One to five, the basics: what a credit is, and documents, not goods. Six to thirteen, how a credit works: banks' promises, amendments, reimbursement.
> 1 S: shelf 1 "1–5 basics" · shelf 2 "6–13 how it works" [F23]

N012 · Fourteen to sixteen, the checking. Seventeen to twenty-eight, the documents. Twenty-nine to thirty-three, dates and tolerances.
> 1 S: shelves 3–5 "14–16 checking" · "17–28 documents" · "29–33 dates, tolerances" [F23]

N013 · Thirty-four to thirty-seven, what banks are not liable for. And thirty-eight and thirty-nine, transfer and assignment.
> 1 S: shelves 6–7 "34–37 disclaimers" · "38–39 transfer, assignment" [F23]

N014 · Today we live on three shelves: checking, documents, dates. Plus a short visit to reimbursement, article thirteen.
> 1 G: shelves 3, 4, 5 glow; a small tag on shelf 2 "Art. 13"

## Chapter 2 · The checking clock

N018 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Which shelf holds the checking?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The checking clock" · H: 3 s

N019 · Fourteen to sixteen. Now picture the desk. The documents land on a checker's table: invoice, bill of lading, insurance.
> 1 S: the scrutiny desk; three documents; an empty five-slot clock above

N020 · Which banks check them? The nominated bank, the confirming bank, and the issuing bank. Each checks on its own.
> 1 N: "examined by: nominated · confirming · issuing" [F2]

N021 · They check the documents alone, on their face. Not the goods, not the factory, not phone calls with the buyer.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(a): on the documents alone · on their face" [F2]

N022 · How long does each bank get? Quick one. Five calendar days, or five banking days?
> 1 G: the clock · H: 3 s

N023 · Five banking days, following the day of presentation. That day itself doesn't count. Weekends and the bank's holidays don't count either.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(b): max 5 banking days following the day of presentation" [F3] · 2 B: underline "banking"

N024 · So, documents reach the bank on Thursday. Friday is day one. Monday two, Tuesday three, Wednesday four, Thursday five.
> 1 S: the clock fills Fri, Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, one slot per word

N025 · And if the L-C expires inside those five days? The bank still gets all five. Expiry doesn't shorten the clock.
> 1 N: "expiry inside the 5 days: no change" [F3]

N026 · U-C-P five hundred said a reasonable time, up to seven banking days. Six hundred made it a firm five.
> 1 C: card "UCP 500: reasonable time, ≤ 7 banking days → UCP 600: max 5" [F3]

N027 · Now, what is the checker looking for? Not identical words. Data must not conflict, within a document, between documents, or with the credit.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(d): need not be identical · must not conflict" [F5]

N028 · The goods description is strict only in the invoice. There, it must match the credit. Elsewhere, general terms are fine, if they don't conflict.
> 1 N: "invoice: corresponds with the LC · others: general terms" [F6]

N029 · Two quick rules. A condition with no document to prove it is ignored. And a document dated before the L-C is fine, if not after presentation.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(h): non-documentary condition ignored · Art. 14(i): may be dated before the LC" [F8, F9]

N030 · Who decides what "conflict" means in practice? I-S-B-P, International Standard Banking Practice. Many of you think it is a second rule book. It isn't.
> 1 C: trap card "ISBP is a rule ✗ · practice that supports UCP ✓" [F21]

N031 · It's the checkers' practice under U-C-P six hundred. The syllabus calls it I-S-B-P seven forty-five. The current edition is eight twenty-one, from twenty twenty-three.
> 1 S: timeline "645 (2002) → 681 (2007) → 745 (2013) → 821 (2023)" · 2 B: box "745 = syllabus · 821 = current" [F21]

N032 · Now three desk cards. The invoice: issued by the beneficiary, to the applicant, in the credit's currency. It needn't be signed.
> 1 S: desk card 1 "invoice: by beneficiary · to applicant · LC currency · no signature needed" [F12]

N033 · And if the invoice is above the credit amount? A bank may accept it, as long as it pays no more than the credit allows.
> 1 N: "Art. 18(b): may accept; pays ≤ LC amount" [F12]

N034 · The bill of lading. Signed by the carrier, shipped on board a named vessel, full set of originals. On-board date is the shipment date.
> 1 S: desk card 2 "B/L: carrier signs · on board · full set · on-board date = shipment date" [F13]

N035 · It must be clean: no note of damaged goods or packing. The word "clean" needn't appear. Goods carried on deck: not acceptable.
> 1 N: "clean: no defect clause · 'clean' word not needed · on deck ✗" [F14]

N036 · Insurance. Issued by an insurance company or underwriter, not a broker. No cover notes. Dated no later than shipment.
> 1 S: desk card 3 "insurance: insurer, not broker · no cover note · dated ≤ shipment" [F15]

N037 · And if the credit is silent on cover? At least a hundred and ten per cent of the C-I-F or C-I-P value. You met C-I-F in our Incoterms class.
> 1 B: circle "110%" · 2 N: "≥ 110% of CIF / CIP value" [F15]

N038 · Suppose the checker finds a problem. The bank may refuse. But it must send one notice, listing every discrepancy, by the fifth banking day.
> 1 C: card "Art. 16: one notice · every discrepancy · what happens to the documents · by day 5" [F11]

N039 · The issuing bank may ask its buyer to waive the discrepancy. That doesn't stop the clock. A bank that misses these steps can't refuse later.
> 1 N: "waiver request: clock keeps running · miss the steps → precluded" [F11]

N040 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Five calendar days, or banking?
> 1 S: card "5 ... days?" · H: 3 s

N041 · Banking. Two. Insurance from a broker: acceptable?
> 1 N: "banking ✓" · H: 3 s

N042 · No. Three, the twist. The refusal notice lists two discrepancies. Next day the bank finds a third. Can it add it?
> 1 N: "broker ✗" · H: 3 s

N043 · No. One notice, every discrepancy. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: five banking days, one complete notice.
> 1 N: "one notice ✓" · 2 S: the desk; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 3 · The date calendar

N044 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Documents reach the bank on Thursday. Which day is day one?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · The date calendar" · H: 3 s

N045 · Friday. Now three dates rule every presentation. The latest shipment date, the expiry date, and twenty-one days after shipment.
> 1 S: calendar strip, March 2027; three empty tags "latest shipment · expiry · shipment + 21"

N046 · The twenty-one days count when the credit asks for an original transport document, like a bill of lading. Calendar days, not banking days.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(c): original transport document → present ≤ 21 calendar days after shipment, and ≤ expiry" [F4]

N047 · Her bill of lading shows shipped on board Thursday, the fourth of March. Expiry is Sunday the twenty-eighth. What's her last day?
> 1 S: tag "on board: Thu 4 Mar" · tag "expiry: Sun 28 Mar" · H: 6 s

N048 · Thursday the twenty-fifth. Four plus twenty-one. The earlier date wins, so expiry on the twenty-eighth doesn't help her.
> 1 S: the strip fills 5 → 25; 25 circled "last day" · 2 B: cross out 28 [F4]

N049 · Many of you think only the expiry date matters. Students lose this mark every cycle. Two limits; the earlier one wins.
> 1 C: trap card "only expiry matters ✗ · earlier of expiry and shipment + 21 ✓"

N050 · Now change one thing. She ships on the eleventh. Eleven plus twenty-one is the first of April. Now expiry, the twenty-eighth, comes first.
> 1 S: tag moves "on board: Thu 11 Mar" · "shipment + 21: Thu 1 Apr" · 28 circled

N051 · But the twenty-eighth is a Sunday, and Monday the twenty-ninth is a bank holiday. The bank is simply closed.
> 1 S: 28 and 29 shaded "bank closed"

N052 · For a closure like that, the expiry moves to the first banking day after. Tuesday the thirtieth. That's Article twenty-nine.
> 1 C: card "Art. 29: closed on expiry / last presentation day → first following banking day" [F16] · 2 S: arrow 28 → 30

N053 · One thing never moves: the latest shipment date. If she ships late, no holiday saves her.
> 1 N: "latest shipment date: never extended" [F16]

N054 · Now the cyclone. The port city's banks shut from the twenty-sixth to the thirty-first. Expiry falls inside. Does she get extra days?
> 1 S: storm band over 26–31 "cyclone (illustrative)" · H: 3 s

N055 · No. That's force majeure: acts of God, riots, wars, strikes. A credit that expires during the interruption is not honoured afterwards.
> 1 C: card "Art. 36: force majeure → no extension · expired credit not honoured after reopening" [F17]

N056 · Her way out is an amendment. The buyer and the issuing bank agree a new expiry, and she accepts it.
> 1 N: "remedy: amend the expiry (applicant + issuing bank; beneficiary accepts)" [F17]

N057 · [lightly] So pray for holidays, not cyclones.
> 1 S: a small sun icon beside the holiday; the cloud fades

N058 · Pause check for chapter three. One. Twenty-one days: calendar or banking?
> 1 S: card "21 days?" · H: 3 s

N059 · Calendar. Two. Expiry on a bank holiday: what happens?
> 1 N: "calendar ✓" · H: 3 s

N060 · First banking day after. Three, the twist. Expiry during a strike that shuts the bank?
> 1 N: "Art. 29 ✓" · H: 3 s

N061 · No extension, Article thirty-six. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: closed for a holiday, it moves; closed by force majeure, it doesn't.
> 1 N: "Art. 36: no extension ✓" · 2 S: the calendar; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 4 · Getting the money back

N062 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Which date never moves?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · Getting the money back" · H: 3 s

N063 · The latest shipment date. Now, a London bank negotiated her documents and paid her. How does it get its dollars back?
> 1 S: the relay; a London bank in the middle lane; "paid ✓"

N064 · Often the issuing bank says: claim from my dollar account at a New York bank. You met Nostro accounts in our second class.
> 1 S: the A02 Nostro picture; a New York bank added, holding "issuing bank's USD account"

N065 · That's a bank-to-bank reimbursement. Its rule book is U-R-R seven twenty-five, again from the I-C-C.
> 1 C: card "URR 725 · ICC · bank-to-bank reimbursements · in force 1 Oct 2008" [F20]

N066 · Four words. The issuing bank. The claiming bank, here London. The reimbursing bank, here New York. And the reimbursement authorisation.
> 1 S: three labels appear "issuing · claiming · reimbursing" · 2 C: card "reimbursement authorisation" [F20]

N067 · The authorisation is the issuing bank's instruction to the reimbursing bank: pay the claiming bank. It stands apart from the L-C.
> 1 N: "authorisation: separate from the credit" [F20]

N068 · Many of you think New York checks the documents. It never sees them. It doesn't even get the credit.
> 1 C: trap card "reimbursing bank checks documents ✗ · acts on the authorisation only ✓" [F20]

N069 · And London needn't certify that the documents complied. The authorisation mustn't ask for it.
> 1 N: "no compliance certificate" [F19, F20]

N070 · Must New York pay? Only if it gave its own promise, a reimbursement undertaking. Otherwise, it pays on the authorisation, but isn't bound to.
> 1 N: "bound to pay only under its own reimbursement undertaking" [F20]

N071 · It has up to three banking days after receiving the claim to process it. And its charges go to the issuing bank, unless agreed otherwise.
> 1 C: card "URR: ≤ 3 banking days to process a claim · charges: issuing bank" [F20] · 2 B: circle "3"

N072 · And if New York doesn't pay? The issuing bank still owes London, plus interest and costs. Article thirteen of U-C-P.
> 1 N: "UCP 13: issuing bank stays liable" [F19]

N073 · Pause check for chapter four. One. Who checks the documents: the reimbursing bank?
> 1 S: card "reimbursing bank checks?" · H: 3 s

N074 · No. Two, the twist. Days to process a claim: three or five?
> 1 N: "no ✓" · H: 3 s

N075 · Three under U-R-R. Five is the checking clock under U-C-P. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter four: the reimbursing bank follows the authorisation, never the credit.
> 1 N: "URR 3 · UCP 5 ✓" · 2 S: the Nostro picture; finished parts dim to grey
