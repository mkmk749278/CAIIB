## Chapter 2 · The checking clock

N018 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Which shelf holds the checking?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The checking clock" · H: 3 s

N019 · Fourteen to sixteen. Handing the documents to a bank under the L-C is called presentation.
> 1 S: the scrutiny desk; three documents land on it; an empty five-slot clock above · 2 N: "presentation = handing in the documents"

N020 · Which banks check them? Think.
> 1 S: three empty labels over the desk · H: 3 s

N021 · The nominated bank, where she presents, acting on its nomination. Then the confirming bank, and the issuing bank.
> 1 N: "examined by: nominated (acting on its nomination) · confirming · issuing" [F2]

N022 · They check the documents alone, on their face. Never the goods.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(a): on the documents alone · on their face" [F2]

N023 · What are they checking against? A complying presentation. That means the L-C's terms, the U-C-P articles, and standard banking practice. 
> 1 C: card "complying presentation = LC terms + UCP articles + international standard banking practice (Art. 2)" [F2, F21] · 2 B: underline "practice"

N024 · Last class I asked: how many days does each bank get? Five calendar days, or five banking days?
> 1 G: the clock · H: 3 s

N025 · Five banking days, following the day of presentation. A banking day is a day the bank is normally open. Say hers shuts on weekends.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(b): max 5 banking days following the day of presentation" [F3] · 2 B: underline "banking" · 3 N: "banking day = bank regularly open at that place"

N026 · Documents arrive Thursday. Friday is day one. Monday two, Tuesday three, Wednesday four, Thursday five.
> 1 S: the clock fills Fri, Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu, one slot per word

N027 · And if the L-C expires inside those five days? The bank still gets all five. Expiry doesn't shorten the clock.
> 1 N: "expiry inside the 5 days: no change" [F3]

N028 · U-C-P five hundred said a reasonable time, up to seven banking days. Six hundred made it a firm five.
> 1 C: card "UCP 500: reasonable time, ≤ 7 banking days → UCP 600: max 5" [F3]

N029 · What does the checker look for? Not identical words. Data must not conflict, within a document, between documents, or with the credit.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(d): need not be identical · must not conflict" [F5]

N030 · Say the L-C says "goods must be new", but asks for no document to prove it. The checker ignores that line.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(h): a condition with no document named → ignored" [F8]

N031 · And a document may be dated before the L-C was issued. Never after it is presented.
> 1 N: "Art. 14(i): dated before LC issue ✓ · after presentation ✗" [F9]

N032 · A problem the checker finds is a discrepancy. Then the bank may refuse. But it sends one notice: every discrepancy, and what it does with the documents.
> 1 C: card "Art. 16: one notice · every discrepancy · documents held / returned / per instructions · by day 5" [F11] · 2 N: "discrepancy = documents don't comply"

N033 · The issuing bank may ask its buyer to waive, that is, accept the discrepancy anyway. That doesn't stop the clock.
> 1 N: "waive = accept it anyway · clock keeps running" [F11]

N034 · A bank that misses the notice, or the five days, loses the right to refuse. The rules say it is precluded.
> 1 N: "miss the notice → can't refuse later (precluded)" [F11]

N035 · Her bank finds the documents complying, pays her, and posts them to Hamburg. The courier loses them. Must Hamburg still pay?
> 1 S: a courier van; the envelope vanishes · H: 3 s

N036 · Yes. Found complying and sent on, they must be paid for, even if lost on the way.
> 1 C: card "Art. 35: complying + forwarded → issuing bank pays, even if lost in transit" [F22]

N037 · Pause check for chapter two. One. Five calendar days, or banking?
> 1 S: card "5 ... days?" · H: 3 s

N038 · Banking. Two, the twist. The refusal notice lists two discrepancies. Next day the bank finds a third. Can it add it?
> 1 N: "banking ✓" · H: 3 s

N039 · No. One notice, every discrepancy. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: five banking days, one complete notice.
> 1 N: "one notice ✓" · 2 S: the desk; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 3 · Three documents

N040 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. A complying presentation: L-C terms, U-C-P articles, and what third thing?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Three documents" · H: 3 s

N041 · Standard banking practice. Its book is I-S-B-P, International Standard Banking Practice. Many of you think it is a second rule book. It isn't.
> 1 C: trap card "ISBP is a rule ✗ · practice that supports UCP ✓" [F21]

N042 · It's how checkers apply U-C-P six hundred. The syllabus calls it I-S-B-P seven forty-five; that's the name you'll see in the exam. The current edition is eight twenty-one, from twenty twenty-three.
> 1 S: timeline "645 (2002) → 681 (2007) → 745 (2013) → 821 (2023)" · 2 B: box "745 = syllabus · 821 = current" [F21]

N043 · Now three desk cards. First, the invoice. Quick one. Must the exporter sign it?
> 1 S: desk card 1, blank · H: 3 s

N044 · No. Issued by the beneficiary, to the applicant, in the credit's currency. It needn't be signed. Its goods description must correspond with the credit's.
> 1 S: desk card 1 "invoice: by beneficiary · to applicant · LC currency · no signature needed · description corresponds" [F6, F12]

N045 · Elsewhere, general terms are fine. An invoice above the credit amount? A bank may accept it, but pays no more than the credit.
> 1 N: "others: general terms · Art. 18(b): may accept; pays ≤ LC amount" [F6, F12]

N046 · Second, the bill of lading. Signed by the carrier, the master, or an agent for either. Shipped on board a named vessel. And the full set of originals.
> 1 S: desk card 2 "B/L: carrier / master / named agent signs · on board · full set" [F13]

N047 · The on-board date is the shipment date. Now, the B-L says "two cartons torn". Can it still be clean?
> 1 N: "on-board date = shipment date" [F13] · 2 S: a stamp "2 cartons torn" · H: 3 s

N048 · No. Clean means no note of damaged goods or packing. The word "clean" needn't appear. Goods carried on deck, out in the open: not acceptable.
> 1 N: "clean: no defect clause · 'clean' word not needed · on deck ✗" [F14]

N049 · Third, insurance. It must come from an insurance company or an underwriter, a person who takes the risk, or their agents.
> 1 S: desk card 3 "insurance: insurer / underwriter or their agent" [F15]

N050 · So a broker signing only as broker fails. A broker signing as the insurer's agent is fine. And a cover note, a temporary promise of cover, is never accepted.
> 1 N: "broker as broker ✗ · as insurer's agent ✓ · cover note ✗" [F15]

N051 · Dated no later than shipment, unless it shows cover from the shipment date. Quick one. If the credit names no cover amount?
> 1 N: "dated ≤ shipment, or cover effective from shipment" [F15] · H: 3 s

N052 · At least a hundred and ten per cent of the C-I-F or C-I-P value.
> 1 B: circle "110%" · 2 N: "≥ 110% of CIF / CIP value" [F15]

N053 · Pause check for chapter three. One. Is I-S-B-P a rule?
> 1 S: card "ISBP: rule?" · H: 3 s

N054 · No, practice. Two, the twist. The insurance is dated a day after shipment, but says cover starts from shipment. Accept?
> 1 N: "practice ✓" · H: 3 s

N055 · Yes. Cover started in time. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: each document has a few checks; learn those, not the whole book.
> 1 N: "accept ✓" · 2 S: the three desk cards; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 4 · The date calendar

N056 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Insurance from a broker, signing only as broker?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · The date calendar" · H: 3 s

N057 · Not accepted. Now three dates rule every presentation. The latest shipment date, the expiry date, and twenty-one days after shipment.
> 1 S: calendar strip, March 2027; three empty tags "latest shipment · expiry · shipment + 21"

N058 · The twenty-one days count when the credit asks for an original transport document, like a bill of lading, and sets no period of its own. Calendar days, not banking days.
> 1 C: card "Art. 14(c): original transport document → present ≤ 21 calendar days after shipment (unless the LC sets its own period), and ≤ expiry" [F4]

N059 · Her bill of lading shows shipped on board Thursday, the fourth of March. Expiry is Sunday the twenty-eighth. What's her last day?
> 1 S: tag "on board: Thu 4 Mar" · tag "expiry: Sun 28 Mar" · H: 6 s

N060 · Thursday the twenty-fifth. Four plus twenty-one, before the expiry.
> 1 S: the strip fills 5 → 25; 25 circled "last day" · 2 B: cross out 28 [F4]

N061 · Many of you think only the expiry date matters. It doesn't. Two limits; the earlier one wins. Documents presented too late are called stale.
> 1 C: trap card "only expiry matters ✗ · earlier of expiry and shipment + 21 ✓ · late = stale"

N062 · She ships on the eleventh. Eleven plus twenty-one is thirty-two. March has thirty-one days, so that's the first of April.
> 1 S: tag moves "on board: Thu 11 Mar" · "shipment + 21: Thu 1 Apr"

N063 · Now expiry, the twenty-eighth, comes first. But it's a Sunday, and Monday the twenty-ninth is a bank holiday. Her bank is simply closed.
> 1 S: 28 circled · 2 S: 28 and 29 shaded "bank closed"

N064 · For a closure like that, the expiry moves to the first banking day after: Tuesday the thirtieth. That's Article twenty-nine.
> 1 S: arrow 28 → 30 on "Tuesday the thirtieth" · 2 C: card "Art. 29: bank where documents are presented closed on expiry / last presentation day → first following banking day" [F16]

N065 · So, our opening question. For a holiday, yes, she gets more time. But one date never moves: the latest shipment date.
> 1 N: "holiday → Art. 29 ✓ · latest shipment date: never extended" [F16]

N066 · A different story, on its own calendar. Her L-C expires on Wednesday the seventeenth. A cyclone shuts her bank from Tuesday the sixteenth to Friday the nineteenth.
> 1 S: a new strip, 15–22 March; expiry Wed 17 boxed; storm band Tue 16 – Fri 19 "cyclone (illustrative)"

N067 · The bank reopens on Monday the twenty-second. Can she present then?
> 1 S: Mon 22 "reopens" · H: 6 s

N068 · No. That's force majeure: events nobody can control, like storms, riots, wars or strikes. A credit that expires during the closure is not honoured afterwards.
> 1 C: card "Art. 36: force majeure → no extension · expired credit not honoured after reopening" [F17]

N069 · Her way out: an amendment of the expiry, agreed by the banks, and accepted by her.
> 1 N: "remedy: amend the expiry (issuing bank · confirming bank, if any · beneficiary accepts)" [F17]

N070 · [lightly] So pray for holidays, not cyclones.
> 1 S: a small sun icon beside the holiday; the cloud fades

N071 · One more shelf-five rule. "About" or "approximately" in an L-C allows ten per cent either way, on whatever it refers to.
> 1 C: card "Art. 30(a): 'about' = ±10% of the amount, quantity or unit price it refers to" [F18]

N072 · Without it, a quantity may still vary five per cent, unless counted in units. Partial shipments are allowed, unless the L-C says no.
> 1 N: "Art. 30(b): quantity ±5% (not for units / items; amount not exceeded) · Art. 31: partial shipments ✓ unless prohibited" [F18]

N073 · Pause check for chapter four. One. Twenty-one days: calendar or banking?
> 1 S: card "21 days?" · H: 3 s

N074 · Calendar. Two, the twist. Expiry on Sunday, Monday a holiday, so it moves to Tuesday. But on Tuesday a strike shuts the bank. More days?
> 1 N: "calendar ✓" · 2 S: strip Sun · Mon · Tue; Tue gets a strike band · H: 6 s

N075 · No. Tuesday's closure is force majeure, Article thirty-six. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter four: closed for a holiday, it moves; closed by force majeure, it doesn't.
> 1 N: "Art. 36: no extension ✓" · 2 S: the calendar; finished parts dim to grey

## Chapter 5 · Getting the money back

N076 · Chapter five. First, from chapter four. Which date never moves?
> 1 S: chapter title "5 · Getting the money back" · H: 3 s

N077 · The latest shipment date. Now, her Indian bank paid her under its confirmation. How does it get its dollars back from Hamburg?
> 1 S: the relay; her Indian bank "paid ✓"; an arrow "dollars back?" toward Hamburg · H: 3 s

N078 · Hamburg says: claim from my dollar account in New York. Its own Nostro, as in our correspondent banking class.
> 1 S: the A02 Nostro picture; a New York bank added, holding "Hamburg bank's USD account"

N079 · That's a bank-to-bank reimbursement. Its rule book is U-R-R seven twenty-five, from the I-C-C. It applies when the authorisation says so.
> 1 C: card "URR 725 · ICC · in force 1 Oct 2008 · applies when the authorisation says so" [F19, F20]

N080 · Four words. The issuing bank, Hamburg. The claiming bank, her Indian bank. The reimbursing bank, New York. And the reimbursement authorisation.
> 1 S: three labels appear "issuing · claiming · reimbursing" · 2 C: card "reimbursement authorisation" [F20]

N081 · The authorisation is Hamburg's instruction to New York: pay the claiming bank. It stands apart from the L-C.
> 1 N: "authorisation: separate from the credit" [F20]

N082 · Quick one. Does New York check the documents?
> 1 G: the New York bank · H: 3 s

N083 · Many of you would say yes. It never gets them, and it ignores the credit, even if sent a copy. And no one certifies to it that they complied.
> 1 C: trap card "reimbursing bank checks documents ✗ · acts on the authorisation only ✓ · no compliance certificate" [F19, F20]

N084 · Must New York pay? Only if it gave its own promise, a reimbursement undertaking.
> 1 N: "bound to pay only under its own reimbursement undertaking" [F20]

N085 · It has up to three banking days after receiving the claim to process it. Its charges go to the issuing bank, unless agreed otherwise.
> 1 C: card "URR: ≤ 3 banking days to process a claim (+ any pre-debit notice period) · charges: issuing bank" [F20] · 2 B: circle "3"

N086 · And if New York doesn't pay? Hamburg still owes her bank. That's Article thirteen of U-C-P.
> 1 N: "UCP 13: issuing bank stays liable" [F19]

N087 · Pause check for chapter five. One. Who checks the documents: the reimbursing bank?
> 1 S: card "reimbursing bank checks?" · H: 3 s

N088 · No. Two, the twist. Days to process a claim: three or five?
> 1 N: "no ✓" · H: 3 s

N089 · Three under U-R-R. Five is the checking clock under U-C-P. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter five: the reimbursing bank follows the authorisation, never the credit.
> 1 N: "URR 3 · UCP 5 ✓" · 2 S: the Nostro picture; finished parts dim to grey
