High 3 · Medium 9 · Low 12. A good lesson for a beginner: a clear "rule book the L-C chooses" opener, a five-slot
clock I can see, a calendar that fills day by day, the "earlier date wins" trap, and a clean holiday-versus-force-
majeure contrast in the "one thing" line. Every chapter after the first opens with recall, every chapter has a pause
check with a twist and a "one thing" line, one light line in the whole lesson, no sentence over 25 words. What stops

eighteen rules in four minutes with one real question, and the three document cards are pure lists (H2); and in
chapter 4 a London bank appears from nowhere in a story where her bank is Indian (H3). Several words are used without
their meaning: discrepancy, waive, cover note, underwriter, force majeure, banking day.

## Three things I'd remember
1. Five **banking** days to check, one complete refusal notice.
2. Twenty-one **calendar** days after shipment, but never after expiry: the earlier date wins.
3. Closed for a holiday, the expiry moves (Art. 29); closed by force majeure, it doesn't (Art. 36).
