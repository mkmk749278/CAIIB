# A19b · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Eight original exam-style questions (6 MCQs + 2 keyed-in numericals) written strictly from A19b_fact_sheet. All names and figures are illustrative. Answers and one-line reasons citing [F#] appear at the end.
1. A Singapore-based fund invests ₹4 crore for a 3% equity stake in an unlisted Indian manufacturing company on a repatriation basis. Under the FEM (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, this investment is classified as:
(a) Foreign Portfolio Investment because it is below 10%
(b) Foreign Direct Investment regardless of the percentage held
(c) External Commercial Borrowing because the company is unlisted
(d) Deemed domestic investment because Singapore is not a land-border country
2. An FPI acquires shares in a listed Indian company that take its holding (with its investor group) to 10.4% on a fully diluted basis, without prior concurrence of the investee company. Under RBI's November 2024 operational framework, the FPI must:
(a) Reclassify the entire stake as FDI within 7 trading days
(b) Hold the excess shares for a minimum 1-year lock-in period
(c) Compulsorily divest the excess holding within 5 trading days from settlement
(d) Seek post-facto compounding from RBI Regional Office within 30 days
3. Under the June 2026 liberalisation of Schedule III of the FEM (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, which of the following statements is correct for listed equity purchases on a repatriation basis?
(a) Only NRIs and OCIs may invest, capped at 5% individual and 10% aggregate
(b) Any individual person resident outside India may invest, capped at below 10% individual and 24% aggregate
(c) Any individual or foreign corporate may invest, capped at 24% individual and 49% aggregate
(d) Only citizens of FATF member countries may invest, capped at 10% individual and 49% aggregate
4. Which of the following investors is NOT permitted to contribute to the capital of an Indian Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) operating in a 100% automatic sector with no FDI-linked performance conditions?
(a) A foreign company incorporated in Japan
(b) An NRI investing on a repatriation basis
(c) A SEBI-registered Foreign Portfolio Investor
(d) An OCI investing on a non-repatriation basis
5. Under DPIIT Notification G.S.R. 108(E) dated 4 February 2026 (superseding G.S.R. 127(E)), what is the maximum annual turnover ceiling for a regular Startup and a Deep Tech Startup, respectively?
(a) ₹100 crore and ₹200 crore
(b) ₹200 crore and ₹300 crore
(c) ₹100 crore and ₹250 crore
(d) ₹250 crore and ₹500 crore
6. Which of the following transactions is permitted under India's foreign exchange framework as at 31 December 2026?
(a) Foreign direct investment in a Nidhi company under the Government route
(b) A foreign trademark licensing agreement with an Indian online lottery operator
(c) Subscription by an OCI, on a non-repatriation basis, to a chit fund authorised by the State Registrar of Chits
(d) Issue of optionally convertible debentures to a foreign investor under the NDI Rules
7. (Keyed-in numerical) An Indian company issues 2,00,000 partly paid equity shares to a non-resident investor at ₹120 per share (face value ₹10 plus premium ₹110). What is the minimum upfront amount (in ₹ lakh) that must be received at the time of issue?
8. (Keyed-in numerical) A listed Indian company has 120 lakh equity shares, 20 lakh CCDs (converting 1:1), and 10 lakh share warrants (converting 1:1). An FPI investor group holds 14.5 lakh equity shares. Calculate the group's holding percentage on a fully diluted basis (rounded to 2 decimal places) and state whether it is FPI or FDI.
Answers
   1. (b) — Any repatriable equity investment by a non-resident in an unlisted Indian company is FDI regardless of percentage [F5].
   2. (c) — Without prior Government approval (where applicable) and investee company concurrence, an FPI breaching 10% must compulsorily divest the excess within 5 trading days from settlement [F10, F11].
   3. (b) — Third Amendment Rules 2026 (12 Jun 2026) and RBI Circular 14 (15 Jun 2026) opened Schedule III to all individual PROIs with <10% individual and 24% aggregate limits [F24].
   4. (c) — Schedule VI bars FPIs, FVCIs, and Pakistan/Bangladesh citizens or entities from investing in LLPs [F29].
   5. (b) — G.S.R. 108(E) (4 Feb 2026) doubled the regular Startup turnover ceiling from ₹100 crore to ₹200 crore (10 years) and set ₹300 crore (20 years) for Deep Tech Startups [F31].
   6. (c) — Although chit funds are prohibited for FDI, FEMA 396/2019-RB Schedule 1 para 1.C(3) allows NRIs/OCIs to subscribe without limit on a non-repatriation basis to State-authorised chit funds [F46].
   7. ₹60 lakh (₹60,00,000) — Rule 2(k) Explanation (ii) requires at least 25% of total consideration including share premium upfront: 2,00,000 × 120 × 25% = ₹60,00,000 [F35].
   8. 9.67% — FPI — Fully diluted shares = 120 + 20 + 10 = 150 lakh; 14.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = 9.67%, which is below 10% [F6, F7, F9].
