# A19b · Numericals (c1, 2026-11-24)
All names, amounts, share counts, and dates are illustrative. Every calculation uses only the IIBF basic calculator (+ − × ÷ % √, 12 digits, no scientific keys), and is solved two different ways to verify both match. Rates/percentages to 2–4 decimals; rupees to 2 decimals.
Rules applied: Listed Indian company ≥ 10% on a fully diluted basis = FDI; < 10% = FPI [F6]. Fully diluted basis = all convertible instruments exercised [F7]. Partly paid shares: ≥ 25% upfront (incl. premium), balance in 12 months [F35]. Share warrants: ≥ 25% upfront, balance in 18 months [F36]. Convertible notes (startups): minimum ₹25 lakh in a single tranche [F39].
Q1 · Worked in full: Listed company with CCDs (N025–N027)
Data: Apex Auto Ltd (listed, illustrative) has 80,00,000 (80 lakh) equity shares and 20,00,000 (20 lakh) CCDs converting 1:1. An FPI holds 9,00,000 (9 lakh) equity shares. FPI or FDI?
* Method 1 (percentage on fully diluted denominator): Fully diluted shares = 80 + 20 = 100 lakh. FPI % = 9 ÷ 100 × 100 = 9.00%. Since 9.00% < 10%, remains FPI [F6, F7].
* Method 2 (10% threshold share count): 10% of 100 lakh = 100 × 10 % = 10 lakh shares. FPI holds 9 lakh shares (9 < 10, 1 lakh below threshold) → FPI. Both methods match.
* Commonest wrong answer (trap): 9 ÷ 80 × 100 = 11.25% (wrong — ignores the 20 lakh CCDs).
Q2 · Faded check: Listed company with share warrants (N028–N029)
Data: 80 lakh equity shares + 10 lakh share warrants converting 1:1. FPI holds 9 lakh shares.
* Method 1: 80 + 10 = 90 lakh. 9 ÷ 90 × 100 = 10.00%. Since 10.00% ≥ 10%, crosses into FDI [F6, F7].
* Method 2: 90 × 10 % = 9 lakh shares. FPI holds 9 lakh shares (hits 10% mark) → FDI. Both methods match.
V · Your turn: Listed company with CCPS (N030–N031)
Data: Listed company has 60 lakh equity shares + 15 lakh CCPS converting 1:1. FPI buys 8 lakh equity shares.
* Method 1: 60 + 15 = 75 lakh. 8 ÷ 75 × 100 = 10.6667% (10.67%) ≥ 10% → FDI (must divest or reclassify within 5 trading days) [F6, F7, F10].
* Method 2: 75 × 10 % = 7.50 lakh shares. FPI holds 8 lakh (8 − 7.5 = 0.50 lakh excess) → breaches 10% → FDI. Both methods match.
Homework (N115)

* Method 1: 50 + 10 = 60 lakh. 5.5 ÷ 60 × 100 = 9.1667% (9.17%) < 10% → FPI [F6, F7].

Practice Sum 1 · Partly paid equity shares upfront payment (Practice Q7)
Data: 2,00,000 partly paid equity shares issued to a foreign investor at ₹120 per share (face value ₹10 + premium ₹110). Minimum upfront amount?
* Method 1 (total consideration first): 200000 × 120 = ₹2,40,00,000. 24000000 × 25 % = ₹60,00,000 (₹60 lakh) [F35].
* Method 2 (per-share upfront first): 120 × 25 % = ₹30/share. 200000 × 30 = ₹60,00,000 (₹60 lakh). Both methods match.
* Trap: Taking 25% of face value only (₹5 lakh) is wrong because Rule 2(k) Expl. (ii) includes share premium [F35].
Practice Sum 2 · Fully diluted basis with multiple instruments (Practice Q8)
Data: Listed company has 120 lakh equity shares, 20 lakh CCDs (1:1), and 10 lakh share warrants (1:1). FPI investor group holds 14.5 lakh equity shares.
* Method 1: 120 + 20 + 10 = 150 lakh. 14.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = 9.6667% → 9.67% → remains FPI (< 10%) [F6, F7, F9].
* Method 2: 150 × 10 % = 15.00 lakh. Shortfall = 15 − 14.5 = 0.50 lakh; 0.5 ÷ 150 × 100 = 0.3333%; 10 − 0.3333 = 9.67%. Both methods match.
