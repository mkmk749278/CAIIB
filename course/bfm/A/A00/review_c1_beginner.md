# A00 class edition (script_class.md, c1): beginner + teaching-method review

- **Reviewer**: independent session, fresh context (2026-11-21). Reading as a bank officer new to forex, evening, on a
  phone, English as a second language; then the 30-item checklist in `research/pedagogy/teaching_method.md` §7.
- **Compared with**: `script.md` v3 (locked).
- **Scope**: teaching and clarity only. No new facts proposed; where a fix would need a fact the sheet does not
  carry, it says so.

## Verdict: ADOPT WITH FIXES

This now feels like a class. v3 explained well; c1 makes me answer. I counted 27 places where I have to say
something before the teacher does. Every chapter has a hook, a "remember my question?", a pause check and "the one
thing". The opening question is answered in so many words in N85u. The blank map (N87a–f) and the mixed round
(N86a–f) are the two biggest gains over v3. I would come out of the class able to answer most exam questions on
A1.1–A1.3.

Four things still lose a beginner or break the method:
1. **N99a–b**: I am asked "forward premium or forward discount?" without ever being told what a premium or a
   discount is. "Think first" is asking me to guess.
2. **Two names for one idea**: "settlement date" (N02, N03, N58) and "value date" (N59 on).
3. **Three holds under 3 s** on lines that ask me to commit (N36, N57, N66).
4. **Some rules are taught but never retrieved**: the merchant forward holiday rule (N75–76), devaluation vs
   depreciation (N85d), and the six players. Later chapters recall only the chapter just before them.

All the fixes are wording or cue changes, and none adds a fact. Sentence length passes: no sentence is over 23
words (longest: N54, N85v). Estimated length is about 23 minutes with holds. Chapters run 3.0–4.2 minutes.

## Where I, as a beginner, got lost or bored (line by line)

- **N05** "Deal?" In a forex class, "deal" is the word for a trade (N56, N58, N63…). For an L2 listener the first
  "deal" sounds like the topic. Small, but easy to fix.
- **N16** "replacing FERA". I don't know what FERA is.
- **N20** Pocket two, "drawn in rupees, but payable in a foreign currency". This is the hardest line in chapter one,
  and it has no example on the board. The example comes only in the check (N32). I followed pocket three better
  than pocket two.
- **N28** I'm asked "strong rupee or weak?" before "strong" has been defined. N29 rescues it. But the same idea comes
  back as chapter four's "one thing" (N99f) in different words, so I don't connect the two.
- **N36** 2 s is not enough time to decide.
- **N41–N43** Six players and five motives, read out as lists, and I'm never asked to recall them. Here I drifted.
  The map answer (N87c) gives only the counts ("six players, five motives"), so I can't check myself.
- **N58–N59** Is a "settlement date" the same as a "value date"? It's never said.
- **N75–N76** A rule "only for a customer's forward contract". I haven't been taught what a customer's forward
  contract is, and the rule is never asked again. I'd forget it by chapter five.
- **N99a** "Forward premium, or forward discount? Think first." I can't think. Nobody told me what either word means.
  So I guess, and the answer (N99b) becomes a fact to memorise with nothing to hang it on. Then the mixed round
  (N86e) calls it "the tricky one", and I agree.
- **N85d** Devaluation vs depreciation is a classic MCQ pair, and it is said once, fast, inside a three-term line.
  It is never asked.
- **N85l** "A short list is banned or capped by the rules": I'm not sure what this means for me at the counter.
- **N86f** "Less, rewatch that chapter tonight": which chapter? I just answered questions from five chapters.
- **Ids**: ch4 runs N82–N99f, then ch5 starts at N85a. N85 vs N85a, N86 vs N86a and so on are confusing for anyone
  matching takes to lines.

## Findings

**F1. High · N99a–N99b · forward premium/discount asked before it is defined.**
The learner cannot reason to the answer, so the retrieval turns into a coin toss and the trap lands as a fact with
nothing behind it. "Interest rate parity" and "premium/discount" are also not written on the board when first spoken
(checklist 18).
Fix, option A (preferred): give premium and discount a one-line meaning. **This needs a fact-sheet row first**
(row 20 has the pairing but not the definitions). Have the expert add it with its source, then use:
> N99a · One more idea, and a second trap. Interest rate parity. It's about the price for a future date, the
> forward price. If the forward price is higher than today's, the currency is at a premium. Lower, a discount. Now
> guess. The currency with the higher interest rate: premium or discount?
> cue: 1 S: timeline "today | future date"; "interest rate parity" writes on its words · 2 S: "forward above today =
> premium · below = discount" · 3 C: pause card · H: 4 s

Option B (no new fact): keep the definitions for A05. Be honest that this is a pairing to learn now, and give it a
board hook:
> N99a · One more idea, and a second trap. It's called interest rate parity, and it's about the price for a future
> date. We'll do the sums in A-zero-five. Today, just one pairing. Have a guess. The currency with the higher
> interest rate: forward premium, or forward discount?
> N99b · Discount. Say it with me. Higher interest… forward discount. So if the rupee is at a discount, the dollar
> is at a premium.
> cue N99b: 1 C: rule card "higher interest → forward DISCOUNT" · 2 B: circle "discount" on the word
Change N86e "the tricky one" to "the trap one" so it doesn't signal that it is unlearnable.

**F2. Medium · N02, N03, N58, N59 · two names for one concept.**
Pick **value date**, because FEDAI uses it and the map, check and close already use it. Say "settlement date" once,
as the plain meaning.
> N02 · …you'll explain how the rupee's rate is set, and you'll name the value date of any deal.
> N03 · …Settlement dates came up in June twenty twenty-three too. → "Value dates came up in June twenty twenty-three
> too."
> N58 · Four words first. Cash. Tom. Spot. Forward. Each one is a value date, counted from the deal day.
> N59 · Value date means the day the money actually changes hands. The settlement day. Say it once. Value date.
Exam chip N03: "Value dates · Jun 2023".

**F3. Medium · N36, N57, N66 · commit holds under 3 s.**
The method's anti-pattern list counts any pause prompt under 3 s. Set **N36 H: 3 s, N57 H: 3 s, N66 H: 3 s**. In
N66 also replace "Decide." with "Decide now, yes or no."

**F4. Medium · N26 (and N31, N33, N86b, N87b) · the three pockets have no short names to chant.**
"Say it with me" fits exactly three short items, and this list is the core of chapter one. Give the pockets short
labels once, then use only those words in every check.
> N26 · Three pockets. Say them with me. One: foreign-currency balances. Two: rupee paper, foreign pay. Three:
> drawn abroad, rupee pay. … Once more, faster. Balances. Rupee paper, foreign pay. Drawn abroad, rupee pay.
> cue: 1 G: pocket 1, 2, 3 in turn; each pocket's short label writes on its words and stays · H: 1 s
Then N31 "Yes, pocket one. Foreign-currency balances."; N33 "Pocket two. Rupee paper, foreign pay. Pocket three
would need it drawn abroad and paid in rupees."; N86b "Yes. Pocket three. Drawn abroad, rupee pay."

**F5. Medium · N82, N85a · recall is only one chapter back.**
The template asks chapter 3 to recall 1 and 2. Add one question that reaches further back:
> N82 · Chapter four. Why rates move. Two quick ones first. From chapter three: spot settles on which day? [H 3 s]
> (N83 answers.) And from chapter one: a draft drawn in Dubai, payable in rupees. Foreign exchange? [H 3 s] Yes.
> Pocket three.
(If that steals mixed-round Q1, switch that question to London or another city.)
> N85a · Chapter five. The big question. First, two from earlier. Higher inflation in India: rupee stronger or
> weaker? [H 3 s] Weaker. And from chapter two: a company fixes today the rate for dollars it pays in three months.
> Hedging or speculation? [H 3 s] Hedging. Protecting, not betting.
Split N85a/N85b so that each answer sits right after its hold.

**F6. Medium · N76 · forward-contract holiday rule never retrieved.**
Add a pause line straight after N76, using only N75–N76's facts:
> N76a · Try it. A customer's forward contract falls due on a holiday announced a month ago. Deliver before or
> after? [H 3 s]
> N76b · Before. Known early, go back. If it had been declared suddenly, the next working day.
> cue: 1 G: the "known early → back" arrow · 2 B: circle "back"
Also add "merchant forward on a holiday" to the map answer N87d: "…Spot on the second working day. Holiday on a
customer's forward: known early, go back."

**F7. Medium · N85d / after N85h · devaluation vs depreciation taught once, never asked.**
This is a standard MCQ pair. After N85h ("devalued in two steps"):
> N85h-a · Quick one. In July ninety-one the cut was official. Devaluation, or depreciation? [H 3 s]
> N85h-b · Devaluation. Official cut. Depreciation is the market's fall.
> cue: 1 S: compare card from N85d returns small · 2 B: circle "official" on the word
And slow N85d down by splitting it: "Two words to keep apart. An official cut in a fixed rate is a devaluation. A
market fall in a floating rate is a depreciation." Then "And a market rise is an appreciation." as its own line.

**F8. Medium · end of ch4 (N99d) and ch5 (after N85u) · end checks too thin.**
Ch4 has one question; ch5 has no closing check after the FBIL lines.
> After N99e add · Second. Higher interest rates in India, more money flows in. Today, does the rupee strengthen
> or weaken? [H 3 s] Strengthen. That's today's see-saw. The forward discount is a different question.
> After N85u add · One more. The F-B-I-L reference rate. A rate R-B-I forces on your deal, or a benchmark? [H 3 s]
> A benchmark. Published by F-B-I-L at about one-thirty.

**F11. Medium · N85u · let the learner be the officer ("I fully got it").**
The best way to end the lesson's big question is for the learner to say the answer to the customer from N08–N09.
Add after N85u:
> N85u-a · Now you be the officer. Your customer asks, "Who changed it? R-B-I?" Answer him out loud, in one line.
> [H 5 s]
> N85u-b · Something like this: "Sir, the market moved overnight. R-B-I only stops wild swings. Our board is the
> interbank rate plus our margin."
> cue: 1 S: the customer from N08 returns with his bubble · 2 S: the officer's bubble fills on the model answer
("Sir" here is the officer speaking to a customer, not the teacher persona. If that reads badly, use "Sorry, the
market moved overnight…".)

statute's test is where it is drawn and what it pays in. Change N32 to "A traveller's cheque in rupee terms, but
