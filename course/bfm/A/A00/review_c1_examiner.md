## 2. Findings

### High

**1. N56–N57, N70–N71 · the Friday exporter story gives the wrong settlement for a counter deal.** The story says "An
exporter sells you dollars… When do the rupees reach my account?" and answers "Tuesday… his salaries need another
plan". A customer selling dollars at the branch is a merchant deal. Unless he books it value spot, it is a ready deal
(FEDAI 5.2: ready = same day). The bank credits him at TT buying the same day, and A01 v6 teaches exactly that (N43,
N46: "the bank has already paid this exporter in rupees"). As written, the lesson plants the belief that counter
customers wait two working days, and the very next lesson contradicts it. The value-date rule is right; the story
around it is wrong.
**Fix.** Make it an explicit spot booking and give the right way out:
- N56 → "Now a branch story. Friday afternoon. An exporter books a spot deal with you to sell dollars. He asks, "When do the rupees reach my account? I pay salaries on Monday.""
- N71 → "Tuesday. Spot is the second working day. If he needs the rupees today, he asks for a cash deal, value today, instead of spot."
- Cue N56: bubble adds a tag "spot deal". Cue N71: small note "need it today → cash deal".

### Medium

**2. Mixed round N86a–N86f · badly graded and missing A1.2 and A1.3 depth.** Q2 (Wednesday spot, no holiday) and Q4
(bank buys from bank → interbank) are about 95% easy, and Q1 repeats the chapter 1 London draft almost word for
word, so it tests recognition, not transfer. The round has no question on factor groups and none on regimes or India's
path except FBIL, although chapter 5 is the biggest chapter. The exam mixes in exactly these (Q5, Q10, Q12 above).
**Fix (same five slots, same holds):**
- N86a Q1 → "One. A rupee draft issued by a bank in Chennai, payable in Mumbai. Foreign exchange?" N86b answer → "No. It fits none of the three pockets. Two. A deal on Thursday, spot, and Friday is a holiday. Which day?"
- N86c answer → "Tuesday. Monday is day one, Tuesday is day two. Three. Who publishes the rupee's reference rate?" (keep)
- N86d Q4 → "F-B-I-L. Four. Government limits on capital flows. Fundamental, technical, or speculative factor?"
- N86e answer → "Technical. Five, the tricky one…" (keep Q5).
- Optionally replace Q3 with "Since when is the rupee's rate market determined?" (March 1993) if FBIL is judged too easy. The reference-rate fact is already checked in the map recall (N87f).

**3. N17 · FEMA's wide "currency" definition is missing (fact row 1).** The fact sheet's first testable point (s. 2(h):
currency includes cheques, drafts, TCs, LCs, bills, promissory notes, credit cards and RBI-notified instruments such as
debit and ATM cards; only s. 2(i) "currency notes" means notes and coins) is not taught. "Which of these is (not)
currency under FEMA" is a natural definition question (Q2).
**Fix: add one line after N17.**
- N17a · "And careful: under FEMA, currency isn't only notes. It includes cheques, drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit, bills, promissory notes, even credit and debit cards. Only "currency notes" means notes and coins."
- Cue: 1 S: card line "s. 2(h) currency = notes + cheques · drafts · TCs · LCs · bills · PNs · cards" [F1] · 2 B: box around "currency notes = notes and coins only".

**4. N94 · four fundamental factors are listed with no direction.** "Then growth, the government's finances, foreign
investment and political stability" names them but never says which way each pushes. Case questions ask exactly that
("a widening fiscal deficit and political uncertainty: rupee?"; depth A1.2 items 1 and 5). Only foreign investment
gets a direction, later, in N99e.
**Fix:** N94 → "Higher interest rates pull money in, and strengthen the rupee. So do strong growth, sound government finances, foreign investment coming in, and political stability. Their opposites weaken it." Cue 2 S: "growth · sound fiscal · FDI/FPI in · stability → ₹ stronger; opposites → weaker".

**5. N97–N99f · no "spot the irrelevant factor" case (depth A1.2 item 5; blueprint "spot the irrelevant one").** The
fact sheet blueprint and the depth checklist both name this form. The lesson has none, and N99d's three options are
all relevant.
**Fix:** extend the chapter 4 check after N99e with one line:
- N99e2 · "One more, the exam's favourite twist. Which of these does NOT move the rupee-dollar rate? India's inflation. U-S interest rates. Japan's rainfall." · H 4 s · N99e3 · "Japan's rainfall. India's inflation and U-S rates both move it, because the rate compares the two countries."
- If time is short, drop N99 (PPP name) into the N98 cue as a note instead.

**6. N85a, N99d · inflation is said without "than abroad".** N97–N98 teach that relative inflation matters ("higher
than in the U-S"), but the recall opener N85a ("Higher inflation in India makes the rupee stronger, or weaker?") and
N99d's option ("Higher inflation in India") drop the comparison. Examiner review v2 flagged the same wording in script
v3 and it was fixed there; the class edition brought it back. Exam options often hinge on "relative to trading
partners".
**Fix:** N85a → "…Higher inflation in India than in the U-S makes the rupee stronger, or weaker?" N99d → "…Higher inflation in India than in the U-S. …" Card text to match.

### Low

**7. N72, N79 · spot counts working days in both currencies' centres.** The lesson counts Indian holidays only. A
USD/INR spot date also skips a New York holiday. Exam risk is small, but a learner may later meet "US holiday"
questions in A05. **Fix (only after checking FEDAI 5.2 / interbank conventions, and adding a fact-sheet row):** add to
N72 "…and a holiday in either country counts." Otherwise leave as is.

**8. Fact row 13, interbank holiday rule (FEDAI 8.09) not taught.** It is on the fact sheet but rarely asked. **Fix:**
none needed in the video; put it in `practice.md` notes or the A05 lesson. Record the choice in the ack.

**9. Fact rows 33–34 · FBIL detail.** Cross rates for EUR, GBP and JPY (JPY per 100) and the wider currency list from

**10. N03 · exam chips.** The recalled Dec 2025 Q71 ("rates quoted are for spot unless otherwise mentioned") is the
strongest evidence for N68 but is not shown. **Fix:** add chip "Spot as base quote · Dec 2025" to the N03 cue (no
spoken change needed).

**11. N51 · pause check too easy.** "Is there a forex exchange building in Mumbai where all deals happen?" was already
answered 15 lines earlier with a red cross. It is near 100% success, and arbitrage (N47–N48) is never checked.
**Fix:** N51 → "First. Rupee to dollar to euro and back to rupee, for a profit. Simple or compound arbitrage?" N52
answer → "Compound. Three currencies. Second. Your branch sells dollars to a student…".

**12. Line ids out of sequence.** Chapter 5 uses N85a–N85v after N99f, and N85, N86, N87, N88 also exist as separate
lines earlier. Ids are unique (no duplicates found), but anything that sorts by id would put chapter 5 inside chapter
4. **Fix:** renumber chapter 5 as N100–N121 and the later blocks N122 on, or confirm `timeline.py` keeps file order.

**13. Length (outside the examiner's remit, noted for the owner).** Spoken lines are about 2,920 words. With holds that
