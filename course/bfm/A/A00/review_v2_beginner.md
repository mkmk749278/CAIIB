## Findings

**1. Medium · N22–N24a · The end of chapter 2 is now the densest stretch of the lesson.**
In about 90 seconds the learner gets: value date, what FEDAI is, four deal names, spot as the default quote,
"forward periods are counted from the spot date", the skip-holidays rule with a case, and the forward-delivery
holiday rule with "three working days". On a phone after work, I would remember the four boxes and "spot is not
today", and lose N23a and N24a. Two small changes help without cutting a fact:
- N23a, plain the jargon "forward periods":
  > N23a · Spot is the market's base rate. A dealer's quote is a spot rate unless it says otherwise. And a one-month
  > forward is counted from the spot date, not from today.
- N24a, add a check-in before the new rule and a hook after it (the rule said a second way, PLAYBOOK §5b):
  > N24a · Still with me? One more date rule, only for a customer's forward contract. If its delivery date turns out
  > to be a holiday known at least three working days ahead, deliver on the working day before.
  > N24b · If the holiday is sudden, deliver on the next working day. Known early, go back. Sudden, go forward.
  > That's chapter two. Deep breath.

  Cue: the hook "known early ← · sudden →" as a small card under the two arrows. (N24a's second sentence is 23 words;
  fine for the voice, but the split above gives the ear a rest.)
