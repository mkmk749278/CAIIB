## Findings

### 1 · Medium · N17, N24, N26, N34, N87b (and cue N17.2–3, N24.2) · "foreign currency = the money itself" shown as notes only
**Problem.** N17 says foreign currency is "Dollar, euro, yen, dirham. That's the money itself", the cue draws four
banknotes and boxes "the money itself", and the hook (N24, N34, N87b) repeats it. Under FEMA, "currency" (s. 2(h))
"includes all currency notes, postal notes, postal orders, money orders, cheques, drafts, travellers cheques,
letters of credit, bills of exchange and promissory notes, credit cards" and instruments RBI notifies (debit and
ATM cards, FEMA 15/2000-RB). So a dollar cheque or a dollar draft is *foreign currency* itself, not a pocket item.
Fact sheet row 1 says in so many words: "foreign currency is not just the note". v3 guarded against this by drawing
"a note and a cheque" under the "foreign currency" tag (v3 N12 cue); the class edition dropped the cheque and
boxed "the money itself" over notes. Exam risk: "Under FEMA, 'currency' includes (a) currency notes (b) cheques
(c) credit cards (d) all of these" — a learner taught "notes = the money itself" picks (a).
**Source.** FEMA 1999 s. 2(h), 2(i), 2(m) (re-read 2026-11-21); fact sheet #1.
**Fix.** Replace N17 with:
> N17 · FEMA defines foreign currency simply. Any currency other than Indian currency. And in FEMA, currency is wide. Not just notes, but cheques, drafts, even cards. Dollar, euro, yen, dirham, in any of those forms. That's the money itself.

Cue N17: `1 S: card line "s. 2(m) foreign currency = any currency other than Indian" [F1] · 2 S: a USD note, a USD
cheque and a card draw; small tag "s. 2(h) currency: notes, cheques, drafts, cards…" · 3 B: box around "the money
itself"`. N24 cue 2: "the note and the cheque beside it, label 'foreign currency'". The hook lines (N24, N34, N87b)
can then stay as written.

### 2 · Low · N37, N38, N51–N52, N54, N87c · "There's no building" stated as absolute
**Problem.** "No. There's no building. The forex market is over the counter." Spot and forward FX is OTC, but
currency futures and options do trade on exchanges in India (NSE, BSE), and the fact sheet itself (row 31) names the
"exchange-traded currency derivative segments" through which RBI acts. A learner who later meets "currency futures
on a recognised stock exchange" (A-module derivatives, C01) may think this lesson was wrong.
**Source.** Fact sheet #8, #31 (RBI AR 2025-26 V.20).
**Fix.** N37: "No. There's no building for the main market. The forex market is over the counter. O-T-C. Banks and
dealers deal with each other directly, mostly on screens." N52: "No. The main market is over the counter. Second.
Your branch sells dollars to a student. Interbank, or merchant market?" (N54 and N87c can stay.)

### 3 · Low · N85v, N87f · "never fixes it" / "never fixes"
**Problem.** Overstated in the very chapter that teaches the history: under the par value and basket peg, and for
the official 40% leg of LERMS, the rate *was* set officially (fact sheet #27: "an official rate determined by the
Reserve Bank"). An exam option "Under LERMS, the official rate was determined by RBI" is true; "never" invites a
wrong strike-out.
**Source.** Fact sheet #22–#28, #30.
**Fix.** N85v: "…India's rate is market determined since nineteen ninety-three, and R-B-I steadies it, it doesn't
fix it." N87f: "The market, since March nineteen ninety-three. R-B-I steadies, it doesn't fix. F-B-I-L publishes
the reference rate. That's the whole map." Cue for both: "steadies, doesn't fix".

### 4 · Low · N85e · "India started with a par value system"
**Problem.** v3 (N37) said "after the Second World War"; the class edition cut it, so "India started with" reads as
if the rupee began life on a Bretton Woods par value. Before that it was tied to sterling. Minor, but it is a date
line learners may memorise.
**Source.** RBI-DRG (fact sheet #23–#24); v3 N37.
**Fix.** "Now India's road, in five posts. Post one. After the Second World War, India had a par value system, a
fixed official rate, under the Bretton Woods system."

### 5 · Low · N85r · reference rate "built from real spot trades" for every currency
**Problem.** Only USD/INR is computed from actual spot trades; EUR, GBP and JPY reference rates are derived as cross
rates (fact sheet #33). As spoken, "the reference rate ... built from real spot trades" covers all of them.
**Source.** FBIL Reference Rate Methodology v2 §4, 7–11 (fact sheet #33).
**Fix.** "The dollar rate is built from real spot trades in a random fifteen-minute slot between eleven-thirty and
twelve-thirty. It comes out at about one-thirty in the afternoon." Cue 2 add: "€ £ ¥ via cross rates".
