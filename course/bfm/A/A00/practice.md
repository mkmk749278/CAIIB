# A00 practice (our own questions)

1. Under FEMA 1999, which of these is **foreign exchange**?
   a) only foreign currency notes b) a draft drawn by a bank in London, payable in Indian rupees
   c) a rupee cheque drawn by a Mumbai resident on a Mumbai bank d) Indian currency notes held abroad
   **Answer: b.** s. 2(n)(iii): instruments drawn outside India but payable in Indian currency are foreign exchange.

2. A spot deal is struck on Thursday. Saturday is not a forex working day and there is no holiday. Value date?
   a) Thursday b) Friday c) Monday d) Tuesday
   **Answer: c.** Spot is the second working day (FEDAI 5.2): Friday is day 1, Monday is day 2.

3. The value date of a deal falls on a holiday declared three weeks in advance. The deal is settled on:
   a) the next working day b) the preceding working day c) the same day anyway d) the bank's choice
   **Answer: b.** A known holiday moves delivery to the preceding working day; a sudden one to the next (FEDAI 1.4, 5.2).

4. Which of these is most likely to **strengthen** the rupee?
   a) a wider current account deficit b) higher inflation in India than abroad
   c) large foreign portfolio inflows d) inflation in Brazil
   **Answer: c.** Inflows add dollar supply. (a) and (b) weaken the rupee; (d) is not relevant to USD/INR.

5. Under interest rate parity, the currency with the higher interest rate trades at a forward:
   a) premium b) discount c) par d) it depends on the spot rate
   **Answer: b.** The forward price offsets the interest gain (sums in A05).

6. The rupee's exchange rate became market-determined with the unification of the dual rates in:
   a) September 1975 b) March 1992 c) March 1993 d) August 1994
   **Answer: c.** LERMS (March 1992) was the dual-rate step; unification came in March 1993; August 1994 was
   current account convertibility.

7. Who computes and publishes the USD/INR reference rate today?
   a) RBI b) FEDAI c) FBIL d) CCIL
   **Answer: c.** FBIL, since 10 July 2018 (RBI press release, 4 Jul 2018).

8. Buying dollars against rupees in Mumbai, selling them for euros in London and converting the euros back into
   rupees, all to profit from price gaps, is:
   a) hedging b) simple arbitrage c) compound arbitrage d) speculation
   **Answer: c.** Three currencies or centres: compound (triangular) arbitrage.
