**P1.** An export bill drawn 90 days after sight, not under an LC, is handed to the bank, which pays the exporter now.
The bank has:
(a) purchased it (b) **discounted it** (c) negotiated it (d) collected it
*Reason: usance bill → discounted; sight → purchased; under an LC → negotiated [F1].*

**P2.** An unpaid export bill is crystallised into a rupee liability. FEDAI requires the bank to apply:
(a) the bill buying rate of that day (b) the original bill buying rate (c) **its TT selling rate** (d) the FBIL
reference rate
*Reason: FEDAI 2.1(b)(iii) [F10]. The bank must buy dollars to replace those never received, so it sells to her.*

**P3.** (keyed in) A bill of USD 40,000 was discounted at ₹85.70. It is unpaid; the bank crystallises it 30 days after
the due date at TT selling ₹86.25. Overdue interest is 10.95 % a year on the advance. Find (i) the crystallised
rupee liability and (ii) the overdue interest.
*Answer: (i) `40000 × 86.25 =` **₹34,50,000** · (ii) `3428000 × 10.95 % × 30 ÷ 365 =` **₹30,852** (advance
`40000 × 85.7 =` 34,28,000) [F10, F11].*

**P4.** After crystallisation, the foreign buyer finally pays. The bank converts the dollars at:
(a) **TT buying (or the contracted rate, if any)** (b) TT selling (c) bill buying (d) the crystallisation rate
*Reason: FEDAI 2.1(c) [F13].*

**P5.** (keyed in) A USD 100,000 bill comes back dishonoured before crystallisation, with foreign bank charges of
USD 200. TT selling ₹86.10. What rupee amount does the bank recover, before interest and rupee charges?
*Answer: `100200 × 86.1 =` **₹86,27,220** [F14].*

**P6.** (keyed in) A sight bill of USD 20,000, not under an LC, is purchased at ₹86.00. NTP 25 days; interest 7.3 % a
year. The interest recovered up front is:
(a) ₹0, as it is a sight bill (b) ₹10,320 (c) ₹1,25,560 (d) **₹8,600**
*Reason: `1720000 × 7.3 % × 25 ÷ 365 =` 8,600 [F3, F4]. (a) forgets the NTP; (b) uses 30 days; (c) is a full year.*

**P7.** Under RBI's definition, a **usance** export bill becomes overdue when:
(a) it is not paid within the NTP plus grace (b) **it is not paid on the due date** (c) it is not paid within 30 days
of the due date (d) it is crystallised
*Reason: CF 2(1)(xxvii) [F6]; (a) is the demand-bill test; (c) is a common crystallisation-day policy, not the
definition.*

**P8.** Which statement is **true** at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off?
(a) **each bank sets its crystallisation policy and must make it transparently available to customers** (b) FEDAI
fixes crystallisation on the 30th day after the due date (c) a crystallised exporter no longer has to realise the
export (d) a bill with a fixed due date carries the NTP as well
*Reason: FEDAI 2.1(b)(i)–(ii) [F9]; (b) old rule; (c) EDPMS stays open [F17]; (d) NTP not applicable [F4].*

**P9.** Packing-credit statements. Which is **false**?
(a) Not adjusted by export documents within 360 days → not export credit from the start (b) If exports never happen,
the bank charges its domestic lending rate plus penal charges (c) **RBI requires the limit to be the lower of FOB value
and domestic cost** (d) A running account is only for good-track-record exporters and EOU / SEZ units
*Reason: RBI requires need-based limits; "lower of FOB and cost" is bank practice [A11b F24, Leads]; (a), (b), (d)
are A11b F3, F4, F8.*

**P10.** The bill is paid 10 days before its notional due date. The bank:
(a) keeps all the interest (b) charges overdue interest (c) **refunds interest for the unexpired period and pays or
recovers the notional swap cost** (d) crystallises the bill
*Reason: FEDAI 2.2(c) [F8].*

**P11.** (linked case, keyed in) A USD 80,000 bill at 90 days after sight, not under an LC, is discounted on Fri 9 Apr
2027 at ₹85.60. NTP 25 days; interest 7.3 % a year; overdue interest 10.95 % a year (365-day year). The bank's policy
is to crystallise on the 30th day after the notional due date; TT selling that day ₹86.20. The buyer pays 20 days
after crystallisation; TT buying ₹85.75. Find: (i) interest up front and her credit (ii) the notional due date
(iii) the crystallisation date and her rupee debt (iv) the total due on crystallisation day (v) what she still pays.
*Answer: (i) ₹1,57,504; credit ₹66,90,496 (115 days) · (ii) Mon 2 Aug 2027 · (iii) Wed 1 Sep 2027; ₹68,96,000 at TT
selling · (iv) overdue interest ₹61,632 on ₹68,48,000 for 30 days; total ₹69,57,632 · (v) proceeds ₹68,60,000 at TT
buying; 36,000 short + interest on ₹68,96,000 for 20 days ₹41,376 = **₹77,376** [F3, F4, F10, F11, F13;
numericals P11].*

**P12.** A bill was only **sent for collection** (the bank advanced nothing). When the buyer pays, the bank converts
the dollars at:
(a) TT selling (b) bill buying (c) **TT buying, after the money is credited to its Nostro** (d) the crystallisation rate
*Reason: FEDAI 2.5(a) [F20]; nothing was advanced, so nothing is crystallised.*
