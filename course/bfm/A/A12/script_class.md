## Chapter 1 · Discounting the bill

N009 · Chapter one. Banks use three words here. A sight bill is purchased. A usance bill is discounted. A bill under an L-C is negotiated.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Discounting the bill" · 2 C: card "sight → purchased · usance → discounted · under LC → negotiated" [F1]

N010 · All three are the same idea. The bank pays her now, and collects from Hamburg later.
> 1 S: the counter; rupees go to her now; an arrow to Hamburg marked "later"

N010a · Many of you think the bank has now bought the risk too. It hasn't. If Hamburg doesn't pay, she owes the bank those dollars.
> 1 C: card "purchase / discount = an advance to her · Hamburg doesn't pay → she owes the bank the dollars" [F9]

N011 · Her bill is payable after sight. Which word fits?

## Chapter 2 · Interest up front

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Which rate for discounting, if there's no forward?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Interest up front" · H: 3 s

N021 · Bill buying. Now, the bank pays today but gets the dollars later. So it charges interest, and it takes it up front.
> 1 C: card "interest for the transit and usance period: recovered up front, at once" [F3]

N022 · For how many days? Sixty days after sight. But sight happens only when the bill reaches Hamburg.
> 1 S: the bill travels; a "sight" stamp waits at Hamburg

N023 · So we add the normal transit period, N-T-P, twenty-five days for a foreign currency bill. Sixty plus twenty-five: eighty-five days.
> 1 N: "NTP 25 + usance 60 (+ grace, if any) = 85 days" [F4] · 2 B: circle "85"

N023a · Nobody knows when Hamburg will see the bill, so the bank assumes a date: eighty-five days on. That assumed date is the notional due date.
> 1 N: "notional = assumed · notional due date = 1 Mar + 85 days" [F4]

N024 · A bill due a fixed date after shipment needs no transit period. Its due date is already known.
> 1 C: card "fixed due date (from shipment, from bill date) → NTP not applicable · due date never changed after discount" [F4]

N025 · Interest at seven point three per cent a year, illustrative, on eighty-five lakh eighty thousand, for eighty-five days. Work it out.

## Chapter 3 · Unpaid: crystallisation

N033 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Her bill's notional due date was how many days after discounting?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Unpaid: crystallisation" · H: 3 s

N034 · Eighty-five. From the first of March, that's the twenty-fifth of May. Hamburg doesn't pay. From that day, the bill is overdue.
> 1 S: pin "Tue 25 May 2027 · due" turns red [numericals Q3]

N034a · That's the rule for a usance bill: not paid on the due date. A sight bill is overdue if not paid within the transit period, plus any grace.
> 1 C: card "overdue: usance → not paid on the due date · sight (demand) → not paid within NTP + grace" [F6, F7]

N035 · Remember, she owes the bank those hundred thousand dollars. The bank paid rupees for dollars it still doesn't have. And the rate keeps moving.
> 1 S: the counter; an unfilled dollar slot on the bank's side; a rate needle wobbles

N036 · So the bank moves that risk to her. It turns her dollar debt into a fixed rupee debt. The exam word is crystallisation.
> 1 C: card "crystallisation = her foreign-currency liability → a fixed rupee liability; exchange risk moves to the exporter" [F9] · 2 B: underline "crystallisation"

N037 · On which day? Today, F-E-D-A-I sets no day. Each bank makes its own policy, and keeps it open to customers.
> 1 C: card "day: the bank's own policy · transparently available to customers (FEDAI 2.1(b))" [F9]

N038 · Older books gave a fixed day: thirty days after the transit period for sight bills, after the due date for usance bills.
> 1 C: trap card "'FEDAI says 30th day' ✗ (old rule: sight → 30th day after NTP · usance → 30th day after due date · holiday → next working day) · today: bank's policy; the case gives the day ✓" [Leads]

N038a · A holiday moved it to the next working day. In the exam, the case tells you the day.

N039 · Her bank's policy: thirty days after the due date. Twenty-fifth of May plus thirty. Which date?

N041 · Many of you say bill buying again. No. The dollars never came, and she owes them. So the bank sells her the dollars.

N042 · The T-T selling rate on that day. Here, eighty-six rupees forty paise, illustrative. Her rupee debt?

N043 · Eighty-six lakh forty thousand rupees. The bank's advance was eighty-five lakh eighty thousand. That sixty thousand gap is her exchange loss, for now.

N044 · And interest for the overdue days. The bank takes it on the crystallisation day, from the due date.

N046 · Seventy-seven thousand two hundred twenty rupees, paid that day. The eighty-six lakh forty thousand stays as her rupee debt.

N046a · So on that day, her total dues are eighty-seven lakh seventeen thousand two hundred twenty rupees.
