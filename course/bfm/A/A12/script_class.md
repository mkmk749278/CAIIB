## Chapter 2 · Interest up front

N020 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Which rate for discounting, if there's no forward?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Interest up front" · H: 3 s

N021 · Bill buying. Now, the bank pays today but gets the dollars later. So it charges interest, and it takes it up front.
> 1 C: card "interest for the transit and usance period: recovered up front, at once" [F3]

N022 · For how many days? Sixty days after sight. But sight happens only when the bill reaches Hamburg.
> 1 S: the bill travels; a "sight" stamp waits at Hamburg

N023 · So we add the normal transit period, twenty-five days. Sixty plus twenty-five: eighty-five days. That's the notional due date.
> 1 N: "notional due date = NTP 25 + usance 60 = 85 days" [F4] · 2 B: circle "85"

N024 · A bill due a fixed date after shipment needs no transit period. Its due date is already known.
> 1 C: card "fixed due date (from shipment, from bill date) → NTP not applicable" [F4]

N025 · Interest at seven point three per cent a year, illustrative, for eighty-five days. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "₹85,80,000 × 7.3% × 85 ÷ 365 (illustrative)" · H: 8 s

N025a · Eighty-five lakh eighty, times seven point three, per cent. Times eighty-five, divided by three sixty-five. One lakh forty-five thousand eight hundred sixty.
> 1 S: keys "8580000 × 7.3 % = 626340 × 85 = 53238900 ÷ 365 = 145860" · 2 B: box "interest ₹1,45,860" [numericals Q2]

N025b · So she gets eighty-five lakh eighty, less the interest. Eighty-four lakh thirty-four thousand one hundred forty.
> 1 S: keys "8580000 − 145860 = 8434140" · 2 B: box "credit ₹84,34,140" [numericals Q2]

N025c · If her packing credit is still open, this credit repays it first. That's last class.

## Chapter 3 · Unpaid: crystallisation

N033 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Her bill's notional due date was how many days after discounting?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Unpaid: crystallisation" · H: 3 s

N034 · Eighty-five. From the first of March, that's the twenty-fifth of May. Hamburg doesn't pay. From that day, the bill is overdue.
> 1 S: pin "Tue 25 May 2027 · due" turns red · 2 C: card "overdue: usance → not paid on the due date · demand → not paid within NTP + grace" [F6, F7] [numericals Q3]

N035 · Now the risk. The bank paid her rupees for dollars it still doesn't have. Every day, the rupee value of those dollars can move.
> 1 S: the counter; an unfilled dollar slot on the bank's side; a rate needle wobbles

N036 · So the bank moves that risk to her. It turns her dollar debt into a rupee debt. The exam word is crystallisation.
> 1 C: card "crystallisation = her foreign-currency liability → a fixed rupee liability; exchange risk moves to the exporter" [F9] · 2 B: underline "crystallisation"

N037 · On which day? Today, F-E-D-A-I sets no day. Each bank writes its own policy, and must show it to customers.
> 1 C: card "day: the bank's own policy · transparently available to customers (FEDAI 2.1(b))" [F9]

N038 · Older books say the thirtieth day after the due date. Many banks still use that, and exam cases tell you the day.
> 1 C: trap card "'FEDAI says 30th day' ✗ (old rule) · bank's policy; the case gives the day ✓" [Leads]

N039 · Her bank's policy: thirty days after the due date. Twenty-fifth of May plus thirty. Which date?
> 1 S: calendar strip; pin "crystallisation?" · H: 3 s

N040 · The twenty-fourth of June. Now the big question. Which rate? Think about the counter.
> 1 S: pin "Thu 24 Jun 2027 · crystallise" [numericals Q3] · 2 S: the counter, both sides lit · H: 3 s

N041 · Many of you say bill buying again. No. The bank must now buy dollars to replace the missing ones. It sells them to her account.
> 1 B: arrow OUT, red side "bank sells" · 2 C: trap card "bill buying again ✗ · TT selling ✓" [F10, F15]

N042 · The T-T selling rate on that day. Here, eighty-six rupees forty paise, illustrative. Her rupee debt?
> 1 C: card "crystallisation → TT selling rate (FEDAI 2.1(b)(iii))" [F10] · 2 S: sum card "USD 100,000 × ₹86.40" · H: 8 s

N043 · Eighty-six lakh forty thousand. She got eighty-five lakh eighty. The sixty thousand difference is her exchange loss.
> 1 S: keys "100000 × 86.4 = 8640000" · 2 B: box "rupee liability ₹86,40,000" · 3 N: "8640000 − 8580000 = 60000: her exchange loss" [numericals Q4]

N044 · And interest for the overdue days. The bank takes it on the crystallisation day, from the due date.
> 1 C: card "on crystallisation day: interest for the overdue period · then on the crystallised amount till she pays" [F11]

N045 · Overdue rate ten point nine five per cent, illustrative, on eighty-five lakh eighty, for thirty days. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "₹85,80,000 × 10.95% × 30 ÷ 365" · H: 8 s

N046 · Seventy-seven thousand two hundred twenty rupees.
> 1 S: keys "8580000 × 10.95 % = 939510 × 30 = 28185300 ÷ 365 = 77220" · 2 B: box "overdue interest ₹77,220" [numericals Q4]

N047 · So our opening question. The bank doesn't simply wait. It fixes her debt in rupees, at T-T selling, and adds interest.
> 1 G: "bank waits" dims; "takes the rupees back" lights with "at TT selling + interest"

N048 · Then, on the fourteenth of July, Hamburg pays. Dollars arrive now. Which rate converts them?
> 1 S: pin "Wed 14 Jul 2027 · paid"; dollars arrive at the counter · H: 3 s

N049 · T-T buying. The dollars are already in the bank's account abroad. Here, eighty-five ninety-five: eighty-five lakh ninety-five thousand.
> 1 C: card "proceeds after crystallisation → TT buying (or the contracted rate)" [F13] · 2 S: keys "100000 × 85.95 = 8595000" [numericals Q5]

N050 · Her debt was eighty-six lakh forty, plus twenty days' interest, fifty-one thousand eight forty. What does she still pay?

N051 · Forty-five thousand short, plus fifty-one thousand eight forty. Ninety-six thousand eight hundred forty rupees.
> 1 S: keys "8640000 − 8595000 = 45000 + 51840 = 96840" · 2 B: box "she pays ₹96,840" [numericals Q5]

N052 · One more case: the bill comes back dishonoured before crystallisation. The bank recovers the bill, plus foreign charges, at T-T selling.

## Chapter 4 · The packing-credit statements

N054 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Who decides the crystallisation day today?

N055 · The bank's own policy. Now the second case the exam likes: five statements on packing credit. True or false? Say it before I do.
> 1 S: five statement cards, face down

N056 · One. Packing credit not closed by export documents in three hundred and sixty days stops being export credit from the start.
> 1 C: statement 1 flips · H: 3 s

N057 · True. Ab initio. Two. If the export never happens, the bank keeps charging the export credit rate.
> 1 N: "1 true ✓ [A11b F3]" · 2 C: statement 2 flips · H: 3 s

N058 · False. Domestic lending rate, plus penal charges. Three. Packing credit is normally repaid by the export bill's proceeds.
> 1 N: "2 false: domestic rate + penal [A11b F4]" · 2 C: statement 3 flips · H: 3 s

N059 · True. That's liquidation. Four. Any exporter may have a running account, with no order in hand.
> 1 N: "3 true ✓ [A11b F6]" · 2 C: statement 4 flips · H: 3 s

N060 · False. Only good-track-record exporters, and E-O-U or S-E-Z units. Five. R-B-I fixes the limit as the lower of F-O-B and cost.
> 1 N: "4 false: good track record, EOU / SEZ [A11b F8]" · 2 C: statement 5 flips · H: 3 s

N061 · False, and it's a sharp one. R-B-I says need-based. The lower of the two is bank practice, and how exam sums are set.
