## Chapter 1 · The discount

N008 · Chapter one. First, from A-zero-five. A sixty-days-after-sight bill: what does the bank add to the sixty days?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · The discount" · H: 3 s

N009 · The normal transit period. For a foreign currency bill not under an L-C, F-E-D-A-I fixes twenty-five days. So eighty-five days in all.
> 1 C: card "after sight, FC bill not under LC: 60 + NTP 25 = 85 days (a bank may vary NTP with documented reasons; max 90 days from shipment)" [F2; A05-F15] · 2 B: circle "85"

N010 · Ravi brings the bill on Monday the first of February twenty twenty-seven. The bank plans a due date eighty-five days on: the notional due date. Which day?
> 1 S: date strip; "Mon 1 Feb" tagged · 2 N: "notional due date = planned, not written on the bill" · H: 6 s

N011 · Count in months. February twenty twenty-seven has twenty-eight days, so twenty-seven are left. March adds thirty-one: fifty-eight. Twenty-seven more: Tuesday the twenty-seventh of April.
> 1 S: strip segments "Feb 27 · Mar 31 · Apr 27 = 85" → "Tue 27 Apr 2027 = notional due date" [F2] · 2 N: "notional due date = discount date + NTP + usance (+ grace, if any)"

N012 · With a fixed due date, say sixty days from shipment, there's no transit period to add. The date is already known.
> 1 N: "fixed due date → no NTP" [F2]

N012a · And a sight bill? Only the transit period: twenty-five days of interest, no usance.
> 1 C: card "sight bill: interest for NTP only (25 days)" [F1, F2]

N013 · The bank discounts the bill: it pays Ravi now, and collects from Rotterdam later. Which rate: T-T buying, or bill buying?
> 1 S: A01's counter; two tags "TT buying" · "bill buying" · H: 3 s

N014 · Bill buying. The dollars aren't in its Nostro yet. Here it's eighty-five rupees twenty, worked out as in A-zero-five.
> 1 C: card "discount / purchase / negotiate → bill buying rate (or forward contract rate)" [F1] · 2 N: "₹85.20 (illustrative; A05 method)"

N014a · Quick recall: spot, plus premium for eighty-five days rounded down to two completed months, less the bank's margin.
> 1 C: card "usance bill buying = spot + premium for 2 completed months (85 days, rounded down: bank's favour) − margin (convention)" [A05-F14]

N015 · Rupee value: fifty thousand times eighty-five point two. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "50,000 × 85.20" · H: 8 s

N016 · Forty-two lakh sixty thousand. That's Ravi's advance.
> 1 S: keys "50000 × 85.2 = 4260000" · 2 B: box "advance ₹42,60,000" [numericals C1]

N017 · But he doesn't get all of it. The bank is lending him the money for eighty-five days, so it takes the interest now, upfront.
> 1 C: card "interest for transit + usance recovered upfront" [F1; A05-F13]

N018 · Nine per cent a year, illustrative. Watch the keys once. Forty-two lakh sixty thousand, times nine, then the per cent key.
> 1 S: sum card "₹42,60,000 × 9% × 85 ÷ 365 (case convention)" · 2 S: keys "4260000 × 9 %" · H: 3 s

N018a · Three lakh eighty-three thousand four hundred. That's one full year's interest. We want only eighty-five days of it.
> 1 B: box "383400 = 1 year" · 2 N: "× days ÷ 365 (case convention; use 360 only if the question says so)" [F12]

N018b · So times eighty-five, divide by three sixty-five. Your turn.
> 1 S: keys "× 85 ÷ 365 =" · H: 8 s

N019 · About eighty-nine thousand, two hundred and eighty-five rupees. If you got sixty-three thousand, you used sixty days. Add the transit.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 9 % → 383400 · × 85 = 32589000 · ÷ 365 = 89284.93" · 2 B: box "interest ₹89,284.93" [numericals C2] · 3 N: "60 days → ₹63,024.66 ✗"

N020 · So his account gets forty-one lakh, seventy thousand, seven hundred and fifteen.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 − 89284.93 = 4170715.07" · 2 B: box "credited ₹41,70,715.07" [numericals C2]

N020a · Same bill as a sight bill? Twenty-five days of interest: twenty-six thousand, two hundred and sixty rupees.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 9 % → 383400 · × 25 = 9585000 · ÷ 365 = 26260.27" · 2 N: "credited ₹42,33,739.73" [numericals C2s]

N021 · Pause check for chapter one. One. Which rate for discounting an export bill?

## Chapter 2 · The bill doesn't pay

N024 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. When is Ravi's bill notionally due?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The bill doesn't pay" · H: 3 s

N025 · The twenty-seventh of April. That day passes. No money from Rotterdam.
> 1 S: strip; 27 Apr passes; an empty Nostro

N026 · From that day, Ravi pays overdue interest. That's F-E-D-A-I's rule for usance bills unpaid by the due date.
> 1 C: card "unpaid by the due date → overdue interest" [F3]

N026a · Say Rotterdam pays on Monday the tenth of May, thirteen days late. Does the bank crystallise?
> 1 S: strip "Mon 10 May: USD 50,000 arrives" · H: 3 s

N026b · No. The dollars close the dollar advance. Ravi pays only overdue interest, thirteen days at the higher rate, eleven per cent here.
> 1 C: card "paid late, before crystallisation → no conversion · overdue interest only" [F3]

N026c · Forty-two lakh sixty thousand, eleven per cent, thirteen days: sixteen thousand, six hundred and ninety rupees.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 11 % → 468600 · × 13 = 6091800 · ÷ 365 = 16689.86" · 2 B: box "₹16,689.86" [numericals C2L]

N027 · But our story: nothing comes at all. Does the bank just wait? No. It crystallises.
> 1 G: "bank acts" button lights · 2 S: the word "crystallise" lands on the board

N027a · Remember, the bank bought his dollars on trust. If they don't come, Ravi owes the bank fifty thousand dollars.
> 1 N: "unpaid → Ravi owes USD 50,000"

N028 · Crystallise means: turn his dollar debt into a rupee debt. The bank stops carrying the dollar risk.
> 1 C: card "crystallisation: foreign currency liability → rupee liability" [F4] · 2 N: "the exchange risk moves to the exporter"

N029 · On which day? Many of you learnt: the thirtieth day after the due date. That was an older F-E-D-A-I rule.
> 1 C: trap card "always day 30 (older FEDAI rule) ✗ · bank's own policy, shown to customers ✓" [F4] · 2 B: cross out "day 30"

N030 · Now each bank sets its own day, and must make its policy open to customers. Ravi's bank happens to choose thirty days. Another may choose fewer.
> 1 N: "Ravi's bank policy: 30 days after due date (illustrative)" [F4]

N030a · So in the exam: who decides? The bank's policy. Not a fixed thirty.
> 1 B: box "bank's policy" [F4]

N031 · So, which date? April has thirty days.
> 1 S: strip from 27 Apr · H: 6 s

N032 · Thursday the twenty-seventh of May.
> 1 S: strip "Thu 27 May 2027 = crystallisation" [numericals dates]

N033 · Which rate? Many of you will say bill buying again, the rate he got. It isn't.
> 1 C: trap card "bill buying again ✗ · TT selling ✓" [F5]

N034 · The bank paid him rupees for dollars that never came. Now it sells him those dollars to close the debt. So those who said 'sells it back' were close.
> 1 S: A01's counter flips amber; arrow OUT "bank sells USD to Ravi" · 2 C: card "crystallisation rate = TT selling" [F5] · 3 G: "bank acts" gets a tick "sells USD to Ravi"

N035 · T-T selling is eighty-six rupees ten, illustrative. Fifty thousand dollars, crystallised. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "50,000 × 86.10" · H: 8 s

N036 · Forty-three lakh, five thousand.
> 1 S: keys "50000 × 86.1 = 4305000" · 2 B: box "crystallised ₹43,05,000" [numericals C3]

N037 · The bank advanced forty-two lakh, sixty thousand against these dollars. Now he owes forty-three lakh, five thousand.
> 1 N: "43,05,000 − 42,60,000 = ₹45,000 more than advanced: bought at bill buying, sold back at TT selling (spread + any market move)" [numericals C3]

N037a · The dollar now costs him more. That forty-five thousand gap is his exchange loss.
> 1 B: box "₹45,000"

N038 · And interest for the overdue thirty days, recovered on the day of crystallisation. Eleven per cent, illustrative, on the advance, as the case says. Same keys. Your turn.
> 1 C: card "interest for the overdue period: recovered on the crystallisation date; then till recovery" [F6] · 2 S: sum card "₹42,60,000 × 11% × 30 ÷ 365 (case convention: on the advance, due date → crystallisation)" · H: 8 s

N039 · Thirty-eight thousand, five hundred and fifteen rupees.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 11 % → 468600 · × 30 = 14058000 · ÷ 365 = 38515.07" · 2 B: box "overdue interest ₹38,515.07" [numericals C4]

N040 · So on the twenty-seventh of May, Ravi owes forty-three lakh, forty-three thousand, five hundred and fifteen rupees, now in rupees. In our case, he pays that day.
> 1 S: keys "4305000 + 38515.07 = 4343515.07" · 2 B: box "recovered ₹43,43,515.07 (case: Ravi pays on 27 May)" [numericals C4] · 3 N: "not paid → stays a rupee debt, interest till recovery" [F6]

N040a · Even repaid in rupees, the bill stays open in E-D-P-M-S till the dollars come.
> 1 N: "EDPMS stays open · also: follow-up, ECGC claim if covered → A15" [F13]

N041 · Then, on the sixteenth of June, Rotterdam finally pays. The dollars land in the Nostro. Which rate now?
> 1 S: strip "Wed 16 Jun: USD 50,000 arrives" · H: 3 s

N042 · T-T buying. The money is already in the bank's account. Eighty-five sixty, illustrative: forty-two lakh eighty thousand to Ravi.
> 1 C: card "proceeds after crystallisation → TT buying (or contract rate) · already paid → to Ravi · not paid → first close the rupee debt + interest to date" [F8, F6] · 2 S: keys "50000 × 85.6 = 4280000" · 3 B: box "₹42,80,000 to Ravi" [numericals C5]

N043 · [lightly] Late money is still money. Just a little less of it.
> 1 S: a small coin rolls into Ravi's account · 2 N: "Ravi's net: 41,70,715.07 − 43,43,515.07 + 42,80,000 = ₹41,07,200"

> 1 S: statement card, five empty rows

N059 · One. Packing credit not liquidated by export documents in three hundred and sixty days stops being export credit from the start.
