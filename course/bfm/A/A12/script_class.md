## Chapter 1 · The discount

N008 · Chapter one. First, from A-zero-five. A sixty-days-after-sight bill: what does the bank add to the sixty days?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · The discount" · H: 3 s

N009 · The normal transit period. For a foreign currency bill not under an L-C, F-E-D-A-I fixes twenty-five days. So eighty-five days in all.
> 1 C: card "after sight, FC bill not under LC: 60 + NTP 25 = 85 days" [F2; A05-F15] · 2 B: circle "85"

N010 · Ravi brings the bill on Monday the first of February twenty twenty-seven. When is it notionally due?
> 1 S: date strip; "Mon 1 Feb" tagged · H: 6 s

N011 · Tuesday the twenty-seventh of April: eighty-five days on. Notional means the bank's planned date, not one written on the bill.
> 1 S: strip "Tue 27 Apr 2027 = notional due date" [F2] · 2 N: "notional due date = discount date + NTP + usance"

N012 · With a fixed due date, say sixty days from shipment, there's no transit period to add. The date is already known.
> 1 N: "fixed due date → no NTP" [F2]

N013 · The bank discounts the bill. Which rate: T-T buying, or bill buying?
> 1 S: A01's counter; two tags "TT buying" · "bill buying" · H: 3 s

N014 · Bill buying. The dollars aren't in its Nostro yet. Here it's eighty-five rupees twenty, worked out as in A-zero-five.
> 1 C: card "discount / purchase / negotiate → bill buying rate (or forward contract rate)" [F1] · 2 N: "₹85.20 (illustrative; A05 method)"

N015 · Rupee value: fifty thousand times eighty-five point two. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "50,000 × 85.20" · H: 8 s

N016 · Forty-two lakh sixty thousand. That's Ravi's advance.
> 1 S: keys "50000 × 85.2 = 4260000" · 2 B: box "advance ₹42,60,000" [numericals C1]

N017 · But he doesn't get all of it. The bank takes interest for the eighty-five days now, upfront.
> 1 C: card "interest for transit + usance recovered upfront" [F1; A05-F13]

N018 · Nine per cent a year, illustrative. Forty-two lakh sixty thousand, times nine per cent, times eighty-five, divided by three sixty-five.
> 1 S: sum card "₹42,60,000 × 9% × 85 ÷ 365 (case convention)" · H: 8 s

N019 · About eighty-nine thousand, two hundred and eighty-five rupees.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 9 % = 383400 · × 85 = 32589000 · ÷ 365 = 89284.93" · 2 B: box "interest ₹89,284.93" [numericals C2]

N020 · So his account gets forty-one lakh, seventy thousand, seven hundred and fifteen.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 − 89284.93 = 4170715.07" · 2 B: box "credited ₹41,70,715.07" [numericals C2]

## Chapter 2 · The bill doesn't pay

N024 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. When is Ravi's bill notionally due?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The bill doesn't pay" · H: 3 s

N025 · The twenty-seventh of April. That day passes. No money from Rotterdam.
> 1 S: strip; 27 Apr passes; an empty Nostro

N026 · From that day, Ravi pays overdue interest. That's F-E-D-A-I's rule for usance bills unpaid by the due date.
> 1 C: card "unpaid by the due date → overdue interest" [F3]

N027 · Now our opening question. Does the bank just wait? No. It crystallises.
> 1 G: "bank acts" button lights · 2 S: the word "crystallise" lands on the board

N028 · Crystallise means: turn his dollar debt into a rupee debt. The bank stops carrying the dollar risk.
> 1 C: card "crystallisation: foreign currency liability → rupee liability" [F4] · 2 N: "the exchange risk moves to the exporter"

N029 · On which day? Many of you learnt: the thirtieth day after the due date. That was an older F-E-D-A-I rule.
> 1 C: trap card "always day 30 (older FEDAI rule) ✗ · bank's own policy, shown to customers ✓" [F4] · 2 B: cross out "day 30"

N030 · Now each bank sets its own policy, and must make it open to its customers. Ravi's bank says thirty days after the due date.
> 1 N: "Ravi's bank policy: 30 days after due date (illustrative)" [F4]

N031 · So, which date?
> 1 S: strip from 27 Apr · H: 6 s

N032 · Thursday the twenty-seventh of May.
> 1 S: strip "Thu 27 May 2027 = crystallisation" [numericals dates]

N033 · Which rate? Many of you will say bill buying again, the rate he got. It isn't.
> 1 C: trap card "bill buying again ✗ · TT selling ✓" [F5]

N034 · The bank paid him rupees for dollars that never came. Now it sells him those dollars to close the debt. Selling, at T-T rate.
> 1 S: A01's counter flips amber; arrow OUT "bank sells USD to Ravi" · 2 C: card "crystallisation rate = TT selling" [F5]

N035 · T-T selling is eighty-six rupees ten, illustrative. Fifty thousand dollars, crystallised. Work it out.
> 1 S: sum card "50,000 × 86.10" · H: 8 s

N036 · Forty-three lakh, five thousand.
> 1 S: keys "50000 × 86.1 = 4305000" · 2 B: box "crystallised ₹43,05,000" [numericals C3]

N037 · He got forty-two lakh sixty against these dollars. Now he owes forty-three lakh five. The forty-five thousand gap is his exchange loss.
> 1 N: "43,05,000 − 42,60,000 = ₹45,000 exchange loss (rupee weakened)" [numericals C3]

N038 · And interest for the overdue thirty days, recovered on the day of crystallisation. Eleven per cent, illustrative, on the advance.
> 1 C: card "interest for the overdue period: recovered on the crystallisation date; then till recovery" [F6] · 2 S: sum card "₹42,60,000 × 11% × 30 ÷ 365" · H: 8 s

N039 · Thirty-eight thousand, five hundred and fifteen rupees.
> 1 S: keys "4260000 × 11 % = 468600 · × 30 = 14058000 · ÷ 365 = 38515.07" · 2 B: box "overdue interest ₹38,515.07" [numericals C4]

N040 · So on the twenty-seventh of May, the bank recovers forty-three lakh, forty-three thousand, five hundred and fifteen.
> 1 S: keys "4305000 + 38515.07 = 4343515.07" · 2 B: box "recovered ₹43,43,515.07" [numericals C4]

N041 · Then, on the sixteenth of June, Rotterdam finally pays. The dollars land in the Nostro. Which rate now?
> 1 S: strip "Wed 16 Jun: USD 50,000 arrives" · H: 3 s

N042 · T-T buying. The money is already in the bank's account. Eighty-five sixty, illustrative: forty-two lakh eighty thousand to Ravi.
> 1 C: card "proceeds after crystallisation → TT buying (or contract rate)" [F8] · 2 S: keys "50000 × 85.6 = 4280000" · 3 B: box "₹42,80,000 to Ravi" [numericals C5]

N043 · [lightly] Late money is still money. Just a little less of it.

## Chapter 4 · A packing-credit statement case

N057 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. A collection bill is converted at which rate?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · A packing-credit statement case" · H: 3 s

N058 · T-T buying. Now the exam's other favourite: five statements on packing credit. True or false? Pause on each.
> 1 S: statement card, five empty rows

N059 · One. Packing credit not liquidated by export documents in three hundred and sixty days stops being export credit from the start.
> 1 S: row 1 · H: 5 s

N060 · True. Two. Banks commonly size it on the C-I-F value of the order.
> 1 N: "1 ✓ true" [A11b-F3] · 2 S: row 2 · H: 5 s

N061 · False: the lower of F-O-B value and domestic cost, less margin. Three. A running account needs no order before each drawal.
> 1 N: "2 ✗ FOB / cost, never CIF" · 2 S: row 3 · H: 5 s

N062 · True, for good-record exporters, and orders follow. Four. If the export never happens, the export credit rate still applies.
> 1 N: "3 ✓ true" [A11b-F8] · 2 S: row 4 · H: 5 s

N063 · False: domestic lending rate, plus penal charges. Five. The export bill's proceeds liquidate the packing credit.
> 1 N: "4 ✗ domestic + penal" [A11b-F4] · 2 S: row 5 · H: 5 s

N064 · True. That's the normal way. Three true, two false.
> 1 N: "5 ✓ true" [A11b-F6] · 2 B: box "T F T F T"

N065 · Pause check for chapter four. One. Basis for sizing packing credit?

N066 · Lower of F-O-B and cost, less margin. Two, the twist. Export never happens. Which rate?
