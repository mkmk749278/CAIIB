## Findings

### High

**H1 · N056 → N064 · pause question answer was never taught.** N056 teaches "tell the customer at once". N064 then
asks about a message at 8 p.m. and the answer is "start of the next business day". N058 gave the after-hours rule
only for *crediting the money*, not for *informing*. A learner who listened well answers "at once" and is told he is
wrong. Rewrite N056:
> N056 · Now the newer rules on inward payments, from October twenty twenty-six. When the payment message arrives, the bank tells the customer at once. After hours, at the start of the next business day.

(Cue: add "after hours → start of next business day" to the card [F16].)

**H2 · N030 → N034 · the jump from "Nostro" to "payment systems" is missing.** Chapter 1 says the dollar sits in the
Nostro. N030 says New York debits the Nostro and credits "the other bank". N034 then says "where does the dollar
finally settle? In the payment systems." By ear I can't tell how the two fit, and the one-thing line (N050) "settles
in the Nostro and in the payment systems" stays vague. Add one bridging line after N030 and soften N034:
> N030a · If the payee's bank is another bank in New York, the dollars must cross between the two banks. That crossing happens in America's payment system.
> N034 · M-T seven hundred. Now, that crossing between banks. Every country has its own payment system for it. Two words first: gross and net.

(Check the wording against the fact sheet; it is the standard concept behind F28–F29.)

**H3 · Chapter 2 · too many system names by ear, with no frame.** In about four minutes the learner hears: SWIFT,
MT103, MT202, MT700, ISO 20022, Fedwire, Federal Reserve, CHIPS, The Clearing House, CHAPS, Bank of England, T2,
Eurosystem, TARGET2, RTGS, NEFT, SFMS, IFTAS: 18 names, 7 of them acronyms. The board helps, but on a phone each box
is gone from mind by the next. Three fixes:
1. Give the frame before the list (end of N036):
   > N036 · Gross needs more money in the account. Net needs less. Now the systems. For each, ask two things: who runs it, and is it gross or net?
2. Move history and minor names to the board only:
   > N032 · For payments, SWIFT now uses a newer format, ISO twenty-oh-twenty-two. But exam questions still use the M-T numbers.

   > N040 · In Britain, CHAPS, run by the Bank of England. Sterling, real-time gross. In Europe, T-two, run by the Eurosystem. Euro, real-time gross.

   (Keep "since 22 Nov 2025" and "replaced TARGET2, Mar 2023" on the cue cards only.)
3. After N043, one summary card held 3 s, then let the pause check use it:
   > N043a · So, six systems, one table. Fedwire, CHAPS, T-two and R-T-G-S: gross. CHIPS: net. N-E-F-T: batches.

   (Cue: C: table "system · country · runs it · gross/net"; it stays on the board for N046–N049.)

### Medium

**M1 · N065 · the twist asks about something never taught.** "Can a bank convert its old rupee Vostro account into an
S-R-V-A?" Nothing before it says SRVA is a different kind of account [F24]. Teach it first, in N061:
> N061 · A foreign bank, or an Indian bank's branch abroad, opens a new rupee account with an Indian bank. It's a separate account, not the ordinary rupee Vostro. Exports and imports are then paid in rupees through it.

(Then N065–N066 become true recall.)

**M2 · N055 · the reason does not add up for a beginner.** "The bank's money is stuck… so the bill rate carries a
wider margin. And the days of waiting? Interest, charged separately." If the waiting is paid by interest, why also a
wider margin? I am left confused at the exact moment the lesson's big question is answered. Either give a reason the
fact sheet supports (add a row with its source; F18 is only "Concept"), or make the claim smaller:
> N055 · So the bill rate sits further from the market than the T-T rate. And the interest for the waiting days is charged on top, separately. That's the answer to last class.

**M3 · N063 · "no exchange-rate risk" overstates F25 ("less exchange-rate risk").** Rewrite:
> N063 · Why does it matter? The Indian trader invoices in rupees. So his exchange-rate risk is smaller.

(Board: "INR invoice → less exchange risk for the Indian trader".)

**M4 · Warm-up · no "why it matters" and no class connection** (spec §0, checklist 1). Importance map: A-CORR is Tier C,
recalled 7 times (Nostro/Vostro twice, RTGS/IDRBT four times, MT700 once). One true line after N003:
> N003a · This topic comes up in small, sure questions: Nostro or Vostro, which SWIFT message, R-T-G-S. Easy marks, if the names are clear. Stay with me.

**M5 · Chapter 3 · three ideas in one chapter; SRVA arrives as "Last idea" with no signpost** (checklist 4). The
chapter title promises "rules of the Nostro", not rupee trade. Cheapest fix: rename the chapter "Time, rules, and
rupee trade" and open N060 with a re-entry line:
> N060 · One more account, and it's new in the exam. Trade settled in rupees, through a Special Rupee Vostro Account. S-R-V-A.

**M6 · IDRBT is missing** (examiner-side, noticed while checking N025). Recalled items ask "RTGS developed by?" and
"IDRBT" (3 of the 7 A-CORR items); the fact sheet has "SFMS launched by IDRBT". N043 names only IFTAS. Rewrite:
> N043 · And the messages for both travel on S-F-M-S. India's own messaging system. Built by I-D-R-B-T, the R-B-I's banking-technology institute. Run today by IFTAS, an R-B-I subsidiary.

(Check the "RTGS developed by" wording against the fact sheet before using it; add IDRBT and IFTAS to "Say it right".)

### Low

**L1 · N002 · question with no hold.** "Why?" is asked and the lesson moves on. Add "Hold that thought. We'll answer it
by the end." (no hold needed), so the learner does not feel skipped.

**L2 · N011 · the learner does not commit before the wrong belief is broken.** Make it a question first:
> N011 · So, quick: are Nostro and Vostro two different accounts? Many of you said yes. They're not. It's one account, seen from two sides.

(H: 3 s after "accounts?")

**L3 · N057 / N074 · two words for one rule.** N057 "check the Nostro"; N074 "reconcile". Use one, said with its meaning
once:
> N057 · They must reconcile the Nostro, that is, match it against the statement. Normally at least once an hour. Not just at the end of the day.

**L4 · N044 · "One R-T-G-S rule the exam likes."** Not backed by the recalled items (checklist 25). Say "One R-T-G-S
rule worth knowing."

**L5 · N075 · no scope line.** Add "That covers correspondent banking, the three accounts, and the payment systems
in your syllabus."

**L6 · Lines over 25 words** (N014, N018, N019, N035, N038, N040, N044, N059, N061, N062, N068, N073–N075, N077).
Every sentence is ≤22 words, so by ear they are fine; but the spec gate reads "no line over 25 words". If the gate
means lines, split each at a sentence boundary (e.g. N062 → N062 "No R-B-I approval is needed to open one now." /
N062a "The balance can pay for any permitted deal. Or it can be invested in Indian government and corporate debt.").
N062's "any permitted current or capital account deal" is also the heaviest phrase in the lesson for an ESL ear; the
split above simplifies it.
