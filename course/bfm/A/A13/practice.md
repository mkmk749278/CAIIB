# A13 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-22)

Our own items; figures illustrative. Rules as at 31 Dec 2026. Reasons cite `research/bfm/facts/A5.7-A5.10_A13.md` [F#].

**P1.** An Indian importer gets two years to pay directly from its overseas supplier of machinery. This is:
(a) buyer's credit (b) an ECB (c) **suppliers' credit** (d) EXIM Bank buyer's credit
*Reason: TC para 13, 14(iv): the supplier gives the credit [F11].*

**P2.** Trade credit for import of non-capital goods may be raised for:
(a) up to 3 years (b) **up to one year or the operating cycle, whichever is less** (c) up to 180 days only (d) any
period the lender agrees
*Reason: TC 14(v) [F13]; shipyards / shipbuilders up to 3 years.*

**P3.** Under the automatic route, the trade credit amount per import transaction for a hospital is up to:
(a) **USD 50 million** (b) USD 150 million (c) USD 20 million (d) no limit
*Reason: TC 14(iii): USD 150 million only for oil / gas refining and marketing, airline and shipping companies [F12].*

**P4.** Which is correct about advance remittance for imports at the 31 Dec 2026 cut-off?
(a) above USD 200,000 an SBLC is always required (b) advance is allowed for gold imports (c) PSUs need a Ministry
of Finance waiver above USD 100,000 (d) **the AD permits it if satisfied of the genuine need, and may set its own
threshold above which an SBLC or guarantee is required**
*Reason: R26 reg 10(3), 11 [F3]; (a) and (c) are the old Master Direction, superseded 1 Oct 2026.*

**P5.** An importer without a forward contract retires an import bill of USD 120,000. Bill selling ₹85.75, TT
selling ₹85.60 (illustrative). It pays:
(a) ₹1,02,72,000 (b) **₹1,02,90,000** (c) ₹1,02,60,000 (d) ₹1,02,96,000
*Reason: FEDAI 3.1(a): prevailing bill selling rate; 120,000 × 85.75 [F9; numericals P5]. (a) uses TT selling.*

**P6.** Main evidence of import of goods marked in IDPMS is:
(a) the commercial invoice (b) the bill of lading (c) **the bill of entry** (d) the shipping bill
*Reason: R26 reg 18; OMI C.7.1 [F8]. The shipping bill is the export document.*

**P7.** Trade credit raised for five years for import of capital goods is:
(a) still trade credit (b) **treated as an ECB** (c) not permitted at all (d) supplier's credit by definition
*Reason: TC beyond 3 years falls under ECB [F13].*
