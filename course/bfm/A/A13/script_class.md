## Chapter 1 · The rules for paying

N009 · Chapter one. From the first of October twenty twenty-six, imports follow the same new regulations you met for exports. The old Import Master Direction is gone.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · The rules for paying" · 2 C: card "FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 · in force 1 Oct 2026 · Import MD + 71 circulars superseded" [F1]

N010 · Before any payment, two checks. Does the hospital have an I-E-C? And may this machine be imported under the F-T-P?
> 1 C: card "IEC: 10 characters, same as PAN, from DGFT · FTP: free / restricted (needs an authorisation) / prohibited" [F2, A11 F3]

N011 · How can it pay? In advance, under a letter of credit, or by collection, where the bank sends the documents against payment or acceptance.
> 1 S: three lanes "advance · LC (A08) · collection: D/P, D/A"

N012 · And in which currency? To Nepal or Bhutan, in rupees. To other countries, rupees or any foreign currency.
> 1 C: card "Nepal, Bhutan → INR · others → INR or any foreign currency" [F3]

N012a · One special route. Payments to members of the Asian Clearing Union, neighbours such as Bangladesh and Sri Lanka, go through the A-C-U.
> 1 C: card "ACU members (not Nepal, Bhutan) → through the ACU · units: ACU Dollar, ACU Euro (suspended since 2016), ACU Yen" [F3, F4]

N013 · How long may the hospital take to pay? Some of you say six months from shipment. Others, whatever the contract says.
> 1 S: two buttons "6 months from shipment" · "the contract" · H: 3 s

N014 · Today, the contract. The bank follows up through I-D-P-M-S, and it may extend the time if the reasons are good.
> 1 C: card "pay within the contract period · AD may extend on reasons" [F5] · 2 G: "the contract"

N015 · Six months from shipment was the old rule. If you see it in a book, it's history now.
> 1 C: trap card "'pay within 6 months of shipment' ✗ (old Import MD; AD extended 6 months at a time, up to 3 years) · today: the contract + AD extension ✓" [F6]

N016 · Now advances. The supplier wants money before shipping. Does the bank need a guarantee from abroad?
> 1 S: money arrow leaves before the ship sails · H: 3 s

N017 · The bank first satisfies itself the advance is genuinely needed. Above an amount the bank itself sets, it can ask for a standby L-C or a guarantee.
> 1 C: card "advance: AD satisfied it's genuinely needed · above the AD's own threshold → SBLC or guarantee · same AD for the advance and later payments" [F7]

N018 · Older books give fixed numbers: two hundred thousand dollars, five million under a board policy, a hundred thousand for government bodies. Those are gone.
> 1 C: trap card "USD 200,000 · USD 5 million (Board policy) · USD 100,000 (PSU/Govt, MoF waiver) ✗ old Import MD · today: the AD's own policy ✓" [F8]

N019 · Two fixed rules stay. No advance at all for importing gold or silver.
> 1 C: card "gold, silver: no advance remittance" [F9]

N020 · And if the goods never come, the importer must bring the advance back. If it doesn't, every future advance needs a standby L-C or a guarantee from abroad.
> 1 C: card "import not made → repatriate the advance · not repatriated → future advances only against SBLC / guarantee of an international bank (or an AD's, against its counter-guarantee)" [F10]

## Chapter 3 · Trade credit

N045 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Does an import bill flip its rate on crystallisation?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Trade credit" · H: 3 s

N046 · No. Now, our two offers. Credit for an import is called trade credit. It comes in two kinds.
> 1 S: the two offer cards return

N047 · Suppliers' credit: the supplier itself waits for its money. Buyers' credit: a bank or institution abroad lends to the importer, and the supplier is paid now.
> 1 C: card "suppliers' credit = the overseas supplier gives credit · buyers' credit = an overseas bank / FI lends to the Indian importer; supplier paid at once" [F14] · 2 S: two arrows: supplier→hospital "wait 2 years"; Frankfurt bank→supplier "pays now", hospital→bank "repays later"

N048 · So our opening question. Different things, both trade credit. And not an E-C-B, as long as the credit runs no more than three years.
> 1 G: "different" lights · 2 C: card "trade credit up to 3 years = not ECB" [F15]

N049 · Who may lend? For suppliers' credit, the supplier abroad. For buyers' credit?
> 1 S: lender card, blank · H: 3 s

N050 · Banks and institutions abroad, foreign equity holders, and institutions in an I-F-S-C in India. Overseas branches of Indian banks, but only in foreign currency.
> 1 C: card "buyers' credit lenders: overseas banks, FIs, foreign equity holders, FIs in IFSC · overseas branches/subsidiaries of Indian banks: FCY TC only" [F17]

N050a · And it can be in rupees too. Rupee trade credit is allowed.
> 1 N: "INR TC allowed" [F16]

N051 · How long? Measured from the date of shipment. An M-R-I machine is a capital good. How many years?
> 1 S: calendar from "shipment" · H: 3 s

N052 · Up to three years. For non-capital goods, such as raw materials: one year or the operating cycle, whichever is less.
> 1 C: card "capital goods → up to 3 years · non-capital → 1 year or operating cycle, whichever is LESS (shipyards: 3 years) · from shipment" [F18] · 2 B: underline "whichever is less"

N053 · How much, per import? Fifty million dollars. A hundred and fifty million for oil, airline and shipping companies.
> 1 C: card "per import transaction: USD 50 m · USD 150 m for oil/gas refining & marketing, airline, shipping" [F16]

N054 · And the cost is capped. For foreign-currency credit, the benchmark plus three hundred basis points. For rupee credit, plus two fifty.
> 1 C: card "all-in-cost ceiling a year: FCY = benchmark + 300 bps · INR = benchmark + 250 bps" [F19]

N055 · Benchmark four point two per cent, illustrative. The highest the Frankfurt bank may charge?
> 1 S: sum card "4.20% + 3.00%" · H: 6 s

N056 · Seven point two per cent, all in. That cap still stands. The twenty twenty-six changes removed the cap for E-C-B, not for trade credit.
> 1 S: keys "4.2 + 3 = 7.2" [numericals Q3] · 2 C: trap card "'2026 removed the cost ceiling' → for ECB ✓ · for trade credit ✗" [F19]

N057 · One mix-up to avoid. EXIM Bank's buyer's credit lends to a foreign buyer of Indian exports. Ours lends to an Indian importer.
> 1 C: trap card "import buyers' credit (lends to the Indian importer) ≠ EXIM Bank buyer's credit (lends to an overseas buyer of Indian exports)" [Leads]

## Chapter 4 · Proof of import: IDPMS

N060 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Up to how many years is trade credit not an E-C-B?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · Proof of import: IDPMS" · H: 3 s

N061 · Three. Now, money has left India. How does the bank know the machine really arrived?
> 1 S: the import road; the money arrow out, the ship's hold a question mark · H: 3 s

N062 · The bill of entry: the importer's declaration to Customs when goods land. That's the proof of import.
> 1 C: card "bill of entry = importer's declaration to Customs (home consumption or warehousing) = evidence of import" [F24]

N063 · R-B-I's system I-D-P-M-S puts them side by side. Payments on one pan, bills of entry on the other. The bank matches them.
> 1 S: two-pan scale "outward remittances | bills of entry" · 2 C: card "IDPMS = Import Data Processing and Monitoring System (2016) · twin of EDPMS (A11)" [F25]

N063a · From an E-D-I port, Customs sends the bill of entry in by itself. From a non-E-D-I port, the bank enters it within five working days.
> 1 N: "EDI port → automatic · non-EDI → AD enters within 5 working days" [F24]

N064 · The bank debits the hospital only once it's satisfied the deal is genuine, and updates I-D-P-M-S at the same time. The entry closes once the payment is made.
> 1 C: card "debit only after genuineness check · close / update IDPMS at once · mark off after payment" [F25]

N065 · Small imports get an easy path. Bill of entry up to ten lakh rupees? The importer's own declaration closes it, or one declaration a quarter.
> 1 C: card "≤ ₹10 lakh → importer's declaration, or quarterly bulk declaration" [F26]

N066 · Imported services? An entry only for a firm, not a resident individual, and only above ten lakh.
> 1 N: "services: IDPMS entry only if not a resident individual AND > ₹10 lakh (2nd Amendment)" [F24]

N067 · Older books say: proof within three months of payment, then three months of chasing. That's the old Direction.
> 1 C: trap card "'evidence of import within 3 months, then 3 months' follow-up' ✗ old Import MD · today: IDPMS matching, AD follow-up ✓" [F28]

N068 · And merchanting: goods bought abroad and sold abroad, never landing here. Both legs within six months, and the bank closes both E-D-P-M-S and I-D-P-M-S.
> 1 C: card "merchanting: outward ↔ inward ≤ 6 months (AD may extend) · pay only the seller, receive only from the buyer · both EDPMS and IDPMS closed" [F30]
