## Chapter 1 · Paying for imports

N009 · Chapter one. As with exports, what code must the hospital have before it imports anything?
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Paying for imports" · H: 3 s

N010 · An I-E-C, from D-G-F-T. And the goods must be importable under the Foreign Trade Policy: free, or restricted with a licence.
> 1 C: card "IEC · goods: free, or restricted with authorisation · prohibited: never" [F1]

N011 · Since October twenty twenty-six, the same new regulations cover imports and exports. The old Import Master Direction is gone.
> 1 C: card "FEM (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 · old Import MD superseded 1 Oct 2026" [F1; A11-F1]

N012 · How does the money go? Through a bank. To Nepal or Bhutan, in rupees. To Asian Clearing Union members, through that union.
> 1 C: card "pay through an authorised bank · Nepal, Bhutan: INR · ACU members: ACU mechanism or as RBI directs · others: INR or any FCY" [F6]

N013 · The Asian Clearing Union lets central banks of our region net their payments, like Sri Lanka's and Bangladesh's. Germany isn't in it.
> 1 N: "ACU = regional central banks settle among themselves" [F6]

N014 · By when must the hospital pay? Older books say six months from shipment. Now: within the contract's period, and the bank can extend it.
> 1 C: trap card "6 months from shipment (old MD) ✗ · per the contract; AD may extend ✓" [F2]

N015 · The German maker wants an advance of three hundred thousand dollars first. Can the bank send it?
> 1 S: an arrow "advance USD 300,000?" from the bank toward Germany · H: 3 s

N016 · Yes, if it's satisfied the need is genuine. The bank sets its own limit, above which it wants a standby L-C or a guarantee from the seller's side.
> 1 C: card "advance for imports: AD satisfied it's genuine · AD's own threshold → above it, SBLC or guarantee" [F3] · 2 N: "SBLC = standby letter of credit: a bank's promise to repay"

N017 · Older books give fixed numbers: two hundred thousand dollars without a guarantee, five million under a board policy. Those went with the old Master Direction.
> 1 C: trap card "USD 200,000 / USD 5 million / PSU: MoF waiver above USD 100,000 (old MD) ✗ · AD's own threshold ✓" [F3] · 2 B: cross out "200,000"

## Chapter 2 · Retiring the bill

N023 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Who sets the advance limit above which a guarantee is needed?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Retiring the bill" · H: 3 s

N024 · The bank, its own threshold. Now the spares: a bill for eighty thousand dollars arrives. Paying it off is called retiring the bill.
> 1 S: a bill "spares · USD 80,000 (illustrative)" lands on the desk · 2 N: "retire = pay the import bill"

N025 · Which rate? Many of you will say T-T selling. Think about the work the bank does.

N026 · Bill selling. It handles documents, not just a transfer, so it's a little dearer. Unless the hospital booked a forward contract: then the contract rate.

## Chapter 3 · Trade credit

N037 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Pledge or hypothecation: where are the goods in a pledge?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Trade credit" · H: 3 s

N038 · With the bank. Now our opening question. Two ways to pay for the scanner later. Same, or different?
> 1 G: the two bubbles from N006

N039 · Both are trade credit: credit for imports. But who gives it decides the name.
> 1 C: card "trade credit = credit for imports of capital or non-capital goods allowed under the FTP" [F11]

N040 · The German maker giving two years to pay: that's supplier's credit. The seller himself waits for his money.
> 1 S: bubble 1 relabelled "supplier's credit: the overseas seller gives time" [F11]

N041 · The Frankfurt bank lending to the hospital to pay the maker now: that's buyer's credit. A lender pays the seller; the buyer repays the lender.
> 1 S: bubble 2 relabelled "buyer's credit: a lender abroad pays the seller; the importer repays" [F11] · 2 G: "different" button lights

N042 · Who may give buyer's credit? Banks and financial institutions abroad, a foreign equity holder, or financial institutions in an I-F-S-C, like GIFT City.
> 1 C: card "buyer's credit lenders: banks / FIs abroad · foreign equity holder · FIs in IFSCs · (foreign branches of Indian banks: FCY only)" [F11, F15]

N043 · How much? Up to fifty million dollars per import, automatically. A hundred and fifty million for oil, gas, airline and shipping companies.
> 1 C: card "per import transaction: USD 50 million · oil / gas, airlines, shipping: USD 150 million" [F12]

N044 · How long, from shipment? The scanner is a capital good. Guess.
> 1 S: a clock from "shipment" · H: 3 s

N045 · Up to three years. For non-capital goods, like raw materials: one year, or the operating cycle, whichever is less.
> 1 C: card "capital goods: up to 3 years · non-capital: 1 year or operating cycle, whichever is less" [F13] · 2 B: underline "whichever is less"

N046 · Many of you think any foreign loan for imports is an E-C-B. It isn't. Within these periods it's trade credit. Beyond three years, it's treated as an E-C-B.
> 1 C: trap card "any foreign import loan = ECB ✗ · TC up to 3 years ✓ · beyond 3 years → ECB" [F13]

N047 · The cost is capped too: benchmark rate plus three hundred basis points for foreign currency credit; plus two fifty for rupee credit.
> 1 C: card "all-in-cost ceiling: FCY benchmark + 300 bps · INR benchmark + 250 bps" [F14] · 2 N: "100 bps = 1 percentage point"

N048 · Yes, trade credit can be in rupees. It can switch from dollars to rupees, but never back.
> 1 N: "INR trade credit allowed · FCY → INR ✓ · INR → FCY ✗" [F15]

N049 · Her Indian bank may guarantee the lender, up to the credit's amount and period. But letters of comfort or undertaking for trade credit are not allowed.

## Chapter 4 · IDPMS and proof of import

N055 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. Capital goods: maximum trade credit period?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · IDPMS and proof of import" · H: 3 s

N056 · Three years. Now, how does R-B-I know the scanner really arrived? Through I-D-P-M-S, the Import Data Processing and Monitoring System.
> 1 S: a screen "IDPMS" over the import road [F7]

N057 · It's the mirror of the export system, E-D-P-M-S. Running since twenty sixteen.
> 1 N: "IDPMS: mirror of EDPMS · since 2016" [F7]

N058 · The proof is the bill of entry, the importer's declaration to Customs. From a port linked to Customs, it flows into I-D-P-M-S by itself.
> 1 C: card "evidence of import: bill of entry (number, port, date) · post: postal appraisal form · courier: courier bill of entry" [F8]

N059 · For a port without that link, the bank enters it within five working days. It records every payment, and marks off the entry once paid.
> 1 N: "non-EDI import → AD enters in 5 working days · marks off after payment" [F7]

N060 · And when the bank pays, it closes or updates the entry at the same moment. Up to rupees ten lakh a bill, the importer's declaration can close it.
> 1 C: card "payment → close / update IDPMS at once · ≤ ₹10 lakh: close on the importer's declaration (or quarterly)" [F7]

N061 · Older books say: produce proof of import within three months of an advance. The new rules leave such timelines to the bank's own policy.
> 1 C: trap card "evidence within 3 months of advance (old MD) ✗ · AD's policy and SOP ✓" [F8]
