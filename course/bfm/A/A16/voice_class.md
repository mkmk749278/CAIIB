[warmly] Hi, friends. Tonight money leaves India, and we stand at the counter that lets it go. First, homework. The standard policy paid ninety per cent of a forty thousand dollar loss. Ten thousand came back. How much to E-C-G-C? Nine thousand dollars. Recoveries are shared in the ratio of the loss: ninety-ten. And from two classes back. A bank's net funded exposure to a C-two country is above one per cent of its assets. Provision? One hundred per cent. Good. Today, no exporters. A family. Mrs Lakshmi, a teacher in Coimbatore. Her son studies in the U-S. She walks into her bank's forex counter. She wants to send ten lakh rupees: six for his fees, three for his rent, one as a gift for his friend's wedding. Some of you say: fine, all of it. Others: not the gift, he's not family. Keep your answer. L-R-S came up in every one of the last seven exams. The limit, the gift, the T-C-S chart. Stay with me. Chapter one. FEMA, the foreign exchange law, sorts every payment abroad into two lanes. Current account, and capital account. Capital account: it changes a resident's assets or liabilities outside India, or a non-resident's in India. Like buying shares abroad. Or opening a bank account abroad. Both leave her owning something outside India. Current account: everything else. Fees, travel, medical bills, gifts, interest. One quick test: does it leave her owning or owing something abroad? Yes, capital. No, current. Your turn. Four quick ones: her son's fees. Shares of a U-S company. The gift to his friend. An account in a U-S bank. Fees: current. Shares: capital. Gift: current. Bank account abroad: capital. Who rules each lane? Current account is free, except where the Central Government's rules say no. Capital account: only what the rules allow. Those rules have three lists, called schedules. Schedule one, schedule two, schedule three. Guess: which one is the banned list? Schedule one. Many of you thought one means permitted. No: one is prohibited. On it: lottery winnings, lottery tickets, racing income, and call-back phone services. Schedule two needs a Government ministry's approval. Like sports prize money above a hundred thousand dollars. And schedule three is for individuals, within a yearly limit you'll meet in chapter two. Separately, the rules allow no foreign exchange at all for travel to Nepal or Bhutan. Pause check for chapter one. One. Buying a flat in Dubai: current or capital? Capital: an asset abroad. Two, the twist. Paying abroad for lottery tickets: which schedule? Schedule one: prohibited. [seriously] One thing from chapter one: one bans, two needs a ministry, three is for individuals. Chapter two. First, a recall. Is a gift to a friend abroad current or capital? Current. Now, the scheme itself. The Liberalised Remittance Scheme, L-R-S. How much can one person send in a year? Two lakh fifty thousand dollars per financial year, April to March. Per person, not per transaction. One jar for both lanes. Current and capital purposes share the same two lakh fifty thousand. Who can use it? Resident individuals, minors too. For a minor, the natural guardian countersigns the form. Not companies, partnership firms, H-U-Fs or trusts. A sole proprietor sending from his firm's account? That's his own jar. What can she send it for? Almost any current purpose: travel, studies, medical treatment, gifts, and support for relatives abroad. Now our opening. Can she gift her son's friend a lakh of rupees? Yes. A gift under L-R-S can go to any person abroad. The relative rule is for maintenance, not for gifts. One gift that can't go. Her sister in Chennai holds a U-S account. Mrs Lakshmi can't gift dollars into it. Many of you think: money I bring back frees up the limit again. Does it? No. What's sent is counted. Bring it back, and the jar stays used for that year. What's barred even inside the limit? Margin money or margin calls to exchanges abroad, forex trading abroad, and Schedule one items. And above two lakh fifty thousand? Three cases go higher, without R-B-I: medical treatment, studies, and emigration. The bank releases on an estimate: from the hospital, or the university. Falls sick while abroad? The bank can still release. Any other purpose above the limit needs R-B-I. Credit card spends while she visits him abroad? They don't count against her jar. Her debit or forex card does. Pause check for chapter two. One. The L-R-S limit, per person per year? Two lakh fifty thousand dollars. Two, the twist. Surgery abroad estimated at three lakh dollars. Can her bank release it without R-B-I? Yes, on the hospital's estimate. Medical is one of the three. [seriously] One thing from chapter two: one jar a year, per person; refills don't count. Chapter three. First, a recall. Can a partnership firm use L-R-S? No. Only individuals. Now, at the counter. What does the bank ask Mrs Lakshmi for first? Her PAN. Every L-R-S remittance needs PAN, whatever the amount. Old books say: no PAN up to twenty-five thousand dollars. That was before twenty eighteen. Today, always. Then Form A-two: purpose, a declaration that the money is hers, and not for a banned purpose. Paper or online. She uses one designated branch for her L-R-S remittances. For capital ones, like shares, the bank wants a year's relationship. Can her bank lend her the money to send? It depends on the lane. For capital uses, like U-S shares, no bank credit at all. For current ones, like fees, a loan is fine. And family? Each member has a jar. They can club them, add jars together: Mrs Lakshmi and her husband, for his fees. For a capital use, like a joint account abroad, only co-owners can club. Pause check for chapter three. One. A remittance of five hundred dollars: is PAN needed? Yes: every L-R-S remittance. Two, the twist. Her bank offers a loan for her to buy U-S shares under L-R-S. Allowed? No. No credit for capital account remittances. [seriously] One thing from chapter three: PAN, Form A-two, and no loans for investing abroad. Chapter four. First, a recall. Money brought back: does it refill the jar? No. Now a second law: the income-tax law. When she sends money, the bank may collect tax at source, T-C-S. Many of you think T-C-S is a fee she loses. It isn't. It's an advance on her income tax; it comes back as credit. The meter starts only after ten lakh rupees in the year. That's the tax law's line, not FEMA's dollar limit. All her remittances in the year add up, from April, whatever the purpose. The one that crosses the line pays its own rate above it. Above ten lakh, the rate depends on purpose. For education or medical treatment, two per cent. For everything else, gifts, travel, investment, twenty per cent. And if the fees come from a bank education loan? Nil. And this T-C-S is only on L-R-S, so only on individuals. A company paying for imports? Not this T-C-S. A tour package bought from a tour operator is separate: two per cent from the first rupee, collected by the tour operator. Careful with dates. In twenty twenty-five to twenty-six, education was five per cent above ten lakh. Until March twenty twenty-five, the line was seven lakh. So answer by the year the question names. If none, use today's rates. Back to Mrs Lakshmi. Ten lakh in total: six fees, three rent, one gift. T-C-S? Nil. Ten lakh doesn't exceed ten lakh. The meter hasn't started. Now suppose instead she sends fifteen lakh this year, all for fees, from her savings. T-C-S? Only the five lakh above the line is taxed, at two per cent: ten thousand rupees. Your turn. Same fifteen lakh, but as a gift to her son. T-C-S? One lakh rupees. Five lakh above the line, at twenty per cent. Did you get three lakh? That's twenty per cent of the full fifteen. The first ten lakh is free. Pause check for chapter four. One. T-C-S on fees paid from a bank education loan? Nil. Two, the twist. Is the ten lakh rupee line the same as the L-R-S limit? No. Ten lakh is the tax line; the dollar limit is FEMA's. [seriously] One thing from chapter four: T-C-S is tax credit, not a loss. Back to our opening. Can she send all ten lakh, gift included? Yes, all of it. Gifts can go to anyone abroad, within her jar. And at ten lakh, no T-C-S. Quick mix. One. Buying shares abroad: which lane? Capital. Two. Schedule two: what's needed? A ministry's approval. Three. Her only remittance this year: fifteen lakh for medical treatment, own funds. T-C-S? Ten thousand: two per cent of five lakh. Now the map, from memory. First node: two lanes. Capital changes assets abroad, current is the rest; schedule one bans, two needs a ministry, three is ours. Second: L-R-S. Two lakh fifty thousand dollars a year, individuals only; refills don't count; medical, studies, emigration can go higher. Third: counter rules. PAN always, Form A-two, one branch; no credit for capital uses; family can club. Fourth: T-C-S. Above ten lakh; two per cent education and medical, twenty the rest, nil on a loan; credit, not cost. That's the map. Homework. This year she sends six lakh for fees in May, then an eight lakh gift in August. T-C-S on the gift? Add up first. Next class, her son wants to buy shares abroad, and borrow from his mother. What does FEMA say? [warmly] That's money out of India, by the rules. Switch off, rest up, and I'll see you in the next class.
