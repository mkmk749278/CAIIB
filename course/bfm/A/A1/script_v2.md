# A1 v2 · "Which side of the counter?" (video script)

Replaces `script.md` as the master for the video. Built on PLAYBOOK §5a: misconception first, dialogue, three short
chapters, a prediction pause before every reveal, worked → faded → own-try sums, concrete → picture → formula.

- **Voices**: NARRATOR (calm, warm Indian English, male or female) and MEERA (a young forex officer, a fictional
  character, lively; she voices the answers most candidates give). On screen at the start: "Meera is a fictional
  character. Both voices are AI-generated."
- **Rule version**: FEDAI Rules (10th edition) as in force on 30 June 2026. Rates, names and places illustrative.
- **Numbers**: `numericals.md` (checked by `tools/check/fx_merchant_rates.py`).
- **Length**: about 11 minutes in three chapters (3:30 · 3:45 · 3:30) plus pauses.
- Line ids (N01, M01…) match the voice blocks in `voice_blocks.md`; the video is timed to them.

## Chapter 1 · Which side of the counter? (0:35–4:00)

**[Chapter card: 1 · Which side of the counter?]**

**[Meera at the counter, a speech bubble.]**

M01 · Easy. The father is buying dollars, so he gets the buying rate. The exporter is selling dollars, so he gets
the selling rate.

N04 · That is the answer most people give. It sounds right. And it is exactly backwards.

**[Camera flips 180 degrees over the counter, landing on the bank's side. The flip whooshes; the board reverses
colour.]**

N05 · Here is why. The rate on that board is not the customer's price. It is the bank's price. So we must stand on
the bank's side of the counter.

**[Top view. A line down the middle. Arrows into the bank glow green, arrows out glow red.]**

N06 · When dollars come into the bank, the bank is buying them. It pays you rupees.

N07 · When dollars go out of the bank, the bank is selling them. It takes your rupees.

**[Picture: a vegetable vendor buying tomatoes from a farmer at 20 and selling at 30. Then fade back to the
counter.]**

N08 · Like any shopkeeper, the bank buys cheap and sells dear. The gap pays its salaries.

N09 · So the bank's buying rate is always the lower rupee number, and its selling rate is always the higher one.

**[The father's dollars leave the bank: red arrow out.]**

N10 · Now the father. His dollars leave the bank and go to America. The bank sells. He pays the higher, selling side.

**[The exporter's bill comes in: green arrow in.]**

N11 · The exporter brings dollars in through his bill. The bank buys. He gets the lower, buying side.

M02 · Oh. I was standing on the wrong side of my own counter.

N12 · Everyone does at first. One rule fixes it for good: name every deal from the bank's side.

**[Pause card: "Quick check" · "An NRI son sends 2,500 dollars home to his mother. Bank buys or sells?" · 5-second
timer.]**

N13 · Quick check. A son abroad sends two thousand five hundred dollars home to his mother. Does the bank buy or sell?

N14 · Dollars come in. The bank buys. Green.

## Chapter 2 · The rate ladder (4:00–7:45)

**[Chapter card: 2 · The rate ladder]**

M03 · But there are four prices on the board, not two. Which buying rate? Which selling rate?

N15 · For that we need a second picture. The rate ladder.

**[A wholesale screen: USD/INR 85.40 / 85.42. Text: "interbank: banks trading with banks".]**

N16 · First, where the prices come from. Banks trade dollars with each other all day. Right now the market says
eighty-five forty, eighty-five forty-two.

N17 · The lower number is the bid: what a buyer will pay. The higher one is the ask, or offer: what a seller wants.

N18 · And notice how the price is written: so many rupees for one dollar. That is a direct quote, the way FEDAI's
rules say a bank must quote its customers.

**[The quote becomes the middle rung of a tall vertical ladder.]**

N19 · That market price sits in the middle of our ladder. The counter is retail, so the bank adds its own margin.
Selling rates climb up from the ask. Buying rates step down from the bid.

N20 · How big a margin? FEDAI leaves that to each bank. In your exam, the question always gives it.

**[First rung above and below light up: TT selling, TT buying. A paper plane icon: "TT, money by message, now".]**

N21 · The rungs nearest the middle are the TT rates. TT means telegraphic transfer: money that moves by message,
straight away.

N22 · TT buying is for dollars already sitting in the bank's account abroad. TT selling is for dollars the bank sends
out at once.

**[Outer rungs light up: bill buying below, bill selling above. A clock icon and a document icon.]**

N23 · The outer rungs are the bill rates. With bill buying, the bank pays the exporter today, but the dollars arrive
weeks later. It waits, and it carries the risk. So it buys lower.

N24 · With bill selling, the bank pays a foreign supplier against import documents and handles the paperwork. So it
sells higher.

N25 · Read the ladder from the top. Bill selling, TT selling, the market, TT buying, bill buying. Bills outside. TTs
inside.

**[Data card: interbank 85.40 / 85.42. Margins: TT buying 0.05%, bill buying 0.15%, TT selling 0.10%, bill selling a
further 0.15% on TT selling. ILLUSTRATIVE. A basic calculator slides in.]**

N26 · Let us build the board ourselves. Here are the bank's margins.

N27 · Selling rates go up from the ask. TT selling: eighty-five forty-two, times point one, percent. That is point
zero eight five four two. Add it. Eighty-five point five zero five four.

N28 · Buying rates go down from the bid. TT buying: eighty-five forty, times point zero five, percent. Point zero four
two seven. Take it away. Eighty-five point three five seven three.

**[Faded example: the bill buying line with blanks. Pause card, 8-second timer.]**

N29 · Now you do one. Bill buying: eighty-five forty, less point one five percent. Pause and press the keys.

N30 · The margin is point one two eight one. The rate: eighty-five point two seven one nine.

**[Own try: the bill selling line, no hints. Pause card, 10-second timer.]**

N31 · Last one, all yours. Bill selling is the TT selling rate, plus a further point one five percent.

N32 · Eighty-five point six three three seven. Look: those are the four prices from the opening board.

## Chapter 3 · Five deals, one map (7:45–11:15)

**[Chapter card: 3 · Five deals, one map]**

N33 · Now we put each deal on its rung. FEDAI's rules say which one. You guess first, every time.

**[Rapid round. Each deal card appears with a 4-second timer, then snaps onto its rung with a green or red tag and the
FEDAI rule number.]**

N34 · A remittance arrives from abroad and is paid out in rupees.

N35 · TT buying. Dollars came in, already in the bank's account abroad.

N36 · The father's fee goes out by wire.

N37 · TT selling.

N38 · An exporter's bill is bought by the bank on the day he brings it.

N39 · Bill buying. The bank pays now and waits for the dollars.

N40 · An export bill is sent on collection, and the bank pays only after the dollars reach its account abroad.

N41 · TT buying. Nothing to wait for any more.

N42 · An importer pays for his goods, with no forward contract.

N43 · Bill selling.

**[Back at the counter. Meera frowns at a returned bill.]**

M04 · This export bill came back unpaid. The dollars were coming in, so I convert it at TT buying, right?

**[Pause card: "Meera's right?" 5-second timer.]**

N44 · Careful. That is the trap the exam loves.

**[The camera flips over the counter again, now in amber.]**

N45 · When the bill is not paid, the bank turns the exporter's dollar debt into a rupee debt. That is called
crystallisation.

N46 · The exporter now owes the bank dollars. It is as if the bank sells him those dollars. So it is TT selling, not
TT buying.

N47 · The same flip happens when a forward contract is cancelled. A purchase contract is cancelled at TT selling. A
sale contract at TT buying. We will work those sums in lesson A4.

M05 · So reversing a deal flips me to the other side of the counter.

N48 · Exactly. Amber always flips the side.

**[Back to the opening scene: the father and the exporter.]**

N49 · So, the opening question. The father's eighteen thousand dollars go out at TT selling: fifteen lakh thirty-nine
thousand and ninety-seven rupees, twenty paise.

N50 · The exporter's forty thousand dollar bill is bought at bill buying: thirty-four lakh ten thousand eight hundred
and seventy-six rupees, before the transit interest we meet in lesson A5.

**[Three questions, one after another, 6-second timers; answers with one-line reasons.]**

N51 · Three to lock it in. One: an importer retires a bill with no forward cover. Which rate?

N52 · Bill selling.

N53 · Two: put these in order, highest first. TT buying, bill selling, bill buying, TT selling.

N54 · Bill selling, TT selling, TT buying, bill buying.

N55 · Three: a purchase contract is cancelled on its due date. Which rate?

N56 · TT selling. Amber flips the side.

**[The Module A mind map: the 12 o'clock branch "Rates" grows its nodes as the narrator names them.]**

N57 · One counter. Stand on the bank's side. In is buying, green and lower. Out is selling, red and higher. TTs sit
near the market, bills sit outside. And reversing a deal flips the side.

M06 · Next time, I'm on the right side of the counter.

N58 · Next lesson, we take this ladder into real exam sums.
