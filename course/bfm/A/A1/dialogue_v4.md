## part_3.mp3 · Chapter 2 · The rate ladder (3 turns, 2285 chars)

**1. Meera** (M03–M03)
```
But wait. There were four prices on the board, not two. So which buying rate? And which selling rate?
```

**2. Teacher** (N16–N21)
```
Very good question, Meera. That's exactly where we're going. For that, I want to show you a second picture. We call it the rate ladder. First, where do these prices even come from? Banks are trading dollars with each other the whole day. That's the interbank market. And right now, the market says eighty-five point four zero, and eighty-five point four two. The lower number is called the bid. That's what a buyer is willing to pay. The higher number is the ask, some people call it the offer. That's what a seller wants. Bid lower, ask higher. Also notice how the price is written. So many rupees for one dollar. Or for a currency like the yen, so many rupees for a hundred units. That's called a direct quote. And FEDAI's rules say this is how a bank must quote its customers. Now, this market price goes right in the middle of our ladder. But the counter is retail, right? So the bank adds its own margin on top. That's its earning. So the selling rates climb up from the ask. And the buying rates come down from the bid. Up for selling, down for buying.
```

**3. Teacher** (N22–N27)
```
And how big is that margin? Each bank decides its own. Don't try to memorise any number. In the exam, if a sum needs a margin, the question will give it to you. Everyone with me so far? Good. Now, which rung do we use for which deal? For this, I want you to watch two clocks. On the left, the rupees moving at our counter. On the right, the dollars moving in the bank's account abroad. That account is called the Nostro account. When both clocks move together, at the same time, we use the rungs closest to the middle. These are the TT rates. TT buying, when the dollars have already arrived. TT selling, when the dollars go out right away. But sometimes the bank pays the rupees first, then waits for the dollars, and also has to handle documents. In that case we move to the outer rungs. These are the bill rates. Take bill buying. The bank pays the exporter today, handles his documents, but the money in its account abroad comes only after some weeks. The bank is waiting, it's taking a risk. So it buys at a lower rate. And the interest for that waiting period? That's charged separately. We'll see that in lesson A-five.
```
