# A1 v3 · "Which side of the counter?" (classroom voice)

**Why v3.** The owner watched the r1 cut on 2026-11-14: "the narration feels like a documentary or story narration.
It's not like a class. A class needs to be more conversational, in the most general speaking way. It should not feel
boring at all." v2 was written as voice-over: short dramatic fragments ("Same counter. Same dollar. Same morning."),
a narrator who describes rather than talks *to* you. v3 keeps every fact, number, picture and line id of v2 (so the
Remotion timing still works) and rewrites only the words, as a teacher talking to one officer on the other side of
a table. Style rules: `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` §5b.

- **Voices**: TEACHER (the old NARRATOR track; friendly, relaxed Indian English, AI-generated, no name and no
  back-story) and MEERA (a new forex officer sitting in the class, fictional). On screen at the start: "Meera is a
  fictional character. Both voices are AI-generated." The teacher says "I" and "you", like any explainer, but never
  claims to be a person, a banker or a "Sir".
- **Rule version, numbers, colour rules, visuals**: unchanged from `script_v2.md` (FEDAI Rules as at 30 June 2026;
  `numericals.md`). Picture directions are in v2; only the spoken words are here.
- **Length**: about 13–14 minutes (more words than v2, spoken faster and lighter).

## Opening

N01 · Okay, let's start with a small picture. A forex counter, Monday morning, a busy branch in Visakhapatnam. Look
at the board up there. One currency, the US dollar, and four different prices. Four! Why four? That's what we're
going to sort out today.

N02 · Now, two customers walk in. First, a father. He has to send eighteen thousand dollars for his daughter's
university fee. Second, a shrimp exporter. He's got a forty thousand dollar export bill, and he wants his rupees
today.

N03 · And here's why this matters. If you, sitting at that counter, pick the wrong price for the father, the branch
loses more than two thousand rupees. On just one fee. So it's not a small thing.

N04 · So let me ask you. The father, does he get the higher price or the lower one? Think about it for a second. Don't
tell me yet. Meera's in the class today, she's new on the forex desk, let's hear her first.

## Chapter 1 · Which side of the counter?

M01 · That's easy! The father is buying dollars, so he gets the buying rate. The exporter is selling dollars, so he
gets the selling rate.

N05 · Hmm. Good try, Meera, and honestly, that's what most people say. It sounds perfectly logical. But it's exactly
backwards.

N06 · See, here's the thing. The rate on that board isn't the customer's price. It's the bank's price. The bank is
the one buying and selling. So whenever you read a forex rate, come around and stand on the bank's side of the
counter. Always.

N07 · From there it's simple. If dollars are coming into the bank, the bank is buying them. And it pays you rupees.

N08 · If dollars are going out of the bank, the bank is selling them. And it takes your rupees. That's it. In means
buy, out means sell.

N09 · Think of the money-changer at the airport. He buys cheap and sells dear, right? Same with the bank. The only
difference is, at the bank the gap is a few paise, not a few rupees. But those paise add up. They pay the salaries!

N10 · So, in a direct quote, remember this: the bank's buying rate is always the lower rupee number, and its
selling rate is always the higher one. Buy low, sell high. Just like any shopkeeper.

N11 · Now let's go back to the father. His dollars are leaving the bank, going to his daughter's university. Going
out. So the bank is selling. Which means he pays the higher rate, the selling rate.

N12 · And the exporter? His bill brings dollars in. Coming in, so the bank is buying. He gets the lower rate, the
buying rate. You see how it flips once you stand on the bank's side?

M02 · Oh! So I was standing on the wrong side of my own counter.

N13 · Don't worry, everybody does that at first. Just keep one rule in your head and you'll never get it wrong again:
name every deal from the bank's side.

N14 · Let's check it quickly. A son working abroad sends two thousand five hundred dollars home to his mother. Is the
bank buying or selling? Go on, answer it.

N15 · Dollars are coming in, so the bank is buying. Got it? Good.

## Chapter 2 · The rate ladder

M03 · But wait. There were four prices on the board, not two. So which buying rate? And which selling rate?

N16 · Very good question, Meera. And for that, I want to show you a second picture. We call it the rate ladder.

N17 · First, where do these prices even come from? Banks are trading dollars with each other the whole day. That's
the interbank market. And right now, the market says eighty-five point four zero, and eighty-five point four two.

N18 · The lower number is called the bid. That's what a buyer is willing to pay. The higher number is the ask, some
people call it the offer. That's what a seller wants. Bid lower, ask higher.

N19 · Also notice how the price is written. So many rupees for one dollar. Or for a currency like the yen, so many
rupees for a hundred units. That's called a direct quote. And FEDAI's rules say this is how a bank must quote its
customers.

N20 · Now, this market price goes right in the middle of our ladder. But the counter is retail, right? So the bank
adds its own margin on top. That's its earning.

N21 · So the selling rates climb up from the ask. And the buying rates come down from the bid. Up for selling, down
for buying.

N22 · And how big is that margin? Each bank decides its own. Don't try to memorise any number. In the exam, if a sum
needs a margin, the question will give it to you.

N23 · Now, which rung do we use for which deal? For this, I want you to watch two clocks. On the left, the rupees
moving at our counter. On the right, the dollars moving in the bank's account abroad. That account is called the
Nostro account.

N24 · When both clocks move together, at the same time, we use the rungs closest to the middle. These are the TT
rates. TT buying, when the dollars have already arrived. TT selling, when the dollars go out right away.

N25 · But sometimes the bank pays the rupees first, then waits for the dollars, and also has to handle documents.
In that case we move to the outer rungs. These are the bill rates.

N26 · Take bill buying. The bank pays the exporter today, handles his documents, but the money in its account abroad
comes only after some weeks. The bank is waiting, it's taking a risk. So it buys at a lower rate.

N27 · And the interest for that waiting period? That's charged separately. We'll see that in lesson A-five.

N28 · Bill selling is the other way round. The bank pays the foreign supplier against the import documents, often
before the importer has paid the bank. So the bank sells at a higher rate.

N29 · So let's read the ladder from the top, once, slowly. Bill selling. TT selling. Then the market in the middle.
TT buying. Bill buying. Easy way to remember: bills on the outside, TTs on the inside.

N30 · And just so you know, beyond the bill rates there's currency notes, with the widest gap of all. And in exam
sums, bill rates often have a forward premium added too. Don't worry about these now, we'll come to them later.

N31 · Okay, now let's build the board ourselves. Take out your calculator. These are the bank's margins on the
screen. And one small habit I want you to pick up: use a multiplier, not the percent key. It's cleaner.

N32 · Selling rates go up from the ask. So TT selling is eighty-five point four two, into one point zero zero one,
equals...

N33 · Eighty-five point five zero five four two. Round it to four decimals, and you get eighty-five point five zero
five four. Simple.

N34 · Buying rates come down from the bid. So TT buying is eighty-five point four zero, into point nine nine nine
five, equals... eighty-five point three five seven three.

N35 · Now it's your turn. Bill buying is the bid, less point one five percent. Pause the video, press the keys, and
then come back.

N36 · Done? Eighty-five point four zero, into point nine nine eight five. You should get eighty-five point two seven
one nine. Did you? Great.

N37 · One more, and this time you're on your own. Bill selling is another point one five percent, on top of the TT
selling rate. Pause and try it.

N38 · Okay. Eighty-five point five zero five four two, into one point zero zero one five. Answer, eighty-five point
six three three seven.

N39 · Now be careful here, this is where people slip. That margin goes on the TT selling rate, not on the market ask.
If you just add both margins to the ask, you get eighty-five point six three three six. Only the last digit is off,
but in the exam, that's the wrong option.

N40 · And look at that, these are the same four prices we saw on the board at the start. One small tip: if the
question tells you how to round, just follow what it says.

## Chapter 3 · Five deals, one map

N41 · Now, let's put each deal on its rung. FEDAI's rules tell us which one. But I'll give you the deal first, and you
guess before I tell you. Every time, okay?

N42 · First one. A remittance comes in from abroad and is paid out in rupees.

N43 · TT buying. Because the dollars are already sitting in the Nostro.

N44 · Next. The father's fee goes out by wire.

N45 · TT selling. Easy one.

N46 · An exporter's bill is bought by the bank on the same day he brings it.

N47 · Bill buying. The bank pays now and waits for the dollars.

N48 · Now this one's a little tricky. An export bill is sent on collection. The bank pays only after the dollars reach
its Nostro.

N49 · TT buying! Because by then, the money has arrived. There's nothing to wait for.

N50 · Last one. An importer retires his import bill, and there's no forward contract.

N51 · Bill selling.

N52 · And one thing that overrides all of these. If the customer has a forward contract, then the contract rate
applies, not the board rate. Keep that in mind.

M04 · Okay, I have one. This export bill came back unpaid. We bought it at bill buying, so now I reverse it at bill
selling, right?

N53 · Ah, close, but no. And this is exactly the trap the exam loves. Let's see why.

N54 · Think about it. The bank has already paid this exporter in rupees. Now the dollars are never coming. So he owes
the bank those dollars. What does the bank do? It turns that dollar debt into a rupee debt.

N55 · So it's like the bank is selling him those dollars. But no documents are going anywhere, it's just a plain
conversion. And that's why it's TT selling, not bill selling.

N56 · When a bill isn't paid on its due date and the bank converts it like this, we call it crystallisation. When
exactly? That's set by the bank's own policy. Older books say the thirtieth day, but today's rule leaves it to the
bank.

N57 · And if the dollars do come in later, the bank converts them at TT buying.

N58 · And the same flip happens when a forward contract is cancelled. A purchase contract is cancelled at TT selling,
a sale contract at TT buying. We'll do those sums in lesson A-four.

M05 · So whenever we undo a deal, I flip to the other side of the counter. Always?

N59 · Good question. Let's test it. An unpaid import bill gets crystallised. Does the side flip? What do you think?

M06 · Hmm... no. Nothing's being undone there. The importer is still buying dollars from the bank. So it's bill
selling, same as retirement.

N60 · Perfect, Meera! That's exactly right. FEDAI's rule three point one says the same rate as retirement. Only when
you undo a deal the bank has already made does the side flip.

N61 · So, let's go back to where we started. The father's eighteen thousand dollars go out at TT selling. That's
fifteen lakh thirty-nine thousand and ninety-seven rupees, twenty paise.

N62 · And the exporter's forty thousand dollar bill is bought at bill buying. That's thirty-four lakh ten thousand
eight hundred and seventy-six rupees, before interest for the transit period.

N63 · Okay, two quick questions before we finish. One. An importer sends an advance payment for his goods by wire.
Which rate?

N64 · TT selling. Because it's a remittance going out, it's not an import bill.

N65 · Two. Put these in order, highest first. TT buying, bill selling, bill buying, TT selling.

N66 · Bill selling, TT selling, TT buying, bill buying. Did you get both? Very good.

N67 · So here's the whole lesson on one card. Step one: is the money coming in or going out?

N68 · Step two: do the rupees and the Nostro move together, or does the bank pay first and handle documents? That
tells you the rung.

N69 · And remember: if you're undoing a deal the bank has already made, the side flips.

M07 · Got it. Next time, I'm on the right side of the counter!

N70 · That's it for today. In the next lesson, we'll take this ladder into real exam sums. See you there.
