## Chapter 1 · Which side of the counter?
N006 · Chapter one. Many of you are thinking: the father is buying dollars, so he gets the buying rate. The lower one.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Which side of the counter?" · 2 N: thought bubble "father buys → buying rate?"

N007 · It sounds logical. But it's wrong. And here's why.
> 1 B: strike through the thought bubble

N008 · The rate on that board is not the customer's price. It is the bank's price. Buying and selling are always from the bank's side.
> 1 S: rule card "the rate = the BANK's price" [F1] · 2 B: box "BANK's"

N009 · So ask one thing only. Is the foreign currency coming into the bank, or going out?
> 1 S: the counter from above; an arrow in (green), an arrow out (red) [F2]

N010 · Coming in, the bank buys it. Going out, the bank sells it. In means buy. Out means sell.
> 1 N: "IN → bank BUYS" (green) · 2 N: "OUT → bank SELLS" (red) · 3 B: underline both

N011 · Now, which number is which? Think of a money-changer. He buys cheap and sells dear. The bank is the same.
> 1 S: a money-changer's board, "buy low · sell high"

N012 · India quotes rates directly. So many rupees for one dollar. So the bank's buying rate is the lower number. Its selling rate is the higher one.
> 1 S: card "direct quote: ₹ per 1 unit (or per 100 units)" [F4] · 2 N: "buying = lower ₹ · selling = higher ₹" [F3]

N013 · Back to the father. His dollars are leaving the bank, going to the university. Out. So the bank sells. He pays the higher rate.
> 1 S: father's arrow goes OUT, red · 2 G: the top rates on the board

N014 · And the exporter? His bill brings dollars in. In. So the bank buys. He gets the lower rate.
> 1 S: exporter's arrow comes IN, green · 2 G: the bottom rates on the board

N015 · If you said "buying rate" for the father, don't worry. You were just standing on the customer's side of the counter. Come round to the bank's side, and it's easy.
> 1 S: a small figure walks round the counter to the bank's side

N016 · Pause check. Three quick ones. Answer each before I do. One. A son abroad sends two thousand five hundred dollars home to his mother. Bank buying, or selling?
> 1 S: card "son abroad → mother · $2,500" · H: 3 s

N017 · Buying. The dollars come in. Two. A student pays her foreign college fee by wire. Buying or selling?
> 1 N: "IN → buys ✓" · 2 S: card "student → college abroad" · H: 3 s

N018 · Selling. The dollars go out. Three, the twist. An importer wants dollars, so many of you think "importer buys". Which side is the bank on?
> 1 N: "OUT → sells ✓" · 2 S: card "importer pays supplier abroad" · H: 3 s

N019 · Selling. The importer's dollars go out of the bank. Always the bank's side, never the customer's.
> 1 N: "OUT → sells ✓" · 2 B: box "bank's side"

N020 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: in means the bank buys, out means the bank sells. And buying is always the lower number.
> 1 S: the chapter card fills: "IN = buy (lower) · OUT = sell (higher)"; finished parts dim to grey


## Chapter 2 · The rate ladder
N021 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. An exporter's dollars come in. Is the bank buying or selling? And is that the lower number, or the higher?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The rate ladder" · 2 S: exporter card, arrow IN · H: 3 s

N022 · Buying, and the lower number. Good. Now, the board had four rates, not two. So which buying rate? That's this chapter.
> 1 N: "IN → buys → lower ✓" · 2 G: the four board rates

N023 · Start with where prices come from. Banks deal dollars with each other all day. That's the interbank market. Today, say, eighty-five forty, eighty-five forty-two.
> 1 S: two bank towers trading; quote card "USD/INR 85.40 / 85.42 (illustrative)" [F6]

N024 · The lower number is the bid. The price at which the quoting bank buys. The higher number is the ask, or offer. The price at which it sells.
> 1 N: under 85.40 "bid = bank buys" · 2 N: under 85.42 "ask / offer = bank sells" [F7] · 3 B: underline both words

N025 · The gap between them is the spread. Here, just two paise.
> 1 B: a bracket between the two numbers, label "spread 0.02" [F7]

N026 · Now, your counter is retail. So the bank adds its margin. Selling rates go up from the ask. Buying rates come down from the bid.
> 1 S: the ladder draws: middle rung "interbank 85.40 / 85.42"; arrows up "selling", down "buying" [F10]

N027 · How big is the margin? Each bank decides its own. FEDAI doesn't fix it. In an exam sum, the margin is always given.
> 1 S: card "margins: the bank's own; FEDAI fixes none" [F8] · 2 B: box "given in the sum"

N028 · Now, which rung for which deal? Watch two clocks. The rupees at your counter. And the dollars in the bank's account abroad, the Nostro.
> 1 S: two clocks side by side: "₹ at the counter" · "$ in the Nostro"

N029 · When both move at the same time, we use the rungs nearest the market. The T-T rates. T-T buying, just below the bid. T-T selling, just above the ask.
> 1 S: both clocks tick together · 2 S: the ladder adds "TT selling" above, "TT buying" below the middle [F11]

N030 · When the bank pays first and waits, or handles documents, it goes one rung further out. The bill rates.
> 1 S: the rupee clock runs ahead of the dollar clock · 2 S: ladder adds "bill selling" on top, "bill buying" at the bottom [F11]

N031 · Bill buying: the bank pays the exporter today, but his dollars reach the Nostro weeks later. Bill selling: the bank handles the importer's documents. The interest for any wait is charged separately. That's lesson A-twelve.
> 1 S: exporter paid now, dollar clock "weeks later" · 2 S: an import document folder · 3 N: "interest for the wait: separate (A12)" [F11]

N032 · So the ladder, top to bottom. Bill selling. T-T selling. The market. T-T buying. Bill buying. Say it with me once.
> 1 S: the full ladder, rungs lighting top to bottom [F10] · H: 2 s

N033 · Quick one. Which rate is the very lowest on the board?
> 1 B: circle the bottom rung, blank · H: 3 s

N034 · Bill buying. The bank pays first and waits the longest, so it pays the least.
> 1 N: bottom rung fills "bill buying" ✓

N035 · Now let's build the board ourselves. Calculators out. Margins on the screen: T-T buying point zero five percent. Bill buying point one five percent. T-T selling point one zero percent. Bill selling, another point one five percent, on top of T-T selling.
> 1 S: margin card "TT buy 0.05% · bill buy 0.15% · TT sell 0.10% · bill sell +0.15% on TT sell" [F10] · Music bed out

N036 · T-T selling. The ask, plus point one percent. Press eighty-five point four two, times one point zero zero one, equals.
> 1 S: calculator keys light: 85.42 × 1.001 = · 2 N: display "85.50542"

N037 · Eighty-five point five zero five four two. Rounded to four decimals, eighty-five point five zero five four.
> 1 B: box "TT selling 85.5054"

N038 · T-T buying. The bid, less point zero five percent. Eighty-five point four zero, times point nine nine nine five, equals. Eighty-five point three five seven three.
> 1 S: keys: 85.40 × 0.9995 = · 2 B: box "TT buying 85.3573"

N039 · Your turn. Bill buying is the bid, less point one five percent. Pause, press the keys, then come back.
> 1 S: blank box "bill buying = ?" · H: 8 s

N040 · Eighty-five point four zero, times point nine nine eight five. Eighty-five point two seven one nine.
> 1 S: keys: 85.40 × 0.9985 = · 2 B: box "bill buying 85.2719"

N041 · Last one, and here people slip. Bill selling. Many of you will add the margin to the ask. But it goes on top of T-T selling.
> 1 C: trap card "bill selling = ask + margin ✗ · TT selling + margin ✓" [F10] · H: 3 s

N042 · So, eighty-five point five zero five four two, times one point zero zero one five. Eighty-five point six three three seven.
> 1 S: keys: 85.50542 × 1.0015 = · 2 B: box "bill selling 85.6337"

N043 · Look. The same four rates we saw on the board at the start. And one tip. Multiply by one point zero zero one, rather than using the percent key. It works the same on every basic calculator.
> 1 S: the four boxes slide onto the board; they match · 2 N: "use multipliers, not the % key" · Music bed in

N044 · Pause check for chapter two. Three quick ones. One. In "eighty-five forty, eighty-five forty-two", which is the bid?
> 1 S: card "85.40 / 85.42" · H: 3 s

N045 · Eighty-five forty, the lower one. The bank buys there. Two. T-T rates, or bill rates: which sit closer to the market?
> 1 N: "bid = 85.40 ✓" · H: 3 s

N046 · T-T rates. Money moves at the same time, nothing to wait for. Three, the twist. Who fixes the bank's margin: FEDAI, R-B-I, or the bank?

## Chapter 3 · Which rate for which deal
N049 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Bill selling. Is its margin added to the ask, or to T-T selling?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Which rate for which deal" · H: 3 s

N050 · To T-T selling. Good. Now, FEDAI's rules tell us which rung each deal uses. I'll give you the deal. You say the rate, before I do.
> 1 N: "bill selling = TT selling + margin ✓" · 2 S: the ladder on the left, an empty deal list on the right · Music bed out

N051 · One. A remittance from abroad, paid out in rupees.
> 1 S: deal card "inward remittance" · H: 3 s

N052 · T-T buying. The dollars are already in the Nostro.
> 1 M: deal → TT buying rung [F13]

N053 · Two. The father's fee goes out by wire.
> 1 S: deal card "outward remittance" · H: 3 s

N054 · T-T selling.
> 1 M: deal → TT selling rung [F14]

N055 · Three. The bank buys an exporter's bill the day he brings it.
> 1 S: deal card "export bill bought" · H: 3 s

N056 · Bill buying. The bank pays now and waits.
> 1 M: deal → bill buying rung [F15]

N057 · Four, a little tricky. An export bill is sent on collection. The bank pays the exporter only after the dollars reach its Nostro.
> 1 S: deal card "export bill on collection" · H: 3 s

N058 · Many of you said bill buying. But by the time the bank pays, the money has arrived. Nothing to wait for. So, T-T buying.
> 1 B: strike "bill buying?" · 2 M: deal → TT buying rung [F16] · 3 C: trap card "collection → TT buying, after Nostro credit"

N059 · Five. An importer pays his import bill. He has no forward contract.
> 1 S: deal card "import bill retired" · H: 3 s

N060 · Bill selling.
> 1 M: deal → bill selling rung [F17]

N061 · And one rule above all five. If the customer has booked a forward contract, the contract rate applies, not the board.
> 1 S: card "forward contract → the contract rate overrides" [F26] · 2 B: box "overrides"

N062 · Now a question that splits the class. The bank bought an export bill. On the due date, it isn't paid. The bank converts the unpaid dollars into a rupee debt on the exporter. Which rate?
> 1 S: the export bill card stamped "unpaid" · H: 3 s

N063 · Some of you are thinking bill buying, since it was a bill. Others, bill selling. Both are wrong. It's T-T selling.
> 1 B: strike "bill buying", strike "bill selling" · 2 N: "TT selling" [F18]

N064 · Think it through. The bank paid him rupees for dollars that never came. So he now owes the bank those dollars. The bank is, in effect, selling them to him.
> 1 S: arrow reverses across the counter, red

N065 · No documents move now. It's a plain conversion. So, T-T selling. Converting an unpaid bill into rupees like this is called crystallisation.
> 1 B: box "crystallisation = TT selling" [F18]

N066 · And if his dollars arrive later after all, the bank converts them at T-T buying.
> 1 N: "proceeds later → TT buying" [F19]

N067 · The same flip happens when a forward contract is cancelled. A purchase contract is cancelled at T-T selling. A sale contract, at T-T buying.
> 1 S: card "cancel purchase → TT selling · cancel sale → TT buying" [F22] [F23]

N068 · So, undo a deal the bank already did, and you go to the other side of the counter, on the T-T rung.
> 1 C: rule card "undo → flip sides, TT rung"

N069 · Now test that rule. An import bill under an L-C isn't paid on time, and the bank crystallises it. Does the side flip?
> 1 S: import bill card "unpaid · crystallised" · H: 3 s

N070 · No. Nothing is being undone. The bank sold dollars to pay the supplier, and the importer still owes for them. So it's the same rate as retirement. Bill selling, or his contract rate.
> 1 B: strike "flip?" · 2 N: "import crystallisation = same as retirement" [F21]

N071 · Pause check for chapter three. Two quick ones. One. An importer sends an advance payment for goods, by wire. Which rate?
> 1 S: card "importer's advance by wire" · H: 3 s

N072 · T-T selling. It's a remittance going out, not an import bill. Two, the twist. A customer cancels a forward sale contract. Which rate?
> 1 N: "outward remittance → TT selling ✓" [F14] · H: 3 s

N073 · T-T buying. Undoing a sale, so the bank buys back.
> 1 N: "cancel sale → TT buying ✓" [F23]

N074 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: undo a deal, flip sides on the T-T rung. Nothing undone, nothing flips.
> 1 S: the deal list complete beside the ladder; finished parts dim to grey · Music bed in
