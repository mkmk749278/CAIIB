## Chapter 1 · Which side of the counter?

N006 · Chapter one. Many of you are thinking: the father is buying dollars, so he pays the bank's buying rate.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Which side of the counter?" · 2 N: thought bubble "father buys → buying rate?"

N007 · It sounds logical. But it's wrong. And here's why.
> 1 B: strike through the thought bubble

N008 · The rate on that board is not the customer's price. It is the bank's price. Buying and selling are always from the bank's side.
> 1 S: rule card "the rate = the BANK's price" [F1] · 2 B: box "BANK's"

N009 · So ask one thing only. Is the foreign currency coming into the bank, or going out?
> 1 S: the counter from above; an arrow in (green), an arrow out (red) [F2]

N010 · Coming in, the bank buys it. Going out, the bank sells it. In means buy. Out means sell.
> 1 N: "IN → bank BUYS" (green) · 2 N: "OUT → bank SELLS" (red) · 3 B: underline both

N011 · Now, which number is which? Think of a money-changer. He buys low and sells high. The bank is the same. Only here, the margin is small, and set by the bank.
> 1 S: a money-changer's board, "buy low · sell high"

N012 · From last class. How does India quote the dollar? Directly. So many rupees for one dollar. The bank pays fewer rupees when it buys, and takes more when it sells. So buying is the lower number.
> 1 S: card "direct quote: ₹ per 1 unit (per 100 units for the yen)" [F4] · H: 3 s after "quote the dollar?" · 2 N: "buying = lower ₹ · selling = higher ₹" [F3]

N013 · Back to the father. His dollars are leaving the bank, going to the university. Out. So the bank sells. He pays the higher rate.
> 1 S: father's arrow goes OUT, red · 2 G: the top rates on the board

N014 · And the exporter? His bill brings dollars in. In. So the bank buys. He gets the lower rate.
> 1 S: exporter's arrow comes IN, green · 2 G: the bottom rates on the board

N015 · If you said "buying rate" for the father, you were standing on the customer's side. Come round to the bank's side, and it's easy.
> 1 S: a small figure walks round the counter to the bank's side

N016 · Pause check. Three quick ones. Answer each before I do. One. A son abroad sends two thousand five hundred dollars home to his mother. Bank buying, or selling?
> 1 S: card "son abroad → mother · $2,500" · H: 3 s

N017 · Buying. The dollars come in. Two. A student pays her foreign college fee by wire. Buying or selling?
> 1 N: "IN → buys ✓" · 2 S: card "student → college abroad" · H: 3 s

N018 · Selling. The dollars go out. Three, the twist. An importer wants dollars, so many of you think "importer buys". Which side is the bank on?
> 1 N: "OUT → sells ✓" · 2 S: card "importer pays supplier abroad" · H: 3 s

N019 · Selling. The importer's dollars go out of the bank. Always the bank's side, never the customer's.
> 1 N: "OUT → sells ✓" · 2 B: box "bank's side"

N020 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter one: in means the bank buys, out means the bank sells. And buying is always the lower number.

## Chapter 2 · The rate ladder

N021 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. An exporter's dollars come in. Is the bank buying or selling? And is that the lower number, or the higher?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The rate ladder" · 2 S: exporter card, arrow IN · H: 3 s

N022 · Buying, and the lower number. Good. But the board had four rates, not two. So which buying rate?
> 1 N: "IN → buys → lower ✓" · 2 G: the four board rates

N023 · Start with where prices come from. Last class: banks dealing with each other. Which market is that? The interbank market. Today, say, eighty-five point four zero, eighty-five point four two.
> 1 S: two bank towers trading · H: 3 s after "Which market is that?" · 2 S: quote card "USD/INR 85.40 / 85.42 (illustrative)" [F6]

N024 · The lower number is the bid. The price at which a bank buys. The higher number is the ask, or offer. The price at which it sells.
> 1 N: under 85.40 "bid = bank buys" · 2 N: under 85.42 "ask / offer = bank sells" [F7] · 3 B: underline both words

N025 · The gap between them is the spread. Here, just two paise.
> 1 B: a bracket between the two numbers, label "spread 0.02" [F7]

N026 · Now, your counter is the merchant market, bank with customer. So the bank adds its margin. Selling rates go up from the ask. Buying rates come down from the bid.
> 1 S: the ladder draws: middle rung "interbank 85.40 / 85.42"; arrows up "selling", down "buying" [F10]

N027 · How big is the margin? Each bank decides its own. FEDAI doesn't fix it. In an exam sum, the margin is always given.
> 1 S: card "margins: the bank's own; FEDAI fixes none" [F8] · 2 B: box "given in the sum"

N028 · Which rung for which deal? Watch two things. The rupees at your counter, and the dollars in the bank's account abroad, the Nostro.
> 1 S: two clocks side by side: "₹ at the counter" · "$ in the Nostro"

N029 · When both move at the same time, we use the rungs nearest the market. The T-T rates. T-T means telegraphic transfer: money sent by wire, at once.
> 1 S: both clocks tick together · 2 S: term tile "TT = telegraphic transfer (a wire)" [F11]

N029a · T-T buying sits just below the bid. T-T selling, just above the ask.
> 1 S: the ladder adds "TT selling" above, "TT buying" below the middle [F11]

N030 · When the bank pays first, or handles documents, it goes one rung further out. The bill rates.
> 1 S: the rupee clock runs ahead of the dollar clock · 2 S: ladder adds "bill selling" on top, "bill buying" at the bottom [F11]

N031 · Bill buying: the bank pays the exporter today, but his dollars reach the Nostro weeks later. Bill selling: the bank handles the importer's documents. The interest for the waiting days is charged on top, separately. That's lesson A-twelve.
> 1 S: exporter paid now, dollar clock "weeks later" · 2 S: an import document folder · 3 N: "interest for the wait: separate (A12)" [F11]

N032 · So the ladder, top to bottom. Bill selling. T-T selling. The market. T-T buying. Bill buying. Say it with me once.
> 1 S: the full ladder, rungs lighting top to bottom [F10] · H: 2 s

N033 · Now say it back, top to bottom, without looking. Then tell me: which rate is the very highest?
> 1 S: the ladder hides its labels · H: 5 s

N034 · Bill selling, at the top. And at the bottom, bill buying. The bank pays first, and carries the bill until the dollars arrive. So it sits furthest out on the buying side.
> 1 N: top rung "bill selling" ✓ · 2 N: bottom rung "bill buying"

N035 · Now let's build the board ourselves. Calculators out. The four margins are on the screen. We'll take them one at a time.
> 1 S: margin card "TT buy 0.05% · bill buy 0.15% · TT sell 0.10% · bill sell +0.15% on TT sell" [F10] · Music bed out

N036 · T-T selling. The ask, plus point one percent. Plus point one percent means times one point zero zero one. Press eighty-five point four two, times one point zero zero one, equals.

N037 · Eighty-five point five zero five four two. Rounded to four decimals, eighty-five point five zero five four.

N038 · T-T buying. The bid, less point zero five percent. Less means take it from one: times point nine nine nine five. Eighty-five point four zero, times point nine nine nine five, equals. Eighty-five point three five seven three.

N041 · Last one, and here people slip. Bill selling, another point one five percent. Many of you will add it to the ask. But in exam sums, it goes on top of T-T selling, unless the question says otherwise.

## Chapter 3 · Which rate for which deal

N049 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. Bill selling. Is its margin added to the ask, or to T-T selling?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · Which rate for which deal" · H: 3 s

N050 · To T-T selling. Good. FEDAI's rules tell us which rung each deal uses. I give the deal, you say the rate, before I do.
> 1 N: "bill selling = TT selling + margin ✓" · 2 S: the ladder on the left, an empty deal list on the right · Music bed out

N051 · One. A remittance from abroad, paid out in rupees.
> 1 S: deal card "inward remittance" · H: 3 s

N052 · T-T buying. The dollars are already in the Nostro.
> 1 M: deal → TT buying rung [F13]

N053 · Two. The father's fee goes out by wire.
> 1 S: deal card "outward remittance" · H: 3 s

N054 · T-T selling.
> 1 M: deal → TT selling rung [F14]

N055 · Three. The bank buys, discounts or negotiates an exporter's bill the day he brings it.
> 1 S: deal card "export bill bought / discounted / negotiated" · H: 3 s

N056 · Bill buying. The bank pays now and waits.
> 1 M: deal → bill buying rung [F15]

N057 · Four, a little tricky. An export bill is sent on collection. The bank pays the exporter only after the dollars reach its Nostro.
> 1 S: deal card "export bill on collection" · H: 3 s

N058 · Many of you said bill buying. But the bank pays only once the money has arrived. Nothing to wait for. So, T-T buying.
> 1 B: strike "bill buying?" · 2 M: deal → TT buying rung [F16] · 3 C: trap card "collection → TT buying, after Nostro credit"

N059 · Five. An importer pays his import bill. He has no forward contract.
> 1 S: deal card "import bill retired" · H: 3 s

N060 · Bill selling.
> 1 M: deal → bill selling rung [F17]

N061 · And one rule above all five. If the customer has already booked a contract with the bank, a forward contract, that contract rate applies, not the board.
> 1 S: card "booked contract → the contract rate overrides the card rate" [F26] · 2 B: box "overrides"

N062 · Now a question that splits the class. The bank bought an export bill. On the due date, it isn't paid. The bank converts the unpaid dollars into a rupee debt on the exporter. Which rate?
> 1 S: the export bill card stamped "unpaid" · H: 3 s

N063 · Some of you are thinking bill buying, since it was a bill. Others, bill selling. Both are wrong. It's T-T selling.
> 1 B: strike "bill buying", strike "bill selling" · 2 N: "TT selling" [F18]

N064 · Think it through. The bank paid him rupees for dollars that never came. Now he owes the bank those dollars. In effect, the bank sells them to him.
> 1 S: arrow reverses across the counter, red

N065 · No documents move now. It's a plain conversion. So, T-T selling. Turning an unpaid foreign currency bill into a rupee debt like this is called crystallisation. When to do it is the bank's own stated policy.
> 1 B: box "crystallisation = TT selling" [F18] · 2 N: "when: the bank's own policy (FEDAI 2.1(b))"

N066 · And if his dollars arrive later after all, the bank converts them at T-T buying, or at his contract rate.
> 1 N: "proceeds later → TT buying" [F19]

N067 · The same flip happens when a forward contract is cancelled on or after its due date. A purchase contract, where the bank agreed to buy, is cancelled at T-T selling. A sale contract, at T-T buying.
> 1 S: card "cancel on due date: purchase → TT selling · sale → TT buying" [F22] [F23]

N067a · Cancelled before the due date? Then the forward T-T rate. We'll do those sums in lesson A-zero-six.
> 1 N: "before due date → forward TT rate (A06)" [F24]

N068 · So here's a memory hook. Undo a deal the bank already did, and you go to the other side of the counter, on the T-T rung.
> 1 C: memory card "undo → flip sides, TT rung" (our hook)

N069 · Now test that hook. The bank paid an importer's supplier under a letter of credit. The importer doesn't pay on time, and the bank crystallises it. Does the side flip?
> 1 S: import bill card "paid under the LC · importer late · crystallised" · H: 3 s

N070 · No. Nothing is undone. The bank sold dollars to pay the supplier, and the importer still owes for them. So, the same rate as retirement: bill selling, or his contract rate.
> 1 B: strike "flip?" · 2 N: "import crystallisation = same as retirement" [F21]

N071 · Pause check for chapter three. Three quick ones. One. An importer sends an advance payment for goods, by wire. Which rate?
> 1 S: card "importer's advance by wire" · H: 3 s

N072 · T-T selling. It's a remittance going out, not an import bill. Two, the twist. A customer cancels a forward sale contract on its due date. Which rate?
> 1 N: "outward remittance → TT selling ✓" [F14] · H: 3 s

N073 · T-T buying. Undoing a sale, so the bank buys back. Three. An exporter has a forward contract at eighty-five point three. The bank buys his bill today. Which rate?
> 1 N: "cancel sale → TT buying ✓" [F23] · 2 S: card "exporter: forward contract 85.30 · bill bought today" · H: 3 s

N073a · His contract rate, eighty-five point three. A booked contract beats the board.
> 1 N: "contract rate ✓" [F26]

N074 · [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: undo a deal, flip sides on the T-T rung. An unpaid import bill doesn't flip.
