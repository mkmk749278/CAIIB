# A19 · Practice (c1, 2026-11-24)
Our own questions, written strictly from A19_fact_sheet (rules as at 31 December 2026) and A19_numericals. All figures illustrative. Options use real exam traps (pre-16 Feb 2026 ECB rules, old 2021 49% bank AT1 cap, IDR currency trap). Answers with one-line reasons at the end.
1. Under the February 2026 ECB framework (FEMA 3(R)(5)/2026-RB), a non-financial Indian company with audited standalone net worth of USD 250 million and existing domestic loans of USD 150 million may raise ECB up to: (a) USD 750 million per financial year (b) USD 600 million (300% of net worth less domestic debt) (c) USD 1,000 million (higher of USD 1 billion outstanding ECB or 300% of net worth) (d) 7 times its foreign equity holder's paid-up equity
2. Under the February 2026 ECB framework, what is the all-in-cost ceiling for a 5-year foreign-currency ECB raised by an Indian corporate from an unrelated overseas lender? (a) Benchmark rate + 500 bps (b) Benchmark rate + 550 bps (c) Benchmark rate + 300 bps (d) No fixed all-in-cost ceiling — cost must be in line with prevailing market conditions
3. A Hyderabad manufacturer raises a 2-year ECB (MAMP between 1 and 3 years) in July 2026. Which condition applies? (a) Outstanding 1–3 year manufacturing ECBs cannot exceed USD 150 million, and borrowing cost must meet the Trade Credit ceiling (b) Short-tenor manufacturing ECBs are capped at USD 50 million per financial year (c) MAMP cannot be below 3 years for any Indian borrower (d) 70% of principal and interest must be mandatorily hedged
4. Can an Indian scheduled commercial bank issue a Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) to guarantee an Indian corporate's ECB under the 2026 framework? (a) Yes, up to USD 50 million for manufacturing borrowers (b) Yes, with Board approval and 100% collateral (c) No — entities regulated by RBI are strictly barred from providing any type of guarantee for ECBs (d) Yes, if the overseas lender is an IFSC Banking Unit
5. Under the February 2026 ECB reporting rules (FEMA 3(R)(5)/2026-RB & A.P. DIR Circular No. 25), when must a borrower submit Form ECB 2? (a) Every month within 7 working days, including nil returns (b) Within 7 calendar days from the end of a month in which ECB proceeds were received, debt servicing occurred, or outstanding balance changed (c) Quarterly within 15 calendar days from quarter-end (d) Only once at final repayment
6. (Keyed-in numerical) A borrower delays filing a Form ECB 2 return for a ₹32 crore (₹32,00,00,000) ECB drawdown by 9 months (n = 0.75 year). Calculate the Late Submission Fee (LSF) payable in ₹ under the 1 April 2026 rules (₹7,500 + 0.025% × A × n).
7. Which statement regarding Indian Depository Receipts (IDRs) is correct? (a) IDRs are issued by Indian companies in US dollars to overseas investors (b) IDRs are issued by foreign companies in Indian Rupees only, with immediate repatriation of proceeds, a 1-year lock-in, and a 30-day sale rule for resident individuals on redemption (c) IDRs are automatically fungible into foreign equity shares from Day 1 (d) Foreign banks with Indian branches do not require prior sectoral regulator approval to issue IDRs
8. (Keyed-in numerical) Under RBI's Directions effective 1 October 2025, a scheduled commercial bank has Risk Weighted Assets (RWAs) of ₹2,00,000 crore. What is the maximum amount (in ₹ crore) of Perpetual Debt Instruments (PDIs) it may issue in foreign currency / overseas Rupee-Denominated Bonds for inclusion in Additional Tier 1 (AT1) capital?
________________
Answers (one-line reasons)
1. (c) — Schedule I Para 5 caps ECB at the higher of USD 1B outstanding ECB or 300% of net worth (250 × 3 − 150 = USD 600M < USD 1,000M) [F5, F26].
2. (d) — Fixed AIC ceilings were removed from 16 Feb 2026 for MAMP ≥ 3 years ("prevailing market conditions"); only <3-year manufacturing ECBs face the Trade Credit ceiling [F7].
3. (a) — Manufacturing 1–3 year ECBs are capped at USD 150M outstanding (replacing USD 50M/FY) and must meet the Trade Credit AIC ceiling; 70% mandatory hedging was removed [F6, F7, F9].
4. (c) — Schedule I Para 11(3) bars RBI-regulated entities from issuing any type of guarantee (covering SBLCs under FEMA 8(R)/2026-RB) [F8].
5. (b) — Schedule I Para 16(1)(c) shifted Form ECB 2 to event-based reporting within 7 calendar days from month-end [F10].
6. ₹67,500 — 32,00,00,000 × 0.025% × 0.75 = ₹60,000 + ₹7,500 = ₹67,500 [F11, F28].
7. (b) — IDRs are issued by foreign companies in INR only, immediately repatriated, 1-year lock-in, 30-day resident sale rule on conversion, and foreign banks with Indian presence need prior sectoral regulator approval [F18, F19, F20].
8. ₹3,000 crore — Effective 1 Oct 2025, overseas FCY/RDB AT1 PDIs are capped at 1.5% of RWAs (2,00,000 × 1.5% = ₹3,000 crore), repealing the 2021 49% cap [F23].
