Stay with me: by the end, you will apply the twenty twenty-six E-C-B rules and tell A-D-R, G-D-R, I-D-R, F-C-C-B, and Masala Bonds apart. Meet Deccan Precision Limited, an auto-component manufacturer in Hyderabad. Illustrative. They want to borrow twenty million dollars from abroad for a new plant. Their finance head asks your branch two things: does the old seven-fifty-million annual cap apply, and can our bank issue a standby L-C guarantee? Lock your answer. Many exam guides still teach the pre-twenty-twenty-six E-C-B rules. Let's cross the bridge booth by booth to see the current law. Chapter one: External Commercial Borrowings after sixteen February twenty twenty-six. R-B-I consolidated E-C-B into Schedule One of the Borrowing and Lending Regulations. Old loans with an L-R-N before sixteen February twenty twenty-six stay on old rules, except reporting. First booth: who is an eligible borrower? Any non-individual Indian entity formed or registered under a Central or State Act, if its Act permits E-C-B. So Deccan Precision easily qualifies. Firms under insolvency or restructuring may borrow if their plan permits. Pending FEMA investigations do not bar borrowing once disclosed in Form E-C-B One. Second booth: recognised lenders. Many of you are thinking the lender must be from an F-A-T-F or I-O-S-C-O compliant country. Not anymore. From sixteen February twenty twenty-six, recognised lenders are any person resident outside India, overseas branches of R-B-I-regulated lenders, and I-F-S-C financial institutions. Third booth: borrowing limit. Write this down: the limit is the higher of one billion dollars outstanding E-C-B, or three hundred percent of net worth. Three hundred percent covers total external plus domestic debt on the last audited standalone balance sheet. Financial-sector regulated borrowers are exempt from this cap. Notice the trap: the old seven-fifty-million per financial year cap and seven-to-one equity ratio are gone. Let's work a limit sum together. The commonest wrong answer is six hundred million—picking the lower limb instead of the higher limb. Never fall for that. Fourth booth: Minimum Average Maturity Period, called M-A-M-P. Standard M-A-M-P is three years, and call or put options cannot be exercised earlier. One key exception: manufacturing companies may borrow for one to three years, capped at one hundred fifty million dollars outstanding—replacing the old fifty-million annual cap. Fifth booth: cost of borrowing. Many think the old fixed all-in-cost ceiling still applies. Wrong: R-B-I removed fixed ceilings for three-year-plus E-C-Bs. Cost must match prevailing market conditions, and related-party loans must be at arm's length. Commitment fees and Indian statutory taxes stay outside borrowing cost. Only sub-three-year manufacturing E-C-Bs face a ceiling: the Trade Credit cap, benchmark plus three hundred basis points for foreign currency or two-fifty for rupees. Currency conversion is now two-way: foreign currency to rupee, and rupee back to foreign currency. Now, sixth booth: barred end-uses under Regulation three-A. Barred: chit funds, Nidhi, real estate business and farmhouses, restricted agriculture and plantations, T-D-R trading, non-strategic securities, NPA rupee-loan repayment, and restricted on-lending. Notice what moved off the negative list: ordinary working capital and general corporate purposes are now allowed at three-year M-A-M-P. And the guarantee bar? Entities regulated by R-B-I—banks, A-I-F-Is, and N-B-F-Cs—cannot issue any guarantee, standby L-C, L-o-U, or L-o-C for E-C-Bs. So Deccan Precision's S-B-L-C request fails. One hundred fifty million dollars outstanding. Now follow reporting: first, file Form E-C-B One through your designated A-D bank for a Loan Registration Number. Rule: never allow drawdown before the L-R-N is allotted. If no drawdown happens, the A-D bank may ask R-B-I to cancel the L-R-N. If loan terms change after L-R-N—with lender consent—file Revised Form E-C-B One within seven calendar days from month-end. Changing A-D banks requires a no-objection certificate. Many think Form E-C-B Two is filed every month, even with zero activity. That rule ended. From twenty twenty-six, Form E-C-B Two is event-based. File Form E-C-B Two within seven calendar days of month-end when proceeds arrive, servicing occurs, or balances change. A-D banks forward it in seven days. What if a borrower vanishes? Missing specified returns for four consecutive quarters, unreachable, and inoperative at registered office triggers untraceable status—reported to R-B-I and E-D. From one April twenty twenty-six, Late Submission Fee for a delayed Form E-C-B One or Revised One is a flat seven thousand five hundred rupees. Each delayed Form E-C-B Two counts separately: seven thousand five hundred rupees plus zero point zero two five percent of amount A times years n. Capped at one hundred percent of A, rounded up to the nearest hundred, paid via N-E-F-T or R-T-G-S within thirty days. Let's calculate one. Say a sixteen-crore Form E-C-B Two drawdown return is six months late, so n equals zero point five zero. Press sixteen crore times zero point zero two five percent times zero point five on your calculator. That gives twenty thousand rupees. Operational rules: rupee proceeds reach the A-D bank by next month-end and park in unencumbered fixed deposits up to one year. Same one-year cap abroad. The old seventy-percent mandatory hedging rule for infrastructure borrowers is gone. Security may cover immovable, movable, financial, or intangible assets with domestic lender N-O-C. Finally, converting E-C-B into equity. Even matured unpaid E-C-B may convert under N-D-I Rules—with lender consent, no extra cost, other-lender notice, and M-A-M-P exempt. Chapter three: the second lane on our bridge—depository receipts and overseas bonds. Quick recall: what is the flat L-S-F for a delayed Form E-C-B One? Seven thousand five hundred rupees. Now look at American and Global Depository Receipts—A-D-R in the U-S, G-D-R globally—under the Depository Receipts Scheme twenty fourteen. An Indian company deposits shares with a Domestic Custodian in India; a Foreign Depository issues receipts abroad. Both sponsored and unsponsored D-Rs are permitted. The Domestic Custodian files Form D-R-R within thirty days of issue close and monitors two-way fungibility—re-issuing D-Rs within headroom without breaching sectoral caps. Next: Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds, or F-C-C-Bs. They are hybrid debt with an equity option—still under the nineteen ninety-three scheme, regulated as E-C-B until conversion. F-C-C-Bs stay outside the F-D-I sectoral cap until converted, and conversion is M-A-M-P exempt. Now flip our bridge around for Indian Depository Receipts. Many candidates think I-D-Rs are issued in U-S dollars. Wrong. A foreign company issues I-D-Rs in India through a Domestic Depository in Indian Rupees only. Proceeds are immediately repatriated abroad, under a five-billion-dollar market cap. Foreign banks with an Indian branch or subsidiary need prior sectoral regulator approval. Residents, F-P-Is, and N-R-Is may buy I-D-Rs. Redemption has a one-year lock-in and no automatic fungibility—only partial two-way fungibility up to twenty-five percent yearly. Crucial resident rule: if an Indian individual redeems I-D-Rs into foreign shares, they may hold those shares only to sell within thirty days of conversion. Last instrument: Rupee-Denominated Bonds overseas, called Masala Bonds. Because principal and coupon are fixed in rupees, who bears the exchange rate risk? The foreign investor bears the exchange rate risk, not the Indian borrower. Indian banks may issue overseas rupee bonds for three specific purposes. Additional Tier One Perpetual Debt Instruments, Tier Two capital, and long-term infrastructure or affordable housing bonds. Watch the October twenty twenty-five rule change on A-T-One. From one October twenty twenty-five, overseas foreign-currency or rupee-bond A-T-One P-D-Is cap at one point five percent of Risk Weighted Assets, replacing forty-nine percent. Pause check for chapter three. One: in which currency are I-D-Rs denominated? Two: what is the current cap on a bank's overseas A-T-One P-D-Is? One: Indian Rupees only. Two: one point five percent of Risk Weighted Assets. Remember: A-D-R and G-D-R go abroad; I-D-R comes to India in rupees. Back to Deccan Precision: their twenty-million-dollar E-C-B fits the new cap easily, but our Indian bank cannot issue an S-B-L-C guarantee. Mixed check—three quick twists. One: can an E-C-B be converted from rupees into dollars under twenty twenty-six rules? Yes, two-way conversion is permitted. Two: who files Form D-R-R, and within how many days? The Domestic Custodian, within thirty days of close. Three: does an Indian company's F-C-C-B count inside the F-D-I sectoral cap before conversion? No—outside the sectoral cap until converted into equity. Now fill your blank mind map: branch Borrowing from Abroad has four nodes. First: E-C-B. Higher of one billion or three hundred percent net worth, three-year M-A-M-P, market cost, guarantee bar, seven-day event E-C-B Two. Second: A-D-R, G-D-R, I-D-R. A-D-R and G-D-R file Form D-R-R in thirty days; I-D-R is foreign shares in rupees, one-year lock-in, thirty-day sale. Next: F-C-C-B and R-D-B. F-C-C-B is E-C-B until conversion; Masala Bonds shift currency risk abroad; bank overseas A-T-One P-D-Is cap at one point five percent of R-W-As. Foreign equity investment comes next class. Homework: calculate L-S-F for a thirty-two-crore Form E-C-B Two return delayed by nine months. Next class, we cross the equity lane of this bridge: when a Singapore investor funds an Indian startup, is it F-D-I or F-P-I? [warmly] That finishes our bridge for overseas borrowing and receipts. Put the notebook away, rest well, and I'll meet you in the next class.
