**P1.** An exporter's order is CIF USD 80,000; freight and insurance USD 4,000. Taking ₹85 per dollar
(illustrative), domestic cost ₹70 lakh and a 10 % margin, and using the common bank practice of the lower of FOB value
and domestic cost less margin, the packing credit limit is:
(a) **₹58,14,000** (b) ₹61,20,000 (c) ₹63,00,000 (d) ₹68,00,000
*Reason: FOB = 76,000 × 85 = ₹64,60,000; lower than ₹70 lakh; less 10 % = ₹58,14,000. (b) uses CIF (80,000 × 85 ×
90 %); (c) uses cost. RBI itself only requires need-based limits [F24].*

**P2.** Packing credit not adjusted by export documents within 360 days from the date of advance:
(a) is automatically extended to 450 days (b) **ceases to be export credit from the date of advance** (c) becomes
post-shipment credit (d) must be reported to DGFT
*Reason: CF 235, 292 [F3]. The 450-day relief applied only to credit disbursed till 30 June 2026 [F25].*

**P3.** If the export does not take place at all, the bank charges on the packing credit:
(a) the export credit rate till repayment (b) the TT selling rate (c) no interest (d) **its domestic lending rate
plus penal charges, if any, per its Board policy**
*Reason: CF 293 [F4]. TT selling applies to PCFC not exported in 360 days [F22].*

**P4.** Which is NOT a form of post-shipment credit named in RBI's export credit directions?
(a) export bills purchased / discounted / negotiated (b) advance against bills for collection (c) advance against
duty drawback (d) **advance against a confirmed export order**
*Reason: CF 266 [F13]; (d) is packing credit [F2].*

**P5.** A usance export bill can be financed at the post-shipment stage for at most:
(a) 360 days from the advance (b) 180 days from the bill date (c) **365 days from the date of shipment, including
NTP and grace** (d) 25 days
*Reason: CF 270 [F14]. 25 days is the FEDAI NTP for a foreign-currency sight bill not under LC.*

**P6.** About PCFC, which is correct?
(a) available for deemed exports of all kinds (b) only for imported inputs (c) **for cash exports, domestic and
imported inputs, up to 360 days; if no export, adjusted at the TT selling rate** (d) must be in the invoice
currency
*Reason: CF 294, 295, 297, 304, 305 [F22]. (a) is wrong: PCFC for deemed exports only for supplies to projects funded
by multilateral / bilateral agencies (CF 335); (d) is wrong: PCFC may be in another convertible currency (CF 297).*

**P7.** A running-account packing credit facility:
(a) **needs a good track record (or EOU / SEZ status); bills are marked off on a first-in, first-out basis** (b) is
open to all sub-suppliers (c) needs a firm order before each drawal (d) has no time limit
*Reason: CF 243, 246 [F8].*

**P8.** Which statements on liquidation of packing credit are true? (i) It is normally liquidated from the proceeds of
the export bill, turning it into post-shipment credit. (ii) By agreement it may be repaid from the exporter's EEFC
balance or own rupees, to the extent exports have taken place. (iii) For good-track-record exporters, a bill of
another order may liquidate it (substitution of contract), but not for group concerns.
(a) (i) only (b) (i) and (ii) (c) **(i), (ii) and (iii)** (d) (ii) and (iii)
*Reason: CF 240, 242 [F6, F7].*

**P9.** A packing credit limit of ₹45 lakh (margin already deducted) has drawals of ₹20 lakh and ₹12 lakh. The
exporter asks for ₹15 lakh more. The bank can give at most:
(a) ₹15,00,000 (b) **₹13,00,000** (c) ₹11,70,000 (d) ₹8,50,000
*Reason: 45 − 20 − 12 = ₹13 lakh (`export_pcl.py` P9). (c) deducts a 10 % margin a second time.*

**P10.** Packing credit outstanding is ₹40 lakh. The exporter ships part of the order; the bill of USD 25,000 is
purchased at ₹84.00 (illustrative). Ignoring interest:
(a) the whole PC is closed (b) the bill proceeds go to the exporter first (c) ₹19,00,000 becomes a domestic loan at
once (d) **₹19,00,000 stays as packing credit, to be liquidated within 360 days of each advance**
*Reason: 25,000 × 84 = ₹21,00,000 liquidates part; 40 − 21 = ₹19 lakh remains PC (CF 235, 240) [F3, F6].*
