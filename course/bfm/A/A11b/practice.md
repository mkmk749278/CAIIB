**P1.** CIF USD 3,00,000; freight and insurance USD 20,000; bank's rate ₹88; domestic cost ₹2,40,00,000; margin 10%
(bank's norm: lower of FOB and domestic cost). The drawing power is:
(a) ₹2,37,60,000 (b) ₹2,21,76,000 (c) **₹2,16,00,000** (d) ₹2,40,00,000
*Reason: FOB 2,80,000 × 88 = ₹2,46,40,000; lower = ₹2,40,00,000; × 90% = ₹2,16,00,000 [F9]. (a) uses CIF (3,00,000 ×
88 × 90%); (b) uses FOB and ignores the lower-of rule; (d) ignores the margin.*

**P2.** Packing credit drawn on 1 Mar 2027 is not adjusted by export documents within 360 days. It:
(a) becomes a domestic loan from day 361
(b) **ceases to be export credit from the date of advance (ab initio)**
(c) is automatically extended by 90 days
(d) must be reported to ECGC as a claim
*Reason: CF 235, 292 [F19, F24]; extensions are bank policy within the 360-day wall [F20].*

**P3.** The export order is cancelled and nothing is exported. The bank charges:
(a) the packing-credit rate, as the loan was export credit when given
(b) **its domestic lending rate from the date of advance, plus penal charges under its policy**
(c) the domestic rate only from the date of cancellation
(d) nothing extra if the loan is repaid within 360 days
*Reason: CF 293 [F25].*

**P4.** Which is **not** a way RBI allows packing credit to be liquidated?
(a) proceeds of the export bill purchased by the bank
(b) the exporter's EEFC balance, to the extent exports have taken place
(c) documents of another order, for a good-track-record exporter, if commercially necessary
(d) **the proceeds of the exporter's domestic sales of the same goods, as a routine option**
*Reason: CF 240, 242 [F21–F23]; domestic sale was allowed only under the 2025–26 trade-relief measures, now ended [F29].*

**P5.** The normal transit period for a foreign-currency sight bill not under an LC is:
(a) 20 days (b) **25 days** (c) the sailing time to the port of discharge (d) 90 days
*Reason: FEDAI NTP [F34]; NTP is the time for proceeds to arrive, not the voyage.*

**P6.** The maximum period of post-shipment credit on a usance export bill is:
(a) 180 days from shipment (b) 270 days from shipment (c) **365 days from shipment, including NTP and grace** (d) 9 months
*Reason: CF 270 [F35]; 180 days is the EBR usance limit [F38]; 9 months is FEMA's realisation period [F36].*

**P7.** A running-account packing credit facility:
(a) is available to every exporter, and to sub-suppliers
(b) **is for good-track-record exporters and EOU / SEZ units; later bills clear the earliest drawals first (FIFO)**
(c) needs no export order at any stage
(d) runs for 450 days
*Reason: CF 243–246, 249(1) [F11].*

**P8.** On post-shipment credit covered by ECGC's ECIB, the premium:
(a) must always be recovered from the exporter
(b) **may be borne by the bank, since the cover mainly protects the bank**
(c) is paid by RBI
(d) is paid by the buyer
*Reason: CF 285–286 [F39].*
