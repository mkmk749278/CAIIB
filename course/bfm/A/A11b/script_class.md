## Chapter 1 · Packing credit

N006 · Chapter one. R-B-I started export credit in nineteen sixty-seven, to give exporters working capital at rates close to world rates.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Packing credit" · 2 N: "export credit: since 1967 · working capital at internationally comparable rates" [F1]

N007 · It comes in two halves. Before shipment: pre-shipment credit, or packing credit. After shipment, till the money comes: post-shipment credit.
> 1 S: the money lane: "pre-shipment (packing credit)" left of the ship · "post-shipment" right of it [F2, F3]

N008 · Packing credit pays for buying, making and packing the goods. For a service exporter, the working expenses of the service.
> 1 C: card "packing credit: purchase · processing · manufacture · packing before shipment (services: working expenses)" [F2]

N009 · Quick one. What must she show the bank before it lends?
> 1 G: the order card · H: 3 s

N010 · An L-C from the buyer, or a firm export order. For a proven exporter, the bank may waive that, and take regular statements of her orders instead.
> 1 N: "against an LC or a confirmed order · good track record → bank may waive it, takes periodical statements" [F2, F5]

N010a · Who else can get it? A maker supplying an export house, a sub-supplier through an inland L-C, a service exporter, and a supplier into an S-E-Z.
> 1 N: "also: manufacturer suppliers · sub-suppliers (inland LC) · service exporters · DTA → SEZ supplies" [F4]

N011 · And no export credit is refused just for lack of collateral, if her record is good. It's lent on need.
> 1 N: "need-based · not denied for want of collateral" [F8]

N012 · How much? Here's the norm most banks use. Not R-B-I's words, but the bank's. The lower of two values, less a margin.
> 1 C: card "bank's sizing norm: lower of FOB value and domestic cost · less margin (bank policy, not RBI text)" [F9]

N013 · First, the F-O-B value: the order's value at the Indian port, without freight and insurance. Second, her domestic cost of making the goods.
> 1 N: "FOB = order value at the port, without freight and insurance · domestic cost = what the goods cost her"

N014 · Many of you would take the C-I-F value. Don't. Freight and insurance aren't working capital. She pays them after the goods leave.
> 1 C: trap card "CIF ✗ · FOB ✓ (freight and insurance are not working capital)" [F9]

N015 · Let's size it. Her order is C-I-F two lakh eighty thousand dollars. Freight fourteen thousand, insurance two thousand eight hundred. Press along.
> 1 S: sum card N1 (illustrative) · 2 K: 280000 − 14000 − 2800 =

N016 · Two lakh, sixty-three thousand, two hundred dollars, F-O-B. At the bank's rate of eighty-eight: times eighty-eight, equals. Two crore, thirty-one lakh, sixty-one thousand, six hundred rupees.
> 1 K: display 263200 · 2 K: × 88 = → 23161600

N017 · Her domestic cost is two crore twenty lakh. Which is lower?
> 1 S: two bars "FOB ₹2,31,61,600" · "domestic cost ₹2,20,00,000" · H: 3 s

N018 · The domestic cost. Now the bank keeps a ten per cent margin. Two crore twenty lakh, times ninety, per cent. One crore ninety-eight lakh.
> 1 B: circle the domestic-cost bar · 2 K: 22000000 × 90 % → 19800000

N019 · Your turn. Her sanctioned limit is two crore ten lakh, and she has already drawn one crore twenty lakh. How much more can she draw?
> 1 S: "limit ₹2,10,00,000 · drawn ₹1,20,00,000" · H: 12 s

N020 · The drawing power is the lower figure, one crore ninety-eight lakh. Minus one crore twenty lakh, equals. Seventy-eight lakh rupees.
> 1 K: 19800000 − 12000000 = → 7800000 [N1]

N021 · Two more ways she can borrow. First, a running account. A proven exporter, or an S-E-Z or export-oriented unit, may draw before the order arrives.
> 1 C: card "running account: good track record, or EOU / SEZ units · draw before the LC / order · produce it within the time the bank sets" [F11]

N022 · Later bills clear the oldest loans first: first in, first out. And it's never for sub-suppliers.
> 1 N: "FIFO · not for sub-suppliers · misuse → withdrawn" [F11]

N023 · Second, packing credit in foreign currency: P-C-F-C. In any convertible currency, priced off an international benchmark rate.
> 1 C: card "PCFC: any convertible currency · interest on an ARR benchmark · max 360 days" [F12, F13, F15, F16]

N023a · If she takes P-C-F-C and the export never happens within three sixty days? It's adjusted at the bank's T-T selling rate. She buys the dollars to repay.
> 1 N: "PCFC, no export in 360 days → adjusted at TT selling" [F16, F17]

## Chapter 2 · The 360-day wall

N027 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. What two values does the bank compare?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · The 360-day wall" · H: 3 s

N028 · F-O-B and domestic cost. Now, how long can packing credit run? The bank fixes the period, for each order. But there's one wall.
> 1 N: "FOB · domestic cost ✓" · 2 S: a wall at "360"

N029 · If export documents don't clear it within three hundred and sixty days, it stops being export credit. From the very first day.
> 1 C: card "not adjusted by export documents within 360 days → not export credit ab initio (from day one)" [F19, F24] · 2 B: underline "from day one"

N030 · Many of you think: it becomes a normal loan from day three sixty-one. No. Ab initio means from the start. The cheaper rate is lost for the whole period.
> 1 C: trap card "from day 361 ✗ · ab initio, from the date of advance ✓" [F24]

N031 · How is it normally repaid? Quick one. From her salary, or from the export bill?
> 1 S: two arrows into the PC account · H: 3 s

N032 · The export bill. When the bank buys her bill, the money clears the packing credit. Pre-shipment turns into post-shipment credit.
> 1 S: the bill joins the money lane; PC closes; post-shipment opens [F21]

N033 · Some of you think it can be repaid only from that same export's money. Not so. Her foreign currency account, or her own rupees, may also repay it, to the extent she has exported.
> 1 C: trap card "only from that export ✗ · EEFC or own rupees too, to the extent exported" [F22]

N034 · A proven exporter may even clear it with another order's documents, if it's commercially necessary.
> 1 N: "good track record: substitution of order or commodity" [F23]

N035 · Now the bad case. The Milan order is cancelled. Nothing is exported. What rate does she pay?
> 1 S: the order card stamped "cancelled" · H: 3 s

N036 · The bank's domestic lending rate, from the date of the advance. Plus penal charges, if any, under the bank's policy.
> 1 C: card "no export → domestic rate from the date of advance + penal charges (bank policy)" [F25]

N037 · Let's feel the cost. Fifty lakh, for two hundred days. Domestic rate eleven per cent, packing credit rate eight and a half. The gap is two and a half.
> 1 S: sum card N2b (illustrative) · 2 K: 5000000 × 2.5 %

N038 · One lakh, twenty-five thousand. Times two hundred, divided by three sixty-five, equals. About sixty-eight thousand, four hundred ninety-three rupees more.
> 1 K: display 125000 · 2 K: × 200 ÷ 365 = → 68493.15 [N2]

N039 · One note from twenty twenty-five and twenty twenty-six. R-B-I allowed up to four hundred and fifty days, for credit given till the thirtieth of June twenty twenty-six. That relief has ended.
> 1 C: grey card "trade relief: up to 450 days, credit disbursed till 30 Jun 2026 · ended; new credit: 360 days" [F29, F30]

## Chapter 3 · After shipment

N043 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. No export at all: which rate, from when?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · After shipment" · H: 3 s

N044 · The domestic rate, from the date of advance. Now she has shipped. The bank finances the bill till the money comes. Three main forms.
> 1 N: "domestic rate, from advance ✓" · 2 S: the money lane right of the ship

N045 · It may buy, discount or negotiate her bill. It may lend against a bill sent for collection. Or lend against her duty drawback, the refund of duties.
> 1 C: card "post-shipment: bills purchased / discounted / negotiated · advance against collection bills · advance against duty drawback" [F31, F33]

N046 · For how long? A sight bill gets the normal transit period, N-T-P, set by F-E-D-A-I. For a foreign currency bill not under an L-C, twenty-five days.
> 1 C: card "sight bills: NTP (FEDAI) · FC bill not under LC: 25 days" [F34]

N047 · Many of you think N-T-P is the time for the ship to reach Milan. It isn't. It's the usual time for the money to come back after the bank buys the bill.
> 1 C: trap card "NTP = sailing time ✗ · = time for proceeds to arrive ✓" [F34]

N048 · And a usance bill? Up to three hundred and sixty-five days from shipment, including the transit time.
> 1 C: card "usance bills: max 365 days from shipment (incl. NTP and grace)" [F35] · 2 B: circle "365"

N049 · A few special cases. An undrawn balance, the last few per cent the buyer pays after checking the goods. The bank may finance it, on its judgement.
> 1 N: "undrawn balance: contingent · bank's judgement" [F32]

N050 · Retention money in a project abroad: only the supply part, never the services part. And goods sent on consignment, sold later abroad.
> 1 N: "retention money: supply portion only · consignment exports" [F32]

N051 · She can also borrow in foreign currency after shipment. The bank rediscounts her bill abroad, E-B-R, for usance up to a hundred and eighty days.
> 1 C: card "EBR: rediscount abroad · usance ≤ 180 days from shipment · with recourse is the norm" [F38]

N052 · Who pays for E-C-G-C cover on post-shipment credit? It protects mainly the bank. So the bank may bear the premium.
> 1 N: "ECGC (ECIB): premium may be borne by the bank" [F39]

N053 · And the interest rate? Each bank sets it, by its policy. For a small or medium exporter, a floating loan must be linked to an external benchmark, like the repo rate.
> 1 C: card "rate: bank policy · MSME floating loans: external benchmark (e.g. repo)" [F41]

N054 · The old interest equalisation scheme ended in December twenty twenty-four. Its successor is a pilot interest subvention for M-S-M-E exporters, passed on upfront.
> 1 C: grey card "Interest Equalisation Scheme: ended 31 Dec 2024" [F42] · 2 N: "now: EPM Niryat Protsahan subvention (pilot, MSME, upfront; EXIM Bank from 1 Apr 2026)" [F43]

N055 · Pause check for chapter three. One. N-T-P for a foreign currency sight bill, not under L-C?
> 1 S: card "NTP?" · H: 3 s

N056 · Twenty-five days. Two, the twist. A usance bill: the longest post-shipment period?
> 1 N: "25 days ✓" · H: 3 s

N057 · Three hundred and sixty-five days from shipment. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter three: sight bills get the N-T-P, usance bills up to a year.
> 1 N: "365 days ✓" · 2 S: the money lane; finished parts dim to grey
