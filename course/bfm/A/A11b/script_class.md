## Chapter 1 · Packing credit, and how much

N008 · Chapter one. A loan to an exporter before shipment has two names. Pre-shipment credit, or packing credit.
> 1 S: chapter title "1 · Packing credit, and how much" · 2 S: blue band "pre-shipment = packing credit" from "order" to "ship" [F2]

N009 · It pays to buy, process, make or pack the goods. For a service exporter, the working costs.
> 1 C: card "packing credit: buy · process · make · pack goods before shipment (or service working costs)" [F2]

N010 · R-B-I started this export finance scheme in nineteen sixty-seven, to give exporters working capital at rates like the world's.
> 1 N: "RBI export credit scheme: 1967 · internationally comparable rates · rupees or foreign currency" [F1]

N011 · What does the bank want to see first? Think.
> 1 S: an empty in-tray on the bank desk · H: 3 s

N012 · An L-C in her favour, or a confirmed, irrevocable export order. Or other proof of an order.
> 1 C: card "against: LC · confirmed irrevocable order · other evidence of an order" [F2]

N012a · Banks also check she has an I-E-C, and isn't on R-B-I's caution list or E-C-G-C's specific approval list. That's bank practice.
> 1 C: card "bank checks: IEC · not caution-listed · not on ECGC's Specific Approval List (bank practice)"

N013 · For exporters with a good record, banks shall waive showing each order. Orders exist; she just sends a regular list of them.
> 1 N: "good track record → order / LC waived; periodic statement of orders" [F9]

N014 · Some exporters must buy cotton in season, before any order arrives. For them there's the running account.
> 1 S: a cotton field, "season"; the blue band extends left of "order"

N015 · A running account goes further: packing credit before any order exists, for a good track record, or E-O-U and S-E-Z units. Bills mark off the oldest loan first.
> 1 C: card "running account: no prior order · good track record, EOU / SEZ · orders within a reasonable time · FIFO · not for sub-suppliers (they get PC through an inland LC)" [F8] · 2 N: "waiver: orders exist, list sent · running account: no order yet · FIFO = first in, first out"

N016 · The bank keeps a separate account for each packing credit, and releases it in one go, or in stages.
> 1 N: "separate account per PC · lump sum or stages" [F5]

N016a · If a maker supplies an export house, she can still get packing credit. The export house's bank opens an inland L-C for her.
> 1 C: card "sub-supplier: inland LC by the export-order holder's bank · first stage only · no running account" [F11]

N016b · And her pre-shipment and post-shipment limits are interchangeable. Banks must allow that.
> 1 N: "pre ⇄ post-shipment limits interchangeable" [F24]

N017 · Now, how much? R-B-I gives no formula. It says limits must be need-based, and not refused just for want of collateral.
> 1 C: card "RBI: limits need-based · not denied only for lack of collateral" [F24]

N018 · In practice, banks usually lend the lower of two numbers, the F-O-B value and her domestic cost, less a margin.
> 1 C: card "lower of FOB value and domestic cost, less margin" (label "bank practice · exam method")

N018a · The margin is her own share. The bank won't fund every rupee; she puts in some herself. This is the method exam sums use.
> 1 N: "margin = exporter's own share, not lent"

N019 · F-O-B, free on board, is the goods' value at our port. No freight, no insurance.
> 1 N: "FOB = value at the Indian port, without freight and insurance" (A07)

N020 · Her order is C-I-F a hundred and twenty-five thousand dollars. Freight and insurance cost five thousand. What's the F-O-B?
> 1 S: order card; "CIF 125,000" and "freight + insurance 5,000" · H: 3 s

N021 · A hundred and twenty thousand dollars. Many of you take the C-I-F value. Don't: freight and insurance aren't her export.
> 1 C: trap card "use CIF ✗ · use FOB ✓" · 2 B: cross out "125,000"; box "120,000"

N022 · Take a rate of eighty-five rupees, for illustration. Her domestic cost is ninety lakh, with a ten per cent margin. Work out the limit.
> 1 S: sum card "FOB USD 120,000 × ₹85 (illustrative) · domestic cost ₹90 lakh = 90,00,000 · margin 10%" · H: 8 s

N023 · F-O-B in rupees: one twenty thousand times eighty-five, one crore two lakh. The lower is the cost, ninety lakh.
> 1 S: keys "120000 × 85 = 10200000" · 2 B: box "lower: ₹90,00,000" [numericals S1]

N024 · Less ten per cent: ninety lakh times ten, per cent, nine lakh. Ninety minus nine: eighty-one lakh.
> 1 S: keys "9000000 × 10 % = 900000 · 9000000 − 900000 = 8100000" · 2 B: box "PC limit ₹81,00,000" [numericals S1]

N025 · So our opening question. Neither the full order, nor the full cost. The lower value, less her own margin.
> 1 G: both opening buttons dim; "lower, less margin" lights

N025a · She doesn't take it all at once. Yarn first, thirty-five lakh. Stitching, twenty-eight lakh. Each drawal in its own account.
> 1 S: drawals "₹35,00,000 · ₹28,00,000" stack under the ₹81 lakh bar [F5]

## Chapter 2 · Time, and paying it back

N029 · Chapter two. First, from chapter one. Which value do we never use for the limit?
> 1 S: chapter title "2 · Time, and paying it back" · H: 3 s

N030 · C-I-F. Now time. How long may her packing credit run?
> 1 S: a clock on the blue band · H: 3 s

N031 · The bank sets the period case by case: time to buy, make and ship. It can extend for a genuine delay, but never past the outer line.
> 1 N: "period: set by the bank per case · extension for genuine delay, within 360 days" [F3]

N032 · If no export bill closes it within three hundred and sixty days from the loan, it stops being export credit from the start. Exam word: ab initio.
> 1 C: card "not adjusted by export documents in 360 days → not export credit from the start (ab initio)" [F3] · 2 B: circle "360"

N032a · Released on the fifteenth of January twenty twenty-seven? Day three sixty is the tenth of January twenty twenty-eight. Miss it, and it fails from the fifteenth.
> 1 S: calendar "15 Jan 2027 + 360 days = 10 Jan 2028" · 2 N: "fails ab initio, from 15 Jan 2027" [F3]

N033 · And if she never exports at all? The bank charges its domestic lending rate, plus any penal charge in its Board policy.
> 1 C: card "no export → domestic lending rate + penal charges (Board policy)" [F4]

N034 · You may have read four hundred and fifty days. Banks could allow that, as trade relief, only on credit disbursed up to thirtieth June twenty twenty-six.
> 1 C: trap card "450 days: banks may allow, only for credit disbursed till 30 Jun 2026 · new PC: 360 ✓ · '180 days + extension': old books" [F25]

N034a · For any new packing credit, it's three sixty.
> 1 B: circle "360"

N035 · How is packing credit repaid? Many of you think, from her own pocket. Normally, it isn't.
> 1 S: the hand-over point at "ship"; blue band ends, green band begins

N036 · When she ships, the bank buys or discounts her export bill. Those proceeds repay the packing credit. The exam word is liquidation.
> 1 C: card "liquidation: proceeds of the export bill → pre-shipment turns into post-shipment" [F6] · 2 S: an arrow from the green band pays off the blue band

N037 · By agreement, she may also repay from her E-E-F-C foreign currency account, or her rupees, but only as far as she has exported.
> 1 N: "or EEFC balance / own rupees, to the extent exported" [F6] · 2 N: "EEFC = exporter's foreign currency account in India"

N038 · Hamburg cancels part of the order. Can a bill for a Dubai order repay this loan?
> 1 S: a second order card "Dubai (illustrative)" · H: 3 s

N039 · Yes, for a good-record exporter, if truly unavoidable. It's called substitution of contract. But not with sister or group concerns.
> 1 N: "substitution of contract: good record · commercially necessary · not for group concerns" [F7]

N040 · Pause check for chapter two. One. The outer limit for packing credit: three sixty or three sixty-five days?
> 1 S: card "PC outer limit?" · H: 3 s

N041 · Three sixty. Two, the twist. Her export never happens. Which rate?

N042 · Domestic lending rate, plus penal charges. [seriously] If you remember one thing from chapter two: the export bill repays the packing credit.

## Chapter 3 · After shipment

N043 · Chapter three. First, from chapter two. What normally liquidates, that is, repays, packing credit?
> 1 S: chapter title "3 · After shipment" · H: 3 s

N044 · The export bill. Now the green band, post-shipment credit. Three main forms. Can you name one?
> 1 S: green band with three empty slots · H: 3 s

N045 · One, the bank purchases, discounts or negotiates her export bill. Two, an advance against a bill sent for collection. Three, an advance against her duty drawback.
> 1 C: card "post-shipment: bills purchased / discounted / negotiated · advance against collection bills · advance against duty drawback" [F13] · 2 N: "margin on bills not under LC: bank practice (A12)"

N046 · Duty drawback is the Customs refund of duties on her inputs. The bank lends against it once Customs certifies it provisionally.
> 1 N: "duty drawback = refund of duties on inputs · advance once provisionally certified" [F19]

N047 · Let's repay her packing credit. She sold C-I-F, so her bill is the full hundred and twenty-five thousand dollars. Bill buying rate: eighty-four rupees eighty paise, illustrative.
> 1 S: sum card "bill USD 125,000 (CIF invoice) × bill buying ₹84.80 (illustrative)" · H: 8 s

N048 · One crore six lakh. Her packing credit, fully drawn, plus interest of, say, one lakh thirty-five thousand, is eighty-two lakh thirty-five thousand.
> 1 S: keys "125000 × 84.8 = 10600000" · 2 S: keys "8100000 + 135000 = 8235000" · 3 N: "PC ₹81,00,000 + interest ₹1,35,000 (given; working interest for days: A12)" [numericals S2]

N049 · So the bank keeps eighty-two lakh thirty-five, and credits her the rest: twenty-three lakh sixty-five thousand.
> 1 S: keys "10600000 − 8235000 = 2365000" · 2 B: box "to her account ₹23,65,000" [numericals S2] · 3 N: "transit-period interest: A12"

N049a · Suppose she ships only half: a bill of sixty-two thousand five hundred dollars. Proceeds, fifty-three lakh.
> 1 S: keys "62500 × 84.8 = 5300000" [numericals S2b]

N049b · That repays part. The other twenty-eight lakh stays as packing credit for her next shipment, inside the three sixty days.
> 1 S: keys "8100000 − 5300000 = 2800000" · 2 N: "interest aside · rest stays as PC, within 360 days" [F3, F6]

N050 · How long can post-shipment credit run? For a sight bill, the normal transit period. FEDAI sets it, for example twenty-five days for a foreign currency bill not under an L-C.
> 1 C: card "demand / sight bill → normal transit period (FEDAI), e.g. 25 days, foreign-currency bill not under LC" [F14; FEDAI 2.3] · 2 N: "NTP = time a bill normally takes to be paid"

N051 · For a usance bill, up to three hundred and sixty-five days from shipment, transit and grace included.
> 1 C: card "usance bill → max 365 days from shipment, incl. NTP and grace" [F14] · 2 B: circle "365"

N052 · Three sixty for packing credit, three sixty-five after shipment. That pair is a favourite trap.
> 1 C: trap card "PC 360 · post-shipment usance 365 (from shipment)" [F3, F14]

N053 · Two smaller forms, quickly. First, the undrawn balance. Some trades draw bills for ninety to ninety-eight per cent of F-O-B; the rest may be financed too.
> 1 N: "undrawn balance: bills for 90–98% of FOB; rest paid after the buyer checks; advance at bank's judgement" [F16]

N054 · Second, consignment exports, sent to be sold abroad later. They carry the same interest as cash sales.
> 1 N: "consignment: interest at par with cash-term sales" [F18]

N055 · Hamburg is late. Can she pay off the overdue loan from her own rupees? Yes. But her export entry stays open in E-D-P-M-S until Hamburg pays.

## Chapter 4 · In foreign currency

N059 · Chapter four. First, from chapter three. A sight bill's post-shipment period is called?
> 1 S: chapter title "4 · In foreign currency" · H: 3 s

N060 · The normal transit period. Now, her loan could be in dollars, not rupees: P-C-F-C, pre-shipment credit in foreign currency.
> 1 S: the blue band splits: "₹ PC" · "USD PCFC" [F22]

N061 · Why? Dollar loans often cost less than rupee loans. Her bank prices it on a dollar benchmark rate, and funds it from foreign currency deposits or lines abroad.
> 1 C: card "PCFC: any convertible currency · priced on an alternative reference rate (ARR) · funded from EEFC, FCNR(B), lines of credit" [F21, F22]

N062 · It covers domestic and imported inputs, for cash exports only. Same three sixty days. No export by then? It's settled at the T-T selling rate.
> 1 C: card "PCFC: domestic + imported inputs (imported inputs on supplier's credit → domestic inputs only) · cash exports · max 360 days · no export → TT selling rate" [F22] · 2 B: circle "TT selling"

N062a · Why selling? No dollars came in. To repay a dollar loan she must buy dollars, and the bank sells them.
> 1 B: arrow OUT, red (A01's counter)

N063 · After shipment, her export bill in dollars repays the P-C-F-C. Her bank discounts the bill, then sells it on to a bank abroad.
> 1 S: the bill travels from her bank to a bank abroad

N063a · That second discount is a rediscount. The scheme is E-B-R, Export Bills Rediscounting, mainly for usance bills up to a hundred and eighty days.
> 1 N: "EBR = rediscounting of export bills abroad · mainly usance ≤ 180 days from shipment" [F23]
