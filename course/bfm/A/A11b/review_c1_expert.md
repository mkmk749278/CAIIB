## Verdict: ADOPT WITH FIXES

Every rule, period and form in the script matches the current primary texts: 360 days PC (CF 235, 292), 365 days
usance from shipment (CF 270), NTP from FEDAI with 25 days for a foreign-currency sight bill not under LC (CF 269;
FEDAI 2.3), the three post-shipment forms (CF 266), domestic rate plus penal charges if no export (CF 293), PCFC for
cash exports, domestic and imported inputs, 360 days, TT selling if no export (CF 294–305), EBR up to 180 days (CF
346), and the 450-day relief dates (TRM 2025 para 10; TRM 2026 para 6). No outdated statement found. One High: the
spoken / shown key presses in N024 (and `numericals.md` S1 step 4) use 9 crore instead of 90 lakh, and the checker
did not catch it because it never checks the printed keys. Three Medium points on wording and coverage.

**On the "lower of FOB value and domestic cost, less margin" framing**: accurate. It is not in CF Chapter XV, not in
the old Master Circular, and not in FEDAI; RBI's rule is need-based assessment, not directly linked to collateral
(CF 389). It is the standard way bank textbooks and exam limit sums size packing credit, and the exam's PCL sums
(importance map: 5-mark sum, Jun 2023) expect it. Saying "banks usually" (never "RBI says") is the correct and safe
framing; it gives the same answer in any option list. The only risk is that the card "practice, not an RBI formula"
could make a learner hesitate when a question is worded "as per guidelines": finding 3 adds one line to close that.

## Findings

### High

1. **N024 (and `numericals.md` S1 step 4): key presses use the wrong number.** Ninety lakh is **9,000,000**, not
   90,000,000 (that is nine crore). The cue shows `90000000 × 10 % = 9000000 · 90000000 − 9000000 = 81000000`;
   both lines are wrong (9,00,00,000 × 10% = 90,00,000, and 9,00,00,000 − 90,00,000 = 8,10,00,000). A learner copying
   the keys gets ₹8.1 crore. The spoken words ("ninety lakh times ten, per cent, nine lakh") are right; only the digits
   are wrong. `tools/check/export_pcl.py` passes because it computes with 9000000 itself and never compares against
   the key strings in the script. **Fix**: N024 cue → `"9000000 × 10 % = 900000 · 9000000 − 900000 = 8100000"`;
   `numericals.md` S1 step 4 → `` `9000000 × 10 % =` → ₹9,00,000; `9000000 − 900000 =` → **₹81,00,000** ``. Also N023's
   box "lower: ₹90,00,000" is fine. Suggest (not required) that the checker parse the key strings from
   `numericals.md` so this class of slip is caught next time.

### Medium

2. **N034: "It has ended" is too absolute, and "a relief" hides that it was a bank option.** At the 31 Dec 2026
   cut-off the window for **new** disbursals has closed (TRM 2026 para 6: credit "disbursed till June 30, 2026"), but
   credit disbursed on or before 30 June 2026 may still run to 450 days, i.e. into late 2027, past the May/June 2027
   exam. Both directions say a lender "**may permit**" up to 450 days; the 2025 one only for eligible borrowers
   (listed sectors, outstanding and standard on 31 Aug 2025: TRM 2025 para 7). Card text is right; the spoken line
   should match. **Fix N034**: "You may have read four hundred and fifty days. Banks could allow that, as trade relief,
   only on credit disbursed up to thirtieth June twenty twenty-six. For any new packing credit, it's three sixty."
   (24 words.) Keep the trap card.

3. **N018 / N028: add one line so the "practice" label never costs an exam mark.** The framing is accurate (see the
   verdict note), but a learner may read "practice, not an RBI formula" as "not examinable". **Fix**: after N018 add
   (or append to N019's lead-in) "This is the method exam sums use, so use it whenever a question asks for the
   limit." Keep "banks usually"; do not say "RBI says". Keep the card label but shorten it to "bank practice · exam
   method" (RBI's own rule, need-based, stays on the N017 card).

4. **Depth-checklist items for A11b neither taught nor recorded as left out** (CLAUDE.md layer 4: "covers its
   video's 'Add to our plan' items or its review records why not"; `plans_A.md` §A11 items 9, 10, 13). Missing:
   (a) the **eligibility checklist** teachers give (IEC; not on RBI's caution list; not on ECGC's Specific Approval
   List); (b) **extension** of the PC period within the 360 days (CF 304 refers to "extension of rupee packing credit";
   CF 234 lets the bank set and so extend the period); (c) **margin on non-LC usance bills**. The fact sheet ("Leads")
   correctly says (a) and (c) are bank practice, not RBI rules. **Fix**: add one line after N012, e.g. "Banks also check
   she has an I-E-C, and isn't on R-B-I's caution list or E-C-G-C's specific approval list." (cue: card "bank checks:
   IEC · not caution-listed · not on ECGC SAL (bank practice)"), and one clause to N031: "The bank can extend it for a
   genuine delay, but never past the outer line." For (c), either add "for bills not under an L-C, banks may keep a
   margin" to N045's card, or record in the header's "Not in this video" line that it goes to A12 (discounting), with
   the reason.

### Low

5. **N013: "banks may waive"** → CF 390 says the bank "**shall waive**, ab initio" submission of order / LC for
   exporters with a good track record (with periodical statements). **Fix**: "For exporters with a good record, banks
   waive the order, and take a regular statement of orders in hand instead."

6. **N050: spoken line drops "not under an L-C".** FEDAI 2.3(b) gives 25 days only for foreign-currency DP / sight
   bills **not under LC** (LC and rupee bills differ). The card is right. **Fix**: "…for example twenty-five days for a
   foreign currency sight bill not under an L-C."

7. **N063: EBR "up to a hundred and eighty days" is the main band, not a hard cap**: CF 346 "mainly" usance bills up
   to 180 days from shipment (incl. NTP and grace); CF 347 allows beyond 180 days where FED permits longer usance;
   CF 315 also liquidates PCFC by a foreign-currency loan for DP bills. **Fix** (card only): "EBR: rediscount export
   bills abroad · mainly usance ≤ 180 days from shipment · or FC loan for sight bills". Voice can stay.

8. **N017: "not tied only to collateral"** paraphrases CF 389 loosely. **Fix**: "It says the limit must be need-based,
   not linked to collateral, and never refused only for lack of it."

9. **N061: "Dollar interest rates are usually lower"** is a market observation, not in RBI's text (CF 294: "at
   internationally competitive rates"). It is generally true for USD vs INR, so acceptable; for safety, "Dollar
   interest rates are usually lower than rupee rates, so she pays less" or use RBI's words "rates like the world's"
   (as N010 already does).

10. **N062: "domestic and imported inputs"**: true (CF 294), with one exception worth a card fragment: if her imported
    inputs come on supplier's credit, PCFC is only for domestic inputs (CF 301). Optional; add "(imported: not if on
    supplier's credit)" to the card.

11. **N047–N049: the bill proceeds are credited in full.** Under FEDAI 2.1(a) the bank recovers interest for the NTP /
    usance period upfront on purchase, so the exporter gets slightly less. The cue notes "transit-period interest:
    A12", but the voice does not. **Fix**: end N049 with "…nineteen lakh forty-one thousand, before the bank's
    transit interest. That's next class." (or label the result "before NTP interest" on the box).

12. **`practice.md` P6 (a)** "available for deemed exports of all kinds" is a good distractor (CF 335: only supplies to
    multilateral / bilateral-funded projects), but the reason line does not say why (a) is wrong. **Fix**: add "(a)
    PCFC for deemed exports only for multilateral / bilateral-funded projects (CF 335)" to the reason.
