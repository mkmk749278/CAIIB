## Verdict: ADOPT WITH FIXES
**High 0 · Medium 7 · Low 14.**

A clear, well-paced lesson with a good central picture: two umbrellas, hers over the buyer and the bank's over her.
"Her failure is the bank's risk" (N013a) is the line I'll remember, and N014's recall of A15's "policy vs cover"
lands it. Every chapter opens with a recall, has one misconception committed and broken, a two-question pause check
(easy, then a twist) and a "one thing" line; the report-of-default twist (N054a–N055) is the best check in the lesson;
the your-turn error line (N050a, "did you get twenty-two and a half lakh?") is exactly the mistake I would make; the
close fills the map from memory and answers the opening question. No line is over 25 words. What stops a plain ADOPT:
N028 contradicts the trap card four lines earlier (M1); chapter one fires six cover percentages in under a minute
(M2); chapter two has two different "four months" clocks that a beginner will merge (M3); the "which facility can't
EXIM Bank give?" question never gets a clean one-word answer (M4); N066 (NEIA) sounds the same as a line of credit
(M5); half the cue cards are paragraphs on a phone (M6); and the premium sum never says why "point one two" (M7).

## Three things I'd remember
1. The bank's cover (ECIB) protects the bank against **its own borrower**, the exporter; her policy protects her
   against the buyer. Individual packing credit cover: two-thirds.
2. The bank's clock: report default within four months of the due date or one month of recall, whichever is
   earlier; recall; claim within six months. Recoveries are shared in the loss ratio.
3. EXIM Bank: its own 1981 Act, Government-owned, RBI-regulated AIFI; lends (lines of credit, buyer's credit);
   ECGC insures, EXIM Bank lends.
